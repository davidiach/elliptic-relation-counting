# Research ledger · 27 November 2026

1. **Reconstruct the baselines.** Read the supplied 25-page paper, v4, the section-norm derivation and the relevant packet, sampler, projection and trace records. Compared immutable entry lifts, group-law branches and the two outer reductions. Recomputed the v4 constants. Checked the historical manifest (26 checkpoints, 122 files) and replayed all 56 registered v4 checks including the four archive integrations. Replayed the independent section-norm check on 276,077 normalized sections. These are finite checks of the inputs to the arguments.
2. **Try direct norm transfer.** Repeated or opposite points arise legitimately in the fixed-shift tuples. Replacing the v4 product by a product with repeated abscissas without sign-specific jets loses information. A finite divisor with local jets repairs the field predicate. The weighted split-algebra trace identity is exact but retains the whole address grid. COMPATIBILITY_MAP.md records both the transfer and its complexity.
3. **Try to split a lookup.** Across two equal address halves the first-order coefficient can be a full identity matrix. The multiwire bound and the finite-ring circuit count exclude a cheap universal single-graph replacement with the proposed degree cap. High-rank decompositions remain possible if their state space is paid.
4. **Change grouping and precision.** Verified the frontier mass $2^jS$ in the published fully distributed tree. Replacing the root's 64 groups by $\Theta(m)$ groups brings all terminals to the desired arity after one step. The price is fewer available Hermite levels, a factor $\sqrt{\log m}$ over the ideal expansion envelope. The entry proof carries forward the actual inherited precision. PARTIAL_EXPANSION.md gives the construction. This change saves explicit sibling distribution, but leaves the residual problem.
5. **Test M-matrix completeness.** Write $D$ for the nonnegative, column-sum-$w$ matrix and $t=1/(w+1)$. The power series $\log\det(I-tD)=-\sum_{j\ge1}t^j\operatorname{tr}(D^j)/j$ converges because $t\|D\|_1<1$. It gives $0<\det((w+1)I-D)\le(w+1)^N$. Smith form implies $r^\nu\mid\det$ for nullity $\nu$ over $\mathbb F_r$. One may include these rows first and reduce subsequent prime-field dimension cuts to at most $\nu$ (normalization may cut further). This does not change the asymptotic exponent. In $C_{11}$ take two blocks each containing all ten nonzero points. To represent $5x$ choose two copies of $4x$ from each block. The two orbits of multiplication by four may be scaled independently. For the single-point representation choose four copies of $x/4$; the same spurious scalings satisfy these rows. This is a block-respecting counterexample to deriving completeness from the abstract matrix conditions, not a statement about every sparse LZR seed.
6. **Use integer quadratic moments.** A two-pin sum computes each incidence Gram entry without arbitrary labels. Reducing an unweighted Gram modulo $r$ can lose rank through cancellation. The exhaustive $C_5$ fixture has rank five at all ones with six independent columns. This rules out the simplest fixed evaluation even for an actual shift system.
7. **Perturb the rows symbolically.** Distinct ordered addresses give a unique minimum-weight basis by the greedy prefix-rank property. Cauchy–Binet preserves its nonzero squared minor. This is a single-matroid use of classical determinant isolation; it does not claim a new abstract isolation lemma. Digit monomials factor over address chunks and two point pins suffice for every entry. A naive product of $\Theta(b)$ field representatives would require quadratic dyadic precision. Group the bits into $\sqrt b$-size pieces and reconstruct from small nonnegative digits instead. [Earlier determinant isolation for linear matroids](https://ics.uci.edu/~vazirani/NSV.pdf)
8. **Remove extension-field setup.** A single univariate challenge would have degree larger than the group order. Split the address into a bounded number of public base-$2^{\lfloor b/4\rfloor}$ digits. A product of two monomials assigns the row offset while preserving locality. Nonvanishing follows by formal substitution, even when integer addition would have produced a carry. Sampling from a power-of-two subset avoids rejection for fair-bit generation. Charge one shared challenge for all entries and the solve.
9. **Check arbitrary candidates directly.** A nonzero specialization of the signed one-pin polynomial admits deterministic bitwise extraction by additivity; zero is inconclusive. The full-rank matrix removes the need to pick fresh challenges for adaptive candidates. Verified the algebra and cancellation controls independently in checks/check_fixed_shift_gram.py.
10. **Attempt uniform specialization.** All-ones fails on a real fixture. Distinct binary weights isolate a basis but give degree exponential in $\beta$. Full grid enumeration and a literal extension large enough in *degree* for a transcendental-style test both exceed the bound. A short universal advice list exists by counting; its construction is uncharged only in the explicitly nonuniform statement. No uniform intermediate log-power saving is claimed.
11. **Audit the public Lean trail.** Located the main machine statement, its premise definition, the proved fixed-shift and filter entries, the proved conditional terminal result and separately open machine lemmas. Recorded digests of the displayed code. Accepted proof bodies and an axiom report were unavailable; separate public declaration templates do not decide their dependency closures.

13. **Test sparse polynomial consequences.** Constructed a prime-field list by deterministic primitive-root construction, integer slope separation and geometric progressions. Every surviving direction has no nonzero sparse consequence at the stated degree and term bounds. Determinant nonvanishing alone does not supply such a consequence.
14. **Use every ordered edge of the shift graph.** The dense one-per-block sumset gives a quantitative lower bound on violations. Comparing half-tuple differences characterizes the tables constant on each full fiber. A zero-sum argument then shows that near-complete subfamilies can retain the full row span and lose their entire evaluated contribution at a prescribed list. The exact complete-neighbor-fiber identity is the information this counterexample discards. Proofs and resource accounting are in DETERMINISTIC_POINTS.md.
15. **Check the stack branch by branch.** Individual-degree interpolation proves full-grid rank for $r>2$. The order-two curve over $\mathbb F_5$ gives unguarded rank five, with a concrete common kernel vector. Boolean multilinear coefficients recover the full rank of six on the fallback. The fixed no-wrap guard necessarily selects the full grid for $m\le45$.
16. **Sum over the displacement.** The complete fibers give independent source and target tuples after summing over $T$. Their block covariance has exact rank $N_0-m$. Selecting rank-increasing displacements by prefix counts meets the target cost. Arbitrary selected displacements have unknown logarithms, and the all-one sequence has a coordinate of maximal cyclic recurrence order for each nonconstant direction.
17. **Test the elementary weighted convolutions at fixed addresses.** A binomial Vandermonde bound controls the order at one of each slot factor in the group algebra. In the guarded regime their product always survives. A complete order-257 arithmetic control has a vanishing coefficient at $P$ but 254 surviving coefficients. NEIGHBORING_FIBERS.md records the precise scope of both conclusions.
18. **Keep the prescribed shift and sum all successors.** The actual Stage I collision energy bounds every single-slot pin in a largest block strictly below $r$. Every full tuple fiber consequently has a nonzero incidence moment, including fibers of zero mass modulo $r$. This proves exact rank after summing successors separately for each source and locates surviving contributions at more than $3r/8$ group values for any nonzero residual direction.
19. **Sum both fibers with two pins.** Retain only the source group value and one slot on each side. Each nonempty class has invertible mass.
