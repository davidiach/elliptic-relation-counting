# Joint-characteristic affine shadows and the limits of whole-list representations

27 November 2026. Abstract finite-group follow-up to PR #33 (`2081253f15db254b67799fee75509efac4d94cb8`) and the earlier additive avoidance and remote-point notes. The main new result is an exact constructive averaging algorithm for multiple auxiliary characteristics and supplied stratifications. A complementary family proves that every individual prime shadow can be useless while two jointly suffice, even with full small-coordinate projections and exponential exact unfolding ranks in every order. A further augmentation proves that the disjunction of exact small tensor ranks and unstratified affine shadows (even arbitrarily many) is not universal. These are statements about explicit abstract additive lists only. No universal avoidance solver, improved end-to-end discrete-logarithm exponent, or independent priority claim is made.

## 1. Shared integer lifts, not independent fieldwise carries

Let G=prod_{j=1}^d Z/p_j Z, where p_j>=2 (the local moduli need not be prime). Let A_i be m nonempty explicit lists of canonical integer residue words and C a nonempty explicit list of correction translates. The support to avoid is S=sum_i A_i+C in G. A_i may additionally be given as a union of nonempty classes A_{i,h}; enumerate the branches obtained by choosing one class per list. The branch count must be charged explicitly; we permit overlapping classes and may use a single branch. For each branch B and coordinate j, define L_j=sum_i min_{a in A_{i,h_i}} a_j, U_j=sum_i max_{a in A_{i,h_i}} a_j and W_j=U_j-L_j+1. These intervals contain every unwrapped sum in the branch. Use any finite list of distinct auxiliary primes Q_f, not necessarily disjoint from the local p_j, and any common coordinate order.

In each auxiliary field independently compute the affine space a_f+V_f containing all unwrapped branch sums modulo Q_f: a_f is the sum of chosen class references, V_f is the span of all within-class differences. Compute a reduced row-echelon basis of V_f, with pivot coordinates I_f in the chosen order. Then a vector in that space is parametrized by its values on I_f (subtracting the offset). At a nonpivot coordinate its value depends only on earlier pivot values, because the RREF basis rows whose pivots are later have zero entries there.

If a target z is represented by the branch, there is one c in C and ONE integer word u satisfying L_j<=u_j<=U_j, u_j=z_j-c_j mod p_j, and u mod Q_f in a_f+V_f for every f. Importantly the same integer u must serve all fields. Intersecting the separate relaxed supports after permitting different lifts in each field would be weaker. We call this joint condition the simultaneous shadow relaxation. It may contain false positives; every actual represented target satisfies it. All operations use canonical input integer coordinates, not a false homomorphism from Z/p_j Z to F_Q.

## 2. The deterministic simultaneous-shadow theorem

For each branch and coordinate define

    P_j = prod_{f: j in I_f} Q_f,
    D_j = prod_{f: j not in I_f} Q_f,
    a_j = min(P_j, ceil(W_j/D_j)/p_j),
    B_j = min(P_j, ceil(W_j/lcm(p_j,D_j))).                  (1)

Empty products equal one. The a_j are nonnegative rational weights; B_j are positive integer caps. Denote by L=|C| the number of correction shifts. The following sufficient guard permits avoidance of the entire union of branch relaxations:

    Psi_0 = L sum_B prod_j a_(B,j) < 1,
    K_0 = L sum_B prod_j B_(B,j) within the charged budget.  (2)

**Theorem.** Under (2), there is a uniform deterministic absent-point constructor with bit time polynomial in K_0, the explicit list/branch descriptions, d,m,max p_j, the number of auxiliary fields and their bit lengths. It requires no enumeration of the ambient group or of the full list product. It permits different information-coordinate sets in different fields and different branches. The guard can be tested using exact rational arithmetic before beginning the search. If the guard fails, the theorem promises nothing.

Proof. A state consists of a correction c and the partial RREF coefficients at already encountered pivot coordinates in every auxiliary field. Initially there are L empty states per branch. When trying a target coordinate z_j, enumerate the integers u in [L_j,U_j] congruent to z_j-c_j mod p_j. There are at most m+1 of them for canonical inputs, since the interval has length at most m(p_j-1)+1. At a nonpivot field the required residue of u is determined by the earlier coefficients. At a pivot field extend the coefficients by the residue of u minus the offset. Retain extensions consistent in all fields and deduplicate extensions of the same parent. Parent states are retained with multiplicity if necessary, which can only enlarge our count. An actual represented point keeps at least one state throughout.

For any parent state all nonpivot fields require one residue class mod D_j (CRT). For fixed z_j the acceptable integers lie in at most one residue class modulo lcm(p_j,D_j), or none if the congruences conflict. Each acceptable integer gives one pivot extension; there are at most P_j distinct extensions. This proves the B_j cap. Across all p_j choices of z_j each integer in the one D_j residue class is counted once (the fixed correction is a bijective translation). There are at most ceil(W_j/D_j) such integers. Independently each choice has at most P_j extensions. The average number of extensions of the parent state is therefore <=a_j. These inequalities do not require coprimality between p_j and D_j. They apply equally when no field has a pivot, when every field has a pivot, and when their pivot sets differ.

For a fixed target prefix through j, let n_B be the number of surviving states for branch B. Maintain the rational potential

    Psi(prefix)=sum_B n_B prod_{t>j} a_(B,t).

The expected potential after choosing the next coordinate uniformly is no greater than the current potential, by the per-state bound and linearity, separately for each branch. Try all p_j choices, keep one of minimum exact potential. Starting from Psi_0<1, the final potential is an integer <1, so no state survives in any branch. The chosen word is outside every branch relaxation and hence S. The number of live states at every prefix is bounded by K_0, since B_j>=1. We may stream the p_j alternatives and store only the current best and one trial. All per-state extensions cost polynomial work in the listed parameters, including the O(m) lift enumeration. Products and rational comparisons have polynomial bit lengths: the weights are min of an integer and an integer divided by p_j, so the denominator of each suffix product divides the corresponding suffix product of local moduli. All affine eliminations, corrections and class branches are explicitly charged. QED.

The algorithm extends the PR #33 single-field shadow, which fixes an information set first and then eliminates dependent coordinates. Here a global coordinate order may interleave different fields' pivots; a shared lift can sharply constrain an extension even at a pivot of another field. The weighted potential also combines different supplied affine-cover branches without a common information set. There is no claim that enumerating all class choices is always affordable, that the best fields/order/partition can always be found, or that failure of (2) means the relaxation fills G.

## 3. Explicit separation from every single-prime shadow

We construct a balanced CRT family on the same asymptotic scales as PR #33, with an exponential tensor-width obstruction and full small-coordinate projections. Every individual prime-field relaxation is the WHOLE ambient group, irrespective of whether its particular averaging guard is used. A joint two-prime certificate nevertheless works within polynomial explicit-input cost.

Use b=ceil(log2 r), L_b=log2 b, growing m=Theta(L_b^(1/4)), r odd prime, and choose the distinct primes from [b,4b] and product R as in PR #33. Thus r/(4b)<R<=r, there are s=Theta(b/log b) primes, d=2s coordinates, each prime appearing twice. The corrected support will include the global wrap list C of that note, so 0 in C and |C|<=m^2. The lists in the mixed product will be decoded to original plane points; no low-cost elliptic membership promise is made.

Choose distinct primes Q1,Q2=Theta(m^(4/5)), using Bertrand's postulate twice on disjoint intervals, and write J=Q1 Q2. Take a third prime Q0 between floor(b/(4J)) and twice that bound. For sufficiently large b it differs from Q1,Q2 and from all local primes. Put P=Q0 J. Then P=Theta(b), P<=b/2, Q0>d, log Q0=log b-O(log m), and m(P-1)>=max_f Q_f * max_j p_j - 1 for f=1,2 (not f=0). The last assertion follows from m/Q_f tending to infinity, P=Theta(b), and p_j<=4b. Let k0 be the least integer with Q0^k0>=r. We have s<=k0<d, k0 log2 Q0=b+O(log b), and (k0-s)log2 Q0=O(b log m/log b). For the latter bound use R>r/(4b) and s log2 Q0>=log2 R-s log2(4b/Q0). Choose d distinct nonzero evaluation parameters in F_Q0. Split the k0 consecutive powers into m groups of sizes differing by at most one. Let W_i be the evaluation code with coefficient support in group i. Every W_i projects onto every coordinate subset of size <=floor(k0/m), by the Vandermonde rank of consecutive powers at nonzero parameters.

Construct two small-field linear codes V_f subset F_Qf^d as follows, for f=1,2. Let t_f be the least integer with Q_f^t_f>=d, take the extension field with Q_f^t_f elements, set l_f=ceil(d/t_f), and evaluate polynomials of degree <h_f=floor(l_f/8) at l_f distinct extension elements. Expand in an extension basis, retaining the first d scalar coordinates (truncate the last word if needed). The code has dimension k_f=t_f h_f<=d/4 eventually: the first l_f-1 complete evaluation positions already determine a polynomial. Every projection onto at most h_f scalar coordinates is full, since it involves at most h_f extension evaluation positions, on which values can be prescribed by interpolation. These parameters grow, h_f tends to infinity, and t_f=O(log b/log m). Standard finite field construction here can be charged even by exhaustive search: Q_f^t_f<=Q_f d is polynomial in b, and testing monic polynomials by trial division through lower degrees is polynomial in that number. All code lists can be explicitly generated with polynomial work in their stated sizes and b.

For list i form the core E_i by independently choosing a word from W_i, V_1 and V_2. In each coordinate combine the three field elements by CRT into the unique integer in [0,P), a legal local residue. The core contains 0, and is injectively indexed by these three choices. Set

    A_i = E_i union {J e_j : 1<=j<=d},                      (3)

with e_j the ordinary coordinate vectors. All extra labels are legal because J<P<=min p_j. Each core has n_i^0=Q0^(size group i) Q1^k1 Q2^k2 elements. Define S0=(Q1^k1 Q2^k2)^m and M0=Q0^k0 S0. The core product is M0=r^(1+o(1)): log2 S0=O(b m log m/log b)=o(b), and the individual log sizes are O(b/m). Also min_i log2 n_i^0>=b/m-O(log b). Adding at most d labels per list changes the product by at most (1+d/min n_i^0)^m=1+o(1), so |C| prod_i |A_i|<R^2 eventually, with fixed exponent slack. List sizes are balanced to within the inherited logarithmic bound. All asymptotic statements concern sufficiently large b, not every finite parameter choice.

**Full small-coordinate projections.** Each core E_i projects onto [0,P)^a on any subset of a<=min(floor(k0/m),h_1,h_2) coordinates, by the independent projection properties and CRT. Since m(P-1)>=max p_j-1, each specified local residue on such coordinates is a sum of m integers in [0,P) independently; choosing the corresponding list projections realizes the entire target projection. Thus the actual sumset, even without corrections, has all such projections full. Here a tends to infinity. In particular the coordinate intervals have lower endpoint 0 and upper endpoint U=m(P-1) in every coordinate for the full-list branch. Adding the labels in (3) preserves those endpoints.

**Every single prime fails, even with exact compatibility tests.** For Q=Q_f, f=1,2, the affine shadow is exactly V_f: the cores supply every codeword and the added J e_j reduce to zero. For every target coordinate z_j, the interval [0,U] contains at least Q_f consecutive choices of the lift multiplier t in z_j+t p_j. Since gcd(Q_f,p_j)=1, these attain every residue modulo Q_f. Thus even the zero word of V_f is compatible with every target, using c=0. For any other auxiliary prime Q, the differences J e_j from zero span the whole F_Q^d (J is a unit), and the lifts u=z are in the intervals. So the single-prime shadow relaxation is exactly G for EVERY prime Q, not just primes below a fixed search cap. This argument covers local primes as well. Choosing a different coordinate order or information set cannot change that fact.

**The two-characteristic joint method succeeds.** Use fields Q1,Q2 together. In any common coordinate order their pivot counts are k1,k2<=d/4. For a coordinate with dependent product D_j<=J, W=U+1<=m P. Hence

    a_j <= ceil(W/D_j)/p_j <= m/D_j+1/min p_j <= 2m/D_j

for sufficiently large b (J<=m min p_j). It follows that

    Psi_0 <= |C| (2m)^d / (Q1^(d-k1) Q2^(d-k2))
           <= |C| [O(1) m^(-1/5)]^d < 1.

The exponent uses (4/5)*(3/4)*2=6/5. Also B_j<=ceil(W/p_j)<=m+1, so K_0<=|C|(m+1)^d=2^{O(b log m/log b)}=2^{o(b/m)}. The bit-time bound in section 2 is therefore polynomial in the explicit input size and b. The two auxiliary primes are not required as hidden advice for this family: try pairs of primes <=4b, compute spaces and check (2) with an explicit budget cap, stopping at the first success. A cap polynomial in the input size suffices eventually by these bounds; the polynomial search overhead is charged. This proves a joint-only algorithm on a genuinely different family, not a universal claim.

## 4. Exponential exact tensor ranks persist under the added labels

It is useful to verify that the preceding separation is not merely a low tensor-rank case already handled by PR #33. For a balanced cut with s coordinates on each side, consider first the count tensor of the core lists E_i alone. Fix any output projection on either side. Each fixed coordinate has at most C0=m+1 possible unwrapped totals. For any choices of such totals on the s positions, reduce modulo Q0: they specify s evaluations of the combined polynomial of degree <k0. By Vandermonde rank there are at most Q0^(k0-s) possible coefficient words. Its coefficient groups determine all W_i choices. Even ignoring every constraint over Q1,Q2, at most S0 choices remain. Thus each row or column of the core unfolding has total count at most

    B=C0^s Q0^(k0-s) S0.

The core has total mass M0. There are at least M0/B nonzero columns. After adding the correction C, the row-support cap is |C|B, and 0 in C guarantees at least M0/B nonzero columns remain. The rank of a matrix with row support <=h is at least its number of nonzero columns divided by h: a row basis's union of supports covers every nonzero column. This gives rational rank >=M0/(|C|B^2).

For exact coefficient-modulus reconstruction, take t coefficient primes whose product exceeds the full corrected tuple mass (in particular the core corrected mass); t is at most polynomial in b for the reconstruction scheme of PR #33. Every nonzero integer column of the corrected core survives in some field. By pigeonhole one field has at least M0/(B t) nonzero columns. The same row-support argument gives rank >=M0/(|C|B^2 t) in that field, for each balanced cut. Different cuts may select different fields. No guarantee is made for an individually fixed modulus.

The difference between the augmented corrected matrix and the core corrected matrix is a nonnegative integer matrix of total mass <= |C| M0[(1+d/n_min)^m-1], n_min=min_i n_i^0. Its rank over any field is at most this mass, since its integer nonzero entry count is at most the mass. The ratio of this bound to the core rank lower bound is at most 2 |C|^2 B^2 t m d/n_min eventually. It tends to zero: log2 B=O(b m log m/log b)=o(b/m) by m^2 log m/log b=o(1), whereas log2 n_min>=b/m-O(log b). Therefore rank(A+E)>=rank(A)-rank(E) gives rank >=M0/(2 |C| B^2 t)=2^(b-o(b)) after the augmentation. The same proof applies over Q with t=1. Any coordinate ordering has a balanced cut. Consequently no polynomial-explicit-size cap for the exact tensor train with polynomially many coefficient primes can succeed on the whole count tensor, in any order. This is a width obstruction, not a general computational lower bound. Marginalized tensors or other formats are not excluded.

## 5. A negative answer for *unstratified* whole-list shadows, and a stratified repair

One might hope that tensor compression or some number of whole-list shadows always suffices. This is false for the explicit class just studied. Further add each ordinary unit e_j to each list in (3). Now for every auxiliary prime Q the whole-list differences span all of F_Q^d, since they include 0 and e_j. The intervals still contain every canonical target coordinate z_j. For any number of auxiliary fields, including local moduli, the *same* lift u=z and correction 0 is compatible with every full affine space. Thus the simultaneous whole-list affine relaxation equals G, even if all primes are allowed. The proof in section 4 is unchanged with 2d instead of d in the augmentation bound. Small-coordinate projections remain full. The product bound and balanced list sizes remain unchanged asymptotically. This rules out the specific hoped-for universal disjunction of small exact tensor-train width or jointly low-dimensional whole-list affine shadows. It does not prove that a representation using other integer embeddings, restricted classes, nonlinear equations or a new algorithm is difficult.

In fact the stratified theorem above gives a constructive repair on this example *given* the natural partition into E_i and singleton extra labels. There are at most (1+2d)^m branches. In each, the two field spans are contained in V_f, because singleton choices only change offsets and never contribute differences. The coordinate intervals still have lengths at most mP, so (1) gives the same bound on each branch's potential as in section 3, even if the pivots move. Multiplying by (1+2d)^m preserves Psi_0<1: log of that factor is O(m log b)=o(d log m). The state cap is at most |C|(1+2d)^m(m+1)^d=2^{o(b/m)}. Thus the proof accommodates arbitrary affine offsets across branches and succeeds within polynomial explicit-input cost on the supplied cover. It does not infer a useful partition for arbitrary lists. For this deliberately constructed example the added labels are explicitly recognizable, but there is no general efficient structure-discovery theorem.

This distinction matters: even a counterexample to all global affine shadows does not refute the broader stratified approach. Conversely, being able to partition into singleton classes trivially always works if the full list product is affordable, but doing so in the target large-product regime would conceal the original enumeration cost. A useful universal theorem must bound both the number of class branches and their state caps. Such a theorem is not established here.

## 6. Exact verification and limitations

The selector is tested twice on the same branch in Z/3 Z. Both searches must return the sole absent residue and leave membership queries unchanged. With the potential fixed at 2/3, cap 1 must reject a state bound of 2 before search, and cap 2 must succeed.

Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python -B check_joint_shadows.py` (Python 3.10+, NumPy for the small unfolding fixture, assertions enabled). The transcript records 100 random tiny mixed groups, including different information sets, composite local moduli and auxiliary primes equal to local moduli. Direct Cartesian lift enumeration independently checks joint compatibility; actual count convolution verifies inclusion and all successful absent-point outputs. The averaging inequalities and intermediate state caps are checked exactly. Two classes with different pivot sets in each of 30 fixtures exercise the union potential; a separate nine-branch test uses two corrections. The joint-only repetition fixture has ambient 7^5=16807, 2401 tuples and 210 actual support points. Both individual prime shadows are exhaustively checked at all 16807 targets; full rank for other primes follows from the explicit scaled coordinate units, with finite rank checks for seven further primes. The joint method uses actual peak three states, returns (4,0,1,0,0), and the brute support confirms absence. A full-rank negative control adds units to just one list (enough to make all global spans full). The first attempted negative fixture added units to every list and violated the finite product guard (12^4>7^5); that assertion failed and was corrected, not counted as a successful test. The small CRT-code analogue uses local moduli 31, and fields 2,3,5; its three distinct balanced unfolding ranks are 404,404,403 over F101, checked by exact modular elimination on the full small matrix. Its joint absent point is verified against the actual 8535-point support. Empty large-field coefficient groups in that toy are not asserted to meet the large-parameter construction; the asymptotic separation follows from the proof above, not extrapolation from finite ranks. The shadow algorithm can also decline with a failed numerical guard; the random tests retain all 18 such cases. A second run of the complete verifier reproduced the transcript byte-for-byte.

Background: CRT, Gaussian elimination, Vandermonde interpolation, finite-field extensions, and conditional averaging are classical. This note extends the exact single-characteristic algorithm and width construction of PR #33; it makes no independent priority claim. In particular it does not assert a classification of all sparse mixed-modulus count tensors, all additive lists, or a general covering-radius theorem. No universal deterministic additive probe generator or improved end-to-end exponent follows. The actual remaining structural obligation is to find a uniformly affordable stratification (or a different representation entirely) for every relevant input, with explicit bounds on branches, state complexity, and all auxiliary arithmetic. Independent proof and literature-priority review remain outstanding. All tests are abstract finite-group controls.
