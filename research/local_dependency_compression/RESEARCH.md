# Local dependency compression and prefix-complete probes

27 November 2026. Abstract coding-theory follow-up to [PR #37](https://github.com/davidiach/elliptic-relation-counting/pull/37), reviewed at head `921c6e8e2f78e8d28708648825b8fe603c14aea8`, and main `13d2803e9f56e70d83d036a9ee426eba0d1f4221`. The related joint-characteristic work in PR #36 was inspected at the level of its stated results. This note proves a polynomial-size probe theorem under a checkable local-dependency factorization, strengthens the certificate to allow overlapping groups, and gives a connected high-short-rank counterexample to universal radius-preserving atom compression. It does not solve the unrestricted remote-syndrome problem or establish a new end-to-end discrete-logarithm complexity bound. All examples are abstract finite-field dictionaries, not elliptic-curve experiments. Independent proof and priority review remain outstanding.

## 1. Definitions and the main theorem

Let q be a prime, let H:F_q^K -> F_q^d be an explicit matrix, and let S_w(H)={Hc: wt(c)<=w} for integer w>=1. As in PR #37 let C_s(H) be the span of kernel words of weight <=s, with e_s=dim C_s. For finite-dimensional vector spaces V_i of dimensions r_i, set W=direct_sum_i V_i and let bw(v) count the nonzero blocks of v in W.

A **local-dependency lift** is a supplied factorization H=A P, with P:F_q^K -> W and A:W -> F_q^d, satisfying

    C_(3w)(H) subset ker P.                                      (1)

Since H=A P, the reverse containment ker P subset ker H is automatic. Suppose there is a certified integer b such that bw(Pc)<=b for every wt(c)<=w. For example if every column of P has block weight <=t, take b=tw; more sharply, sum the w largest individual column block weights, capped by the number of blocks. Let R_b be the sum of the b largest block dimensions, using all dimensions when there are fewer than b blocks.

**Theorem 1 (prefix-complete probes).** Let T be an ell-dimensional subspace with ell>R_b. If q>b, an explicitly constructed list E in T, depending only on its basis, q and b, satisfies E not subset S_w(H). For ell>b the list can be generated in deterministic time polynomial in ell,b,d,log q, and has at most

    ell [1 + 2 ((b+1)(ell-1)+1)^2] = O(b^2 ell^3)              (2)

nonzero projective representatives. If q >= (b+1)(ell-1)+1, replace the quadratic term in the square brackets by (b+1)(ell-1)+1. No factor exponential in ell, b, or log q is hidden in these bounds. For q<=b there is a separate explicit construction of size O(ell^3 q^(2b)), with time polynomial in that stated size and the ambient bit lengths. The output is a list containing an absent point, not necessarily a certificate identifying which member is absent.

The rank guard and (1) concern the supplied lift, not the nullity of H. In particular e_(3w) can be almost K. If T is not contained in im H, a basis of T already contains an absent point; when all basis vectors are covered, ell>R_b implies ell>b and there must be at least b+1 positive-dimensional blocks. We may assume this nontrivial case in the construction.

### 1.1 An explicit hyperplane-complement hitting list

We prove the auxiliary lemma directly; the field is prime to keep the bit-complexity statement elementary. The linear-algebra statement extends to prime powers given their field representation.

Put n=b+1 and M=n(ell-1)+1. Take the least positive k with Q=q^k>=M and a fixed extension F_Q of F_q. Choose M distinct elements t of F_Q, for example the first M in a coefficient-word enumeration. For each t and each normalized projective representative phi of the nonzero F_q-linear functionals F_Q -> F_q, take

    x(t,phi) = (phi(1), phi(t), ..., phi(t^(ell-1))).

For any n nonzero linear functionals f_1,...,f_n on F_q^ell, write their coefficients as polynomials p_i(Z) of degree <=ell-1. The union of their root sets contains at most n(ell-1)<M nodes. Some chosen t makes all p_i(t) nonzero. The forbidden phi for each p_i(t) lie in a proper homogeneous hyperplane of the k-dimensional dual space. A union of at most q homogeneous proper hyperplanes cannot cover the dual: since all contain zero, the union size is at most 1+q(Q/q-1)<Q. As n<=q, there is a phi with every phi(p_i(t)) nonzero. Normalizing it does not affect these tests. Thus the terminal list G hits the simultaneous nonzero set of every family of n nonzero forms.

The number of projective functionals is (Q-1)/(q-1), at most 2M by minimality of k, and is one if k=1. Hence |G|<=2M^2, or M in the latter case. If k>1 then q<M and Q<q M<=M^2. An extension representation can be found by enumerating monic degree-k polynomials and checking divisibility by all monic polynomials of degree at most floor(k/2). This takes polynomial time in Q,k,log q and is therefore polynomial in M. If k=1 there is no field setup and only M nodes are used, even for enormous q. All arithmetic and vector output costs are included. This proves the resource statement for G; duplicate and zero terminal vectors can be removed.

For q<=b, it suffices below to hit families of n **linearly independent** forms. Replace M by ((q^n-1)/(q-1))(ell-1)+1. All nonzero projective linear combinations of the n coefficient polynomials are nonzero. By the root bound, at some chosen t the values p_i(t) are linearly independent over F_q. There is then a linear functional phi with phi(p_i(t))=1 simultaneously. The same construction and size proof apply with the new M. The bound is exponential in b in this regime; we do not describe it as polynomial in b.

When q>=M in the first construction, the ordinary moment-curve terminal list of length M is optimal for hitting n arbitrary homogeneous hyperplanes: any set of <=n(ell-1) points can be divided into n groups of size <=ell-1, each contained in a proper hyperplane. This is an optimality statement for the terminal hitting condition, not for the full prefix list or for all avoidance algorithms.

### 1.2 Force witness linearity using only prefixes

Fix a basis u_1,...,u_ell of T, and identify coefficient vectors with points in T. E consists of the basis directions and all nonzero prefixes of every x in G, retaining one normalized projective representative per direction. A prefix of x is x^(j)=(x_1,...,x_j,0,...,0). Normalize relative to the basis. E has at most ell(1+|G|) elements, giving (2); the normalized prefix property survives projective deduplication.

Suppose all E is covered by S_w(H). For each represented projective direction choose a witness f of weight <=w and extend homogeneously to its scalar multiples, with f(0)=0. The prefix identities in the ambient space give kernel words

    f(x^(j)) - f(x^(j-1)) - x_j f(u_j)

of weight at most 3w (including when a prefix is zero or repeated). By (1), P annihilates every such word. Therefore for each terminal vector x,

    P f(x) = sum_j x_j P f(u_j) = L(x),

where L:F_q^ell -> W is a linear map. Also A L sends the basis to u_j and hence is the natural isomorphism onto T. Thus L is injective, and all terminal vectors have bw(L(x))<=b.

Choose ell independent coordinate functionals among the rows of L in arbitrary bases for the blocks. Their coordinates cannot occupy at most b blocks: those blocks have total dimension at most R_b<ell. Choose n=b+1 independent coordinate functionals from distinct blocks. The terminal hitting list contains a point where every chosen functional is nonzero (using either field regime of section 1.1). At that point bw(L(x))>=b+1, contradiction. QED.

The essential change from PR #37 is that a full subspace need not be covered to force the relevant witness identities. A polynomial number of carefully shared prefixes suffices when q>b. The proof is a universal conditional list guarantee; it never computes a sparse witness or assumes an oracle for one. In the degenerate case b=0 all supplied columns have zero lift, hence H=0 and any nonzero point suffices.

## 2. Locality, compression and checkable certificates

### 2.1 Column partitions and a consequence for small short rank

For a partition of the columns into groups H_i, take V_i=span H_i, P(c)=(H_i c_i)_i, and A addition. The block budget is b=w. Condition (1) says exactly that every short kernel word has zero sum within every group. The dimensions r_i can be much smaller than the group sizes, and the block budget does not grow with r_i. Replacing each atom with an arbitrary expansion in a local basis would not have this property: it could cost up to r_i ordinary atoms. We count nonzero groups instead. We are not claiming that S_w(H) equals the entire block-weight ball after compression.

If E0>=e_(3w)(H) is a certified bound, the union J of supports of a short-word basis has size <=3w e_(3w). All short words are supported on J. Use J as one group, and singleton groups for the other columns. Since ker H_J contains that entire short-word space,

    rank H_J <= |J|-e_(3w) <= (3w-1) E0.

For E0>0 this gives R_w <= (3w-1)E0+w-1 (or use w as the bound if J is empty). In particular ell>max(w,(3w-1)E0+w-1) is sufficient; for E0=0 use ell=w+1. The list construction need not know J if this numerical rank guard is certified. For q>w and e_(3w)=0, the new bound is O(w^5) projective probes, compared with the previous full-subspace enumeration of (q^(w+1)-1)/(q-1) directions. This improves that particular previous probe bound; it does not assert that every e-bound allows a small list.

### 2.2 Disjoint information sets certify locality without enumerating short words

Let V_i now be arbitrary supplied subspaces of F_q^d, with their explicit bases. Let U_i be the union of their coordinate supports. An information set I for V_i is a set of r_i rows on which restriction is injective. Obtain disjoint information sets I_(i,1),...,I_(i,h_i) by repeatedly finding a full-rank row basis among the remaining rows and removing it. A nonzero v in V_i has a nonzero entry in each of these sets.

Given an integer s, repeatedly remove any active group i whose h_i is strictly greater than the sum of the largest s-1 counts

    o_ij = #{a : I_(i,a) intersects U_j}

among other active groups j (use all if fewer). Rank-zero groups may be removed immediately. Call the remaining groups J. For every equation sum_i v_i=0 with v_i in V_i and at most s nonzero v_i, all removed groups must have v_i=0. Indeed if a removable group i were nonzero, at most s-1 other active groups can touch fewer than h_i of its information sets; on an untouched set the sum cannot vanish. Induction on rounds justifies simultaneous removals. The aggregate contribution of the surviving groups is then also zero. This proves the claim without assuming that the surviving core is small or independent.

For an actual partition of dictionary columns, apply this test with s=3w and merge the remaining groups into their combined span. The resulting partition satisfies (1). More generally suppose an explicit factorization H=A P represents each atom as a sum of at most t contributions in the supplied subspaces V_i (which may overlap). For a kernel word of weight <=3w, the equation among subspace contributions uses at most 3tw groups. Apply the same test with s=3tw, then coarsen the lift by replacing the remaining group coordinates by their aggregate image in the span of all remaining groups. Use a basis of that span for the new block. All short words are annihilated by this coarsened P, and the per-atom block weight is no larger than t. This yields a verified factorization satisfying (1), regardless of long dependencies between groups. It is also possible to certify (1) by any independent proof that every selection of at most 3tw of the original subspaces is a direct sum. That sufficient condition does not require independence of the entire dictionary.

Greedy row-basis extraction, support counting, at most g rounds of sorting overlaps, and computing the remaining span cost polynomial time in the explicit inputs, including g,d,K,log q. Verification of H=A P and the column block budgets is explicit linear algebra. No enumeration of sparse coefficient patterns is charged in the algorithm. Failure of the resulting R_b guard means only that this certificate did not suffice.

### 2.3 High-short-rank incidence examples

For a prime p, take affine lines in F_p^2 as group supports, coefficient field F_q with q>=p prime, and identify the p positions on each line with distinct elements of F_q. On that line let V_i be evaluations of all polynomials of degree <a, extended by zero outside the line, where 1<=a<=p. Each dimension is a, any a rows on the line form an information set by the Vandermonde determinant, and distinct lines meet in at most one point. Thus h_i>=floor(p/a), and all groups peel at once for s=3w when floor(p/a)>=3w. Supply one normalized representative of every nonzero projective point of V_i as the atoms of that group. The group size is (q^a-1)/(q-1), and its kernel is generated by words of weight <=3: fix a group basis of atoms, express any other atom by successive partial sums of basis multiples, and use the atom representing each nonzero partial sum. Each step gives a relation of at most three atoms. This proves that every atom equals its basis combination modulo short words, hence generates the full local kernel. All local kernels together are independent in coefficient coordinates, and all short words are local by the certificate. Consequently e_(3w)=K-g a exactly, where g=p(p+1). Even very large short-dependency rank is harmless here.

In the tested p=q=13,a=2,w=2 case, d=169 redundant row coordinates, span rank 103, g=182, K=2548, and e_6=2184. Select an ell=5 subspace inside the actual dictionary span, so the question is not trivialized by a missing direction outside that span. The new list has 53 distinct projective probes and contains an exactly checked absent point. Applying PR #37's older integer bound q^ell>B_q(ell+2184,2) within the same span first succeeds at ell=8, using 67,977,560 projective probes. This is a comparison of two sufficient list constructions, not a benchmark against all possible algorithms. The p=q=7,w=1 case similarly has 448 atoms, e_3=336, rank 34 and 11 probes versus 400 by the old bound. These finite examples are not claimed to realize every asymptotic input scale.

## 3. Recognizing groups despite a connected short-dependency graph

The preceding certificate can work even if a partition respecting every short circuit is impossible. Here is one explicit recognition theorem; it does not claim that arbitrary high-short-rank dictionaries have its structure.

Suppose q>=3 and an abstract direct sum W=direct_sum V_i has r_i>=2. Form a dictionary in W containing every normalized projective point of every V_i and at most one bridge direction for each unordered pair of distinct blocks. A bridge has nonzero components in exactly those two blocks. Apply an unknown linear map A that is injective on the sum of any at most six blocks. Normalize the images projectively and forget their grouping. All directions remain distinct and nonzero. Every collinear triple of dictionary directions after mapping lifts to a collinear triple in W: its relation involves at most six blocks, so is preserved by injectivity. Conversely formal collinearity is preserved by A.

**Lemma.** Every projective line containing at least four dictionary directions lies wholly in one original block. To verify this in W, consider a line spanned by two dictionary points. If both are core points in one block, all line points stay there. If they are core points in different blocks, the line contains at most two core directions and the one bridge direction on that pair. A core point and a bridge either have disjoint supports (no mixed nonzero combination is an atom) or share a block. In the shared-block case, at most one mixed direction can cancel that block, and at most one bridge on the two blocks exists; there are at most three dictionary directions in total. Two bridges with different pairs either have disjoint supports, or share one block. In the latter case at most one mixed direction cancels that block; all other mixed directions use three blocks and are not dictionary atoms. Again there are at most three. Distinct bridges cannot share the same pair by hypothesis. This proves the lemma. The lifting of triples means that every dense line of the mapped dictionary has the same incidences in W, proving the claim after A as well.

Compute all two-dimensional spans of pairs of explicit directions using reduced row echelon forms, record their point sets, and union the indices on every projective line with >=4 points. Each original full projective block is one recovered component because q+1>=4 and every two core directions lie on an internal complete line. Every bridge is an isolated component. Compute bases of the recovered core subspaces, then for each leftover direction try all pairs of cores for a linear decomposition. A bridge has a unique pair, by injectivity on at most four blocks. This recovers a two-block lift of every dictionary atom in polynomial explicit-input time (pair spans and pair membership tests, with full linear-algebra costs). Even when the recognition promise is not known to hold, candidate lifts can be verified, and the locality tests of section 2.2 remain sound; do not infer the independence promise from dense lines alone.

If A is in addition injective on sums of at most 6w blocks, the recovered lift annihilates C_(3w), since each atom uses at most two blocks. Theorem 1 applies with b=2w, including when the three-circuit graph is connected by bridges. An explicit family uses V_i of dimension a encoded as vectors (1,t_i,...,t_i^(s-1)) tensor F_q^a for s=6w and distinct t_i in F_q, with g>s and a>=2. Any s groups are independent by Vandermonde, although the entire family is dependent. This uses q>=g. For a supplied such layout, independence follows from the elementary formula; the recognition algorithm alone does not automatically certify that layout on arbitrary data. In the finite fixtures, every s-group subset is independently rank-checked, with the enumeration explicitly charged. For general inputs use a supplied structural certificate or the polynomial peeling criterion, not an uncharged enumeration of all small group sets. The examples with 103 and 265 atoms recover all 8 and 14 groups exactly even though every atom belongs to one connected component under three-point collinearities. The overlapping lift gives certified lists of 110 and 2793 probes respectively. These are positive controls for the theorem, not an algorithm for every connected circuit graph.

## 4. A sharp obstruction to using short rank alone for atom compression

The high e_s branch cannot generally be resolved merely by finding a smaller dictionary with the same sparsity radius. We prove a stronger existential obstruction with connected short circuits and nearly maximal sparse-span cardinality at every radius.

**Theorem 2.** Fix 0<alpha<1. Let integer w tend to infinity, q be primes with q/w tending to infinity, and d/w^2 tend to infinity. There exist explicit-in-the-sense-of-listed dictionaries of K projectively distinct nonzero vectors in F_q^d, with log_q K=(alpha+o(1)) d/w and full ambient rank d, such that

    e_(3w)(H) >= (1-o(1))K,
    |S_j(H)| >= (1-o(1)) B_q(K,j) for every 1<=j<=w,

where the errors are uniform in j. Their minimal circuits of size <=3w connect the whole dictionary. Thus any partition satisfying local short-dependency splitting must put the whole dictionary in one group of rank d, and the partition rank guard fails. Furthermore, if any replacement H' with K' columns satisfies S_w(H) subset S_w(H'), then K'>=(1-o(1))K. If the replacement uses a different radius w', the actual cardinality still forces B_q(K',w')>=(1-o(1))B_q(K,w). These statements limit radius-preserving atom compression and volume bounds; they do not rule out overlapping lifts, other structured certificates or general avoidance algorithms.

**Construction and proof.** Set a=2w and L=floor(q^(alpha d/w)/(q+1)), K0=L q, K=K0+L-1. Work first in a formal direct sum of L spaces F_q^a. In each group use the q vectors (1,t,...,t^(a-1)), t in F_q. Any a of them are independent by Vandermonde. Use the t=0 atom of each group as its designated atom; add a bridge equal to the sum of the designated atoms of each adjacent pair of groups. Every group has q-a independent fundamental relations, each of support a+1, relative to a chosen a-atom basis. The L-1 bridge relations have support three. Together these K-La relations are independent: each has a distinct nonbasis or bridge pivot coordinate, with no other relation using that pivot. They remain relations after any linear map of the ambient formal space. They already force e_(3w)>=K-La=(1-o(1))K. No short relation of weight <=2w occurs among the unbridged formal core atoms, since every individual group has minimum dependence length a+1 and the formal groups are a direct sum.

Choose a uniformly random linear map from F_q^(La) to F_q^d. With probability tending to one it has full row rank (failure <=q^(d-La)), is injective on each local a-space (failure <=L q^(a-d)), and maps all intended directions to projectively distinct nonzero directions. For the last claim, all one-sparse coefficient patterns in the formal dictionary are distinct, and a collision of any pair after mapping has probability q^(-d); the union bound is <=binom(B_q(K,1),2)/q^d. All bounds tend to zero under the parameters. Under these events, the local fundamental circuits remain minimal (the local map is injective) and every bridge relation is a minimal three-circuit. The connectedness follows by the shared basis atoms within each group and the chain of bridges. Any minimal circuit meeting two partition groups violates (1) for the partition, since a nonempty proper subset of a minimal circuit cannot itself sum to zero with those nonzero coefficients. Hence a valid partition is the single whole-dictionary group, with rank d.

For every j<=w the core-only patterns of weight <=j are pairwise distinct in the formal space. Let B_j=B_q(K0,j), and let Z_j count unordered colliding core patterns after the random map. Then E Z_j<=B_j^2/(2q^d). The normalized potential sum_(j=1)^w Z_j/B_j has mean at most w B_w/(2q^d). Take delta=sqrt(w B_w/q^d)=o(1): the latter assertion follows from B_w<=(1+(q-1)K)^w<=q^((alpha+o(1))d), since w=o(d). By Markov, with probability at least 1-delta/2 every j has Z_j<=delta B_j and hence at least (1-delta) B_j distinct core syndromes. At most a fraction w(L-1)/K<=w/q=o(1) of all coefficient patterns of weight <=j touch a bridge (condition on the support size and use the union bound). Thus B_j >= (1-w/q)B_q(K,j). All the required events occur simultaneously with probability 1-o(1); select a successful map. This is an existence theorem with an explicit randomized sampler and analytical success bound, not a deterministic seed-selection or a cheap global verification algorithm. The sample can be listed using polynomial work in its explicit size. The asymptotic parameter choices may use real-valued exponents as mathematical definitions, not an uncharged bit algorithm.

Finally |S_w(H')|<=B_q(K',w), giving the requisite cardinality lower bound. For K'<=c K with fixed 0<c<1, each binomial ratio at weight w is <=c^w, and the lower weights of B_q(K,w) have vanishing relative mass: the ratio of consecutive terms below the top is at most w/((K-w+1)(q-1))=o(1). Thus B_q(K',w)/B_q(K,w)<=c^w+o(1), incompatible with the near-maximal lower bound. This proves K'>=(1-o(1))K. Changing the radius cannot reduce its sphere-counting upper bound below the actual span cardinality either. QED.

Five finite projected-code fixtures exhibit connected short circuits and exact sparse-span counts. In all five the exact cardinality bound alone forbids deleting even one atom from any radius-preserving replacement. For example q=17,w=2,K=53,d=11 has at least 41 independent short words and 352,529 distinct syndromes out of 353,617 coefficient patterns. A dictionary with 52 columns at the same radius has insufficient sphere volume. The small fixtures illustrate the finite count obstruction, not the full asymptotic concentration theorem.

## 5. Verification, literature and scope

Run `python -B verify.py` with Python 3.10+ (standard library; assertions enabled). The recorded `verification.txt` checks 19,572 hyperplane families (exhaustive small cases and seeded larger cases), 8,329 exact small partitions, 695 with locality, 360 triggered partition certificates and 94 covered controls outside the rank guard. It checks the information-set peeling examples, 60 overlapping lifts with 2,274 exact short-word checks and 52 triggered certificates, two automatic dense-line reconstructions including 119 independently checked maximal block subsets, and 523,301 sparse coefficient patterns in five connected projected-code fixtures. The two large affine-line examples test actual absence of one probe by explicit one- or two-atom membership in the toy dictionaries. The general algorithm does not require those enumeration steps. The second run reproduced the transcript byte for byte. None of the finite fixtures certifies the asymptotic random sampler by exhaustively searching all maps; that is proved analytically. All three proof/script/transcript files are intended as a standalone research contribution.

Relevant classical background: homogeneous subspace covering (the q+1 lower bound is proved directly here; see also Mohsen Aliabadi, [Extremal subspace covers in finite vector spaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07051), 2026); information sets and covering radius (references in PR #37); Vandermonde/MDS codes. Explicit subspace designs and evasive sets concern related but different pseudorandomness goals: Guruswami and Kopparty, [Explicit Subspace Designs](https://www.math.toronto.edu/swastik/subspace-designs.pdf), and Brakensiek et al., [Explicit Subspace Evasive Sets via Subspace Designs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14909), 2026. Neither is invoked to solve unrestricted sparse-span avoidance. The elementary root bound, hyperplane union bound, MDS fact and support peeling are classical in isolation; no independent novelty claim for their combination is made.

**Status.** The new result exploits large local short-dependency rank, with no sparsity inflation for a true partition and controlled block-weight inflation for overlapping lifts. Prefix-complete probes yield a substantial improvement over full projective subspace enumeration. Recognition succeeds under explicit full-projective-block/bridge promises; locality can be checked separately. The connected-code obstruction proves that high short rank alone cannot imply useful radius-preserving atom compression or a useful partition. Discovering an affordable overlapping lift or some other certificate for arbitrary dictionaries remains the unresolved constructive branch. There is no general remote-set algorithm or improved end-to-end ECDLP exponent in this note; the main paper is unchanged.
