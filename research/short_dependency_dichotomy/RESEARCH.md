# Short-dependency dichotomies, certified peeling, and generic planted sparse spans

27 November 2026. Abstract finite-field and coding-theory follow-up to [PR #25](https://github.com/davidiach/elliptic-relation-counting/pull/25) (digit lift), [PR #27](https://github.com/davidiach/elliptic-relation-counting/pull/27) (high-growth prescribed covers in the plane), [PR #28](https://github.com/davidiach/elliptic-relation-counting/pull/28) (structured remote points), [PR #30](https://github.com/davidiach/elliptic-relation-counting/pull/30) (graded polynomial spaces), and [PR #31](https://github.com/davidiach/elliptic-relation-counting/pull/31) (thin-sample determinantal geometry). Reviewed main `a5807f4880e0206f43fb1fcf2ab66bc4cefd8512` and the description of open PR #33's mixed-CRT and tensor methods. The purpose is to examine a proposed structured/unstructured dichotomy. The positive result is an exact projective-probe criterion in terms of the *rank of the space generated by short dependencies*, including an efficiently checkable upper-bound certificate. Failure of a subspace probe forces many independent short dependencies. A separate planted-cover theorem shows that nearly maximal sparse-span growth, small Fourier bias, strong polynomial completeness and the failure of all surjective affine low-rank matrix layouts do not, by themselves, guarantee that a prescribed short probe list escapes. These are abstract statements, not a general solution to sparse-span avoidance, a new end-to-end ECDLP complexity claim, or a cryptographic experiment. Independent priority and proof review remain outstanding.

## 1. Short-dependency rank: an exact two-sided subspace theorem

Let F_q be a finite field and let H:F_q^K -> F_q^d be an explicitly specified matrix with columns h_i. Write wt for Hamming weight and define

    S_w(H) = { Hc : wt(c) <= w },
    B_q(n,w) = sum_{j=0}^{min(n,w)} binom(n,j)(q-1)^j,
    C_s(H) = span_{F_q}{ c in ker H : wt(c) <= s },
    e_s(H) = dim C_s(H).

The dimension e_s counts independent short kernel words, not their total number. It can be much smaller than dim ker H even for a high-rate code. These definitions allow repeated columns and zero columns, although the separate construction in section 3 will have projectively distinct nonzero columns.

**Theorem 1 (local defect rank).** Let T be an ell-dimensional linear subspace of F_q^d, and suppose T is contained in S_w(H). For *any* choice of w-sparse witnesses f(x) with Hf(x)=x for x in T, taking f(0)=0, put F=span{f(x): x in T}. Then the kernel of H restricted to F is generated by words of weight at most 3w. In particular, writing e=dim(F intersect ker H),

    dim F = ell+e,    e <= e_(3w)(H),
    q^ell <= B_q(ell+e,w) <= B_q(ell+e_(3w)(H),w).             (1)

There is an explicit extraction of a basis for this kernel from the supplied witnesses, using at most q^ell-1 short words before elimination. If only projective witnesses are supplied, one may use just (q^ell-1)/(q-1)-ell short words before elimination, and their rank still satisfies (1). No decoding algorithm is assumed when witnesses are not supplied.

Proof. Fix a basis b_1,...,b_ell of T. First include the defects

    f(a b_i) - a f(b_i),   a in F_q^*, i in [ell],

of weight at most 2w. For each x=sum_i a_i b_i with at least two nonzero coordinates, choose one of its nonzero coordinates i and include

    f(x) - f(x-a_i b_i) - f(a_i b_i).

Each defect is a kernel word of weight at most 3w. Let U be their span. Induction on the number of nonzero coordinates of x shows that f(x)=sum_i a_i f(b_i) modulo U. Hence F/U is spanned by ell vectors, whose syndromes are independent. Since U is contained in F intersect ker H and H(F)=T, equality of dimensions gives U=F intersect ker H. This proves the first assertions.

For the cardinality inequality, choose an information set of F: ell+e coordinate positions such that restriction F -> F_q^(ell+e) is a linear isomorphism. Such positions exist by Gaussian elimination on a generator matrix. The q^ell distinct witnesses remain distinct after restriction; their weights cannot increase. At most B_q(ell+e,w) vectors of the restricted length have weight <=w. This gives (1).

For projective witnesses, represent each nonzero direction of T by the vector whose first nonzero basis coordinate is one. Choose a witness for each representative and extend homogeneously: f(a x)=a f(x). This preserves both weight and syndrome, also setting f(0)=0. The scalar defects vanish. For a representative x which is not a basis vector, let i be its first nonzero coordinate and use the single defect f(x)-f(b_i)-f(x-b_i). For arbitrary nonzero scalar multiples this identity follows by scaling, so the same induction proves the claim. There are exactly (q^ell-1)/(q-1)-ell such representatives. QED.

This is an information-set version of a sphere-counting inequality, with a local short-word generation theorem in addition. Information sets and sphere-counting bounds are standard in coding theory; see Cohen, Karpovsky, Mattson and Schatz, *Covering Radius — Survey and Recent Results*, IEEE TIT 31 (1985), [PDF](https://sites.bu.edu/mark/files/2018/02/78-covering-radius.pdf). The claim here is the short-defect characterization of the witness-generated kernel, its use for projective subspace probes, and the constructive certificate in section 2; no independent priority claim is made.

### 1.1 Quantitative consequences and near-sharpness

If ell>=w>=1 and n=ell+e, then

    B_q(n,w) <= (euler*n*(q-1)/w)^w,

where `euler=exp(1)`. Indeed choose z=w/[n(q-1)]<=1, upper-bound the partial sum by z^(-w)(1+(q-1)z)^n, and use (1+w/n)^n<=exp(w). It follows from (1) that

    e >= w q^(ell/w)/(euler*(q-1)) - ell.                   (2)

Use the exact integer bound (1) rather than the relaxed real-valued estimate if the latter is negative. For ell=cw, the required dimension has leading scale w q^c/(q-1) whenever that term dominates ell. This is not merely a lower bound on the number of dependencies: it forces independent short dependencies, supported on the columns used by the witnesses.

For w=1, take one normalized vector for each line of an ell-dimensional space T. Then K=(q^ell-1)/(q-1), T=S_1(H), and e=K-ell for the indicated witnesses. This attains (1) with equality. For general w, take the direct sum of w copies of an h-dimensional projective dictionary and T their direct sum (ell=wh). Every point of T has a w-sparse witness by taking at most one column per copy. The witnesses include all individual unit coefficient vectors, so F=F_q^K, and

    K=w(q^h-1)/(q-1),    e=K-wh.

The upper example and (2) match in leading order up to the factor exp(1) as q^h/(q-1) grows much faster than h. Thus the exponential dependence on ell/w in (2) cannot be replaced by a general substantially faster growth assertion. This example is local to a subspace and can be embedded in a larger ambient space; no full ambient coverage is needed.

### 1.2 The positive projective-probe branch

Suppose an upper bound E>=e_(3w)(H) has been certified. Choose ell<=d with the strict **integer** inequality

    q^ell > B_q(ell+E,w).                                    (3)

Then every ell-dimensional subspace contains a missing syndrome. A list of its normalized projective directions, of size (q^ell-1)/(q-1), is an explicit probe set with at least one point outside S_w(H). Scaling cannot change membership. The output does not identify which point is absent. When E=0, ell=w+1 suffices, so any fixed w+1 independent directions give a projective list of order q^w (provided w+1<=d).

For general E, B_q(ell+E,w)<=(1+(q-1)(d+E))^w by coding sparse patterns into w slots. Thus taking ell=1+floor(log_q((1+(q-1)(d+E))^w)), if ell<=d, suffices. Compute the floor by integer powering. The number of probes is bounded by q(1+(q-1)(d+E))^w (and actually smaller by the projective factor). In particular if E is polynomial in d, q is polynomial in w and w grows polylogarithmically in d, the output has size exp(O(w log(d+q))), much smaller than a dictionary of size exp(Theta(d log q/w)) when w^2 log(d+q)=o(d log q). The output cost is explicitly charged and the branch fails honestly if no allowed ell fits the resource cap. Section 2 supplies a checkable upper bound E; a bare assertion that the minimum distance is large is not a certificate.

**Corollary (at least half missing).** If E>=e_(6w)(H) and q^ell>B_q(ell+E,2w), then |T intersect S_w(H)|<=q^ell/2. Otherwise A=T intersect S_w(H) has more than half the points of T. For each x in T, A intersects x+A, so A-A=T. As A-A is contained in S_(2w)(H), Theorem 1 contradicts the assumed inequality. The corresponding projective list has at least half its directions missing: zero is always covered, and each nonzero covered direction accounts for q-1 covered points.

## 2. A polynomial-time certified upper bound by support-overlap peeling

An immediate concern is that e_s itself need not be cheap to compute. The following deterministic criterion needs no enumeration of s-sparse patterns and no supplied hidden matrix layout. It may be conservative.

For a current active set I of column indices, write U_i=supp(h_i), and compute the overlaps o_ij=|U_i intersect U_j| for i,j in I. Call i removable if the sum of the largest min(s-1,|I|-1) numbers o_ij (j!=i) is strictly smaller than |U_i|. Delete all removable columns, repeat until no removal is possible, and call the surviving index set J. Zero columns are not incorrectly removed: the strict inequality fails for them.

**Theorem 2 (certified short core).** All kernel words of weight <=s are supported on J. Consequently

    e_s(H) <= |J|-rank(H_J).                                  (4)

Proof. Induct on removal rounds, assuming every word under consideration is supported in the current active set. If a removable column i had a nonzero coefficient in such a word, its support would have to be covered by the supports of at most s-1 other columns of the word. Otherwise an uncovered row gives a single nonzero contribution to Hc, contradiction. The union covers at most the sum of the selected overlaps, no greater than the top-overlap sum, which by the removal guard is smaller than |U_i|. Thus no such word uses i, and the induction holds even if several removable columns are deleted at once. All short kernel words are therefore supported on J, and their span lies in the zero-extended kernel of H_J. QED.

Tabulating pairwise overlaps costs O(K^2 d) elementary operations with ordinary explicit storage; sorting and re-evaluating at most K rounds costs at most O(K^3 log(K+1)) simple comparisons/operations, and the final rank is ordinary field elimination. A certificate consists of the deletion rounds, their guards, and the final rank. Invertible row changes preserve all kernel words, so one can repeat the certified peeling in several explicit coordinate bases and continue removing columns; every deletion remains sound. No guarantee of useful coordinates or a small core is asserted. The support-cover argument resembles elementary disjunctness/unique-neighbor tests, which are classical; the new use here is to bound the rank of the entire short-relation space and plug it into (3), rather than require the full kernel to have high minimum distance.

This yields a genuine **checkable positive branch**: run the peeling for s=3w (or 6w for the half-density result), compute E by (4), and try (3) under a charged output cap. It returns a certified probe list or honestly reports that this sufficient condition failed. It does not estimate the probability that some other method succeeds. On the other branch, if all proposed probes are actually covered and witnesses are supplied, Theorem 1 certifies a large local short-dependency rank. A large surviving core alone is only a conservative bound, not evidence that its true short-dependency rank is large.

### 2.1 High-rate examples with small short cores

For a prime t>=s, let the rows index the t^a points of F_t^a (a>=2), and the columns be the 0/1 incidence vectors of all affine lines, interpreted over an arbitrary coefficient field F_q (not necessarily F_t). The number of rows is d=t^a and of columns is K=t^(a-1)(t^a-1)/(t-1). Distinct affine lines meet in at most one point, and each support has t points. The top s-1 overlap sum is at most s-1<t. Hence every column is removed in the first round and e_s=0. In particular, taking s=3w, the full kernel dimension can be at least K-d while the projective probe list still has only (q^(w+1)-1)/(q-1) points. This is a genuine high-rate family, though its K grows polynomially in d, not on every scale of the general high-rate problem. Adding a small independent-coordinate core of columns with short dependencies increases (4) by at most the nullity of that core. A path of columns with supports {i,i+1} illustrates why repeated peeling is useful: internal columns may initially have no unique coordinate by the top-overlap test, but deleting the two endpoints of the path iteratively removes all path columns. The finite verifier includes path/core examples and affine-line designs over different coefficient and geometry fields.

## 3. A barrier to a naive low-rank-geometry / unstructured dichotomy

The preceding positive theorem relies on a bound on short-dependency rank. It is natural to hope that when rank geometry or small polynomial certificates are absent, strong growth or Fourier randomness forces a prescribed probe list to escape. The following construction rules out that inference for fixed probe lists of nearly linear size in a broad abstract dictionary class. It extends PR #27's planted-cover idea from colored plane sums with coefficients one to **uncolored sparse linear combinations with arbitrary field coefficients** in arbitrary dimension, and simultaneously excludes all surjective affine low-rank matrix layouts, even as approximate descriptions of most atoms. No lower bound on input-dependent algorithms is implied.

### 3.1 Finite parameter theorem

Take prime q, ambient dimension d, Q=q^d, w>=3, and integers L>=Delta>=1, K=(Delta+2)L with w<=K. Let P_j=B_q(K,j). Define

    b_conf = 2(Delta+1)^2,
    rho_j = min(1, binom(j,2)*b_conf/(K-1)),
    delta = sqrt(w P_w/Q).

Fix any target set E_0 subset F_q^d of at most Delta L points. For degree 1<=t<q, write D_t=binom(d+t,t), and choose gamma,epsilon>0 and integer 1<=u<=d. There is a randomized dictionary construction covering every point of E_0 with at most three atoms. The following properties hold simultaneously except with probability at most the explicit bound (5) below:

1. The K columns are nonzero and pairwise projectively distinct. For each 1<=j<=w,

       |S_j(H)| >= (1-rho_j-delta) P_j.

2. For every nonzero polynomial of total degree <=t, at most a t/q+gamma fraction of the dictionary columns are zeros. In particular these polynomials cannot vanish on the dictionary if that fraction is <1.
3. For **every** surjective affine map F_q^d -> F_q^(a x b) with ab<=d, and every k<min(a,b), at most a 1/(q-1)+gamma fraction of columns have image rank <=k. Thus this property excludes a broad class of rank-ball certificates even with a substantial outlier allowance. It concerns surjective affine layouts, not arbitrary nonlinear or non-surjective rank certificates.
4. For every nontrivial additive character chi of F_q^d, |K^(-1) sum_i chi(h_i)|<=epsilon. Every surjective affine projection to F_q^h with 1<=h<=u maps the dictionary onto all q^h points.

The failure bound is

    delta/2 + binom(P_1,2)/Q
    + (Delta+2) [q^D_t + d^2 q^(d(d+1))] exp(-2 L gamma^2)
    + 4(Delta+2)(Q-1) exp(-L epsilon^2/4)
    + (Delta+2) u q^(u(d+2)) exp(-L/q^u).                    (5)

When (5) is below one, all properties and the prescribed cover coexist. The sampler's construction time is polynomial in K,d,log q, with independent uniform labels drawn for 2L vertices (O(L d log q) entropy and expected fair bits). The success guarantees are mathematical probability bounds, not a claim that every sampled dictionary has the properties or that testing them all is efficient.

### 3.2 Induced matching signatures for arbitrary coefficients

Make a bipartite graph with L vertices in each part, edges (i,i+j mod L) for j=0,...,Delta-1. It is simple, Delta-regular, and is a union of Delta matchings of size L. Its atoms are one vertex atom for each of 2L vertices and one edge atom for each edge. Draw independent uniform X_v in F_q^d. A vertex atom is X_v, an edge atom at {u,v} is c_e-X_u-X_v. The constants c_e include all elements of E_0; pad arbitrarily if necessary. Then c_e is the sum of its edge atom and its two endpoint atoms, so the target cover is exact, regardless of the random draw. There are K atoms in total.

A coefficient pattern with support size i is **good** if its chosen atom footprints (a singleton for a vertex, the two endpoints for an edge) are disjoint and there is no graph edge between different footprints. Its formal coefficient vector in the variables X_v has precisely those footprints as its nonzero support; on a selected edge both endpoint coefficients are the negative of the edge coefficient, and at a selected vertex the coefficient is the vertex coefficient. Crucially, no sign restriction is needed. The induced graph on this nonzero support has components of size one or two: the latter are exactly the chosen edge footprints. Thus the formal coefficient vector uniquely determines every good coefficient pattern, even across different support sizes. The empty pattern also has a distinct signature.

For any atom, every conflicting atom has a footprint meeting the closed graph neighborhood of its footprint. There are at most 2(Delta+1) vertices in that neighborhood, and at most Delta+1 atoms incident to a vertex. Hence the conflict degree is at most b_conf. For a uniform support of size i, the union bound over pairs shows that at most a fraction binom(i,2)b_conf/(K-1) is bad. Each support has exactly (q-1)^i coefficient patterns. Summing over i<=j proves that at least (1-rho_j)P_j patterns of weight <=j are good.

For two different good patterns, the difference of their formal coefficient vectors is nonzero. Therefore their realized syndromes collide with probability exactly Q^(-1), because a nonzero linear form of independent uniform vertex labels is uniform, regardless of the constants c_e. Let C_j count unordered collisions among good patterns of weight <=j. Then E C_j <= P_j^2/(2Q). Set Z=sum_{j=1}^w C_j/P_j. Its expectation is at most w P_w/(2Q); Markov gives Pr(Z>delta)<=delta/2. Since a collision class of size b loses b-1 distinct syndromes, no more than binom(b,2), on this event all radius bounds in property 1 hold. All singleton patterns with nonzero coefficients, and the zero pattern, are good. A union bound over the P_1 patterns shows that they are injective except with probability at most binom(P_1,2)/Q. This ensures nonzero, projectively distinct columns, stronger than mere distinctness. Unlike the previous colored plane cover theorem, the induced-footprint condition is sufficient to distinguish arbitrary field coefficients and requires no large-girth graph; choosing the graph to be a union of matchings makes the remaining probability estimates elementary.

### 3.3 Matching marginals exclude structured descriptions

Partition the atoms into Delta+2 groups of length L: the two vertex parts and the Delta edge matchings. Within each group the labels are independent uniform points of F_q^d. This holds for a matching because its endpoints are disjoint; the constants c_e do not change uniformity. Different groups need not be independent, and our proofs use only a union bound across them.

For a fixed nonzero polynomial of degree <=t<q, the elementary Schwartz–Zippel bound gives zero probability <=t/q for a uniform point. Hoeffding bounds the probability that a particular group has zero fraction above t/q+gamma by exp(-2L gamma^2). There are fewer than q^D_t nonzero polynomials. Union over groups and polynomials gives the first term inside the third summand of (5), and averaging the group fractions proves property 2.

For an a-by-b uniform matrix let s=min(a,b), l=max(a,b). Expose its s rows of length l (or transpose first). The probability of a rank loss is at most sum_{i=0}^{s-1}q^(i-l)<=(q-1)^(-1). A surjective affine map sends a uniform point to a uniform matrix. For each shape with ab<=d there are at most q^((ab)(d+1))<=q^(d(d+1)) affine maps, including those that are not surjective; there are at most d^2 shapes. Apply Hoeffding to the event of nonfull rank, then the union bound as above. A rank-k ball with k<s is a subset of the nonfull-rank matrices. Thus no separate union over k is necessary. This proves property 3, including the stated approximation/outlier interpretation.

For each nontrivial character, its real and imaginary parts on a uniform point lie in [-1,1] and have mean zero. If a group's complex mean has magnitude >epsilon, at least one of the two real component means has magnitude >epsilon/sqrt(2). Two-sided Hoeffding and the union bound give failure <=4 exp(-L epsilon^2/4) per character and group. There are Q-1 nontrivial characters. Averaging the means proves the bias claim. Finally, for a fixed surjective affine map to F_q^h and a fixed output value, the chance that a group misses that value is (1-q^(-h))^L<=exp(-L/q^h). There are at most q^(h(d+1)) maps and q^h values. Sum over 1<=h<=u and all groups, bounding by the last summand of (5). This proves property 4. The union of the five kinds of failure events is bounded by (5). QED.

### 3.4 A growing-parameter family with volume slack and no simple dichotomy

For example let w and prime q tend to infinity, q polynomially bounded in w, d/w grow, and assume

    w=o(d),   q log(d+q)+log(w+1)=o(d log q/w).

These hold in a broad small-field regime with w polylogarithmic in d and q polynomial in w. Fix 0<alpha<1. Set Delta=w, L=floor(q^(alpha d/w)/(w+2)), K=(w+2)L; integer rounding is harmless and L>=Delta eventually. Then log_q K=(alpha+o(1))d/w and P_w<=(1+(q-1)K)^w<=q^((alpha+o(1))d), so the volume slack is real. In (5) take t=floor(q/4), gamma=epsilon=q^(-1/4), u=floor((log_q L)/2). The two collision terms tend to zero; rho_w=O(w^4/K)=o(1). For the other terms, D_t<= (d+t)^t=L^o(1) by the displayed assumption, and all polynomial factors in d,w,q are L^o(1). Thus L gamma^2 and L epsilon^2 dominate the logarithms of the respective union counts; L/q^u>=sqrt(L) dominates u(d+2)log q as well. The failure bound tends to zero. The construction therefore gives dictionaries of high code rate 1-d/K tending to one, with nearly maximal cardinality for every sparse-span radius j<=w, low Fourier bias, all affine projections of dimension up to u surjective, and polynomial zero fractions <=1/4+o(1) through degree floor(q/4). At most an o(1) fraction of the atoms lies in any proper rank ball under any surjective affine matrix layout. Nevertheless **any prescribed probe list of size at most Delta L=(1-o(1))K can be covered** by some such dictionary. If that prescribed list is a projective subspace of sufficient dimension, Theorem 1 certifies the corresponding large rank of the short-dependency space; no contradiction to the high-growth properties follows.

This rules out a particular naive dichotomy: the absence of an (approximately) common low-rank matrix model, no small-degree annihilator, high sparse-span growth and strong Fourier/projection diagnostics do not guarantee that an arbitrary fixed near-linear list escapes. It also rules out improving the elementary near-linear universal list lower scale simply by restricting to those generic-looking inputs. The result does **not** rule out universal lists of larger constant times K, input-dependent probe generation, arbitrary nonlinear structure, or other forms of compact algebraic certificates. In particular the construction has explicit planted dependencies, which can be a target for stronger, more global algorithms. It is not a computational hardness theorem.

## 4. Verification and next target

Both defect-certificate routines validate the length, weight and syndrome of each supplied witness before forming defects. The negative fixtures supply a target outside the span of a repeated binary column and a weight-two witness for a radius-one target with the correct syndrome. Both routines must reject both fixtures.

Run `python -B verify.py` (Python 3.10+, standard library only, assertions enabled). `verification.txt` records the exact transcript. The verifier tests 4,132 subspace/dictionary/radius triples (all 127 nonempty binary projective dictionaries in dimension 3, all 2D/3D subspaces; 75 seeded ternary dictionaries), 1,589 covered subspaces, 10,083 exact defect words, 3,024 projective defect words and 70 triggered half-density guards. Five direct-sum controls include the sharp w=1 bound. One hundred finite peeling checks compare the certificate with exhaustive short-word enumeration; 13 take multiple rounds. The two path/core examples and two affine-line designs separately exercise the constructive branch. The 117-column design has full nullity 90 but e_3=0, certified by peeling, and all three selected projective probes happen to be absent. The second design has full nullity seven but e_6=0, with seven probes. These toy incidence designs do not realize every intended exponential parameter scale.

Four planted arbitrary-coefficient dictionaries enumerate 289,130 coefficient patterns and 67,790 good formal signatures, checking every radius and the indicated collision inequalities. All prescribed targets have verified three-atom representations. These small examples can have large conflict-bound losses and do not impose the asymptotic union-bound guards. The tests also exhaust 353 matching-label assignments, 1,451 small matrix ranks, and 1,502 nonzero polynomial zero counts; the simultaneous global Fourier, projection and affine-layout guarantees in the large theorem are analytical, not empirically certified by those local checks. Reproducible Python pseudo-randomness is used only for finite controls, not as a proof of independent uniform sampling at scale.

Related literature: Sudakov and Tomon, *Evasive Sets, Covering by Subspaces, and Point-Hyperplane Incidences* (2024), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-023-00601-1), studies intersection bounds for affine subspaces and their coding-theoretic applications; Huang, Li and Zhong, [*Range Avoidance and Remote Point: New Algorithms and Hardness*](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.79), addresses the general search setting; the earlier project notes discuss rank-metric geometry and remote-syndrome algorithms under structural promises. None of these sources is used as a solver for the present unrestricted problem. The sphere-counting, support-overlap, concentration, and induced-matching ingredients are standard in isolation; priority for this combination is unverified.

**Research status.** We have a fully proved, checkable subspace-probe criterion, a quantitative short-dependency alternative when a subspace is covered and witnesses are supplied, and a rigorous obstruction to turning standard local genericity diagnostics into the desired universal two-branch solver. The unresolved high-rank branch is to use many independent low-weight dependencies in a *global constructive* way (for example via verified sparse compression or a new certificate); having a large local relation space alone does not imply a useful rank-ball layout or a cheap compressed representation. We did not establish that branch or a general deterministic avoidance algorithm. No change to the main paper or its conditional complexity claims is warranted.
