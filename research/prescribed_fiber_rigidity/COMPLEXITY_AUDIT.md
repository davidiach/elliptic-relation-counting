# Bit complexity and the selection gap

Write \(b=\lceil\log_2 p\rceil\), \(L=\log_2 b\). The application uses the whole group of a nonsingular curve over \(\mathbb F_p\), \(p>3\), of prime order \(r\), with a generator \(P\) and a group target \(Q\). Hasse gives \(\log_2 r=b+O(1)\). The structural theorem takes an abstract cyclic group, ordinary indexed blocks and their mathematical sumset density. It requires no computation of the sumset and holds independently of every complexity premise.

| Stage on a uniform deterministic multitape machine | Worst-case bit resources and dependency |
|---|---|
| Construct and enumerate blocks | LZR Theorem 3.1 under closed Theorem 2.2. Order and conversion are polynomial in \(b\). With \(m\asymp L^{1/4}\), \(D=2^{O(b/m+\log b)}\). Seed prefix optimization uses polynomially many closed counts of \(O(mb)\) edges. Log time \(O(b/m+mb/\sqrt{\log(mb)}+\log b)\), space at most time, including retained coordinates. Small-group direct cases are separate. |
| Initialize with a known nonzero shift | Doubled coverage gives a tuple at zero and one at \(P\) by prefix self-reduction. Subtraction commutes with deleting references. One such row suffices for the algebra. The stronger existing integer subsystem can be used at its stated cost. |
| Find one target or identity triple | Fix any third half-tuple from the enumerated blocks and use doubled decomposition on the remaining displacement. Alternatively each prefix of a \(3m\)-slot family uses closed ordinary counts. No arbitrary score lookup occurs here. |
| Verify a supplied certificate of length \(h\le d+1\) | Check all \(3m(h+1)\) slots, group sums and indexed membership against the seed or stored blocks. Accumulate reduced incidences and check the sum of weights is one. Test membership in \(W\) and transport the supplied right sides by field elimination. For \(k\) explicit rows a deliberately loose bound is \((k+D+h+m+b+1)^{O(1)}\) bit time and space. Naive elimination on dense arrays, extended Euclid and sequential scans meet this bound. Residues need \(O(b)\) bits, count entries before reduction are at most \(3m\). |
| Given an explicit candidate list | Test whether the correct affine hull contains zero using the quotient equations together with a row of ones. Polynomial time and space in its explicit length and the parameters above. Certificate bits for tuple addresses and weights are \(O((d+2)(mw+b))\) when \(w\) denotes the native slot width. |
| Implicit entry access | For the three-slot-copy architecture the unweighted counts for any prescribed point pins and prefixes still use \(O(mb)\) edges, degree \(O(b^{3/4})\), and \(O(mb)\) integer precision by unique witnesses. Fixed-order incidence moments expand into polynomially many such counts for fixed order. Signs and reductions take place outside the network. |
| Construct a sufficient target list | **Unresolved.** Each target already has at least \(s(2s-r)\) ordered triple representations. Explicit enumeration costs at least this many output records in the literal row model. Enumerating all \(M^3\) triples, where \(M=\prod |F_i|\), and checking sums is an elementary upper bound, with polynomial work per record and streaming elimination. Neither cost meets the target scale by this approach. |

For the fixed-shift polynomial trial add the digit overhead \(O((\sqrt b+m)\log(b+2))\) to log time. Balancing \(b/m\) against \(mb/\sqrt L\) gives \(m\asymp L^{1/4}\). For the centered degree-\(h\) seed, \(h\asymp m\) puts \(O(m^3b)\) edges in a moment query, giving \(m\asymp L^{1/8}\) and a seed exponent only. There is no optimization of \(m\) that turns an unpriced witness finder into an end-to-end bound.

If a *proved* finder supplied a new projected point outside each current proper affine hull excluding zero in bit time \(T_{\rm step}(b)\), there would be at most \(d\le D\) calls. The total would be the seed and decomposition costs plus \(D\,T_{\rm step}(b)\) and polynomial explicit linear algebra. This formula states the missing quantitative obligation. No parameter estimate for \(T_{\rm step}\) follows from the mixed-center theorem. The single-fiber theorem introduces no advice or randomness. The published LZR baseline uses the additional boundary-preserving construction for its deterministic endgame.

## Cost of constructing a certificate under the joint class promise

Fix a rational \(\eta_0>0\) independently of \(b\). Include the time and memory \(T_B,S_B\) spent obtaining the given genuine equations, and their explicit bit length \(L_B\). Use dense elimination to compute a quotient normal form for each basis column, partition by equality, form the exact rational product of fractions (bit length \(O(m\log(N_0+1))\)), and compare to \(\eta_0\). These steps and final evaluation cost \((L_B+D+m+b+1)^{O(1)}\) bit operations and space. Native label lists come from the original Stage I setup; repeated labels from different blocks have separate indices. There is no preprocessing advice.

With \(C=\lceil8/(7\eta_0^2)\rceil,\ \rho=1-1/(256C^2)\), take \(k\ge3\) so \(C\rho^{k-2}\le1/2\). Take odd \(J\) so \((2/3)^J(4/3)\eta_0^{-(3^k-1)}<7\eta_0^k/16\). Both choices use only rational arithmetic. Each of two searches costs \(O(kmb)\) prefix decisions. Expanding order \(J\) takes at most \((1+km(N_0+1))^{O(J+1)}\) point/prefix counts per decision, with \(N_0\) the total number of enumerated labels as in LZR. For fixed \(k\) each count has \(O(kmb)\) edges and degree \(O_k(b^{3/4})\); an occurrence fixed to a literal can be pinned on its ordinary encoding. The scalar output is an exact integer of \(O(kmb+1)\) bits. Summation adds \(O((J+1)\log(2+km(N_0+1)))\) bits and uses ordinary signed integer addition. No weighted high-arity lookup or dyadic-to-odd substitution occurs. The finite verification illustrates full signed mass recomputation under every chosen prefix.

Including setup, search and solving for the row coefficients, worst-case time on the promised branch is
\[
 T_B+(L_B+D+m+b+1)^{O(1)}
   +2^{O_{\eta_0}(b/m+mb/\sqrt{\log b}+\log b)} .
\]
Space is bounded by \(S_B\) plus the latter two terms. The construction is uniform for a fixed rational promise and uses no random bits. Balance \(m\asymp(\log b)^{1/4}\). Allowed size and cost of the explicit subsystem are part of the bound. In the first-order case \(k=3,J=1\) already work without Fourier exponentiation. The constants in the general guarantee make it asymptotic; allowing \(\eta_0\) to decay with \(b\) changes the optimization and is not covered. For the finitely many group orders \(r<16C\) the unrestricted logarithm can be obtained by enumerating the group; a retained-only representation need not exist there. For the complementary unpromised inputs this analysis gives no improved complete exponent.

For the omitted-block certificate one may enumerate all \(2^m-1\) nonempty block subsets on the original seed. For each let \(M_I\) be the product of its explicit block sizes; compare \(7r C_I\le8M_I^2\) using its single scalar collision count \(C_I\). The exact integers use \(O(mb)\) bits. The added term \(O(m)\) in log time fits the same envelope. On the existing deletion-resilient polynomial masks, sort the block modal fractions and use the best \(m-\lfloor\alpha m\rfloor\). Setup uses the previous seed's \(2^m\) guard terms, a fixed mask degree depending on \(\alpha<1/2\), and block enumeration time \(2^{O_\alpha(b/m+\log b)}\). Prescribed point pins can use canonical point encodings directly even without an injective threshold value for a polynomial mask. Each tuple has a unique encoding; domain validity is still the mask predicate. Constants in this branch depend on fixed \(\alpha,\eta_0\). The \(T_B,L_B,S_B\) charges are unchanged.

The short-augmented-lift construction uses five short tuples per unweighted count, two prescribed target-sum constraints with one common tuple, and at most two literal pins. A polynomial number \(O(mb\,(m(N_0+1))^2)\) of such counts finds both witnesses by a single prefix search. External squared-score coefficients have \(O(\log(1+R(v)))\) bits. Canonical counts have \(O(mb)\) bits; signed summation adds \(O(\log(N_0+1)+\log(1+R(v)))\) bits. Fraction-free solving of a \(D\)-row rational basis and reducing to primitive coordinates have polynomial bit complexity in the explicit input; minor bounds control the intermediate integers. The original Stage I predicates give quarter-power bit time and space with its sole closed premise, plus \(T_B,S_B\) and any supplied-lift cost. This conditional bit estimate cannot be assigned to the implicit membership of arbitrary PR #10 sets. Their unconditional conclusion is a polynomial count-query reduction once the basis is supplied. The small fixture verifier uses exact dynamic programming in the *explicit synthetic group* instead of asserting an implementation of the asymptotic tensor contractor.
