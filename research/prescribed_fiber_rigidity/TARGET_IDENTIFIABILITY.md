# Which target functional is determined?

Let \(V=\mathbb F_r^D\) in reduced block coordinates, let \(B\) contain verified equations with right sides \(b_B\), and put \(W=\operatorname{row} B\). For a **fixed** functional \(\ell\), identifiability means \(\ell\in W\). Elimination either returns \(\eta\) with \(B^\top\eta=\ell\) or a homogeneous direction \(u\) with \(Bu=0,\ \ell u\ne0\). In the first case its value is \(\eta^\top b_B\).

### Changing representations

Fix \(T_0\) with three short tuples of total zero. Every triple \(T\) of total \(Q\) gives a potentially different vector \(v_T=C(T)-C(T_0)\) with the same true answer. Since all triples have three points per block, the unknown reference labels cancel. Even when \(Q=0\), both the zero functional and other zero-valued functionals may occur.

**Exact projection law.** For any \(W\), the affine hull of the \(v_T\) modulo \(W\) is the coset of \((\ker\bar\pi+W)/W\) through any \(v_T+W\). This follows from the integral presentation after reduction. If \(\bar\pi(W)\ne0\), primality makes this coset the entire quotient. If \(\bar\pi(W)=0\) and \(Q\ne0\), it excludes zero. A collection of known homogeneous value-zero relations alone cannot calibrate an arbitrary nonzero target. The shift rows with right side one supply exactly the missing hypothesis.

An affine basis of the projected points has \(d+1\) members. Pick weights \(\alpha_j\) with \(\sum\alpha_j=1\) and \(\sum\alpha_jv_{T_j}\in W\). With \(\eta^\top B=\sum\alpha_jv_{T_j}\) the certificate value is \(\eta^\top b_B\). The coefficients may be any field elements. They do not have to describe a probability distribution.

| Question | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Does a certificate exist for every eligible target? | Yes, under the stated density and calibration hypotheses. The same \(T_0\) works for all targets. |
| Does a prescribed or even some single decomposition work? | Every target has at least one failing representation when \(d>0\). In the verified \(C_7\) example, no single target decomposition works at any of its six nonzero targets relative to the fixed \(T_0\). |
| Is a supplied list sufficient? | Test whether its projected affine hull contains zero. It may succeed before spanning the quotient. |
| Is the list produced deterministically within the target cost? | Open. Positive counts for admissible triples give a first decomposition but provide no general method to leave an explicitly given proper affine subspace. |

**Precise selection problem.** For one \(Q\), maintain the affine hull \(H\) of previously seen projections. If \(0\notin H\), any new projected point outside \(H\) increases dimension. At most \(d\) such increases suffice after the first point. Equivalently one can choose an affine functional vanishing on \(H\) and nonzero at zero, and seek a tuple violating it. The linear part lifts to a vector annihilating \(W\). A nonzero such vector cannot be a genuine slope because \(W\) contains a nonzero displacement. Thus the single prescribed-fiber discrepancy is symbolically nonzero. Finding a violation within the required bit budget is the remaining construction step. A procedure may also reach zero using fewer than \(d\) increases. [Resource accounting](COMPLEXITY_AUDIT.md)

## A deterministic selector when quotient classes concentrate

Compute canonical coset representatives of \(e_{i,a}+W\) by row reduction, including \(e_{i,b_i}=0\). Choose a largest coset class \(C_i\) in each block, breaking ties by native address. Let \(\eta=\prod_i |C_i|/|F_i|\). For any fixed rational \(\eta_0>0\), if \(\eta\ge\eta_0\), then on sufficiently large primes **the given row space itself certifies every target**. The identity reference can depend on \(W\). All retained \(k\)-copy tuples have the same quotient coordinate \(\sum_i k[e_{i,C_i}]\). A constructed representation at \(Q\) minus a constructed representation at zero therefore lies in \(W\). Its transported right side is the target scalar. No calibration row has to be postulated separately under this promise.

The all-target constructor depends on two different laws. The untrimmed one has chi-square ≤1/7. Restricting the product law to an event of probability \(\eta\) gives a trimmed sum law of normalized second moment at most \(8/(7\eta^2)\). Fourier decay on the prime cycle and a fixed number of convolutions turn this into a positive lower bound at each prescribed total. To *find* a valid tuple, count ordinary untrimmed fibers with a signed polynomial in the number \(H\) of excluded occurrences. The bound on the conditional exponential moment controls the tails of the Bonferroni truncation. Every strictly positive leaf is legal. This avoids having to perform a quotient-class lookup on a private address inside a tensor. The full proof gives computable constants. [Manuscript](manuscript.pdf)

For \(\sum_i(1-|C_i|/|F_i|)<1/4\) use just the first-order weight \(1-H\) on triples. On the \(C_{11}\) example the verifier constructs both endpoints and checks their augmented row span, including the right side, for **all eleven** targets with modular rank \(15/16\). In contrast, with the all-reference triple fixed at its translated value, every individual nonzero displacement in the same example fails. The two results use different choices of baseline, allowed when constructing a fresh target certificate. [Restricted-search transcript](restriction_verification.txt)

On the full input class there is no proved \(\eta_0\) for the actual rank-recovery algorithm, nor a proof that one may always select or efficiently add rows to reach that condition. This theorem has a checkable success branch and does not bound the worst-case time after that branch fails.

### Certifying targets while ignoring entire unresolved blocks

Choose any nonempty subset \(I\) whose untrimmed short law has chi-square \(\le1/7\) and for which the product of largest quotient-class fractions is \(\ge\eta_0\). Use only the retained blocks in both endpoints. The incidence difference embedded by zeros elsewhere belongs to the full \(W\). A representative at every target can thus be found even when every omitted block has only singleton classes. The deletion-resilient seed lets one omit any fixed fraction strictly below one half, provided the remaining product satisfies the bound. This condition tolerates \(\Theta(m)\) completely diffuse blocks. With \(W\) equal to the whole coordinate subspace supported on those remaining blocks, the retained product is one although the all-block product tends to zero. Such a space is spanned by genuine padded shifts on the actual deletion-resilient seed. Obtaining an explicit calibrated basis in budget is not guaranteed. [Proof](manuscript.pdf)

## An independent criterion that works with diffuse quotient labels

Suppose \(Bv=h b_B\bmod r\), with integer labels including the zero references, and \(0<2R(v)<r\), where \(R(v)\) is the sum of blockwise ranges. The integer score is nonconstant and cannot be affine with slope from the finite prime group to the integers. The prescribed-fiber theorem gives two triples sharing their third tuple with nonzero integer score difference. Sum the squares of those differences over valid five-half witnesses and bisect a positive prefix. The difference cannot wrap since only two copies on each side vary. If their incidence difference \(\Delta\) belonged to \(W\), writing \(\Delta=\gamma^\top B\) would give \(\gamma^\top b_B=\Delta\lambda=0\) and then \(\Delta v=0\bmod r\), impossible. At corank one these are the two distinct projected target points required to reach zero, relative to any identity reference. Elimination gives the affine weights. An arbitrary target's *previously fixed* single functional may remain ambiguous.

For a full rational basis compute the primitive augmented vector \((v,h)\) from \(Bv=h b_B\) over the integers and test its height and the remaining equations. In the PR #10 example \(h=1\) and \(v=d-1\), where group labels have the form \(\kappa c+d\) with four small offsets. Genuine selected shift rows satisfy \(av=1\bmod r\) and \(|av|\le8m\); hence \(av=1\) as integers for \(r>8m+1\). The offsets need not be known to compute \(v\). Fixed \(c\) allows at most four labels, so the quotient concentration theorem may fail on every nonempty subset while the short-lift theorem still works.
