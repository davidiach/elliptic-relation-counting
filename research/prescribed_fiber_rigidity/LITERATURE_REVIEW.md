# Prior results and applicability

The searches covered Freiman homomorphism extension, dense subsets of prime cyclic groups, partial abelian addition tables, presentations of factor-base relation lattices, rank condensers, isolation, matching and black-box identity testing. The two claims for priority review are the quantitative mixed-center bound with an adversarial prescribed sum, and the charged constant-joint-mass restricted decomposition on the original Stage I seed. Neither the underlying additive tools nor the generic conditional-expectation method are claimed as new.

### Additive, lattice and counting ingredients

Cauchy–Davenport gives the \( |A|+|B|-1 \) growth driving the mixed-center proof. Freiman homomorphism extension and partial Latin-square group presentations provide two closely related frameworks. Conlon–Gowers treat extension on sparse random sets. Partial Latin-square embeddings allow separate row, column and symbol labels. Our scores are shared across the symmetric slots, and the same prescribed center must test every nonaffine score. The relevant coefficient-matrix characterization of read-once width is classical. [Balandraud](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.06419) · [Conlon and Gowers](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~dconlon/homomorphisms.pdf) · [Cavenagh and Wanless](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.2707) · [Forbes and Shpilka](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.2408)

Fourier inversion, Parseval, the elementary arc bound, Hölder, the alternating Bonferroni lower bound and prefix selection by positive signed mass are classical ingredients. The contribution combines these into an *implementable restricted-fiber search*: the conditional marginals come from the **untrimmed** low-energy law; the higher convolution lower bound comes from the **trimmed** law. The final prefix circuit only counts untrimmed tuples with boundedly many literal pins. Neither enumeration of the trimmed fiber nor a unit-cost evaluator for an arbitrary predicate on a private slot is used.

### Closest concurrent work

The Long Mathematics presentation theorem uses integer rows \(v-u-e_j\) on a domain surjecting onto the group, with prescribed displacement for every generator. PR #9 already proves the complete fixed-generator integral presentation in reduced coordinates and its \(d+1\) target-augmentation theorem uses *all* pairs at displacement \(Q\). It also gives an exact criterion for an individual pair using equal quotient signatures. Our single-target-fiber presentation imposes equal-total contrasts at one fixed value, even with an identical third tuple; our constructive sufficient condition produces two equal-signature representations at specified endpoints without an implicit signature lookup. The existence of short affine certificates and the integral lattice of all fixed-generator rows are prior results. [Translation presentation, pinned revision](https://github.com/long-mathematics/deterministic-discrete-logarithms/tree/b11be05092e0f95862d9c7a8aef57e27e9a231c2) · [PR #9](https://github.com/davidiach/elliptic-relation-counting/pull/9)

PR #4 directly applies finite-difference expansions to *violations* with concentrated scalar error labels, and bounds mixing of arbitrary trims using a deletion-resilient seed. Its joint-modal implications matter: if the hypothesis here holds, every scalar direction in the current residual space has summed nonmodal fraction at most \(-\log\eta_0\) (natural logarithm); the same is true for later subspaces. Its existing scalar modal separator can then be relevant to full-table recovery with its own test family and consistency hypotheses. Our result uses the original chi-square seed and arbitrary explicitly listed retained sets; it certifies the target from the original supplied equations. It does not claim a new class of curves beyond all prior conditional separators. [Residual kernels, §§1 and 3](../short_relations_adaptive_rank/RESIDUAL_KERNELS.md) · [Short relation research](../short_relations_adaptive_rank/RESEARCH.md)

I did not locate an exact earlier statement of the sharp single-prescribed-fiber result or the bounded-pin constructor for arbitrary constant-joint-fraction trims in the sources reviewed. This is a provisional comparison, and independent additive-combinatorics and complexity review could identify further antecedents.

**PR #10 appeared at the final revision refresh.** Its obstruction combines pointwise-flat unweighted blocks, bounded integer offsets around a high-variance line, and a rational basis of unit-shift rows. It counts identifiable *individual* displacements. In the same construction fixing the residual label leaves at most four points per block, defeating a universal quotient-modal-mass inference. The new positive application observes that the inhomogeneous rational solution records precisely those small offsets and turns them into an exact two-witness selector. PR #4's positive integer quadratic search sought rationally independent shift rows. Here the squared integral discrepancy is attached to the *augmented* solution and to two triples on the *same prescribed fiber*. The height inequality proves modular nonvanishing at the leaf. I did not find this two-witness consequence in the prior sources inspected.
