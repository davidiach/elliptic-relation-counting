# Second adversarial review

## Attempts to falsify the constructive claim

1. **A constant retained fraction could still sit on consecutive elements.** This invalidates a one-step mixing claim. The proof derives a quantitative gap for every nontrivial character from \(r\sum\nu^2\le C\) and the spacing of prime-order roots. An arc of half-angle \(\pi/(16C)\) has at most \(r/(8C)\) possible elements for \(r\ge16C\), so its mass is below one half. Parseval then costs only \(C\) on the remaining Fourier energy. Taking a fixed \(k\) gives an all-\(t\) lower bound. It is essential that the group has prime order. A proper subgroup in a composite cyclic group can invalidate the conclusion.
2. **Conditioning on the total couples all slots.** Using independence after conditioning gives an incorrect bound. For one copy the exact likelihood ratio is \(p^{*(k-1)}(t-v)/p^{*k}(t)\le4/3\). Only this marginal is compared to independent untrimmed slots. Hölder controls the product across the \(k\) dependent copies. The proof needs \(k\ge3\) even when untrimmed halves are close in chi-square, since \(p\) itself may vanish.
3. **The lower bound may be positive for almost all targets while missing a worst-case target.** The characteristic-function estimates are uniform in \(t\); the survival probability used is the conditional probability \(\eta^k\nu^{*k}(t)/p^{*k}(t)\), not the total survival fraction. The same constants serve every target for a fixed \(\eta_0\).
4. **An excluded leaf might have positive signed weight.** For odd \(J\), the weight is zero for \(1\le H\le J\) and equals \(-\binom{H-1}J\) for \(H>J\). The gap from the good indicator is bounded by \((2/3)^J3^H\). Choosing \(J\) after \(k\) gives strict positive mass at the root. The parent is exactly the sum of its children; the root estimate need not persist after conditioning on a prefix. Exact finite tests include a root with negative minimum mass at orders one and three and positive mass at five.
5. **External labels may require a high-degree lookup.** The inclusion–exclusion expansion enumerates *literal* labels from the excluded list at at most \(J\) distinct occurrences. Each fixed occurrence can use unary bit pins, and the original group arithmetic and membership predicates are retained. All summation and signs are external. \(J,k\) can be very large. Treating them as constants requires a fixed lower bound on \(\eta\), independent of the input size.
6. **Two constructed representations might cancel the wrong offsets.** They have identical per-block arity and all their labels have the same coset within each block. Their quotient difference is zero. Their actual group difference is \(Q\). Solving the explicit transposed system and transporting the right side gives that scalar. This recovers the value of a newly chosen functional, and need not identify another functional fixed in advance.
7. **Corank one might imply a large joint class automatically.** An abstract one-dimensional quotient can distinguish arbitrarily many labels per block. Prior research gives a genuine high-variance residual line for a constructed seed family; it does not establish the behavior of the prescribed selection algorithm. The new Stage I corank-one family has *large* joint class but defeats the fixed all-reference baseline. Its rows lie in a rational hyperplane, so it cannot be presented as a failure of a full-rational basis. No uniform conclusion follows in either direction for the actual integer extractor.
8. **The stronger finite conclusion might just be a reshaping of the previous modal separator.** Under joint concentration each scalar residual direction is concentrated as well. With the necessary seed and test equations the old separator can recover more rows. The independently useful mathematical statement here takes arbitrary explicit retained lists and the original low-energy untrimmed law, and constructs prescribed representations with restricted labels. Applying it changes neither \(W\) nor its known right sides. The package claims this constructive statement and gives no new worst-case guarantee for full recovery.

## Attempts to falsify the structural claims

* The mixed-center proof treats a multivalued score on a half fiber first. Only after descending to the support does it use constant derivatives and a nonempty triple intersection. Distinct centers are counted by Cauchy–Davenport on the two levels, not by counting individual violations. The third tuple can be shared because its additive score cancels in the comparison.
* The quotient by the common-third lattice can have torsion. Taking the quotient itself as the score group tests all torsion at once. Its generators come from single-slot changes; the map back to \(G\) proves the quotient has *exactly* order \(r\). A proof over \(\mathbb Q\) alone could not establish this. The integer interval sharpness example becomes affine modulo \(r\); it proves no optimal finite-field threshold.
* Full affine span in a quotient does not imply that one of its generating points is zero. An affine combination with arbitrary field weights is allowed because their sum is one. The finite \(C_7\) example checks the distinction. Prior PR #9 has a stronger counterexample in another architecture, where a full rational basis and arbitrary source values are permitted.
* The unpinned rank formula uses a proper one-block set, \(r\ge5\), and ordered triples. Constants and odd scores are in the radical; even mean-zero scores have eigenvalue \(-48\), a unit for these primes. The pinned proof uses disjoint reflection orbits and \(0<n<r\). Neither step carries over automatically to large multiblock cells or to the repository's original pinning matrix.
* A bad coefficient cut certifies a width lower bound for the specified occurrence-variable order, and hence for an any-order bound on that encoding. It survives weighting by the specified nonaffine discrepancy. It says nothing directly about the compressed digit polynomial, a single favorable interleaved order, or another representation.
* Finite checks use exact integers and modular elimination, including HNF comparisons of the restricted and full lattices. They exercise examples; none establishes the asymptotic support, cost, or uniformity claims.

### Judgment

The strongest algorithmic theorem supplies every target on a checkably restricted class of explicit subsystems, assuming closed contraction for costs. The strongest unconditional theorem presents the exact integral kernel on one adversarially prescribed triple fiber. General deterministic selection on unconcentrated quotient labels, and the complete fixed-positive-saving ECDLP exponent, remain open. The scope of the priority claim and the source version discrepancy are recorded in the [literature review](LITERATURE_REVIEW.md) and [independent audit](INDEPENDENT_AUDIT.md).

**Review of omitted blocks.** A dense full sumset alone does not ensure density after deleting a block. The new branch explicitly tests the collision guard on each candidate subset, or uses the simultaneous guard already proved for the deletion-resilient seed. The surviving coordinate vectors are still reduced modulo the *full original* row space; projecting the equations onto fewer columns could lose their known right sides and is unnecessary. In the separation example, arbitrary doubled tuples on the surviving subset cover the group and may be padded identically on omitted blocks. This proves that a suitable genuine row space exists, without providing its basis cheaply.

**Independent check of the late short-lift result.** Three separate facts are necessary. Mixedness is over \(\mathbb Z\), the two-copy integer range is strictly less than \(r\), and genuine zero displacement makes any expression in the given rows have right side zero. Dropping the second would allow wrap. Dropping the third would invalidate the inference from \(\Delta v\ne0\) to \(\Delta\notin W\) for an inhomogeneous candidate. No division by \(h\) is used. At larger corank two distinct points need not surround zero affinely; only progress is proved there. PR #10 really has \(d-1\) as its integer solution because each chosen row has displacement one, kills the obstruction direction, and has bounded offset dot product. On its fixture the verifier independently reconstructs the integer lift from a genuinely realized square basis, obtains its modular kernel, checks the 49 identifiable single displacements, checks positive aggregate squared mass and certifies all 101 outputs. The random-augmentation law in its existence proof does not turn into an affordable factor-membership circuit.
