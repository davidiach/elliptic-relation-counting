## Fixed-shift determinant and dyadic reconstruction

Doubled coverage supplies common successors. A homogeneous solution therefore descends to the group, advancing by the prescribed generator fixes its value, and explicit consistency and normalization remove offsets in the unreduced formulation. Deleting one reference per block in the shift-only formulation removes the invariant block constants. Distinct row keys include the slot order and distinguish indexed copies. For a matroid basis chosen greedily, prefix optimality gives componentwise minimal sorted keys. Any other basis differs in at least one such key, hence has strictly larger key sum. In Cauchy–Binet the unique lowest-degree nonzero term has a nonzero squared coefficient over the odd field. Duplicate incidence vectors from distinct keys cause no tie in the minimal basis weight.

The few-variable substitution recovers precisely the univariate determinant. The explicit offset is multiplied as its own monomial, avoiding any assumption about digit carries. The finite sampling grid has size \(2^{2\lfloor b/4\rfloor}<r\) per coordinate for sufficiently large \(b\); total degree is at most \(2Ns(2^{\lfloor b/4\rfloor}-1)\), where \(s=O(1)\) is the number of digits. The small initial range is finite. Fixed-length trials, elimination and verification give zero-error expected bounds. The union bound over inputs of a fixed length produces advice, with no uniform method to discover it. [Fixed-shift theorem](../../MAIN_RESULT.md)

For the dyadic step, expand a product of \(q=O(\sqrt b+m)\) weights as a sum of products of nonnegative base-\(2^t\) digits of their canonical representatives. Each closed count is bounded by \(2^{E+qt}\), with \(E=O(mb)\) edges and \(qt\le S=O(mb)\). Precision \(E+qt+1\) recovers the entire integer. Weight shifts, signs, and reduction modulo \(r\) are external. Tensor access computes the addressed field power and a digit in polynomial bit time. At most \(J^q\) digit tuples add \(O(q\log(b+2))\) to log time. No ring homomorphism from dyadic residues to the odd field is used. This supports specified-point evaluation. It does not produce a deterministic full-rank point.

## Integer selection and conditioned completion

Prefix Gram queries evaluate the exact nonnegative integer \(\sum(a\cdot u)^2\) on a rational null vector cleared of denominators. A positive child leads to a rationally independent row, and full raw modular rank implies full rational rank. Minor bounds control the bit lengths of nullspace lifts at every iteration. After \(D\) steps Hadamard gives \(|\det B|\le(8m)^{D/2}\), since each block has two positive and two negative occurrences. Modular corank \(d\) implies \(r^d\mid\det B\) by Smith factors. This proves the stated floor bound. All \(O(DB)\) prefix Gram queries for \(B\)-bit keys, their \(D^2\) entries and explicit eliminations fit the target cost. No implication about a given target follows from the rank fraction.

For independent selected rows \(W\), expand \([z^k]\det(A^\top A+zW^\top W)\) as a sum of squared full minors containing all \(k\) selected rows. In an orthonormal adapted basis this equals \(\Delta(W)\) times the determinant on the orthogonal complement. For a *single* rational completion, Gram–Schmidt bounds the squared volume by \(\Delta(W)(8m)^d\), regardless of the number of choices. For a stalled modular quadratic extractor the lifted nullspace supplies divisibility \(r\) of off-block entries and \(r^2\) of the null-null block; factoring on rows and columns gives \(r^{2d}\) on the determinant coefficients. In the suffix search, subtracting two nested completion sums selects the least remaining key without counting permuted completions. These arguments retain \(\Delta(W)\). They neither guarantee a unit determinant for the selected completion nor permit indefinite iteration with bounded projected coefficients. ## Moment seeds

For the centered \(h\)-th moment, expansion cancels a tuple isolated in all blocks; the cancellation uses \(h\)-wise independence *within* each block and independence *across* blocks. With \(R\) the modular rank of the equality-pattern matrix, at most \(r^{\ell-R}\) assignments survive the group-sum equations. If \(D_h=h-R\) and the union of supports in its left kernel has size \(s_*\), each class meeting that union has at least two such members. The total merger count satisfies \(C\ge m\max(D_h,s_*/2)+(h-s_*)/2\). This gives \(D_h+(C-mD_h)\ge h/4\): for \(D_h<h/4\) split at \(s_*=2D_h\); the other case is direct. Bounding the \(B_h^m\) equality patterns and summing the centered potential across targets uses implicit repeated-sum counts, with targets common to the tuples. At \(h=1024m\) the query size \(O(h^2mb)\) yields log cost \(O(m^3b/\sqrt{\log b})\), balanced with \(b/m\) at the eighth power of \(\log b\). This is a pointwise seed bound; the quarter-power extraction uses the original seed.

For deletion resilience, fixed \(\alpha<1/2\) permits a fixed \(h\) satisfying \(1/(1-\alpha)<2-1/h\). Ranking class partitions of \(h\) tuples after subtracting the first target equality gives \(R\ge\max_i(\ell_i-1)\). The fully distinct pattern supplies the leading term. Other patterns lose a factor \(1/q'\) or \(1/K\); all \(B_h^m\) patterns fit when \(h\) is fixed. The change from the ambient size \(r-1\) to \(r\) costs \(1+O_h(m/r)\). There are at most \(2^m\) block subsets in the simultaneous potential. Its nonnegative terms yield relative volume and moment bounds, and the strict exponent inequality gives no wrap for the maximum retained count. This deletion is of entire blocks. Arbitrary label restrictions inside blocks in the new constructive theorem are separately proved. ## Pinning, contraction and concurrent research

* A1 applies Lucas to the units digit of every remaining binomial coefficient after pins. It needs \(m=r-1\). A2 arranges the same strict units-digit violation at small arity using binomial weights; choosing the reserved maximum label repairs consistency for the explicit rows. Its short-law chi-square grows as \(\Theta(\sqrt r)\). Neither example has all actual Stage I hypotheses.
* A3 splits on zero versus nonzero forced difference. The zero case uses the unit cell size. Otherwise the difference graph is a proper induced subset of the prime cycle, so its edge vectors are independent after restricting to the unpinned block. A4 uses the true log vector on a genuine shift to extract a scalar constraint before subtracting it from the moment equation. A remaining incidence radical can survive in multiblock cells.
* B1's three core-budget terms multiply to \(b^3/\sqrt{\log b}\). Restoring the lookup degree caps the stated parameter by \(\log_2(4m)\). The multiple-level inequality minimizes an expression built from **upper** bounds. B2's summed same-class covariances are \(1/q-1/D,(R-1)/D,0\) in its stated incidence configurations. Their average does not control the best packing. B3's balanced binary cut counts crossing edges in an explicit residual complete bipartite tensor layout; a compressed representation need not materialize that cut.
