# Remaining mathematical obligations

1. **Select one targeted discrepancy at ordinary Stage I cost.** Given an already recovered \(W\), a fixed identity triple and the affine hull \(H\) of target projections so far, assume \(0\notin H\). Choose a linear functional on \(V/W\) whose affine level contains \(H\) and excludes zero. The mixed-center theorem supplies a nonzero discrepancy on this one target. Construct a violating tuple using \(2^{O(b/(\log b)^\delta)}\) deterministic bit operations for some fixed \(\delta>0\), including all work to evaluate the test. At most \(D\) such calls suffice. At quarter-power arity the size of the *explicit* column dimension can be absorbed in the time bound for \(\delta\le1/4\). This is the most direct remaining selection problem.
2. **Control the quotient labels of an actual extraction algorithm.** Establish a fixed lower bound on the product of modal fractions for a deterministically obtainable explicit subsystem, or construct two matched-signature endpoints through another property of the actual masks. A full rational basis can leave a nontrivial modular direction. Rank fraction estimates place no general mass bound on cosets. The earlier high-variance construction concerns a different chosen family. Either a proof for the prescribed output or a counterexample realized by that output would change the current assessment.
3. **Replace constant joint mass by a weaker efficiently testable property.** The existing proof uses mass once for the trimmed law's normalized second moment and again for the conditional probability of a good leaf. For decaying mass both the convolution length and the necessary expansion order may grow. A useful refinement must give their joint cost, charge the search at both endpoints, and preserve a positive logarithmic saving after rebalancing \(m\). A lower bound for support size alone cannot pay for a private membership predicate.
4. **Understand the prime-field endpoint of the density theorem.** The interval obstruction is integral. Determine the sharp density necessary for detecting field-valued nonaffine scores on a fixed triple fiber, with and without the identical-third requirement. A better field threshold alone would not construct the needed witnesses.
5. **Find cheap noncancelling projections on the actual multiblock cells.** The one-block third-pin theorem uses injectivity of disjoint orbit vectors. For several free blocks the analogous map can have dependencies. Characterize their intersection with the finite-field averaging radical. Then price the number of pins, classes, and required contractions.
6. **Exploit compressed addresses in PIT.** The weighted discrepancy has exponential coefficient rank in one occurrence-variable order. This leaves alternate orderings and digit substitutions open. Any proposed condenser should specify its local weight circuit and total number of queries; any extension field must be compiled and charged.
7. **Extract a targeted integral combination.** One prescribed fiber has determinantal divisor \(r\) over the integers, and the complete generator-shift family has divisor one. Locate a combination giving the desired quotient projection with small construction time, even when every selected rational basis has an unwanted prime divisor. Computing the gcd on an already listed ambient matrix would leave the main cost unresolved.

None of these questions assumes a computational lower bound from PR #7's residual cost expression or its explicit cutwidth model. The complete deterministic exponent remains open.

**Short augmented lifts.** At corank one, finding any \(Bv=h b_B\bmod r\) with \(0<2R(v)<r\) is a second concrete route, independent of the modal product branch. Test the primitive rational lift first. Show that every actual full-rational extraction output admitting neither a small lift nor a concentrated surviving block set has a different affordable separation criterion; or construct actual outputs on which both tests fail. For higher corank, one small lift gives one exterior contrast, and an iteration needs control of the next lift. PR #10 passes the short-lift test and hence cannot establish failure of this strategy.
