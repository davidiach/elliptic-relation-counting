# Integral presentations and selected minors

Consider three lattices in \(\mathbb Z^D\).

| Generators | Integral quotient |
|---|---|
| All differences of triple incidences within one fixed target, even with a common third tuple | \(C_r\) whenever \(3|S|>2r+1\) |
| All fixed-generator differences between doubled tuples, with doubled coverage | The trivial group |
| One rationally independent selection of \(D\) genuine rows | A finite group whose order is the absolute value of the selected determinant |

For the first row, give each half-tuple its incidence class in the quotient by the common-third generators. The mixed-sum lemma forces a homomorphism from \(G\) into this quotient that sends every \(a-b_i\) to its corresponding basis class. The canonical map back to \(G\) fixes each such difference. They generate \(G\), so these maps are inverses. For the second row, a class attached to a doubled tuple descends to its group value using a common successor. The successor relations then make it constant all around the generator cycle. Changing a single slot kills each coordinate. Both arguments work over the integers before choosing a modulus.

Consequently the gcd of maximal minors in the first family is \(r\), and it is \(1\) in the second. The first family is full rank over the rationals, but loses exactly one rank at \(r\). Its torsion is intentional. Its integer saturation is the whole ambient lattice, so replacing its equations of right side zero by equations for that saturation would erase the true slope. The second family has a genuine right-hand side of one per row. An integer expression of a coordinate in its generators transports right sides only if the coefficients of that expression are known.

The gcd alone does not force one minor equal to the gcd. For example rows \((2)\) and \((3)\) generate \(\mathbb Z\), although neither is an integer basis by itself. In the first family some full minor has \(r\)-valuation exactly one. Computing it cheaply remains a selection problem. The ratio of two full determinants, without accounting for their valuations and other factors, is not a certified modular target formula.

**Conditioned completion.** In the existing notation, the coefficient of \(z^k\) in \(\det(G+zW^\top W)\) equals the sum of squared completion minors. The real-nullspace identity bounds the sum by \(\Delta(W)\Lambda^d\). A single completion instead satisfies \(\det(B)^2\le\Delta(W)s_0^d\) when each added squared row norm is at most \(s_0\). The contribution \(\log_r\Delta(W)\) can grow with the already selected rank. Neither the exact index result nor an iteration of Hadamard on a new coordinate system removes this term. The Long Mathematics source likewise gives a universal presentation using translations by every indexed generator. Here the distinctive generators are the contrasts of triples *all having one prescribed total and sharing their third tuple*. The index-\(r\) result for that restricted family uses density and a mixed-center inequality. **Primitive augmented null vector.** For a square rationally nonsingular raw \(B_0\) with right side \(b_0\), compute a primitive integral generator \((v,-h)\) of the rational kernel of \([B_0\mid b_0]\). Its entries are signed maximal minors divided by their gcd; no minor of the implicit full system is needed. Test \(Bv=h b_B\bmod r\) on all supplied rows and \(0<2R(v)<r\). This geometry of the *augmented* lattice can break an actual corank-one obstruction while leaving the selected determinant divisible by \(r\). In the \(C_{101}\) example \(R(v)=12\) and the rational basis has modular rank \(41/42\). There is no general bound on this primitive height. [Target criterion](TARGET_IDENTIFIABILITY.md)
