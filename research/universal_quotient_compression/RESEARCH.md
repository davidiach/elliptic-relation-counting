# Universal rank compression and the additive-avoidance bottleneck

27 November 2026. Research follow-up to PR #13 (`bef28551c5fdd352cc3e641cd25e7b3f6ec08eb8`), #14 (`aa321921572ebae9d455c9f584517523b2811ee5`) and #15 (`c266e3d2ec3370dea5fcf00b9d10e4a2151fea20`); baseline `main` at `04a8b3d8bb904af8a30aa996f1af4027b52f6dd4`. These are abstract finite-group and linear-algebra statements with a conditional resource application to the ordinary counting/decomposition interface of LZR, ePrint 2026/3020, Theorem 2.2. No improved end-to-end uniform deterministic ECDLP exponent is proved. The goal is to eliminate auxiliary promises on the rank extractor, and isolate a single simpler total-search problem rather than add another speculative dichotomy.

## 1. Universal deterministic compression of the residual quotient

Use the notation of PRs #14–15. Let V=F_r^D, D=sum_i(n_i-1), with n_i nonempty indexed block sizes and one deleted reference per block. Let pi:V->G be the public group-valued linear map on coordinate differences. Let W be the row space of any supplied consistent explicit modular system containing a calibrated nonzero-value row. Let U=W intersect ker(pi), obtained by subtracting the known right side times a calibrated unit-value row. Thus H=V/U has dimension h=d+1 where d=D-dim W is the residual modular dimension. The projected column sets C_i include zero and the images of every nonreference column. Block i occurs w_i times in a tuple. Write w=sum_i w_i. Assume the chosen tuple family covers G and an ordinary decomposition oracle is available.

**Theorem.** For every integer t>=1 such that t^w<r there is a deterministic procedure, using at most D ordinary decompositions and polynomial work in D,b,t^w, that enlarges W by verified zero-value rows until its residual dimension satisfies

    d_final <= max(0, sum_i w_i(ceil(n_i/t)-1)-1).

There is no additional hypothesis on the distribution of quotient labels, integer lift heights, cancellation, modal class fractions, or initial selected equations. The theorem is an automatic high-dimensional specialization of the persistent-cover theorem of PR #15, rather than a proof that its line-cover hypothesis always holds.

Proof. Partition each explicitly listed block into at most t nonempty classes of size at most ceil(n_i/t); no geometric optimization is needed. For each class, take the affine hull of its projected columns in H. Its dimension is at most ceil(n_i/t)-1. A sum of one such class-flat for every occurrence covers the corresponding tuple projections. Consequently the complete support is covered by at most t^w affine flats, each of dimension at most

    k = sum_i w_i(ceil(n_i/t)-1).

These flats are specified by origins and spanning directions, not enumerated as point sets. As zero-value rows are added, each successive quotient is a linear image of H, and the images of the original cover still have dimension at most k. Whenever h>max(1,k), all these flats are proper. For each flat find an affine hyperplane containing it by field elimination. Order the quotient coordinates and assign every hyperplane to the last coordinate on which its defining functional is nonzero. Fix coordinates in that order, avoiding the at most t^w forbidden residues attached to the current coordinate. Because t^w<r, one of the first (#forbidden+1) residues is available. This constructs a missing point z without scanning F_r. Lift z to x in V, decompose g0+pi(x) into an allowed tuple X, and append x-c_X with right side zero. The row has zero group value and nonzero class in H, since z is outside the support. It cannot lie in W, because the zero-value subspace of W is U. It therefore increases modular rank by one and reduces h by one. Recompute or project the same flats, stopping at h<=max(1,k). This proves the bound. All field operations, projections, and avoidance work are polynomial in the explicitly listed columns and flats, including a polynomial number of rank increments. QED.

**Application at the original quarter-power budget.** Take the original LZR blocks and doubled tuples, so w_i=2, w=2m, m=Theta((log b)^(1/4)), where b is the field-prime bit length (the group order has bit length b+O(1)). Take

    t=2^floor(b/(2m^2)).

Then t^(2m)<=2^(b/m)<r eventually. Building and processing the entire cover costs 2^{O(b/m)} up to polynomial overhead in the explicit dimension and b, and the ordinary decompositions cost 2^{O(mb/sqrt(log b)+b/m)} in total. The latter includes the initial Stage I block enumeration, the explicit calibration row, and polynomially many ordinary prefix counts. If using an already supplied explicit subsystem, charge its construction as well. The calibration row can alternatively be constructed by one decomposition of P and one of O, then subtracting their reduced incidences. No pointwise short coverage is required; the original doubled coverage suffices. All added dense zero-value rows are directly verified by public group arithmetic; their possibly large coefficients are not used in any bounded-row-norm argument.

Since ceil(n_i/t)-1 <= n_i/t, the new universal guarantee is

    d_final <= 2 N0 / t,       N0=sum_i n_i.

The original dense short sumset implies prod_i n_i >= 7r/8. AM-GM gives D=N0-m growing much faster than m; thus N0/D<=2 eventually. In particular

    d_final / D <= 4 * 2^(-floor(b/(2m^2)))
               = 2^(-Omega(b/sqrt(log b))).

Compared with the earlier 1-O(log m/b) rank-recovery theorem, the unresolved *fraction* is exponentially smaller on the b/m^2 scale. This holds for all eligible input blocks with the stated coverage and is not a conditional small-support criterion. The number of unresolved dimensions can nevertheless be exponentially large in b/m, since D is that large. In particular the bound does not imply full rank or target identifiability. It does not circumvent the corank-one examples in PRs #9–12.

The general tradeoff t^w<=C0, C0<r, gives d_final <= w*max_i n_i / floor(C0^(1/w)) when that denominator is positive. The product of cover sizes is charged. Taking C0 near r is NOT a valid quarter-power algorithm. Taking any fixed C>0 and t=2^floor(C b/(2m^2)) is affordable asymptotically (the constant in the bit-time exponent grows with C); taking C unbounded is not justified at fixed exponent.

## 2. One total-search problem suffices for the whole remaining algorithm

Define **AdditiveAvoid(2)** as follows. Input: an odd prime r, m nonempty explicit lists of points L_i in F_r^2, with prod_i |L_i|<r^2. Output: a vector not in L_1+...+L_m. The promise implies existence by elementary counting. Repeated occurrences can be represented by repeated lists. This is a restricted additive range-avoidance problem. Membership verification may itself be expensive; the needed algorithm must guarantee correctness and not merely output a random vector with high probability.

**Reduction.** For any universally covering short-tuple family with prod_i n_i<r^2, given an ordinary decomposition interface and an affordable deterministic AdditiveAvoid(2) routine for its parameter range, the calibrated rank problem is solvable by at most D calls to that routine and at most D ordinary decompositions, plus polynomial work on explicit matrices. There is no hypothesis on the residual quotient or its corank.

Proof. If h=1, the calibrated system already has full rank. Otherwise choose a surjective linear map T:H->F_r^2 (e.g. the first two coordinates in a computed quotient basis). The projected tuple support is exactly sum_i T(C_i). Enumerate each individual projected column list, which has at most n_i distinct points. Their product is still <r^2. Apply the avoidance routine and lift its missing point through T and the quotient. An ordinary decomposition produces a verified rank-increasing row as in section 1. Repeat; each step reduces h by one, and the product-of-sizes bound remains valid regardless of the new quotient or new T. When h=1, the consistent calibrated system determines the true difference table; ordinary decompositions at the target and identity give the target value. QED.

**Resource consequence on the common prime-order curve class.** PR #13's globally pointwise seed (rather than its prescribed-only seed) supplies universal short coverage with m=Theta((log b)^gamma), for gamma<1/6, block size <=2^{O(b/m)} and W0=prod_i n_i=r^{1+o(1)}<r^2. Here use s=0 in its batched polynomial mask construction, with redundancy K=2^{floor(b/(64m))}. Its conditional setup and decomposition envelope is

    log2 T_seed = O(b/m + m^(2+epsilon) b/sqrt(log b))

for any fixed epsilon>0, up to lower-order terms. The actual short fibers have pointwise nonempty support, so the adaptively selected probes of the reduction are legitimate. Suppose AdditiveAvoid(2) for these explicit lists is solvable in deterministic bit time polynomial in their total explicit length and b. Then, conditional on LZR's closed theorem and the justified seed/compiler, the entire deterministic problem would have worst-case time and space 2^{O(b/(log b)^gamma)} for every fixed gamma<1/6. This is a conditional implication with a new unresolved algorithmic assumption, NOT an established saving. No additional condition on the actual basis-selection output is needed in the implication.

Even a sublinear-in-arity exponent suffices. Write N for the total explicit list size including harmless polynomial factors in b. If an avoidance algorithm runs in N^{O(m^theta)} poly(b) bit time for any fixed 0<=theta<1, with no advice or randomness, the total log time is

    O(b/m + m^(2+epsilon)b/sqrt(log b) + m^theta b/m).

Taking m=Theta((log b)^(1/(2(3+epsilon-theta)))) gives a fixed positive saving exponent

    delta = (1-theta)/(2(3+epsilon-theta)).

This charges up to D calls: their multiplicative factor is absorbed by the b/m term. Space is at most time. A deterministic routine costing N^{Theta(m)} or enumerating the product lists does not establish a positive saving by this route. The reduction makes the remaining goal a single explicit combinatorial algorithm, even on corank-one outputs, rather than an unproved universal disjunction of the short-lift, concentration, small-support, or geometric-cover tests.

**Doubled-tuple variant.** With the original Stage I and doubled tuples, the product of the repeated list sizes is r^{2+o(1)}<r^3. The same reduction using AdditiveAvoid(3) therefore yields rank progress whenever h>=3. Even a polynomial-time routine in dimension three would only force corank <=1 on those doubled blocks, not solve the final plane. A two-dimensional avoidance call there is not justified merely by the tuple cardinality, and the short prescribed-fiber result of PR #13 does not guarantee coverage at new adaptively chosen points. These are the two distinct reasons to use the globally covered short seed for the full conditional reduction.

### Connection to known total-search problems and remote points

The usual remote point problem asks for a vector of large Hamming distance from a supplied subspace. In the quotient setting a vector remote by more than w from U has a syndrome outside every span of w standard columns, hence outside the support of all w-occurrence tuples (which have at most w nonzero coordinates). The syndrome characterization is classical; see Volk, *Improved Algorithms for the Remote Point Problem*, ECCC 2026/203, Lemma 2.1. This is a stronger requirement than AdditiveAvoid: arbitrary linear combinations of w columns include many vectors not obtainable as the specified sum of incidences. At codimension two the remote-point route may be impossible (every syndrome is a linear combination of at most two independent columns), whereas our bounded-incidence sumset can still have size <r^2. Volk's finite-field theorem assumes dimension at most a fixed fraction of the ambient dimension; U has dimension D-h close to D in the residual regime. His rational-field construction cannot just be reduced modulo r. Thus neither theorem supplies the missing avoidance algorithm. The known general circuit range-avoidance literature likewise does not establish a polynomial-time algorithm for this succinct additive problem. This note supplies a reduction, not a hardness theorem for the restricted problem, and does not assert that the cited literature's barriers apply to it.

## 3. A constructive short-fiber integral torsion presentation

A separate structural result uses stronger punctured coverage: short tuples cover G even after deleting any single block. This follows from the globally pointwise seed with s=1 in PR #13. No enumeration of all fibers is necessary. It gives a small, explicitly constructible set of *genuine short-fiber contrasts* whose integer lattice already contains r Z^D. This does not resolve r-torsion.

Choose a deterministic ordinary representative T_g for each queried group value g, consistently reusing it whenever g recurs. Set v(g)=c(T_g)-c(T_0) in Z^D, using reduced integer incidences. For any specified g,h construct an explicit integer identity

    v(g)+v(h)-v(g+h) = sum of at most 4m short-fiber contrasts.

Take T_0 and T_h and let the within-block changes A_i->B_i have total h. Start with pair of group values (g,h). At step i choose a short tuple of the first current value having A_i in slot i and a short tuple of the second current value having B_i in slot i. Punctured coverage guarantees both via ordinary pinned decomposition. Swap the two labels; the new values change by +B_i-A_i and its negative. The sum of the integer incidences is exactly unchanged. Include the contrast between the chosen representative of each original value and its pinned tuple, and the contrast between each swapped tuple and the representative of its new value. All four are genuine same-fiber contrasts. Summing telescopes over the m steps, which end at (g+h,0), and proves the identity. No score or logarithm is evaluated. The construction uses O(m) short/pinned decompositions for one specified addition.

For each coordinate e_j corresponding to a nonreference point A in block i, write Delta=A-R_i as a group point. The all-reference tuple and its change at slot i give contrast identities expressing e_j modulo the chosen contrast lattice as v(g0+Delta)-v(g0). Include the preceding transport for (g0,Delta); then e_j=v(Delta) in the quotient. Follow the ordinary binary addition chain for the known integer r applied to Delta, using the transport for each addition. Its terminal group point is O. This gives r v(Delta)=v(O)=0 in the quotient, hence r e_j lies in the generated integer lattice L. For every j this uses O(m b) genuine short-fiber contrasts and ordinary decompositions. Therefore

    r Z^D subset L subset ker(Z^D -> G),

and L has full rational rank. The cokernel is an elementary abelian r-group, all Smith factors being 1 or r. This is an explicit small r-saturation construction (saturation here means containment of rZ^D, not saturation to Z^D). It eliminates all non-r torsion without listing all short relations. The actual number of r factors need not be one; the full group-valued kernel has index r, but the constructed lattice may have greater index. Every generated contrast has squared norm <=2m. Hadamard on an independent rational subset gives the previous relative deficiency bound, not a proof of full modular rank. The construction does not need to perform integer lattice operations on implicit arrays.

Unlike an existence statement about the full integral lattice (PRs #9 and #11), this explicitly generates a small sublattice with an exact torsion-exponent guarantee. The s=1 pointwise seed makes all pinning and additions affordable at the conditional exponent gamma<1/6. For arbitrary universal short coverage one can obtain a closely related lattice using doubled-arity addition contrasts, but the transport argument keeps every individual generated row at the original short arity. Nothing here reduces the unknown true logarithm by dividing a relation by r; doing so would be invalid over the group-order field.

## 4. Finite verification and limitations

Run `python -B check_universal.py` (SymPy required). It uses only synthetic cyclic-group points with explicitly supplied ground-truth coordinates. Four partition-cover fixtures use exact field elimination and greedy hyperplane avoidance, with 36 verified independent rank increases in total. One finite case has k>=h and honestly stops without improvement. Three small plane-reduction fixtures finish full rank using 39 additional verified rows; **the toy avoidance routine exhaustively lists its tiny projected support** and is not an efficient asymptotic avoidance algorithm. The transport fixture uses one-block-deletion full coverage, explicitly verifies all 888 short contrasts, and checks via exact HNF that r times every coordinate belongs to their integer lattice. Its Smith factors happen to contain a single r; the proof only guarantees factors 1 or r. The finite compression parameter choices illustrate the combinatorics, not the asymptotic fractional improvement (which follows from the above bound). No experiment implements the closed contractor or attacks deployed systems. `verification.txt` records the completed run.

**Strongest proved advance:** a universal rank-compression bound d/D=2^(-Omega(b/sqrt(log b))) at the original conditional quarter-power cost, no new promise on quotient structure. Separately, a reduction of the entire uniform deterministic problem to two-dimensional additive range avoidance with a precise sufficient sublinear-in-arity complexity bound, and a small constructive integral torsion presentation. No faster end-to-end exponent has been proved. The missing avoidance algorithm is a genuine load-bearing open obligation, not replaced by a numerical rank percentage or by the toy exhaustive oracle. Proof-chain assurance for the inherited contractor and independent priority/proof review remain outstanding.
