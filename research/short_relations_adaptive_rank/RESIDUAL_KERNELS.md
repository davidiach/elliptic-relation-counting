# Residual-kernel selection, modal concentration, and a dense high-variance obstruction

Research follow-up to RESILIENT_SEEDS_AND_VARIANCE.md (developed against 018c9bb374a07a0067b22412f58b2e1f744a9e57; branch also contains DISPLACEMENT_KERNELS.md at b10fbc31a3d4750ef8a879f4cd865362cf224ce3). 27 November 2026. All computational bounds are conditional on the closed Boolean contraction premise of LZR Theorem 2.2 and the previously stated seed theorem. The algebraic and combinatorial statements below do not assume that contractor. No universal faster deterministic DLP exponent is proved.

## 1. The multi-multiplier variance criterion is equivalent to modal concentration

Let u be a homogeneous residual direction on the indexed factor columns. Write p_i for the empirical distribution of its field labels on block i, e_i=1-max_a p_i(a), E=sum_i e_i, and D0=sum_i (1-sum_a p_i(a)^2). Then E<=D0<=2E: the collision probability is at most the modal probability and at least its square.

For a multiplier z in F_r, let V_z be the total within-block variance of real lifts of z u/r taken in arbitrary intervals of length one (different cuts may be used per block and multiplier). For a nontrivial-character-bias <=eta multiset S of multipliers, suppose at least a fraction 1-beta of its entries have V_z<=v, with eta+2 beta<1. Then

    E <= D0 <= 4 pi^2 v / (1-eta-2 beta).

Conversely, choosing the cuts centered at each scaled modal label gives V_z<=E/4 for EVERY multiplier.

Proof. Put M_i(z)=E_{a~p_i} exp(2 pi i z a/r). The magnitude is independent of the choice of lifts. For two independent lifts X,X', 1-|M_i(z)|^2=E[1-cos(2 pi(X-X'))]<=4 pi^2 Var(X). For every z this deficit is at most 2(1-sum p_i^2), because equal labels contribute zero. Averaging over the small-bias multiset gives average deficit >=(1-eta)(1-sum p_i^2). Removing the bad beta fraction subtracts at most 2 beta(1-sum p_i^2). Sum over blocks and upper bound the retained average by 4 pi^2 v. For the converse put the modal lift at the center of the interval: all nonmodal lifts are at distance <=1/2, so variance is at most their second moment about that modal lift, <=e_i/4.

In particular the sufficient multi-multiplier hypothesis in RESILIENT_SEEDS_AND_VARIANCE.md section 3 (eta=1/4, beta=1/8) entails E<=8 pi^2 v. Thus that hypothesis is, up to absolute constants, the same sublinear-modal-mass promise. This does not rule out a single favorable multiplier for a special test or stronger phase arguments that do not use the multi-multiplier hypothesis.

Clarification to the distinction made in that note: for a homogeneous direction u, the hypothetical false table lambda+u has group error points [u_j]P, exactly. The true table need not be computed to obtain these errors. As P generates a prime-order group, equality of these error points is exactly equality of the scalar labels u_j. For an arbitrary inhomogeneous candidate y this comparison is instead with [y_j]P-A_j.

The earlier modal separator (RESEARCH.md section 5) can therefore be applied directly to a homogeneous direction. Subtract a modal label in each block, which cancels in a doubled-tuple signed difference. For a test shift its homogeneous discrepancy is the sum of signed nonmodal deviations minus c, where c=0 for a known scalar shift and c=u_j for a point shift. The robust doubled-tuple test family has some test with failure fraction >=1/8. Under the near-uniform doubled law each tuple marginal conditioned on a shift is dominated by twice its independent law. Let B be the set of nonmodal occurrences in the pair. Independence in each unconditional doubled tuple and Cauchy-Schwarz between the two conditional marginals give E[5^|B|]<=2 exp(48 E). For a fixed tuple pair let f(T) be the violation indicator after retaining just the deviations in T subset B, and P_t its Boolean finite-difference expansion through order t evaluated at B. In an ordering of B, repeatedly expand f on one occurrence; the remainder is a sum of binom(|B|,t+1) differences of order t+1 at intermediate base sets, each of absolute value at most 2^t (there are 2^t positive and 2^t negative terms in [0,1]). Thus L_t=P_t-2^t binom(|B|,t+1)<=f(B) and E[f(B)-L_t]<=2^(t+1) E binom(|B|,t+1)<=exp(48 E) 2^(-t). Use t=min(4m,ceil(70 E)+5); the full expansion is exact if t=4m. On a gap-bearing test the signed mass is positive. Every term of L_t is a function of at most t+1 pinned occurrences, with exact coefficient computable outside the graph. Expand by explicit point enumeration and ordinary unweighted counts, and bisect prefixes preserving positive mass. This extracts a genuine row nonzero on u. Complexity is that of the previous modal certificate; no new arbitrary lookup is required.

## 2. Constructively search the whole kernel for sparse-block directions

Trying only a basis of the residual kernel may miss a clustered direction. Let K0 be the current homogeneous kernel including the explicit doubled consistency rows and P normalization. Choose one reference point a_i per block. The difference-coordinate map

    Delta(u)=(u_i,A-u_i,a_i)_{i,A!=a_i}

is injective on K0: if all labels are constant c_i per block, the consistency rows give c_i=2 sum_j c_j; since r does not divide 2m-1, all c_i=0 and the dedicated consistency rows give zero there too. Let n_i=|F_i|, n'=sum_i(n_i-1). Given an exact matrix basis of K0, the dimension of its intersection with any prescribed pattern of constant blocks is computed by ordinary elimination, by imposing zero differences outside the allowed blocks. Enumerating every subset S of at most k blocks (<=2^m choices) finds a nonzero direction supported on at most k blocks whenever one exists, independently of the choice of basis. Such a direction has E<=k. Apply section 1's separator and repeat. Up to N independent-row increments are possible. If all these intersections become zero, the output kernel is certified to have block-support distance greater than k; in particular

    dim K0 <= n' - max_{|S|=k} sum_{i in S}(n_i-1).

Indeed projection of Delta(K0) onto the complement of any such S is injective. A target coordinate already constant on the affine solution space can be returned with verification even if the full kernel is nonzero.

For k=O(m^theta), theta<1, total log bit time is O((k+1)b/m + mb/sqrt(log b) + m + log b) under the same closed contraction premise. All linear-algebra and subset-search costs are charged. This gives a deterministic partial rank-reduction algorithm and a checkable stopping condition. It is not a guarantee of full rank. Finer prescribed coordinate chunks can be handled by the same intersection test; enumeration of arbitrary supports of exponential size is not within the budget.

A counting observation illustrates why a geometry-of-numbers idea alone is insufficient. If d=dim K0 and r^d>H^n', partition each difference-coordinate field representative into H nearly equal integer intervals. Two codewords share a cell, giving a nonzero direction with all differences at most ceil(r/H) in absolute lifted size, hence variance <=m(ceil(r/H)/r)^2 at multiplier one. This is only an existence argument, not an efficient algorithm, and does not give small variance for most of a small-bias list or a phase gap for that multiplier.

## 3. Arbitrary small point deletions preserve mixing

This structural consequence of the joint seed works without new polynomial mask assumptions. Let mu_i be uniform on F_i. Suppose that for every set I of a=m-k blocks the convolution mu_I has chi-square distance to uniform at most epsilon. Replace each F_i by ANY subset F'_i of size >=(1-delta)n_i, with uniform law nu_i. Set tau=epsilon^(1/(2a)). Then

    chi^2(nu_1*...*nu_m) <= binom(m,k) (tau+delta)^(2k) (1-delta)^(-2m) (1+epsilon).

Proof. Normalize Fourier transforms by taking expectations of characters. For any nontrivial character, order the m absolute Fourier coefficients of mu_i. The product of the largest a is <=sqrt(epsilon), by Parseval for that retained subset. Hence at least k+1 coefficients are <=tau. On a trimmed block |hat nu_i|<=(|hat mu_i|+delta)/(1-delta), by writing mu_i as a mixture of the retained and deleted portions. For each character choose k of the small indices J. Their squared product in the trimmed law is <=((tau+delta)/(1-delta))^(2k). On I=complement J, the nonnegative convolution satisfies nu_I(g)<=(1-delta)^(-a)mu_I(g) pointwise. Its total Fourier square sum is <=(1-delta)^(-2a)(1+epsilon). Partition or overcount the characters by their selected J and sum. This proves the bound including all arbitrary trims, not just random ones.

For a fixed positive deletion fraction alpha<1/2 and k=floor(alpha m), the prior exponential epsilon gives tau=2^(-Omega(b/m^2))=o(1). Any fixed delta>0 with H_2(alpha)+2 alpha log2(delta)-2 log2(1-delta)<0 suffices for chi-square 2^(-Omega(m)); such delta exists. For example alpha=1/4, delta=1/16 works. Support therefore occupies 1-2^(-Omega(m)) of G. Integer no-wrap on full fiber counts is inherited by trimming. This is a uniform structural resilience guarantee, not a claim of an efficient membership encoding for arbitrary retained subsets: arbitrary label-dependent trims may reintroduce the original lookup barrier. The finite check uses explicit small arrays and exact rational inequalities.

## 4. Asymptotic obstruction: dense no-wrap blocks with a high-variance residual line

The prior structural properties alone cannot imply the desired universal low-variance direction lemma. Here is an existential construction with genuine relation rows and valid explicit consistency equations. It is not a counterexample to the prescribed polynomial masks, to a specified lexicographic decomposition rule, or to every possible adaptive rank-selection algorithm.

Let G=<P> be cyclic of odd prime order r, b=ceil(log2 r), m growing as a fixed positive power of log b. Fix 0<alpha<1/2 and a=m-floor(alpha m). Let K=2^floor(b/(32m)), n=ceil((Kr)^(1/a)), and D=2^ceil(b/m). Then D^m>=r, D/n=2^(-Omega(b/m)), n^2/r=o(1), and 2^{O(m)}K/n=o(1). Choose fixed h with 1/(1-alpha)<2-1/h, as in the prior joint seed.

In each block i=0,...,m-1 include a dictionary of D+4 points:

    A_i,j = [kappa j D^i + 1]P,   0<=j<=D,
    -P, 2P, -2P.

Choose nonzero kappa avoiding collisions or O in each dictionary. The jD^i residues are distinct in a block; collisions with references or O exclude at most O(mD) scalar choices, less than r. Points repeated across blocks remain distinct indexed columns. Represent dictionary points by (c,d) with group value [kappa c+d]P; digit points have c=jD^i modulo r, d=1, and references c=0,d in {-2,-1,1,2}. Adjoin n uniformly sampled distinct nonidentity points from outside each dictionary, independently across blocks. For such a point A choose (for this existential construction in scalar coordinates) c=(log_P A-2)/kappa mod r, d=2. Define the homogeneous direction u on all block columns by u_A=c, and on the dedicated P,Q columns by u_P=0 and u_Q=c_Q, choosing Q a primary point with c_Q!=0.

**Dictionary fiber bound.** For any subset of dictionary blocks, the multiplicity of any group sum is <=M_D=2^{O(m)}. The integer coefficient sum of the selected jD^i lies in [0,2D^m], and the offset sum in [-2m,2m]. For each offset, the group equation fixes the coefficient sum modulo r, allowing O(1+D^m/r)=2^{O(m)} integer lifts. Each integer sum has at most 2^m digit choices in [0,D] by peeling base-D digits (at most two choices of j with a prescribed residue at each digit; omitted blocks are fixed to zero). The zero digit has at most four offset options. This proves the bound.

**Joint chi-square density for the augmented blocks.** For a retained subset I, |I|>=a, write d_i=D+4 and W_I=prod(n+d_i). In the collision energy of two tuples, classify the choices per block as dictionary/dictionary, dictionary/primary in either order, primary/primary equal, or primary/primary distinct. Each distinct primary variable is included with probability at most (n/(r-d_i-1)) to the power of the number of distinct primary variables in that block, by sampling without replacement. If any primary variable has nonzero coefficient in the equality of sums, fixing the other variables and dictionary choices forces it. Thus for ell distinct primary variables there are at most r^(ell-1) such assignments per dictionary choice. Summing these patterns (overcounting by including all patterns) gives expected energy at most

    (1+O(mD/r)) prod_{i in I}(d_i^2+2d_i n+n+n^2)/r.

The remaining patterns have the same primary choice on both sides in every primary-using block, and dictionary choices on both sides elsewhere. The dictionary collision count is at most M_D times its tuple count, so summing over primary block subsets contributes at most M_D W_I. Dividing by W_I^2/r gives expected nonnegative chi-square at most O(m/n+mD/r)+M_D r/W_I=2^(-Omega(b/m)), since W_I>=Kr. Summing over <=2^m retained subsets and using Markov gives simultaneous exponential chi-square bounds with probability tending to one (take the square root of the small expectation as a common upper threshold).

**No wrap.** For the primary-only subset J of k>=a blocks, the same h-fold collision partition argument as the prior seed applies: each exact equality partition has ell distinct variables and rank R>=max_i(ell_i-1), and inclusion probability at most (n/(r-D-5))^ell. The all-singleton pattern gives the normalized leading bound 1+O_h(mD/r); other full-rank patterns lose a factor <=1/n, and lower-rank patterns a factor <=r/n^k<=1/K, with at most B_h^m patterns. The expected excess of the normalized h-th moment is exponentially small. Markov and a union bound therefore give simultaneously max primary-only fiber <=(1+o(1)) n^k/r^(1-1/h) for all k>=a. Now split an augmented retained tuple into primary-using subset J and dictionary complement I\\J. For k<a its fiber count is bounded by n^k M_D <=n^(a-1) M_D<r by an exponentially small factor. For k>=a, use the dictionary cardinality times the primary-only maximum:

    (D+4)^(|I|-k) (1+o(1)) n^k/r^(1-1/h)
      <= r^(m/a -1+1/h+o(1)).

Here log_r(D+4)=1/m+o(1/m), log_r n=1/a+o(1/m), and the expression in k is increasing. The exponent is strictly below one by the choice of h. Summing <=2^m primary/dictionary patterns preserves strict no-wrap for every retained fiber. These arguments prove existence with the same qualitative exponential chi-square and pointwise count promises as the previous seed.

**High variance for every scalar and every cut.** For each nonzero z, the expectation of exp(2 pi i z c/r) on a random allowed primary point has magnitude at most (D+5)/(r-D-5)=o(1). For n independent draws the probability its empirical mean has magnitude >1/3 is exp(-Omega(n)), by Hoeffding on real and imaginary parts. Conditioning on distinct draws changes this by at most a factor 1/(1-O(n^2/r)), because draws are uniform on a set of r-D-5 points. Union bound over m(r-1) characters shows that every primary block simultaneously has character bias <=1/3 with probability tending to one. Adding the dictionary fraction D/(n+D)=o(1) makes the augmented bias <=1/2 eventually. All density/no-wrap and Fourier events can hold together, since each holds with probability tending to one. For ANY lifts X of scaled labels in a length-one interval, |E exp(2 pi i X)|>=Re E exp(2 pi i(X-E X))>=1-2 pi^2 Var X. Therefore every block has variance >=1/(4 pi^2), every nonzero z has V_z>=m/(4 pi^2), irrespective of cuts. Every nonzero vector of the line spanned by u has this property.

**The line is a genuine residual kernel.** The following equations, in addition to the normalization, are allowable doubled-tuple relations of the known scalar-shift / point-shift family. A zero-shift relation can use two occurrences from the same block; all unused occurrences agree on both sides.

* Within each block the scalar shifts 1 and 2 tie the homogeneous values at P,2P,-P,-2P. In addition use the point-shift equation for indexed P from the same pair changing P to 2P. Its difference with the known scalar shift 1 forces the indexed P homogeneous value to zero. All four references are then zero. (The explicit small-bias scalar list in RESEARCH.md includes 0,1,2.)
* The equality A_i,j+P=A_i,j-1+A_i,1 for 2<=j<=D forces u_i,j=j u_i,1. Between adjacent blocks, A_i,D=A_i+1,1; compare one occurrence in each block, filling with P in the other, to equate their homogeneous values. All dictionary labels are now multiples of the one parameter u_0,1, with exactly the coefficients c above.
* For EVERY indexed point, including dedicated P,Q, choose a valid doubled consistency decomposition. Write its canonical c in base D with m digits (D^m>=r); take one corresponding digit point per block. Fill the second slot of each block with a reference point, initially -P, so the offset sum is zero. To achieve d=2 replace one filler by P; d=-1 replace one by -2P; d=-2 replace two by -2P; d=1 replace one by P and another by -2P. This gives exactly the correct group sum [kappa c+d]P and homogeneous sum c times the parameter. The consistency rows thus force every other column, and the dedicated normalization holds.

These equations have the true table as a solution, u in their homogeneous kernel, and force every homogeneous value to be its designated coefficient times a single scalar. Thus their residual kernel is exactly the high-variance line; the target coordinate can still vary. The construction uses arbitrary blocks, not the polynomial parity masks or their selected seed. Its proof of the density/no-wrap properties is existential. The consistency decompositions are explicit valid choices, not asserted to be the prescribed lexicographic ones. It defeats any universal lemma deduced solely from those structural seed guarantees, validity of the selected relations, and the inclusion of consistency/normalization rows. It does not prove that the actual algorithm on the actual selected seed reaches such a kernel.

## 5. Exact finite checks and frontier

Run check_kernel.py (Python 3.10+, NumPy for exact int64 operations on bounded toy arrays). See kernel_verification.txt. The obstruction is instantiated on the actual prime-order curve y^2=x^3+5x+19 over F_953, order 1009. Direct point counting and elliptic group-law checks verify all 162 genuine equations, with four 28-point blocks. All five retained subsets after at most one block deletion have full support, max fiber 698<1009, and max chi-square 0.07248452. The coefficient matrix has rank 113 of 114; the target coefficient on the residual line is nonzero. Exact circular cut minimization over all 1008 nonzero multipliers gives minimum aggregate variance 0.18133368 (0.04533342 per block). Every proper block-support intersection is zero. The finite example illustrates rather than instantiates the asymptotic exponential energy thresholds. Sixty separate residual-subspace fixtures verify 480 intersection tests by exhaustive enumeration. Forty arbitrary-trim fixtures check the exact rational Fourier inequality (the rational tau bound is obtained by powering, not trusting approximate roots). None implements the asymptotic contractor.

This round establishes a more efficient search within a useful class, an equivalence explaining its limitation, stronger support resilience, and a structural barrier that includes genuine equations on densely covering blocks. It does not establish a faster deterministic worst-case ECDLP algorithm. The next promising direction must use further arithmetic structure of the actual masks or address modular averaging directly, rather than rely on low variance inferred from density alone. The newly added DISPLACEMENT_KERNELS.md theorem fixes raw rank at every prescribed displacement under punctured support; it makes the remaining finite-field averaging radical especially worth studying. No result here resolves that radical. Independent proof review, upstream premise verification, and novelty assessment are outstanding.
