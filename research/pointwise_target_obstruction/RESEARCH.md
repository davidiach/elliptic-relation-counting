# Pointwise flatness and the failure of single-pair target identification

27 November 2026. Follow-up to merged PRs #4, #7, #8 and open PR #9. Baseline main: 04a8b3d8bb904af8a30aa996f1af4027b52f6dd4. This is an existential structural obstruction for ordinary unweighted block sets in abstract prime-order groups. It is **not** a counterexample to the specified polynomial masks, the lexicographic basis selection, or the complete algorithm. No new faster deterministic ECDLP complexity bound is claimed.

## 1. The theorem

Let r tend to infinity through odd primes, b=ceil(log2 r), and m=Theta((log b)^gamma) for any fixed gamma>0. Let G=<P> have order r. Put

    K=2^floor(b/(64m)), delta=K^(-1/16), n=(Kr)^(1/m).

There exist indexed nonidentity unweighted blocks F_0,...,F_(m-1) with |F_i|=(1+o(1))n and with the following simultaneous properties.

* The one-per-block count d(g) satisfies |d(g)-K|<=K delta for every g in G. Thus full support, the corresponding pointwise flatness scale of POINTWISE_SEEDS.md, and positive one-pin short-pair counts strictly below r all hold.
* For doubled tuples (two occurrences from each block), the complete reduced fixed-shift system at P has full column rank N=sum_i(|F_i|-1) over F_r.
* Some full rational basis of N genuine rows at P has modular rank exactly N-1. Its row span W has the following property: exactly min(r,16m+1) target displacements have any individual doubled-tuple difference in W. For sufficiently large r this is a vanishing fraction of all targets.

The construction does not assert efficient deterministic generation of these adversarial blocks or selection of that basis by the repository's actual order. The random additions below are used only to prove existence. In particular, the theorem does not refute the possibility of a better selection strategy on these very blocks.

## 2. A digit dictionary and a fixed-shift sublattice

Take D=2^ceil(b/m). For sufficiently large b, D<r, D^m>r, and 4mD+1<r. Pick a nonzero kappa in F_r such that within each block the dictionary

    A_(i,j)=[kappa j D^i+1]P, 0<=j<=D,
    -P, 2P, -2P

consists of distinct nonidentity points. For any j>=1, avoiding a collision with O,-P,2P,-2P excludes at most four values of kappa; digit points are mutually distinct since D is invertible modulo r and j ranges over fewer than r values. This proves existence of kappa. Each dictionary has L=D+4 points, including P as A_(i,0).

Augment every dictionary by arbitrary further distinct nonidentity points. Each point in block i has an integer offset d_A in {-2,-1,1,2} and a coefficient c_A modulo r such that A=[kappa c_A+d_A]P. Use c_A=jD^i and d_A=1 for digit points (allow the displayed integer lift), c_A=0 for the other reference points with their indicated offsets, and d_A=2 and c_A=(log_P A-2)/kappa modulo r for every added point. These scalar coordinates describe the existential example, not an algorithm computing them.

Use P as reference in every block; the reduced coordinate for P is zero. Let u be the vector of the c_A modulo r, so u_(0,A_(0,1))=1. For a doubled tuple X write c(X) for its reduced incidence vector and d(X) for the sum of its integer offsets. Any difference row a=c(Y)-c(X) of shift [q]P satisfies

    q = kappa (a dot u)+d(Y)-d(X)  (mod r).

Let L_bad be the integer lattice generated by all genuine unit-shift rows a for which a dot u=0 modulo r. Then

    L_bad = {z in Z^N : z dot u=0 mod r}.

Proof: inclusion is immediate. In the quotient Z^N/L_bad write e_(i,A) for the reduced unit coordinates. First all three nonreference reference coordinates vanish: the single changes P->2P and -2P->-P give unit rows, as does changing the two occurrences (-P,2P) to (P,P). Their u scores are zero.

Whenever two padded doubled tuples X and X' have equal group value and equal u score modulo r, and X has a reference P slot, replace that P in X by 2P to obtain Y. Then both Y-X and Y-X' are genuine unit rows in L_bad. Subtract them to get c(X')-c(X) in L_bad. This elementary device realizes all following equalities as contrasts of actual unit-shift rows, not as independently assumed zero-shift relations.

Within block i use the identity A_(i,j)+P=A_(i,j-1)+A_(i,1) for 2<=j<=D, padding all other slots by P. It gives e_(i,j)=j e_(i,1). For adjacent blocks A_(i,D)=A_(i+1,1), compare the tuples with that respective single digit point and all other slots P. It gives e_(i+1,1)=D e_(i,1). Thus all dictionary coordinates are their displayed integer digit coefficients times e_(0,1). There is a free reference slot in each first tuple when m>=2.

For an added point A of coefficient c in [0,r) and offset 2, compare a tuple with just A and otherwise P to a tuple with one base-D digit of c in every block and one reference filler per block. Use 2P for one filler and P for the others. Both have coefficient c and total offset 2m+1. The equality forces e_(i,A)=c e_(0,1). Finally compare the all-P tuple with the tuple encoding the integer r in base D, using one digit point and one P filler per block. They have equal group value and u score modulo r. The resulting contrast forces r e_(0,1)=0. Since the quotient is generated by e_(0,1) and maps onto F_r by z->z dot u, its order is exactly r. This proves the lattice identity.

In particular L_bad has full rational rank and modular rank N-1. Choose N-1 modularly independent genuine generating rows and extend them by one rationally independent genuine row from L_bad; the resulting full rational basis has modular span W=u^perp. This is an existential choice, not a claim about the specified integer-extraction ordering.

The dictionary alone covers every group value by short tuples: base-D digits encode any desired coefficient in [0,r), and one digit point per block gives offset m. Consequently doubled tuples cover G as well. To verify full raw rank of the complete unit-shift system, any homogeneous null direction defines a tuple score depending only on the group value, by comparing tuples with a common successor. The score is unchanged under translation by P, hence constant; varying a single slot then gives zero in every reduced coordinate. So the complete system has full modular rank even though the displayed selected basis does not.

## 3. Exact target ambiguity and a coefficient-height barrier

A target is individually identifiable from this selected basis precisely if some difference a_Q at shift Q is in W. Since W=u^perp and |d(Y)-d(X)|<=8m for doubled tuples, such a shift must be a residue in [-8m,8m]. Conversely all those residues occur by using reference points alone: the set of differences of offsets in {-2,-1,1,2} is every integer in [-4,4], and there are 2m slots. Every resulting difference has zero u score. Thus the count is exactly min(r,16m+1), not just an upper bound. The corresponding short-tuple count is min(r,8m+1). This rules out even arbitrary choice of both endpoints' decompositions; in particular for a target outside that interval no single pair with source value O works.

There is a separate limited statement about combining target equations. If integer lifts alpha_i of field coefficients satisfy sum alpha_i=1 modulo r and sum alpha_i a_(Q,i) is in W, then

    q = sum_i alpha_i (d(Y_i)-d(X_i)) (mod r),
    ||q||_r <= 8m sum_i |alpha_i|,

where ||q||_r is the least absolute integer representative. Hence a bounded-height combination cannot identify targets far from zero; for q near r/2 the necessary l1 height is Omega(r/m). This is not a bit-complexity lower bound, because field coefficients of that magnitude still have O(b) bits. Multiple target equations can indeed remove the residual line (see PR #9's complete-augmentation rank theorem). No efficient universal selection of those equations follows here.

## 4. Strong pointwise flatness after augmentation

The lattice and target arguments tolerate arbitrary added points. We now prove existence of augmented blocks matching the stronger pointwise-uniform count promise, not merely dense support. This adapts the centered-partition argument of POINTWISE_SEEDS.md to mixed deterministic and random slots.

### 4.1 Dictionary convolution bound

For any subset of the dictionary blocks, including the option to adjoin O to each dictionary, its convolution multiplicities are bounded by M=2^O(m), uniformly in the subset. Indeed let j_i in [0,D] (zero for omitted blocks) denote the digit, with the zero digit allowing at most five offsets. The integer coefficient C=sum_i j_i D^i lies in [0,2D^m]. For a fixed total offset in [-2m,2m] the group equation fixes C modulo r, allowing at most 1+2D^m/r=2^O(m) lifts. For each integer C at most 2^m digit strings are possible by peeling base-D residues, with at most two choices of a digit in [0,D] for a given residue. At most 5^m offset choices remain. Multiplying gives the stated bound. In particular for a subset of s unextended blocks its sumset has size between L^s/M and L^s. For the empty subset use the identity delta distribution.

### 4.2 Random model and a weighted moment lemma

Keep the dictionary of each block and independently retain every other nonidentity point with probability

    theta=(n-L)/(r-L-1),    q0=r theta.

These parameters are valid for sufficiently large r. Indeed L/n=2^(-Omega(b/m^2)), and q0/n=1+o(1/m). The expected block size is n. Chebyshev's inequality gives simultaneous block sizes (1+o(1))n with probability 1-o(1), for instance using relative error n^(-1/4).

For a subset J of k>0 random slots, convolve their random indicators with the deterministic dictionary convolution w from the complementary slots. Let its support size be s, its largest coefficient at most M, and mu=q0^k s/r. At a specified target g the mixed count is

    Z(g)=sum_X w(g-sum X) I_X,

where X ranges over the ambient allowed random points in those k blocks, and I_X is the product of the k corresponding Bernoulli indicators. For even h and q0>=1 the following bounds hold:

    E[Z(g)^h] <= M^h B_h^k max(1,mu)^h;

    E[(Z(g)-E Z(g))^h]
       <= 2^h M^h B_h^k mu^h K_*^(-h/4)
       if q0,mu >= K_* >= 1.

Here B_h is the Bell number. Proof: for an ordered h-array of tuples, let pi_i be the equality partition of the h indices in random block i, ell_i its class count, ell=sum ell_i, and R the rank of the h-by-ell matrix of class indicators over F_r. For the nonzero-weight tuple arrays each RHS belongs to g-supp(w). Choose R independent rows, then their RHS have at most s^R choices; fixing the remaining ell-R free variables gives at most r^(ell-R) assignments for each. Dropping distinctness and ambient restrictions only enlarges this bound. The expectation of the product of indicators is theta^ell. Multiplying by the weight bound M^h gives at most

    M^h mu^R q0^(ell-kR)

per pattern. Since each block's class indicators are independent, ell_i<=R and ell<=kR. The raw estimate follows by summing at most B_h^k patterns.

For the centered moment expand products of J_X=I_X-theta^k. An isolated tuple (one sharing no point with any other tuple in any random block) gives zero by independence and zero mean. For each remaining pattern the absolute expectation is <=2^h theta^ell: expanding over subsets of tuple factors, each term has exponent ell_U+k(h-|U|)>=ell. Put d=h-R and C=kh-ell. The normalized bound on a pattern is therefore

    2^h M^h mu^h mu^(-d) q0^(-(C-kd)).

For completeness, the rank inequality from POINTWISE_SEEDS.md applies to these weighted patterns without change. Let S0 be the union of supports of the kernel of the transpose of the incidence matrix; put l=|S0|. Every class meeting S0 contains at least two indices in S0, since its class-sum vanishes on every kernel vector. Each block therefore has at least l/2 mergers in its restriction to S0, and at least d mergers by rank-nullity in that restriction. Outside S0, nonisolation implies at least (h-l)/2 further mergers across the blocks: a class meeting S0 contributes one merger for each added outside index, and a purely outside class of size j>=2 contributes j-1>=j/2. Thus

    C >= k max(d,l/2)+(h-l)/2.

In particular C-kd>=0, and either d>=h/4 or C-kd>=h/4 (separately use l<=2d or l>2d in the displayed bound). Hence the normalized factor mu^(-d) q0^(-(C-kd)) is <=K_*^(-h/4). Summing the patterns proves the centered estimate. The only changes to the earlier proof are the s^R count of RHS choices and the deterministic weight factor M^h.

### 4.3 Uniform control of every mixed term

Write the full count d(g) as the sum of 2^m mixed terms Z_J(g), one for each set of random slots. The all-dictionary term is deterministic. From the dictionary support bounds, D^m/r=2^O(m), and q0/n=1+o(1/m), uniformly for k=|J|,

    2^(-O(m)) K^(k/m) <= mu <= 2^O(m) K^(k/m).

Take h=8192m and T=K delta/2^(m+2). All factors 2^h M^h B_h^m and the extra 2^O(mh) factors below are 2^O(m^2 log m)=2^o(b).

If k>=m/2, both q0 and mu exceed K^(1/3) eventually, so apply the centered bound with K_*=K^(1/3). Since mu<=2^O(m)K and delta=K^(-1/16), Markov gives

    Pr(|Z_J-E Z_J|>T)
        <= 2^O(m^2 log m) K^(-h/12+h/16)
        = 2^O(m^2 log m) K^(-h/48)
        = 2^(-8b/3+o(b)).

If 0<k<m/2, then mu<=2^O(m)K^(1/2). The first-moment bound E Z_J<=M mu=o(T) follows either by fixing one variable or directly counting assignments. The raw h-th moment gives

    Pr(Z_J>T/2) <= 2^O(m^2 log m) K^(-7h/16).

For large b this also bounds the probability of |Z_J-E Z_J|>T. These probabilities are o(1/(r 2^m)), so a union bound yields simultaneously all mixed deviations <=T with probability 1-o(1). Their sum is <=K delta/4. No independence between different mixed terms or targets is required.

### 4.4 The mean itself is pointwise flat

For the mean block indicator write f_i=theta 1_G+h_i with

    h_i=(1-theta) 1_dictionary_i - theta 1_{O}.

Its total mass is n. Expanding the convolution of all f_i, every term containing 1_G is constant. Hence

    E d(g)=K+(h_0*...*h_(m-1))(g) - (prod_i sum_x h_i(x))/r.

The absolute value of the convolution term is bounded by the extended dictionary multiplicity M, and the absolute final term is at most (D+5)^m/r=2^O(m). Thus E d(g)=K+2^O(m) in the sense of an absolute error bound. As 2^O(m)=o(K delta), the mixed deviations prove |d(g)-K|<=K delta for every g simultaneously, with probability tending to one. Intersect with the block-size event from section 4.2. This establishes existence of the required augmented blocks.

Finally, writing W0=prod_i |F_i|=sum_g d(g)<=rK(1+delta), any one-side one-pin count for short-pair displacement t is a sum of opposite-side fiber counts over W0/|F_i| tuples. It is positive and at most

    K(1+delta) W0/|F_i|
      <= r K^2(1+delta)^2/(D+4) < r

eventually, since K^2/D tends to zero. This verifies the numerical no-wrap promise as well as flatness. The proof does not require punctured full support (which is incompatible with universal one-pin integer no-wrap for the earlier large redundant regime).

## 5. Verification, scope and next question

Run check_flatness.py (NumPy and SymPy required, assertions enabled). The integer lattice tests use exact Smith normal forms and actual selected unit-shift rows, not the contrast rows as unverified input. The order-13 example also checks every selected row using the actual elliptic group law on E/F7: y^2=x^3+3. Other fixtures use explicit cyclic groups. Joint (coefficient,offset) support is enumerated exactly to count individually identifiable targets. Short fiber and one-pin counts are exact integer convolutions. The weighted moment test expands the centered and raw moments by equality partitions and compares with exhaustive independent Bernoulli masks. See verification.txt. The finite toy fixtures do not realize the enormous asymptotic parameters or the vanishing relative-error promise; the latter is proved by the above existence argument, not inferred from the tests.

On C_4093 with m=3, the fixture has rank 63 over Q and 62 modulo 4093, the selected lattice has index 4093, every short fiber is present, all one-pin counts are 829..3776, and only 49 of 4093 targets are individually identifiable. On C_1009 with m=2 the corresponding number is 33 of 1009. These are finite examples, not timings for any DLP solver.

Comparison: RESIDUAL_KERNELS.md establishes a high-variance residual line for dense deletion-resilient blocks with genuine consistency equations. PR #9 gives a finite single-pair obstruction and characterizes the full integer relation lattice. The present argument uses only genuine unit-shift rows for its deficient sublattice, matches the much stronger pointwise flatness/no-wrap numerical promise, and gives an asymptotic bound on the fraction of identifiable individual targets. It does not show that the deterministic polynomial mask seed can realize this example, that its canonical order returns the bad basis, or that a multi-row target certificate cannot be efficiently constructed.

The new theorem directs future work away from deriving target identifiability solely from rank, integer saturation of the complete lattice, support and flatness. The remaining possible positive route must exploit the actual selected basis, the concrete masks, or a method of locating multiple target relations. No value of the positive end-to-end saving exponent has been established. The full upstream contraction premise and literature priority remain unverified; the proof here is self-contained apart from standard elementary rank, moment and group algebra arguments.
