# Automatic affine-core peeling and an exponential barrier to explicit stratification

27 November 2026. Abstract finite-group follow-up to PR #36 (`37171fc06945267461e345895b9550860dd15b67`) and PR #33 (`2081253f15db254b67799fee75509efac4d94cb8`). We address the precise remaining hypothesis in #36: finding affine class partitions automatically. A deterministic peeling theorem recovers a sufficient partition in the presence of bounded arbitrary outliers, without supplied affine coordinates or labeled exceptions. More fundamentally, the proposed universal disjunction of affordable explicit stratifications and low exact count-tensor ranks is false for arbitrary permitted lists. A high-probability existence construction rules out *every* choice of auxiliary primes and every explicit stratification with fewer than 2^(b-o(b)) branches on the same family that has exponential exact unfolding ranks. These are representation-class statements, not lower bounds for all algorithms or for the actual elliptic masks. No improved end-to-end discrete-logarithm exponent is asserted.

## 1. Automatic partition discovery by affine-basis peeling

Let G=prod_(j=1)^d Z/p_j Z, and identify its elements with their canonical integer words X=prod_j [0,p_j-1]. The local moduli need not be prime. Let A_i be nonempty explicit subsets of X. Fix F distinct auxiliary primes Q_f and integer budgets e_i>=0. Suppose (only for the guarantee) that each A_i has a nonempty subset C_i with |A_i minus C_i|<=e_i, such that its images lie in affine spaces a_(i,f)+V_(i,f) over each F_(Q_f). The C_i, spaces, and coordinates are NOT supplied.

**Peeling theorem.** There is a uniform deterministic polynomial-time algorithm outputting a partition of A_i into one core A_i' and at most F e_i(d+1) singleton exceptions, whenever |A_i|>F e_i(d+1). The core lies in the same promised affine space in every auxiliary field. The output's actual affine ranks and intervals can be verified by ordinary elimination. The method uses only the explicit lists, fields and budgets. It need not recover exactly the original hidden core, but it does recover a usable partition.

Proof. Start with R=A_i. For each field Q_f, repeat e_i times: select any linear basis of the lifted vectors (1,a) for a in R over that field, and remove the selected words. Each such basis has at most d+1 members. The promised affine space lifts to a linear subspace consisting of the span of (1,a_(i,f)) and the vectors (0,v), v in V_(i,f). If the current remainder has any member outside this linear subspace, any basis of its span contains at least one member outside it. All such members are among the original <=e_i exceptions. If an outside member remained after e_i rounds, every one of those rounds would have removed a distinct outside member, and there would have been at least e_i+1 originally. Contradiction. Processing another field only removes points, so containment in every previously processed space persists. At most F e_i(d+1) words have been removed, and the size assumption ensures nonempty R. Compute its affine hull in every field. Restore all original words belonging to the intersection of these hulls, testing membership with linear algebra. This includes R, preserves every promised affine containment, and cannot increase the number of removed words. Use the restored set A_i' as one class and each remaining word as a singleton. QED.

The size guarantee allows the same core to be translated arbitrarily inside the canonical integer box; there is no distinguished zero label. If the proposed budgets are too small or R becomes empty, the output is still a legitimate partition (in the latter case use singletons), but no small-state guarantee follows from this theorem. A candidate partition is always tested by the exact downstream guard. Polynomial running time includes the e_i loops (cap e_i at |A_i| if necessary), scanning words, and field elimination. This is not a robust-subspace estimator relying on a probabilistic general-position assumption.

### 1.1 Automatic sufficient guard for the joint-shadow selector

Let C be the correction list of PR #36, L=|C|, and P=prod_f Q_f. Let W_j=1+sum_i(max_(a in A_i) a_j-min_(a in A_i) a_j) be the whole-list interval width, and k_f=dim(sum_i V_(i,f)). Let H0=prod_i(1+F e_i(d+1)), an upper bound on the number of discovered class branches. For every branch, the affine direction span in field f is contained in sum_i V_(i,f); a singleton contributes no directions. Its dimension k'_f is thus at most k_f regardless of affine offsets or the actual pivot coordinates. The branch interval width is <=W_j.

Write D_j for the product of primes in which coordinate j is dependent in the common coordinate order of #36. Its weight there satisfies a_j<=ceil(W_j/D_j)/p_j<=(W_j+P-1)/(p_j D_j). Because prod_j D_j=prod_f Q_f^(d-k'_f), the total initial potential obeys

    Psi_0 <= L H0 [prod_j ((W_j+P-1)/p_j)] / prod_f Q_f^(d-k_f).

Likewise each worst-case state multiplier B_j of #36 is <=ceil(W_j/p_j). Hence

    K_0 <= L H0 prod_j ceil(W_j/p_j) <= L H0 (m+1)^d.

If the first displayed bound is strictly below one and the second fits the charged budget, the discovered partition succeeds by #36's exact prefix averaging, without any knowledge of the hidden core or directions. The actual, often better guard is computed by enumerating the discovered branches and their own intervals/ranks. One may try a polynomial number of auxiliary fields or pairs from a polynomial-size pool, and a polynomial number of budgets, always checking the explicit branch/state cap before enumeration. No claim is made for exhaustive search over unbounded numbers of fields or over all nonuniform budgets simultaneously.

In particular this removes the supplied-partition hypothesis for the **specific augmented family** of #36 sections 3 and 5. There, Q_1,Q_2=Theta(m^(4/5)), k_f<=d/4, d=Theta(b/log b), m=Theta((log b)^(1/4)), p_j in [b,4b], and each original core has at most 2d added arbitrary words (provided they are canonical). The original listed augmentation has W_j<=m P_orig where P_orig=Theta(b) is the three-prime CRT product in #36; even arbitrary canonical outliers give W_j<=m(p_j-1)+1. Thus (W_j+Q_1 Q_2-1)/p_j<=2m eventually. With F=2, e_i=2d, the number of discovered branches is <=(1+4d(d+1))^m. The initial potential bound is at most L (1+4d(d+1))^m [O(1) m^(-1/5)]^d, which tends to zero. Its state bound is 2^{O(m log b+d log m)}=2^{o(b/m)}. The planted cores have sizes >=2^{b/m-O(log b)}, far above the peeling removal bound. Trying all pairs of primes <=4b and the budget 2d has polynomial overhead. The whole abstract construction is therefore polynomial in explicit input size and b in this family, without labels for the exceptions, a supplied partition, or advice identifying the useful primes. The earlier proof of exponential tensor ranks remains applicable to the specific constructed augmentation; it is not needed for this peeling guarantee.

## 2. The integer-lattice box closure controls every prime shadow at once

A universal partition-discovery theorem of the proposed type is nevertheless impossible for arbitrary allowed lists if the number of explicit branches must be small. The reason does not depend on finding the best auxiliary primes.

For a nonempty class D subset X, choose a reference a in D and define

    L_D = span_Z {x-a:x in D},
    K(D) = (a+L_D) intersect prod_j [min_(x in D) x_j,max_(x in D) x_j].

The integer words K(D) are in the canonical box X; identify them with group elements of G. This closure contains D and is monotone under inclusion of classes. For any prime Q, x in K(D) implies x mod Q belongs to the affine hull of D mod Q, because the lattice expression reduces modulo Q. The *same integer x* works for every prime, including local primes. More strongly, even an exact integer-lattice-and-interval relaxation of the class contains K(D) by definition.

Let a partition or cover of A_i be given by nonempty classes D_(i,h), each contained in A_i; define U_i=union_h K(D_(i,h)). For arbitrary x_i in U_i, choose one containing class for each. The integer sum u=sum_i x_i lies in that branch's interval box and in its affine hull of sums modulo every auxiliary prime. Thus sum_i U_i+C is contained in the union of the exact simultaneous-shadow relaxations, even if each branch has its own primes and coordinate order. If any two U_i,U_j have sizes >|G|/2, their group sum is G: for each z the two sets U_i and z-U_j intersect. All other U_l and C are nonempty, so the complete union relaxation then equals G. This rules out all exact compatibility tests based solely on those branches, not just the sufficient numerical guard of #36. It even holds if every prime is used simultaneously, or if the classes are strengthened to their individual integer-lattice box closures before adding them.

## 3. A counting theorem excluding all small stratifications

Let H=|G|=prod_j p_j and Pmax=max_j p_j. Put

    t=d+d ceil(log_2(d Pmax)),
    J=1+(t+3) ceil(log_2 H),
    T=floor(n/(2J)).

Assume each of m lists consists of n independent uniform words of X (we enforce distinctness separately). With probability at least 1-m 2^(-n/2), simultaneously for each list, **every cover by at most T nonempty classes** has closure union of size >H/2, provided T>=1. Consequently on this event any explicit class-stratified affine-shadow certificate of an absent point needs at least (T+1)^(m-1) class branches. This conclusion does not require the classes to be disjoint. If the full true product n^m is smaller than H, missing points really exist; they are simply hidden from all such small covers.

Proof of the counting claim. Every lattice L_D has a generating subset of at most t differences from the reference. To see this, choose a maximal rationally independent set of s<=d differences. They span the same rational subspace as L_D. Let L0 be their integer span, and S their rational span intersect Z^d. Choose s coordinate rows for which the s-by-s basis minor is nonzero. The projection is injective on the rational span; its image of S lies in Z^s and the image of L0 has index equal to the absolute minor determinant. Hence [S:L0] is at most that determinant, which is <=(d Pmax)^d by Hadamard (or the elementary determinant expansion). For s=0 the lattice is zero and no differences are needed. If a remaining difference is outside the current lattice, adjoining it reduces the positive finite index in S by an integer factor of at least two. Thus at most d ceil(log_2(d Pmax)) further differences suffice; stop when all differences are in the generated lattice. This proves the bound t without requiring a short Euclidean basis algorithm or assuming saturation.

Encode a closure by a reference from X, up to t other words from X whose differences generate the lattice (pad by the reference), and two endpoint words of X specifying the interval box. The endpoint words need not themselves belong to the class. There are at most H^(t+3) possible closures. This counts every possible rank and lattice, not merely the affine spaces over a bounded set of primes. Thus all unions of at most T closures are described by at most (H^(t+3)+1)^T <=2^(J T) choices, padding with empty sets. For a fixed union of size <=H/2, the probability it contains n iid uniform words is at most 2^-n. Union bound gives probability <=2^(JT-n)<=2^(-n/2) for one list. Union over m lists proves the claim. An arbitrary cover of that list has its closure union containing all sampled words, so the count applies to all covers simultaneously, including ones chosen adaptively from the list.

Finally, if a successful certificate used <=T classes for two different lists, both corresponding closure unions would have density >1/2 and section 2 would force the entire relaxed union to be G. Therefore at least m-1 lists must use >=T+1 classes, and the product of class counts is >=(T+1)^(m-1). The proof concerns explicit enumeration of all class choices in #36's architecture; it does not assert that compressed unions cannot exploit further structure. QED.

The elementary finite-class union bound is a standard uniform-convergence argument; the point here is the simultaneous integer-lattice description bound independent of the auxiliary characteristics. No general complexity lower bound is implied by it.

## 4. Simultaneous exponential unfolding ranks in the original CRT parameter range

The same random lists can also force large exact convolution ranks. We give the details to distinguish this from an unsupported assertion that random sparse tensors are generically high rank.

Use the abstract CRT setting of #33: r odd prime, b=ceil(log_2 r), m=Theta((log_2 b)^(1/4)), distinct prime moduli in [b,4b] of product R in (r/(4b),r], s=Theta(b/log b) such primes, and d=2s with each prime occurring twice. Then H=R^2. Let n=ceil(r^(1/m)), computed via integer powering and binary search. Draw m independent lists of n iid uniform words in X, independently across words and coordinates. The probability any list has a repeated word is <=m n(n-1)/(2H)=o(1), so the following events can be intersected with distinctness. On distinctness these are ordinary unweighted sets, and decoding CRT words gives legitimate plane lists. No particular factor-base predicate or group-coverage property is claimed.

Their total tuple product M=n^m=(1+o(1))r satisfies the original fixed-slack product condition, even after the at most m^2 global wrap corrections C containing zero. With J from section 3 polynomial in b, T=floor(n/(2J)) has log_2(T+1)>=log_2 n-O(log b). Thus with probability 1-o(1) every successful explicit stratified certificate requires

    (T+1)^(m-1) >= 2^{b-O(b/m+m log b)} = 2^{b-o(b)}

branches. We next show that every coordinate ordering simultaneously forces high count-tensor width.

For any balanced coordinate cut I, |I|=s, put H_I=prod_(j in I) p_j. Its log differs from log R by O(s) since each modulus is between b and 4b. Thus log_2 H_I=b+O(b/log b+log b) uniformly. For any fixed projected value y, let Z_I(y) count index tuples whose projected sum is y. Take h=ceil(4m^2) and B=2^ceil(b/m^2). We prove

    E Z_I(y)^h <= Bell(h)^m max(1,M/H_I)^h.                (A)

Expand the h-th power over h ordered tuples. For each of the m lists, partition the h occurrences by equality of their chosen indices. Let ell be the total number of classes and R0 the rational rank of the h-by-ell class incidence matrix. Each row has exactly m ones. Columns for one list are disjoint nonempty indicators, so each list's class count is <=R0, and ell<=m R0. The number of index assignments for this pattern is at most n^ell. The matrix has the same rank over every local prime: all larger rational minors vanish integrally, and one nonzero R0-minor has absolute value <=m^(h/2)<b for sufficiently large b, because h log m=o(log b). In each local coordinate in I the corresponding entries of the sampled words are independent uniform field elements; the h linear equations have probability at most p_j^(-R0) (zero if inconsistent). Independence across coordinates gives probability <=H_I^(-R0). The contribution is at most n^ell H_I^(-R0)<= (M/H_I)^R0. There are at most Bell(h)^m patterns. This proves (A). It is a with-replacement index calculation, valid before conditioning on distinctness.

The logarithm of the right side of (A) is o(b): Bell(h)<=h^h, m h log h=o(b), and h log_2 max(1,M/H_I)=O(m^2 b/log b+m^2 log b)=o(b). Hence Markov gives Pr(Z_I(y)>=B)<=2^(-4b+o(b)). There are <=2^d balanced cuts and at most 2^{b+o(b)} projected values per cut, so a union bound gives Z_I(y)<B simultaneously for every balanced cut and projected value with probability 1-o(1). This is a proof of the high-probability statement, not a claim that small random experiments establish it.

For each balanced unfolding, the nonnegative integer count tensor of the main lists has total mass M, every row and column total <=B, and at least M/B nonzero columns. Adding L=|C|<=m^2 correction shifts yields a sum of translated tensors; row supports have size <=LB, while the zero correction preserves at least M/B nonzero columns. A row basis of rank t0 and row support <=LB can cover at most t0 LB columns. Therefore its rational rank is >=M/(L B^2). The same argument works for the exact modular reconstruction used in #33: among any t coefficient primes whose product exceeds LM, each nonzero integer column survives in at least one field. Some field has >=M/(Bt) nonzero columns and hence rank >=M/(L B^2 t). When t is polynomial in b this is 2^{b-O(b/m^2)-O(log b)}=2^{b-o(b)}. The field may depend on the cut. Every coordinate order has a balanced cut; all were included in the high-probability event. No numerical tensor approximation is analyzed here.

Intersect the no-repeat event, the closure-cover event and the all-cut moment event. All have probability tending to one. Thus there exist balanced unweighted plane lists of the relevant cardinality scale, with fixed product slack and actual missing points, for which BOTH of the following fail: (i) every affordable explicit class enumeration using integer boxes and simultaneous affine shadows over arbitrary auxiliary primes, even with exact compatibility testing; (ii) every coordinate ordering of the exact expanded count tensor with polynomial-in-explicit-size bond cap and polynomial-length coefficient CRT reconstruction. The existence construction is probabilistic, not an efficiently deterministic explicit family or a lower bound on symbolic union processing, alternative integer embeddings, nonlinear methods, or algorithms tailored to actual selected factor bases. It is a negative answer to the universal representation dichotomy for these two specific classes of methods.

## 6. Status and scope

The positive contribution solves the unlabeled bounded-outlier discovery problem for the specific previous structured family and generalizes it to arbitrary simultaneous affine cores. The negative contribution proves that no theorem claiming small explicit affine stratifications OR small exact tensor-train ranks on every permitted abstract input can hold. The counterexample is not an impossibility result for all deterministic avoidance algorithms or for a different compressed representation. It also does not exclude a theorem about the actual factor-base masks, which impose extra structure not enforced by this existence family. No uniform improved end-to-end exponent, independent proof-chain review, or literature priority claim is made. The lattice index argument, finite-class union bound, elementary moment method and Gaussian elimination are classical in isolation; the combined representation barrier is the contribution to review. The remaining route must exploit actual input-specific arithmetic or a genuinely different representation rather than assume arbitrary sparse lists always possess these affine/tensor structures.
