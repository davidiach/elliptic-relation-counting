# Exact moment-oracle barriers to modular rank recovery

27 November 2026. Consolidation of two AI-assisted research notes developed against main at 04a8b3d8bb904af8a30aa996f1af4027b52f6dd4 and reviewed against main at 18ce38e4b108a73497f0fa89be4883578255fead (merged PR #15). The results concern abstract finite integer matrices and a restricted row-index interval oracle. They do not improve the project's uniform deterministic ECDLP exponent, establish a computational lower bound for its contractor, or show that its actual complete elliptic relation families realize the counterexamples. Independent proof review and literature priority remain outstanding.

## 1. Why preserve this follow-up?

The earlier [integer completion note](../short_relations_adaptive_rank/INTEGER_COMPLETION.md) establishes near-complete modular rank from exact Gram positivity. The newer [integral lattice audit](../integral_lattice_audit/RESEARCH.md), section 3, already gives an abstract exact-Gram interval-oracle obstruction with bounded row norms and logarithmic dimension, even with a toy selected relation interpretation. We do not claim priority for that observation. The new contributions below strengthen different parameters:

| Result | Degree of exact moment agreement | Row count below r? | Distinct rows? |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Planar cut/cycle blocks, repeated-block oracle | 3 | Yes | Not globally |
| Sparse tagged planar blocks | 3 | Yes | Yes, in lexicographic order |
| Binary additive cosets and tags | Arbitrary prescribed t >= 2 | No | Yes |

The first two constructions give a deterministic interval-query lower bound of Omega(r/log r), including a constant fraction of violating rows in the full-rank cases. The third gives Omega(r) queries through arbitrary prescribed degree t, with a larger row count. The no-wrap score-diversity criterion in section 5 is a positive boundary result. Do not combine the favorable parameters of the three separate constructions into one theorem.

The latest [compact quotient cover note](../compact_quotient_covers/RESEARCH.md) uses an explicit group map and additional quotient structure. The present oracle does not model that information. Similarly it excludes arbitrary pin constraints, key-dependent weights, direct arithmetic access, and other statistics. Its relevance is to the sufficiency of exact aggregate moment information alone.

## 2. Planar cut/cycle construction

**Theorem 1.** For every odd prime r there are equal-length lists of integer rows of dimension d=O(log r), entries in {-1,0,1}, squared row norm at most 5, and full rational rank, with identical exact coordinate monomial sums through degree three (including degree zero) but modular ranks d-1 and d. All rows have last coordinate 1, so the same affine solution satisfies every RHS equal to one.

**Proof.** The sole non-elementary graph-existence input is Theorem 3.1 of Alon, Bucić and Gishboliner, [The spanning tree spectrum: improved bounds and simple proofs](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23648v2), arXiv:2503.23648v2 (2025). For sufficiently large modulus r and coprime integers a,b it supplies a simple planar graph G with O(log r) vertices and a distinguished edge e with tree counts tau(G/e)=a and tau(G-e)=b modulo r. Take (a,b)=(1,-1). Thus G-e is connected, tau(G)=0 modulo r and the deleted-edge tree count is a unit. For the finitely many remaining odd primes use a cycle of length r, increasing the absolute constant in the asymptotic size bound if necessary.

Choose a plane embedding of G. Put n=|E(G)|. Let U be the oriented edge-by-vertex incidence matrix with one vertex column removed; let V be the edge-by-face signed boundary matrix with one face column removed. Both have independent columns over every field, by connectedness of the primal and dual graphs (bridges give zero dual incidence). Cut/cycle orthogonality gives U^T V=0 over Z, and Euler's formula gives a total of n columns. Therefore A+=[U,V] and A-=[U,-V] are square and rationally invertible, with identical exact Gram matrix diag(U^T U,V^T V).

Write L=U^T U. Kirchhoff's matrix-tree theorem gives det L=tau(G). For the row w^T associated with e, det(L-ww^T)=tau(G-e) is a unit modulo r. Since the difference has rank at most one, L has modular corank one. Over F_r, im V=ker U^T by orthogonality and the dimension count. Each x in ker L uniquely determines y with Ux+Vy=0, so A+ has modular corank one. A- does as well. Their kernel intersection is trivial: addition and subtraction of the two equations imply Ux=Vy=0 because r is odd. Thus the stacked matrices have rank n modulo r.

For each row a of either matrix, take the pair (a,1),(-a,1). Denote the resulting 2n-row lists by H and J respectively. Pairing cancels all odd moments in the first n coordinates, and the degree-two moments are their common Gram entries; the last coordinate is constant. Consequently H and J agree in all exact monomial sums of degree at most three. Each has full rational rank d=n+1 and modular rank d-1, and their union has full modular rank. Compare H stacked with H to H stacked with J. Every row has at most four incidence entries and a constant entry, so its norm squared is at most five. Planarity gives n=O(log r). This proves the theorem. QED.

**Dense violation variant (Lemma 2).** In addition, for sufficiently large odd r the two base blocks H,J can be chosen so that a fixed positive fraction of J rows have nonzero modular score on the unique null line of H, with n=Theta(log r).

Put ell=ceil(log_2 r). Use the cited graph theorem for the coprime pair (-ell,1) to find G0 with distinguished edge e, t=tau(G0/e)=-ell mod r and u=tau(G0-e)=1 mod r. Replace e with a path of ell edges, obtaining G. A spanning tree either uses all path edges (t choices), or omits one of them (ell choices of omitted edge, u trees each). Thus tau(G)=t+ell*u=0 modulo r. Deleting any of the new path edges leaves precisely u spanning trees: all surviving path edges are forced and the rest is a tree of G0-e. For a nonzero x in ker L, every path-edge incidence row w has w^T x != 0 mod r; otherwise (L-ww^T)x=0 would contradict the unit deleted-edge count. If (x,y) is the corresponding kernel vector of A+, then A-(x,y)=2Ux. Both paired rows of J for each path edge violate the extended vector (x,y,0). At least ell/n of J violates it, and ell<=n=O(log r). QED.

## 3. Interval-oracle lower bound and distinct tags

**Oracle definition.** A list of rows of known length is accessible only through queries for a contiguous interval of its row indices; a query returns the complete exact integer sums of all coordinate monomials through a specified total degree. A singleton exposes its row and degree zero returns the count. Arbitrary computation on replies is free. The input representation is not otherwise exposed. In the promised search problem an algorithm must return an actual row key with nonzero modular score on a supplied direction. A known modular rank-(d-1) sublist is permitted.

First repeat the dense planar blocks of section 2. Give the algorithm a fixed H block, and M further blocks of identical length L0=2n, each H or J, with M positive and even. All H is the rank-deficient comparison input. A promised full-rank input has exactly M/2 J blocks. Its violating fraction is at least M/(2(M+1)) times the fixed positive fraction within J. The fixed H gives both the supplied direction and the known sublist. The common affine solution and full rational rank are present in all cases.

A contiguous interval partially intersects at most two blocks. An entirely included or excluded block may be changed between H and J without altering any returned moment. Run a deterministic algorithm on the all-H transcript. After Q<M/4 queries at least M/2 free blocks are untouched by any partial intersection. Changing M/2 of these to J preserves the transcript but changes rank. For promised search, reserve one additional untouched block if necessary to avoid the output key's block. If the algorithm has not output by that depth, a full-rank input shares the transcript to that depth. Hence both rank decision and promised search have worst-case query lower bound Omega(M). This is an indistinguishability proof, not an assumption that the all-H comparison input is a promised full-rank input.

Take M maximal even with (M+1)L0<r. For large r, M=Theta(r/log r). Every entry of every returned integer moment has absolute value <r, including counts, since all row entries have magnitude <=1. This is entrywise no-wrap, not a claim about determinant valuations. It proves the sparse logarithmic-dimension cubic lower bound.

**Tag lemma (Lemma 3).** Let H,J be any two nonempty equal-length lists with distinct rows within each block, entries in {-1,0,1}, a constant coordinate 1, and equal exact moments through degree t. Assume H has rational full rank and modular corank one, J with H spans the full modular space, and a fraction delta>0 of J violates H's null direction. In h groups of q-1 new binary coordinates, encode a tag from {0,...,q-1}^h by a zero vector for symbol 0 and the corresponding unit vector otherwise. There are B=q^h tags, of length K=h(q-1), each of squared norm <=h. Prefix each row of a block H or J with its tag, one block for each tag, and sort the concatenated row vectors lexicographically. This is a true lexicographic order, independent of which type of block occupies each tag interval.

Fix H at the zero tag and all K unit tags. The zero tag supplies the original row span; subtracting a copy of one H row at zero from its copy at a unit tag supplies each tag-coordinate direction. Hence the fixed rows certify rational full rank and modular rank d+K-1. The all-H matrix has exactly the old null line extended by zeros; even one J block completes rank. All rows are distinct. Every whole-block moment of total degree <=t factors into a constant tag monomial and an original moment of degree <=t, so is unchanged by swapping types. The previous interval argument applies to the F=B-1-K free blocks, changing floor(F/2) untouched blocks and avoiding the output key if necessary. For F=Theta(B) and large B the full-rank cases have violation fraction Omega(delta), and the decision and promised search lower bounds are Omega(F).

Apply this to the dense planar blocks: each H and J is distinct within itself because A+ and A- are rationally invertible, so no two rows of either are equal or negatives, and none is zero. Let h>=2, q=floor(((r-1)/L0)^(1/h)), and assume q>=2h. Then B L0<=r-1, and B>= (q/(q+1))^h (r-1)/L0 >= exp(-1/2)(r-1)/L0. Also 1+h(q-1)<=B/2. Hence F=Theta(r/log r). The dimension and norm satisfy

    D = O(log r + h (r/log r)^(1/h)),   max squared row norm <=5+h.

This proves the same cubic lower bound with no duplicate rows, a genuine lexicographic order, entrywise no-wrap and a known modular rank-(D-1) sublist. For fixed h this trades dimension for bounded row norms; it does not retain the logarithmic dimension of the repeated-block example. No claim is made for fourth-order agreement at these parameters.

## 4. Arbitrarily high moment order (different size regime)

**Theorem 4.** For any prime r and integer t>=2, put k=t+1, b=ceil(log_2 r), D=1+kb. There are equal-size distinct binary row lists H_0,H_1, of r^t rows each and dimension D, such that all integer coordinate monomial sums through degree t agree. Each has rational full rank and modular corank one, their union has full modular rank, and every row of H_1 has score one on a specified generator of H_0's null line. All rows have first coordinate one and therefore admit the same all-ones-RHS solution.

For sigma in F_r, take all tuples of k shares with sum sigma, and encode a tuple as (1,bit(x_1),...,bit(x_k)), with canonical b-bit representatives. Any j<k shares have exactly r^(k-1-j) completions, independent of sigma. A monomial of degree at most t touches at most t share blocks, giving exact moment equality. This is the classical single-parity-check orthogonal-array property.

For a coefficient field K, a kernel functional is c+sum_i f_i(x_i)=0 on these tuples, where each f_i is a linear combination of bit coordinates, so f_i(0)=0. Choose distinct i,j and a third share to balance the sum. Replacing x_i by x_i+h and x_j by x_j-h gives f_i(x_i+h)-f_i(x_i)=f_j(x_j)-f_j(x_j-h), for arbitrary residues and starting points. Thus the increments are independent of starting point and common to all shares, and the f_i coincide with an additive map F_r -> (K,+). In characteristic zero or characteristic unequal to r the map vanishes and c=0. The bit vectors for 2^j, 0<=j<b, are coordinate units with valid representatives, so every coefficient vanishes: rational full rank follows. In characteristic r over F_r the common additive map is x -> alpha*x, and c=-alpha*sigma. The kernel line is generated by z_sigma=(-sigma,w,...,w), w=(1,2,...,2^(b-1)). The two lines have trivial intersection and every H_1 row scores one on z_0. QED.

Tag these blocks via Lemma 3 with h=b, q=2. For large r, B=2^b, F=Theta(r); dimension is 1+(t+2)b and total row count B*r^t=O(r^(t+1)). The exact degree-t interval lower bound is Omega(r) even with distinct lexicographically ordered binary rows and a constant fraction of violations in the promised cases. This works for any t>=2, including growing t, **but the counts wrap and the norm bound grows**. This does not strengthen every parameter of the cubic example.

## 5. Positive boundary results

**Proposition 5 (score diversity).** For any list of fewer than r rows and supplied direction z over F_r, suppose the nonzero modular scores a dot z take at most t distinct values. The power sums P_j=sum_a (a dot z)^j mod r for j=1,...,t all vanish if and only if all rows kill z.

If the nonzero scores s have positive multiplicities n_s and there are k<=t of them, the first k power sums form a homogeneous Vandermonde system with entries s^j, j=1,...,k. Its determinant is a unit: the usual Vandermonde determinant times the product of the nonzero distinct scores. Vanishing forces each n_s=0 modulo r, contradicting 0<n_s<r. The converse is immediate. Since an interval inherits these hypotheses, degree-t exact coordinate moments permit violation detection and bisection to a violating row using O(log |A|) interval queries. This query count does not charge the number of moment entries, and the score-diversity promise is not established for the actual relation families.

Consequently matched degree-t exact moments, one list annihilating z and the other not, require at least t+1 distinct nonzero scores in the latter if counts do not wrap. This threshold is sharp for modular scalar moments: if t+1 divides r-1, the distinct elements of the multiplicative subgroup of order t+1, each once, have zero sums for the first t powers but not the next. That does not prove sharpness for full integer moment tensors. The binary-coset example has only one nonzero score and size r^t, divisible by r. In general first-moment equality and a common nonzero score on the violating list force its size to be divisible by r.

**Proposition 6 (full-marginal thinning obstruction).** Suppose nonempty multisets H,J are supported on distinct additive sum cosets in F_r^k and all their (k-1)-share marginals agree as integer counts. They must each be the same positive integer multiple of the complete coset. Indeed translate and scale to coset sums 0,1. Parameterize by the first k-1 shares u, with multiplicities h(u),j(u). The marginal omitting the last share gives h=j, and the marginal omitting share i gives h(u)=j(u+e_i) for every i<=k-1. Translation invariance makes h constant. Thus at least r^(k-1) rows are required in this full-marginal model. This does not rule out smaller binary moment matches without full share-marginal equality.

**Proposition 7 (sparsity upper bound).** For binary rows with at most s nonzero entries, exact moments through degree s determine the entire multiset by descending support-size inversion: the product of coordinates indexed by S counts every row containing S, and contributions from strict supersets can be subtracted. For ternary rows in {-1,0,1} of support at most s, the signed-support selector product_{i in S}(x_i^2+epsilon_i*x_i)/2 has degree at most 2s. Descending inversion of these counts determines the multiset. These are informational upper bounds, not efficient algorithms with a large ambient dimension.

## 6. Reproduction and limitations

NetworkX 3.6.1 requires Python 3.11 or later and excludes Python 3.14.1. The recorded run used Python 3.13.5. Run these commands from this directory with assertions enabled.

    python -m pip install networkx==3.6.1
    python -B graph_base.py
    python -B verify.py

The exact output is recorded in [graph_verification.txt](graph_verification.txt) and [verification.txt](verification.txt). Both transcripts were reproduced byte for byte before submission. NetworkX supplies only finite graph construction and planar embeddings; integer determinants, moments and modular eliminations use exact arithmetic. The graph fixture generator searches two elementary spanning-tree recurrences modulo small primes; it is not a constructive implementation of the asymptotic graph-existence theorem.

The first verifier checks 13 graph fixtures through prime 104729, 1091 exact interval-invisibility comparisons, and 13 dense variants including all the path-edge deletions. All 13 base pairs differ at degree four. The second checks 17 binary coset pairs through degree six, six distinct tagged graph examples, 101 tagged interval checks, 5824 scalar moment cases and 36 sharpness examples. The finite tagged cases illustrate the construction, not the asymptotic guard q>=2h in every case. These checks do not establish wider literature priority or validate the project's actual elliptic relation systems.

Prior art: the graph-existence theorem cited above, classical Kirchhoff matrix-tree and cut/cycle orthogonality (also see Biggs, [Chip-firing and the critical group of a graph](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/EMIS/journals/JACO/Volume9_1/m6g7032786582625.fulltext.pdf), 1999), classical orthogonal arrays and single-parity-check codes, Vandermonde interpolation, and support inversion. Cavenagh and Wanless, [Latin trades in groups defined on planar triangulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.2707), is related to matched marginals and integral presentation matrices; no theorem from it is used.

The abstract frontier is whether higher-order agreement, entrywise no-wrap, low sparsity, distinct rows and small dimension can coexist, and which extra structural promises force separation. For this project the independent question is what can be established for the actual implicit group-law relation family and its richer query interface. No result here settles that question, changes the current manuscript, or claims a faster deterministic ECDLP algorithm.
