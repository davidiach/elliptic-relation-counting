# Compact quotient covers: persistent affine-flat completion and joint box certificates

27 November 2026. Follow-up to PR #14 (`aa321921572ebae9d455c9f584517523b2811ee5`), PR #13 (`bef28551c5fdd352cc3e641cd25e7b3f6ec08eb8`), PR #12 (`8a2ee0b78d1ea37e74a8d2ff700ed3bb819213f0`) and main `04a8b3d8bb904af8a30aa996f1af4027b52f6dd4`. The purpose is to avoid explicitly enumerating a large quotient support. The new statements are conditional rank-completion criteria with explicit cost accounting, plus a deterministic structural example that separates them from the earlier scalar tests. They are not an end-to-end deterministic ECDLP exponent improvement. The finite verifiers use only abstract mathematical cyclic-group fixtures; the bit-time applications inherit the closed LZR Theorem 2.2 exact ordinary-count interface.

## 1. Reuse the calibrated quotient, not just the homogeneous score

Adopt PR #14's notation. The reduced incidence space is V=F_r^D. The public group map pi sends a coordinate vector to the corresponding group linear combination of point differences from block references. The unknown true scalar table lambda satisfies pi(x)=[x dot lambda]P. We use the map itself, never assume that lambda is available. An explicit consistent modular subsystem B lambda=b_B has row space W, with a calibrated row a of right side one. Set U=W intersect ker(pi), computed by taking the span of the rows B_i-(b_B)_i a. Let H=V/U, h=dim H=d+1, where d is the residual dimension of the calibrated subsystem. The map pi_bar:H->G is onto. For each tuple X write c_X for its reduced incidence, g0 for the reference total, and S={c_X+U} for the tuple support in H. Thus val(X)=g0+pi(c_X).

PR #14 proves that any z outside S yields a rank increase via one ordinary tuple decomposition, assuming universal coverage by the tuple family. Lift z to x in V, decompose the group point g0+pi(x) into X, and append x-c_X with right side zero. This row has group value zero but is outside U, hence outside W. We are not restricting new rows to bounded-norm fixed-shift rows; the new equation is independently checkable by ordinary group arithmetic. The present note develops compact certificates that z lies outside S without constructing S or assuming a scalar projection has a missing residue. A valid cover containing S is enough. We explicitly price the geometry on the already enumerated factor labels; arbitrary residual-label lookups are not inserted into the contractor.

## 2. A persistent affine-flat cover guarantees several rank increases

**Theorem (persistent cover).** Suppose the complete tuple family covers G. Let the initial support S in H be contained in a union of C explicitly supplied affine flats A_j=z_j+L_j, each of dimension at most k, with C<r and k<h. Assume the cover and an ordinary decomposition procedure are available. Then a deterministic procedure adds genuine independent zero-value equations until the calibrated residual dimension is at most max(0,k-1). In particular, if k=1 it gives full modular rank in exactly d additional decompositions. All extra work, apart from those decompositions, is polynomial in D,C,b and the explicit input size. For k=0 the same conclusion of full rank holds. The procedure does not enumerate the points in any flat, which can contain r^k points.

Proof. When the subsystem is enlarged by verified zero-value rows, U grows to U'. The quotient H'=V/U' is an ordinary linear image of H. The image of S is covered by the images of the same C flats, all still of dimension at most k. Compute explicit images by choosing lifts of the initial origins and spanning directions in V once, then reducing these lifts modulo U'. If h'=dim H'>k and h'>=2, all flats are proper in H'. For each flat find a nonzero functional f_j annihilating its direction space by Gaussian elimination, and set beta_j=f_j(z_j). Its flat is contained in the affine hyperplane f_j(z)=beta_j.

Avoid these hyperplanes without enumerating the field. Order the h' coordinates and attach each constraint to the last coordinate at which f_j is nonzero. Assign coordinates from first to last. At coordinate i, each attached constraint forbids exactly one residue, computable from earlier assignments by division by its nonzero coefficient. There are fewer than r forbidden residues in total, so choose the least nonnegative residue not forbidden. One may just sort the forbidden values or test the first #forbidden+1 integers. No previously satisfied constraint involves a later coordinate. The resulting z is outside every hyperplane and therefore outside the support. Apply the rank-increase operation of section 1. Its new row increases rank by one, and the cover can be projected again. Stop when the system is full rank or h'<=k. Since the calibrated residual dimension is h'-1, the bound follows. QED.

This proof is elementary hyperplane avoidance, rather than a general solution to the circuit range-avoidance problem. The classical covering-radius analogy concerns syndromes outside spans of a few columns, but our certificate concerns a specifically structured sumset of fixed incidences. It is not a claimed improvement to generic covering-radius algorithms.

### 2.1 Constructing and checking a flat cover using quotient cosets

A useful special case gives a common direction subspace L of dimension k in the initial H. Project every block's explicit incidence column set (including reference zero) into H/L. Compute the possible tuple sums of these projected labels by a capped exact Minkowski-sum dynamic program, keeping one representative in H per coset. If at most C<=C0<r distinct cosets occur, the initial S is covered by the C corresponding affine translates of L. The intermediate supports are nested because every occurrence includes zero, so stopping at C0+1 distinct cosets is sound. Every projected addition is explicit polynomial-size field arithmetic. There is no need to list the contents of any coset.

For k=1 one can try the line spanned by the calibration row image, and all nonzero differences between explicitly projected incidence columns as candidate directions. There are polynomially many candidates. A supplied direction can be checked independently, or found by this search if it occurs among the candidates. The calibration direction recovers the special homogeneous joint-support criterion of PR #14: quotienting H by that line identifies H/line with V/W. Other choices of L can succeed even if that homogeneous support already has at least r elements. The cover persists after every rank increase, so **no fresh promise on each successive residual basis is necessary**. This is stronger than repeatedly testing individual scalar projections and hoping they remain small.

If affine covers are instead supplied separately for each block, their Minkowski sums are affine flats of dimension at most the sum of their direction dimensions. Enumerating the combinations is justified only when the product of cover sizes fits the cap. A single joint cover is more useful because it remains valid under all later quotients.

### 2.2 Charged bit-time branch

With D,C0<=2^{O(b/m)} and m=Theta((log b)^(1/4)), use the original doubled-tuple coverage and the LZR ordinary count/decomposition interface. Given the explicit subsystem and enumerated labels within budget, the capped searches, projections, field elimination, verification and at most D extra decompositions cost at most 2^{O(b/m+mb/sqrt(log b))} in bit time and space. This is the conditional quarter-power scale. If using short tuples instead, require a proved universal short decomposition routine, e.g. the globally pointwise seed of PR #13 with its own exponent below 1/6. Merely controlling prescribed fibers is not a proof of this coverage. The direction or cover condition is an additional checkable hypothesis, not a theorem about every actual extractor output.

## 3. Cyclic interval and box covers: large support without missing scalar projections

For a start residue a and integer width 0<=ell<=r-1 define the circular arc I(a,ell)={a,a+1,...,a+ell} modulo r. In particular width zero is a singleton and width r-1 is the whole field. The exact Minkowski sum of two such arcs is I(a+b,min(r-1,ell+ell')). A box in F_r^k is a Cartesian product of k arcs, and box addition is coordinatewise. These formulas work across wrap, including whole-field intervals.

Let T:H->F_r^k be any surjective explicit linear projection with fixed k<=h. Choose its coordinates as k independent functionals in U-perp. Suppose each projected block-column set is contained in a union of J_i explicitly represented cyclic boxes. If block i occurs w_i times per tuple, then the projected tuple support is contained in the union of the Minkowski-sum boxes, at most C=prod_i J_i^{w_i} boxes. When C<=C0, all these boxes can be generated directly at polynomial cost in C0,D,b for fixed k. It is not necessary to enumerate the often much larger number of points in them.

**Exact complement procedure.** Split each wrapped arc into at most two ordinary integer intervals, hence each box into at most 2^k nonwrapped boxes. Collect all start and end+1 coordinates in the first dimension, together with 0,r. On each integer slab between consecutive endpoints, membership in every box's first-coordinate interval is constant. Recurse on the remaining coordinates using the boxes that contain that slab. The one-dimensional base merges sorted intervals and finds a gap. The recursion has at most (2^k C+1)^{O(k)} elementary operations on O(b)-bit endpoints. If a gap exists it returns an explicit integer point in the complement; if the cover is full it honestly reports failure. This is exact even when C>r or the covered cardinality is close to r^k, but the claimed complexity requires C fit the intended cap and k be fixed.

If a missing projected vector z is found, solve T(x+U)=z by field elimination and apply the rank-increase operation of section 1. This works even when EVERY nonzero scalar projection of S is surjective. It also works when all nonzero augmented score lifts have large circular widths. A box-cover certificate guarantees one rank increase, or full completion at corank one. Unlike affine flats, arbitrary boxes need not stay boxes under the quotient change; for larger corank, a subsequent successful box test is required. Do not infer the persistent-cover conclusion for boxes.

### 3.1 Constructing compact scalar covers from the explicit labels

For a chosen modular dilation and an integer width H0, sort the canonical residues in each block. Starting at the least uncovered residue, take the forward interval of width H0 (truncate at r-1), and continue. The greedy cover is optimal for these forward intervals on the line. If the points admit a cover by J circular arcs of width H0, splitting wrap and greedy covering gives at most 2J such intervals. One may try all widths that are powers of two, and a polynomial list of dilations (e.g. inverses of nonzero explicit label differences). These are checkable candidates, not a guarantee of a useful dilation. The same construction works with arbitrary explicit covers and coordinate transforms supplied by another argument. A cap on the number of intervals controls the work even when each interval is very long. The scalar overcover can omit a residue even though the original support exceeds the cap for explicit support enumeration by many orders of magnitude.

### 3.2 Finding a two-dimensional line-box cover

In a two-dimensional projection, try coordinate changes that take two independent differences of projected labels to the coordinate directions. For explicit columns this costs at most polynomially many trials (all ordered pairs of nonzero differences). Within each block look for a cover by at most J horizontal/vertical lines. For fixed J, branch on an uncovered point: one of its horizontal or vertical lines must be selected. This has depth at most J and at most 2^J leaves. Replace each selected line by the shortest cyclic arc through the points it covers in the varying coordinate. Taking all points on the selected line is safe, although it may enlarge the cover; if desired assign intersection points to just one of their lines and try the at most 2^{O(J^2)} possible assignments. A shortest cyclic arc is computed by sorting distinct residues and removing the largest circular gap. Try the combinations of these small covers across blocks, explicitly charging the product of counts. With J constant and m polylogarithmic, 2^{O(m)} combinations and boxes are affordable. Even a greedy line-cover attempt is safe if the resulting actual boxes are checked to contain all labels and the returned point is outside their sum. The theoretical theorem uses the cap on the exhaustive candidate search; the verifier uses a restricted fast path on some larger controls and records its failure.

With the original ordinary decomposition and cost assumptions, a successful fixed-dimensional cover test with all construction and C0<=2^{O(b/m)} fits the same conditional quarter-power bit budget in the doubled-coverage branch. The short-coverage branch has the resource caveat of section 2.2. Neither an affine-flat nor a box cover is proved to exist cheaply for every actual residual system.

## 4. A deterministic flat digit family defeats every scalar test but has a compact joint cover

This structural example is stronger than a finite accidental rescue. It requires no random augmentation or asymptotic probabilistic statement. It uses known scalar coordinates to describe mathematical block sets, so it is not the prescribed parity-affine membership seed and has no exhibited cheap ordinary membership/count circuit. Its purpose is to separate the sufficient conditions and test the geometric proof. It is not an efficient ECDLP algorithm for these blocks.

Let r tend through odd primes, b=ceil(log2 r), m=Theta((log b)^gamma) with fixed gamma>0. Set K=2^floor(b/(64m)), delta=K^(-1/16), and choose the integer radix A=ceil((rK/2^m)^(1/m)). Put M=A^m and W0=(2A)^m. Then M>r eventually, A<r, K/A tends to zero exponentially in b/m, and W0/r=K(1+O(m/A)). Choose a nonzero zeta avoiding all residues j A^i with |j|<=A-1 and 0<=i<m. There are O(mA)=o(r) forbidden residues, so the least allowed zeta is O(mA). For each i, include exactly 2A distinct points with coordinates (c,t)

    d_(i,j)=(0,j A^i),
    e_(i,j)=(j A^i,j A^i+zeta),    0<=j<A,

in F_r^2. The actual group point is R+[t]P, for one common reference R chosen so none is the identity. This is possible because the union of the t-values has at most 2mA<r elements; choose R avoiding their negatives. Within a block the t-values are distinct: each digit progression has distinct entries, and cross-type collisions would violate the choice of zeta. Thus these are ordinary unweighted blocks, not multiset weights. The reference is d_(i,0). The homogeneous auxiliary label u is the c-coordinate; the true difference table lambda is the t-coordinate.

### 4.1 Exact pointwise coverage and no-wrap

For every one of the 2^m assignments of digit types, the j-digits independently range over [0,A-1]. Their integer positional sum is a bijection onto [0,M-1] before reduction. If k e-type digits are used, add k*zeta to the t-sum. Hence each fixed type assignment contributes either floor(M/r) or ceil(M/r) representations at every group value, shifted by the common reference total and the type offset. Consequently the full short-tuple count obeys

    |d(g)-W0/r| <= 2^m  for every g,
    |d(g)-K| <= K delta  eventually.

The second assertion follows from 2^m/K=o(delta) and m/A=o(delta). Full group coverage follows already from the all-d type. The positive one-side one-pin short-pair masses are at most max_g d(g) times W0/(2A), which is <=r K^2 (1+delta)^2/(2A)<r eventually. All the strong numerical support, flatness and integer no-wrap promises of PR #10 are thus met by this explicit digital construction. These are direct count identities; they do not rely on any inference from the finite tests.

### 4.2 Exact index-r basis using genuine sparse rows

Let J be the lattice in Z^D generated by the following verified zero-value differences of doubled tuples together with one calibration row d_(0,0)->d_(0,1) of right side one. Each zero-value contrast has equal (c,t) sum on the two sides, so all rows annihilate u modulo r. All comparisons below use at most two occurrences per block on each side, padding missing slots by the references. We use genuine zero-value contrasts and one unit-shift row; we do NOT claim that this is a basis consisting only of fixed-generator shift rows.

In Z^D/J, write the class of any reference as zero. The d-digit recurrence d_j+ref=d_(j-1)+d_1 expresses its class as j times the class of d_1. The carry d_(i,A-1)+d_(i,1) equals d_(i+1,1) with appropriate reference fillers, so all d-classes are multiples of the calibrated class, which is zero. The point e_(i,0) has equal (c,t) sum to the d-digit encoding of zeta (M>r), so its class is zero. The e-digit recurrence e_j+e_0=e_(j-1)+e_1 now expresses its class as j times the class of e_1. For cross-block e carries, compare the tuples using e_(i,A-1), e_(i,1), e_(i+1,0) with e_(i,0), e_(i,0), e_(i+1,1), padding the second slot in block i+1 by the reference. These have identical c-sums and t-sums (three e offsets on both sides). They give the carry of the class by A. Finally compare the base-A e-encoding of r with all e-zero digits, using one reference filler per block; this gives r times the class of e_(0,1) equal to zero. All equations are genuine: the comparison uses the actual group points and does not assert equality of different group values.

Every class is generated by e_(0,1), of order dividing r. The homomorphism z->z dot u modulo r kills every displayed row and maps e_(0,1) to one. The quotient is therefore cyclic of order exactly r. Indeed there are exactly D=m(2A-1) listed rows (one calibration; m e-zero comparisons; 2m(A-2) within-block recurrences; 2(m-1) carries; one terminal r relation). They form a square integer basis of determinant +/-r and modular rank D-1, with homogeneous kernel span(u). The zero-value subspace of its modular row span is the intersection of ker(u) and ker(lambda). Both are independent: d_(0,1) has c=0,t=1 and e_(0,1) has c=1. The calibrated quotient H consequently has dimension two, and the pair (c,t-c) is a valid coordinate system on it.

### 4.3 All scalar criteria fail, but the joint cover is small

In coordinates (x,y)=(c,t-c), the projected block lies on the lines x=0 (d-type with y=j A^i) and y=zeta (e-type with x=j A^i). Their sumset in H over short tuples contains both the entire line x=0 (all d types) and the entire line y=m*zeta (all e types), since M>r. Every nonzero scalar linear functional on H is nonzero on at least one of their directions, so its scalar support is **all F_r**. Thus every nonzero augmented solution h_0 lambda+k_0 u has full short-tuple score support; its integer block-range sum is at least r-1, ruling out the short-lift criterion 0<2R<r. A zero modular solution with a nonzero reference-zero integer lift has width at least r. The scalar missing-support test and the homogeneous joint-support cap <r from PR #14 fail even before doubling the tuples. In each block the largest homogeneous quotient class is c=0, of size A+1 out of 2A, so the product across all m tends to zero. No proper subset of blocks can have the dense untrimmed law needed in PR #11's omitted-block branch: it has at most (2A)^(m-1)=W0/(2A)=o(r) tuples. Its support is at most that size, and Cauchy-Schwarz gives normalized second moment at least r/support size. Thus both concentration criteria fail asymptotically, despite the exact flatness of the full law and the genuine square rational basis.

**Yet the projected short support has a cover by at most 2^m boxes omitting almost the entire plane.** Use the two line boxes per block. The varying coordinate's arc can have width min(r-1,(A-1)A^i); if including the extra intersection point on the d-line, enlarge the y-arc by at most zeta=O(mA). The c-line has no d-point with y=zeta by the forbidden-difference choice. For a fixed assignment of lines, the largest block i=m-1 varies along just one axis. On the other axis the total varying width from the remaining blocks is at most A^(m-1)+m*zeta=O(A^(m-1)) (eventually m>=3). This remains true after circular addition, with fixed offsets translated. Each box therefore has cardinality at most r*O(A^(m-1)+1). The total covered cardinality is bounded by

    2^m r O(A^(m-1)+1) = O(r^2 K/A) = o(r^2).

The explicit line-cover search in section 3.2 finds the correct axes: the differences between the j=0 and j=1 points of each type in block zero give two independent coordinate directions, so trying projected-label differences includes this coordinate change. A cover of two lines exists; for sufficiently large A any two-line cover must use the two occupied long lines, and replacing them by shortest arcs only improves the width bound just used. Thus the construction can be recognized from the explicit quotient labels and equations without a hidden c/t table in the selection step. A missing projected vector is found at polynomial cost in D,2^m,b. One ordinary short decomposition would then recover full modular rank under an affordable decomposition interface. The actual digital sets are supplied by their scalar definitions and do not have a proved cheap ordinary membership/decomposition circuit at the quarter-power scale. In particular this is not a claim that those sets solve the general DLP. Doubling this family fills the two-dimensional joint support (one d and one e digit per block); the box criterion explicitly uses short coverage. It must not be applied to doubled tuples without rechecking the cover.

## 5. Verification and limitations

`python -B check_box_cover.py /path/to/PR12/research/dual_lift_obstruction/check_dual_lift.py` uses NumPy and SymPy. Without the PR12 path it runs the self-contained synthetic geometry, scalar, persistent-cover and digital fixtures; the argument adds six exact PR12-derived tests. The verifier computes quotient bases from the supplied modular rows, not the hidden label scores, and verifies every extra equation by abstract cyclic-group arithmetic and exact modular elimination. Its toy group decomposition is direct enumeration, not an implementation of the asymptotic contractor. All linear algebra and geometry tests are exact. The auxiliary-prime rank certificates prove rational rank, and the small integer lattices also have their Smith forms checked. The digital tests independently compare the formula of section 4.1 against exact convolution and compute one-pin masses. For the largest digital fixture, verifying two complete axis lines proves all scalar projections surjective; the smaller tests exhaust every projective scalar direction. The square-basis digital construction is used with its actual zero-value and unit-shift right sides. The PR12-derived cases use their genuine fixed-shift rows and include the independently checked Smith forms from that verifier.

The persistent cover checks begin with supplied consistent explicit systems at coranks 2,3,4 whose initial homogeneous projected support has at least r elements, and discover a common line from actual projected column differences. Six, ten and fifteen cosets suffice, respectively; each finishes with exactly the required number of verified additional rows. These fixtures are not claimed to be sparse fixed-shift bases or actual mask extractor outputs. The compact-cover DP and hyperplane avoidance do not enumerate the points of the initial or later affine lines. The two scalar interval examples have actual support cardinality at least 1531 while the explicit support cap is 500; the compressed complement uses only 28 interval sums. The finite interval examples do not claim that every earlier short-lift criterion fails; section 4 supplies the stronger asymptotic separation for the joint test.

The two-dimensional box test succeeds on all four digital fixtures and five of six PR12-derived controls. The order-257 augmented PR12 control fails the capped fast-path test and is retained; no conclusion about the existence of another cover follows. The digital order-4093 fixture has exact modular rank 253/254 initially, 16,249 distinct joint short-tuple projections, all 4,094 scalar directions surjective, and four cover boxes. One checked row yields full rank. Its short fibers have 4..6 representations and every short one-pin mass is 512..521, below r. The order-1009 augmented PR12 test likewise recovers full rank from 144 boxes, despite all its scalar directions being surjective. The completed scripts use bounded greedy line covers for augmented controls and a short explicit direction-candidate list; the proof states the cost of the broader exhaustive candidate search. An initial more exhaustive toy run exceeded its time budget; the bounded fast path is the actual reproduced run. The finite parameters are illustrative and not a test of the asymptotic relative-flatness threshold.

**Remaining load-bearing obligation.** Obtain an affordable universal projected-range avoidance or discrepancy-selection theorem for the actual masks and the actual extractor's residual quotient. The persistent affine-flat and compact box cover tests are new sufficient branches, not proof that any one branch always works. In particular there is no universal relation between a large-dimensional residual quotient and a small affine-flat cover. The additive map here resembles the circuit range-avoidance problem; a generic covering-radius volume bound or low-depth circuit result does not automatically produce a charged missing-vector finder in this model. The initial circuit-range and covering-radius literature scan did not establish priority for these exact combined statements, nor does it supply a complete algorithm.
