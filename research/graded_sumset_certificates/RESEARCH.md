# Graded sumset interpolation, translation-span barriers, and hidden Hankel certificates

PR #28's structured rank-ball and interface-profile methods concern different classes; its description was checked. All results here are finite-field statements. ## 1. Graded interpolation under addition

Let G=F_p^d for a prime p. Use reduced polynomial functions (each exponent <p). Let c(A) be the largest t in [0,d(p-1)] for which evaluation on a nonempty A is injective on reduced polynomials of total degree <=t.

Use R=F_p[z_1,...,z_d]/(z_i^p). For a in G, with canonical integer digits a_i, set g_a=prod_i (1+z_i)^a_i. Its coefficient at z^alpha is b_alpha(a)=prod_i binom(a_i,alpha_i). These binomial polynomials are a reduced basis, triangular by degree with invertible diagonal. Because (1+z_i)^p=1, g_a g_b=g_(a+b). Define W(A)=span{g_a:a in A}. Bilinearity gives W(A+B)=span{uv:u in W(A),v in W(B)} exactly.

Order monomials by increasing total degree, then lexicographically. Let L(A) be the set of possible least nonzero exponent vectors of elements of W(A). Gaussian elimination computes L(A), the pivot profile in this order. If alpha in L(A), beta in L(B), and alpha+beta has every coordinate <p, choose elements with those least monomials. Their product has least monomial alpha+beta: the monomial order is compatible with addition, the leading coefficient is nonzero, and truncation deletes monomials instead of rewriting them. Consequently

    L(A+B) contains (L(A)+L(B)) intersect [0,p-1]^d.

The dimension of evaluation through degree t is the number of L(A) entries with degree <=t, since the binomial basis preserves the degree filtration. Thus all degree <=c(A) exponents occur. Every alpha with |alpha| <=sum_i c(A_i) decomposes into nonnegative exponent vectors alpha^(i) of degrees <=c(A_i), by allocating its units sequentially. Every partial sum is coordinatewise <=alpha. Iterating the inclusion proves

    c(A_1+...+A_m) >= min(d(p-1),sum_i c(A_i)).

This includes degrees above the characteristic. Full affine span gives c(A)>=1. The full pivot-profile inclusion can be strictly stronger than this scalar bound. It is a lower bound on evaluation rank, not an existence theorem for a small separating polynomial.

## 2. Exact product-space algorithm with no partial-sum enumeration

Take any nonempty coordinatewise downward-closed index set E inside [0,p-1]^d, D=|E|. The complement monomials form an ideal in R, so projection onto E is an algebra quotient. Compute a basis of each projected W(A_i) by evaluating b_alpha at the explicit inputs. Starting with the identity, successively multiply the current basis and the next basis, taking a linear basis of all products. This gives exactly the image of W(S), S=sum A_i. It is not a heuristic or a numerical approximation.

With N=sum |A_i|, straightforward streaming elimination and naive truncated multiplication take O(N D^2+m D^4) field operations, up to polynomial overhead for exponent indices and binomial evaluations. Indeed each product step generates <=D^2 products, each with <=D^2 coefficient pair contributions, followed by streaming row elimination with O(D^2) operations per vector. One can use O(D^2) field elements of working storage in addition to the explicit input and monomial metadata. Precompute the retained exponent-sum table; binomial evaluations can use a recurrence with invertible denominators because exponents<p. The maximum individual exponent in a lower set of size D is <=D-1. The bit cost, including that setup, is bounded by (N+m) D^{O(1)} poly(d,log p).

If the final span is deficient, a nonzero coefficient vector in its ordinary kernel gives a nonzero reduced polynomial P=sum c_alpha b_alpha vanishing on S. To find P(x)!=0 deterministically, fix each coordinate in turn. For a variable of degree a<p, at least one of the values 0,...,a leaves a nonzero polynomial in the remaining variables (univariate interpolation of a nonzero coefficient polynomial). Evaluate the coefficient vectors on those trial values and keep a nonzero one. The resulting x is absent from S, with a checkable polynomial certificate. If the span is full, report no separator in the selected space, not S=G unless E is the full reduced box.

For total degree t, D<=binom(d+t,t). In the abstract small-field parameters p=poly(m), d=Theta(b/log m), N=2^{O(b/m)}, take t=floor(d/m^theta), fixed 0<theta<=1, and growing polylogarithmic m. Then log D=O(t log(e(d+t)/t))=O(b/m^theta). At theta=1, this fits polynomial-in-N scale 2^{O(b/m)} under these parameter bounds, conditional on existence of a separator. This replaces the dependence N^{O(t)} of direct anchored-subset enumeration by polynomial dependence on N and D. It is a statement about the abstract polynomial test only, not a proof that every digit-lifted input has an affordable separator.

## 3. Sharp translation-span obstruction

For a nonzero reduced polynomial function P define tau(P)=dim span{P(.+a):a in G}. Any translation-invariant space containing P has dimension >=tau(P). This quantity is unchanged under invertible affine coordinate maps. If x^alpha is the greatest monomial of P in graded lex order, then

    tau(P) >= prod_i (alpha_i+1).

Proof: for every beta<=alpha apply prod_i Delta_(e_i)^{beta_i} to P, with Delta_v P(x)=P(x+v)-P(x). These functions belong to the translation span. The resulting greatest monomial is x^(alpha-beta) with nonzero coefficient, by the falling factorials for alpha_i<p; smaller monomials are either killed or remain smaller under the multiplicative order. Shifts and differences do not increase any individual degree. Distinct beta give distinct greatest monomials and hence independent functions.

Let C be any L-element proper nonempty subset of F_p and let 0<=h<d. Write T={x:# of i with x_i outside C <=h}. Then among all nonzero reduced polynomials vanishing on T, the minimum total degree is L(h+1), and the minimum translation dimension is (L+1)^(h+1).

Proof: order field elements with C first, and take the univariate Newton basis N_j(x)=prod_(l<j)(x-a_l), 0<=j<p. Expand in its tensor basis. For every J of size <=h, the grid with unrestricted coordinates in J and coordinates in C outside J belongs to T. Its evaluation basis forces all coefficients with alpha_i<L outside J to be zero. Thus every surviving Newton index has at least h+1 coordinates >=L. The greatest ordinary monomial has the same exponent as the greatest surviving Newton index. Apply the preceding lemma. Both lower bounds are attained by prod_(i=1)^(h+1) prod_(c in C)(x_i-c); its translates stay in the box of degree <=L in those h+1 coordinates. For C containing zero, T=B(h)+C^d, with B(h) the Hamming ball of radius h. Unrestricted extra coordinates do not weaken the same argument.

### 3.1 Original-plane examples with nearly maximal growth

For m tending to infinity with m log m=o(log r), r prime, put A=ceil(r^(1/m)), n=m^3 A, g=ceil(r^(1/(2m))). Construct mn distinct points forming a B_m-set by the packed power sums of #27. Choose a prime q>=mn, q>m, with q<=2mn+O(1); for t=0,...,mn-1 pack the m digits t^j mod q, j=1,...,m, in two words of base B=m(q-1)+1, max word length ceil(m/2). The guard B^ceil(m/2)<=r holds eventually since its logarithm is (1/2+o(1))log r. No carries or wrap occur in any m-term sums; Newton identities over F_q recover the multiset. Padding gives uniqueness for all smaller orders. Partition into m disjoint n-element cores.

For each i=0,...,m-1 augment core i by the square digit set {(a g^i,b g^i):0<=a,b<g} and both axis digit sets {(a A^i,0),(0,a A^i):0<=a<A}, interpreted in F_r^2. Pad to n+g^2+2A distinct points. Each retained subset of k factors has at least n^k different sums from the cores. The fractional loss from the cardinality product upper bound is O(m^-2), uniformly in k<=m. Individual doubling is at least binom(n+1,2), within 1-O(m^-3) of maximal. The full cardinality product is r^(1+o(1))<r^2. The axis digit sets cover both complete axes, so every nonzero scalar projection is full. The square digit sets cover [0,L-1]^2, L=g^m>=sqrt(r), L<r eventually. By the thick-grid theorem with h=0, every nonzero separating polynomial has translation dimension >=L+1>=sqrt(r). The construction is deterministic with polynomial cost in explicit list size and log r. No Fourier-bias bound is claimed for these packed cores.

### 3.2 Carry-expanded examples: exponential translation dimension

In #25's digit model, let b=log_2 r, m grow polylogarithmically in b, p prime between (2m+1)^c and 2(2m+1)^c for fixed c>8, d=2 floor(log_p r), t0=floor(d/(8m)), h=m t0. If each lifted input contains the Hamming ball B(t0), their sum contains B(h). The independent carry correction contains the box [-m,m] in all but the two least-significant coordinates, zero there. Wrap corrections include zero. Consequently the enlarged support contains a product of two unrestricted coordinates with the thick ball T_{h-2,C} in d-2 dimensions, C=[-m,m] in F_p, L=2m+1. Its separators have tau >=(2m+2)^(h-1)=2^{Omega_c(b)}. A nonzero polynomial in the full space vanishing on this product has greatest Newton index satisfying the same restriction in the d-2 coordinates, so free coordinates do not invalidate the bound.

The input ball fits the size budget: |B(t0)|<= (t0+1)(e d(p-1)/t0)^t0 < r^(1/m)/poly(m) eventually, since log p=c log(2m+1)+O(1) and d=2b/log_2 p+O(1). Use digit decoding to produce plane input points, and pad if needed to size about r^(1/m). The original product remains r^(1+o(1)), the enlarged product at most r^(1+2/c+o(1)), and p^d=r^(2-o(1)). This is a lower bound for the carry-box overapproximation, NOT the true plane sumset, and not a claim about actual curve-factor-base instances. It rules out small translation-invariant polynomial spaces for this test even with arbitrary coordinate changes; it is not a lower bound on all avoidance algorithms.

## 4. A constructive structural escape: Hankel rank

Let K be a given finite field, m nonzero in K, and |K|>=2k+3. For v in K^(2k+1), H(v) is the (k+1)-square Hankel matrix with entries v_(i+j), indices starting at zero. Suppose an invertible linear map T takes affine lifts (1,a), a in list A_i, to vectors of Hankel rank <=u_i with sum u_i<=k. Then every sum z has H(T(m,z)) of rank <=k, by linearity and subadditivity. Verify all local rank bounds directly, without a sumset enumeration.

To construct an absent point, e_k has invertible Hankel matrix (reverse identity). Put ell=(T^-1)_0, the first row. If ell(e_k)=0, choose j with ell_j!=0. The determinant of H(e_k+a e_j) is a polynomial in a of degree <=k+1 with nonzero constant. Among any k+2 distinct nonzero values one has nonzero determinant and ell(e_k+a e_j)!=0. Call the chosen vector v. Scale it by m/ell(v) and apply T^-1 to obtain (m,z) with full Hankel rank. This z is certified absent. The field size guard allows these trials. Work is polynomial in the explicit lists, k and the cost of K arithmetic. In a separate uncolored vector-space problem (no affine slice), any dictionary whose mapped columns have rank <=u cannot span T^-1 e_k using w arbitrary-coefficient columns if wu<=k. This follows directly from rank subadditivity; no universal dictionary classification is claimed.

### 4.1 Recovering a hidden rational normal curve map

Suppose 2k+3 distinct projective points from a common disguised rational normal curve are available as a frame. Write D=2k. Let V be the invertible matrix of the first D+1 lifted columns, a=V^-1 v_(D+1), b=V^-1 v_(D+2), numbering columns from zero. Require all a_i,b_i nonzero and t_i=b_i/a_i distinct. Set

    P(t)=(1,t,...,t^D),
    lambda_i=prod_(j!=i)(-t_j)/(t_i-t_j),
    T=[ P(t_i) lambda_i/a_i ]_i V^-1.

Then T is invertible by the Vandermonde determinant. It maps the initial points into the moment curve, maps v_(D+1) to P(0), and maps the last to a nonzero multiple of P(infinity)=e_D. The first identity is Lagrange interpolation at zero. For the last, t_i lambda_i is a common nonzero constant times the leading coefficient of the i-th Lagrange polynomial, so the corresponding sum is a multiple of e_D. Under the common-curve promise, a fractional linear parameter change sends the last two curve parameters to 0 and infinity; in those coordinates their frame-coordinate ratios are proportional to the finite parameters t_i by the same interpolation calculation, and a common parameter scaling removes the constant. Agreement on a projective frame determines the map up to an overall scalar. Thus this explicit T recovers the curve model on all points of the common curve, including those not used in the frame.

A frame can be taken from one promised rank-one list with enough distinct points. Without a promise, try the specified frame and check invertibility and EVERY local Hankel rank; return failure if the rank budget fails. Successful certificates are valid regardless of whether the input actually came from a curve. This does not solve arbitrary hidden extension-field bases, unknown secant varieties, or arbitrary dictionaries. It is different from the rank-ball overlap method in PR #28. Classical rational normal curve geometry and Hankel secant identities are not new; the formula above is an elementary specialization.

## 5. Explicit high-growth, small-bias examples in digit space

There is a second construction, useful for testing which hypotheses explain a successful certificate. Let K=F_{p^s}, Q=p^s, p>2m, n=(Q-1)/(p-1). Choose distinct nonzero c_i in F_p, and list the moment vectors (t,t^2,...,t^J) for t in the norm coset C_i={t:t^n=c_i}. Here m<=J<=2m. Every coset has n parameters. Disjoint cosets and Newton identities give maximal retained-subset support and maximal within-list unordered doubling. Expanding in a fixed F_p basis preserves these assertions even if the last extension coordinate is truncated, provided the first m complete coordinates remain.

Each nontrivial base-field additive character pulls back to a nontrivial trace character of a nonconstant polynomial f(t) of degree <=J. Write the coset as a*(K^*)^(p-1). The complete additive Weil bound for f(a y^(p-1)), whose degree is <=J(p-1) and not divisible by p, yields absolute normalized bias <=J sqrt(Q)/n after removing zero and dividing by p-1. This is an analytic estimate, not an experimental assumption. In the large-Q parameter range it tends to zero and eventually is <1/(p-1) for p polynomial in m. In particular each unaugmented factor affinely spans the base space. For J=2m, the usual Hankel determinant gives a separator of degree m+1 over K; at least one base-field component is nonzero as a function (construct an absent point with full Hankel rank). Section 1 excludes all reduced separators through degree m. Thus the exact minimum base-field degree is m+1 in this regime, yet the structural rank method avoids generic evaluation-space enumeration in the known K-coordinate model, or under the hidden K-linear curve promise.

For a contrasting high-growth carry-box obstruction, choose the parameters of section 3.2, A=ceil(r^(1/m)), s minimal with p^s>=p m^3 A+1. Then n>=m^3 A, ms<=d, and J=ceil(d/s) lies in [m,2m] eventually. Keep the first d base coordinates of the moment vectors, including the first m complete extension coordinates. Decode them to plane points. Add the decoded B(t0) and the original plane's axis digit sets with radix A. Each list has n+O(A+|B(t0)|) points, with product r^(1+o(1)); lifted list size lies between n and that bound even if some newly added points collide in the digit lift. All retained-subset sums and within-list doubles in the UNCORRECTED DIGIT SPACE are almost maximal, and per-list base additive bias is <=J sqrt(Q)/n+O(m^-3)=o(1). Each lifted list contains B(t0), whose projections onto any subspace of coordinates specified by a linear map of rank <=t0 are surjective (choose independent columns). Original plane scalar projections are full by the added axes. The carry-expanded support nevertheless obeys the exponential translation lower bound in 3.2. This does not prove high growth or small bias in the original plane for the norm core. Nor is an efficient Hankel selector asserted for this augmented family. Construction can be deterministic with polynomial cost in explicit list size: enumerate monic extension polynomials of degree s with a standard irreducibility test, and enumerate norm cosets, decoded balls, and axes. Q is polynomially related to n in the stated regime. The independent carry box remains an overapproximation.
