# Simultaneous failure of the concentration and short-lift criteria

27 November 2026. Follow-up to PRs #9, #10, and #11 of elliptic-relation-counting. Main baseline `04a8b3d8bb904af8a30aa996f1af4027b52f6dd4`; PR #10 head `febe120cc3e69e248a1dd3472f87fa51c9ab8dd2`; PR #11 head `e833763d99b9e17e3c3544e0e01ad409bc111ed6`. All structural claims concern abstract prime-order groups and explicit unweighted block families. No new ECDLP algorithm or complexity improvement is claimed.

## Theorem

Let r tend to infinity through odd primes, b=ceil(log2 r), and m=Theta((log b)^gamma) with any fixed gamma>0. Put K=2^floor(b/(64m)), delta=K^(-1/16), n=(rK)^(1/m). There are ordinary unweighted indexed blocks F_i of nonidentity group points, each of size (1+o(1))n, such that:

1. Their one-copy representation counts d(g) satisfy |d(g)-K|<=delta K at every group value, and every one-side one-pin count for short-pair displacements lies strictly between 0 and r.
2. The complete doubled-tuple fixed-generator reduced incidence system has rank N=sum_i(|F_i|-1) modulo r. There exists a selection of N genuine generator-shift rows forming a rational basis B of modular rank N-1, each with true right side one.
3. For this B no nonzero integer labeling v (zero at the block references) with Bv=h 1 mod r satisfies 0<2R(v)<r, for any scalar h. Here R is the sum of integer block ranges from PR #11. More strongly, for any nonzero modular augmented solution, every block has shortest circular range at least (1-o(1))r.
4. Every block's largest quotient class in F_r^N/row(B mod r) has relative size o(1), uniformly over the blocks. Thus no nonempty subset of blocks satisfies the fixed positive joint retained fraction criterion of PR #11.

Unlike PR #10, this construction **does** admit a single doubled-pair certificate for every target: its dictionary covers the joint group and quotient coordinate. It refutes the disjunction of two sufficient criteria, not universal target identifiability. In particular it does not refute the actual prescribed seed, the canonical basis extraction, or a selection strategy that exploits joint coverage. The random augmentation is an existence proof, not an affordable membership circuit or deterministic seed construction.

## 1. Two-coordinate dictionary

Write scalar coordinates of group points relative to a generator P for the mathematical construction only. Let D=2^ceil(b/m), so D^m>r, D<r and mD=o(r). All points in block i will carry a pair (c,t) with group value [1+tau*t]P. Choose a nonzero zeta in F_r outside {j D^i: |j|<=D, 0<=i<m}. In block i include the pairs

    d_(i,j) = (0,j D^i),
    c_(i,j) = (j D^i,j D^i+zeta),       0<=j<=D.

The first d-pair is the reference P. The t-values within a block are distinct by choice of zeta and invertibility of D. Choose nonzero tau so that neither tau^(-1) nor -tau^(-1) is any of these t-values, and add the pair (0,tau^(-1)) of value 2P in every block. Each choice excludes O(mD) residues, so it exists eventually. Each dictionary has L=2(D+1)+1 points. All are distinct nonidentity points within a block. Shared group values across different indexed blocks are allowed. Augment each block by further distinct nonidentity points, assigning to each added point A an arbitrary c in F_r and t=(log_P A-1)/tau. This assignment does not alter membership or group sums.

The d-digits alone cover the group using one point per block. More strongly, given any pair (C,T) in F_r^2, choose base-D digits of the representative of C in [0,r) for the c-type occurrence in each block (including c_(i,0)). Their pair-sum is (C,C+m zeta). Use d-digits of T-C-m zeta mod r for the other occurrence. The resulting doubled tuple has pair-sum (C,T). Thus all joint coordinates occur.

## 2. Exact lattice of selected genuine rows

Use reduced incidence coordinates with the d_(i,0) references deleted; put u=(c_A) and lambda=(tau*t_A) in F_r^N. Define J as the integer lattice generated by every genuine shift-P reduced row a with a dot u=0. We prove

    J = {z in Z^N : z dot u=0 mod r}.

Inclusion from left to right is immediate. In Z^N/J write e_A for the coordinate classes (references zero). The P->2P change makes every special coordinate zero. If two doubled tuples X,X' have the same pair-sum (c,t) and X contains a reference, change that reference to 2P to get Y. Both Y-X and Y-X' are genuine shift-P rows in J. Their difference yields equality of the two incidence classes. All comparisons below use this device and at least one spare reference in the first tuple. The asymptotic range has m>=2.

* d-digit recursion d_j+reference=d_(j-1)+d_1 gives e_(d,i,j)=j e_(d,i,1); cross-block carries d_(i,D)=d_(i+1,1) give e_(d,i,1)=D^i e_d. Compare base-D encoding of r by d-digits with all references to get r e_d=0.
* Compare the special point 2P with the d-digit encoding of the representative of tau^(-1). Since its class is zero, tau^(-1)e_d=0. Together with r e_d=0, primality kills e_d. Compare each c_(i,0) with the d-digit encoding of zeta: its class is zero as well.
* c-digit recursion c_j+c_0=c_(j-1)+c_1 yields e_(c,i,j)=j e_(c,i,1), since c_0 has zero class. Cross-block carries give e_(c,i,1)=D^i e_c. Compare the c-digit encoding of r with the tuple of all c_(i,0) (using reference fillers on both sides). This gives r e_c=0.
* Compare any added point (c,t), padded by references, with the joint encoding from section 1. Its class is c e_c.

The quotient is generated by e_c of order dividing r. The map z->z dot u onto F_r sends e_c to 1, so the quotient has exactly order r. The asserted equality follows. In fact the proof uses only O(N) genuine rows with coefficients in [-2,2]. Their integer Smith invariants are 1,...,1,r, and their modular rank is N-1. Select N-1 modularly independent ones and extend rationally to a basis B from these same rows. Every selected RHS equals one. Full rank of the complete system modulo r follows from the full tuple coverage: a null direction gives a tuple score constant on each group fiber by comparing to a common successor, invariant under P-translation and hence constant. Varying a single slot kills each reduced coordinate.

For this B its row space W is u^perp. All augmented field solutions Bv=h 1 are exactly v=h lambda+k u with h,k in F_r. The scalar h includes zero, so the characterization covers homogeneous lifts too. For every target Q=[q]P two joint encodings of (0,0) and (0,q/tau) give a genuine doubled-pair difference in W (a fixed reference sum cancels). This is an existence certificate, not a uniform charged procedure for finding one from an implicit group point. It explains the precise limitation of this counterexample: the two criteria can both fail even when the single-pair route succeeds in principle.

## 3. Pointwise flatness of augmented ordinary blocks

The deterministic dictionary convolution of any subset of blocks has maximum multiplicity M=2^O(m), including if identity is adjoined to every dictionary. Indeed fix which slots use the special point, the identity, or either digit type. Their sum equation fixes C=sum j_i D^i modulo r, since digit-type offsets contribute zeta times the number of c-type slots. The digit sum lies in [0,2D^m], so there are at most 1+2D^m/r=2^O(m) candidate integer sums. Peeling base-D residues gives at most 2^m digit representations of each sum. There are at most 4^m type choices. Therefore M has the claimed bound uniformly, and a subset of s dictionary blocks has support between L^s/M and L^s. The empty convolution is the point mass at identity.

Retain each point outside the dictionary and identity independently with probability theta=(n-L)/(r-L-1). This is valid eventually: L/n=2^(-Omega(b/m^2)), n/r=o(1). Let q0=r theta; q0/n=1+o(1/m). The expected size of a block is n, and Chebyshev gives all sizes (1+o(1))n with probability 1-o(1).

We apply the mixed dictionary/Bernoulli moment argument in PR #10, section 4, to the new dictionary bound above. To make the transfer explicit, for a subset of k random slots and a deterministic convolution w of the other slots, let s be its support size and mu=q0^k s/r. Let Z(g) count its mixed representations. The weighted moment estimates for even H are

    E Z^H <= M^H Bell(H)^k max(1,mu)^H,
    E (Z-E Z)^H <= 2^H M^H Bell(H)^k mu^H J^(-H/4)

when q0,mu>=J>=1 for the second bound. The proof partitions H random tuples by equality in each slot. With ell classes and rank R of the H-by-ell class-incidence matrix, there are at most s^R r^(ell-R) assignments with nonzero deterministic weights; the retention weight is theta^ell. Thus the normalized factor is mu^R q0^(ell-kR) and ell<=kR. Centering eliminates isolated tuples. For each remaining pattern the absolute centered indicator moment is bounded by 2^H theta^ell. If d=H-R and C=kH-ell, the support of the transpose kernel, of size l, has at least max(d,l/2) mergers in each slot; outside it nonisolation supplies at least (H-l)/2 additional mergers. Hence C>=k max(d,l/2)+(H-l)/2, and either d>=H/4 or C-kd>=H/4. This proves the centered estimate after summing patterns. Deterministic weights only multiply the bounds by M^H. This is the same elementary argument as PR #10, not a new moment theorem.

Here uniformly 2^(-O(m)) K^(k/m)<=mu<=2^O(m) K^(k/m), by the support bound, L^m/r=2^O(m), and q0/n=1+o(1/m). Take H=8192m and threshold T=K delta/2^(m+2). For k>=m/2 use J=K^(1/3) in the centered estimate: the probability of a deviation above T is at most 2^O(m^2 log m) K^(-H/48)=2^(-8b/3+o(b)). For 0<k<m/2 use the raw estimate and E Z<=M mu=o(T): the probability is at most 2^O(m^2 log m) K^(-7H/16). The all-dictionary term is deterministic. Union over r targets and 2^m subsets gives all deviations simultaneously at most T with probability 1-o(1).

The mean block indicator is theta 1_G+h_i with h_i=(1-theta)1_dictionary_i-theta 1_{O}. Each mean has mass n. Expanding the mean convolution, every term containing 1_G is constant, so the mean count differs from K by

    (h_0*...*h_(m-1))(g) - (prod_i sum h_i)/r.

The extended-dictionary bound controls the first term by M, and (L+1)^m/r=2^O(m) controls the second. Their sum in absolute value is o(K delta). Summing the mixed deviations proves the simultaneous pointwise bound. For W0=prod_i |F_i|<=rK(1+delta), the one-side one-pin short-pair count is positive and at most K(1+delta) W0/|F_i| <= r K^2(1+delta)^2/L < r eventually. Thus the same strong numerical promises as PR #10 hold.

## 4. Simultaneously diffuse augmented lifts and quotient classes

Conditional on the retained point sets, label each added point with an independent uniform c in F_r. Let its t coordinate be the forced value above. With probability 1-o(1), each block has between n/2 and 2n added points. These labels do not affect the flatness event from section 3.

Fix any nonzero pair (h,k) in F_r^2. On the added points, the residues of v=h lambda+k u are h tau t+k c. For k!=0 these are independent uniform residues conditional on the point sets. For k=0 and h!=0, conditional on the number retained, their residues form a uniformly chosen subset of the complement of O and the dictionary in the translated and dilated ambient set. Put epsilon=n^(-1/3). For any fixed cyclic interval of at most floor((1-epsilon)r)+1 residues, the probability that all added labels in a block lie in it is at most (1-epsilon/2)^(n/2) eventually. In the k=0 case use the ratio of the interval size to r-L-1 and sampling without replacement; L/r=o(epsilon). This bound tends to zero exponentially in n^(2/3), and dominates a union bound over m blocks, fewer than r^2 nonzero pairs, and r possible starting positions. Thus simultaneously no such scalar projection fits in a circular interval of width (1-epsilon)r. Any integer lift has at least that width in every block, even allowing arbitrary circular cuts. For the zero pair, a nonzero integer lift with reference zero has some nonzero multiple of r and hence R>=r. This proves the short-lift obstruction for every possible augmented solution, not just the primitive rational lift.

Quotient classes in a block are exactly equality of c labels, since W=u^perp. The probability that any three distinct added points have the same c is at most O(m n^3/r^2)=o(1), by a union bound conditional on the block sizes. Hence any added-label class has at most two members with probability 1-o(1). In the dictionary the largest class is c=0, of size D+3; every other c-type digit is distinct within its block. The largest total class fraction is thus at most (D+5)/((1+o(1))n)=o(1) uniformly. The product on every nonempty block subset is at most the largest of these fractions. All probability events intersect with probability 1-o(1), establishing simultaneous existence. The numerical hypotheses of the PR #11 omitted-block branch therefore do not hold for these supplied bases, even if the remaining untrimmed subset could pass its energy guard.

It verifies actual selected unit-shift rows and the exact Smith form on C101, C257 and C1009; computes minimal circular lift width by exhausting all 1,094,328 nonzero augmented field coefficient pairs across these fixtures; checks joint tuple encodings and direct joint coverage at C101; and computes integer short fiber and pin counts. Every nonzero augmented solution has summed circular width at least 1421. The finite examples illustrate the lattice and width obstruction; they do not reproduce asymptotic pointwise relative concentration or vanishing modal fractions, which come from the existence proof.

This is a structural complement to PR #11: neither its short-lift criterion nor its concentration criterion, nor their disjunction, follows from the numerical seed guarantees and the existence of a full rational basis. It is not a counterexample to the actual masks or any specific extractor's output. Its dictionary also gives all-target individual certificates, so it provides no lower bound on a general selection algorithm. The next useful question is whether actual extractor outputs enjoy a third checkable property (e.g. constructive joint-coordinate coverage) or admit an affordable general discrepancy selector. No positive end-to-end deterministic saving exponent is established. No independent proof-chain or priority review has been completed for this note.
