# Support-complex recognition, sharp tangent thresholds, and homogeneous CRT certificates

27 November 2026. Abstract finite-geometry follow-up to [PR #40](https://github.com/davidiach/elliptic-relation-counting/pull/40), [PR #42](https://github.com/davidiach/elliptic-relation-counting/pull/42), and the algebraic recognition results of #30–31. Reviewed main `2329bc46971a249da363e1ec0897920fb688d048` and #42 at `26d7e7949b6b929c563826d74e12cb7519234882`; the descriptions of #39 and #43–44 were also inspected. This note addresses recognition of explicitly supplied finite-field configurations, not the project's actual arithmetic masks. The three results are complete support-complex reconstruction (allowing a global projection kernel), incomplete-sample tangent reconstruction under explicit interpolation and hyperplane-cover conditions, and a heterogeneous weighted-rank certificate based on homogeneous binary forms. All are structured results, not a universal factorization or avoidance algorithm. No new end-to-end discrete-logarithm complexity claim is made. Independent proof and priority review remain outstanding.

## 1. Complete support complexes: recover the facets, then the overlapping groups

Let q be prime, W=direct_sum_(i=1)^L V_i, with positive dimensions a_i that need not be equal. Let F be any downward-closed family of subsets of [L], containing every singleton. Its maximal members are called facets. Write W_J=direct_sum_(i in J) V_i and B=union_(J in F) W_J. Assume the maximum facet size is t>=1. We receive an explicitly enumerated set S=c+A(B), where c is unknown and A:W->F_q^d is linear, injective on the sum of any at most 4t blocks (or all blocks if fewer). A need not be globally injective. No list of facets, dimensions, center or coordinates is supplied.

**Theorem 1.** If q>t+1, the maximal affine subspaces contained in S are exactly c+A(W_F) for the facets F. All are recovered in polynomial time in |S|,d,log q. If the center is known, the linear variant requires only injectivity on at most 3t blocks and q>t. Both field-size thresholds are sharp as universal statements about maximal affine/linear subspaces, even before projection.

Proof. Every x in S has a unique inverse point f(x) in B, since any two possible inverse points differ in at most 2t blocks. On an affine subspace T contained in S choose x0 in T. The equations for affine addition, f(x+y-x0)=f(x)+f(y)-f(x0), and scalar multiplication about x0 follow by applying A and using injectivity on the at most 4t blocks in the four terms (at most 3t for scaling). Thus f(T) is an affine subspace of W. Let J be its active block indices, those whose projection is not identically zero. At each active block the zero set within this affine subspace is empty or a proper affine subspace, of relative size at most 1/q. If |J|>=t+1, choose t+1 active blocks. Since t+1<q, a union bound gives a point nonzero on all of them, contradicting membership in B. So |J|<=t; the same argument now supplies a point with support J, proving J belongs to F. Hence T is contained in c+A(W_J), and in a facet. The facet images are themselves incomparable: inclusion would imply original inclusion by local injectivity on 2t blocks. This proves the characterization.

When the center is supplied and T is a linear subspace, f is linear by the three-term addition identities. A union of at most q proper homogeneous subspaces cannot cover a positive-dimensional finite-field space: all contain zero, so for n<=q such hyperplanes the union has size at most 1+n(q^(dim-1)-1)<q^dim (dimension one is immediate). The same active-block proof works with t+1<=q. Thus the linear bound needs q>t only.

**Algorithm.** Starting from a point x of S, greedily extend its current affine span by an input point y whenever the entire new affine span is still contained in S. This test enumerates x+U+F_q(y-x) using the current explicit point set. A rejected extension cannot become valid as the current span grows; one pass through S suffices. Stop at a maximal affine subspace. Run this from each point not already covered by a recovered facet. Every facet contains a point with full original support equal to that facet; it belongs to no other facet, including after projection, by 2t-block injectivity. Hence each facet is found. With K=|S| there are at most K starts and K extension trials per start, and at most qK point tests per trial. The explicit dictionary contains at least q points unless it is a singleton, so this is polynomial in K,d,log q, even when q is not small as an abstract field parameter. Membership can be implemented with sorted point keys and binary searches. No search over possible support complexes is required. QED.

**Sharpness examples.** In the complete affine ball of weight <=q-1 in F_q^q, the affine line (z-a)_(a in F_q) has exactly q-1 nonzero coordinates at every z but uses all q coordinates. It lies in the ball and in no facet, showing the affine threshold cannot be weakened to q=t+1. In the linear ball of weight <=q in F_q^(q+1), the two-dimensional subspace consisting of ((x+a y)_(a in F_q),y) has at least one zero coordinate at each point, and uses all q+1 coordinates. It lies in no facet, showing failure at q=t. Neither example is a claim about every small-field support complex. The verifier checks both constructions at primes 2,3,5,7.

### 1.1 Canonical incidence layers, including indistinguishable blocks

Let U_F be the recovered facet directions and let Lcal be the nonzero intersections of one or more U_F. Their affine intersection is nonempty; choose any common point c0. Translating by c0 gives precisely the union of U_F, since c0-c belongs to every U_F. By injectivity on at most 2t original blocks,

    intersection_(F in I) U_F = A(W_(intersection_(F in I) F)).

For each X in Lcal put X^- = sum_{Y in Lcal, Y proper subset X} Y, and choose an arbitrary vector-space complement E_X of X^- in X. Discard zero complements. All of this uses just explicit subspace intersections, sums and linear algebra. There are at most K distinct intersections: each underlying distinct nonempty block subset has a full-support point in B, and 2t-block injectivity distinguishes these points. Iteratively intersecting already found spaces with facets enumerates the entire closure in polynomial work.

**Theorem 2.** In the original formal space, identify two blocks if they belong to exactly the same set of facets. The nonzero E_X are exactly one layer for each equivalence class, with dimension equal to the sum of dimensions of its blocks. Their lifts to W give a direct sum decomposition of W. For every facet, the layers whose spaces X are contained in U_F give a direct sum decomposition of that facet; decompositions agree on all intersections.

Proof. For a block i let J_i be the intersection of all facets containing it. It is a face, and i,j have identical membership signatures iff J_i=J_j: a facet contains i iff it contains J_i. For any intersection face J, each i in J has J_i subset J. The sum of the proper intersections within J contains exactly the blocks with J_i proper subset J. Inside W_J, injectivity transports the complements E_X uniquely back to W. Their projections onto the quotient W_J/W_(proper-closure blocks) are bases. Ordered by increasing |J|, the corresponding basis changes are triangular relative to the original equivalence classes, with invertible diagonal blocks. Thus they span W independently, and preserve each intersection W_J and each facet. Applying A proves the local direct-sum decompositions and their consistency. QED.

The equivalence-class merging is intrinsic to these incidence data. An arbitrary invertible map mixing only blocks of the same membership signature preserves all facet spaces; individual blocks in such a class cannot in general be distinguished from the set. Basis complements need not be unique either. The algorithm returns a valid layered decomposition, not a unique original coordinate system.

If a centered explicit dictionary from this model is to be used as a block lift, each centered atom is represented in the recovered layers of a containing facet, using at most t nonzero layers, and the formal lift lies inside its original facet. Under the additional guard that A is injective on the sum of any at most st original blocks, every dictionary relation of support <=s vanishes in that lift: its sum in W is supported on at most st original blocks, so is zero. This condition is distinct from the 4t recognition assumption; it must be proved separately before drawing any short-relation consequence. On arbitrary data one can still check all returned decompositions explicitly and that they agree on overlaps, but these checks alone do not prove the additional guard. Theorem 1 is complete-data recognition, not an algorithm for all arbitrary sets or incomplete samples.

## 2. Sparse unlabeled samples: exact tangent recovery

Let X be a union of distinct inclusion-maximal affine flats F_i of dimensions r_i in F_q^d. They need not arise from a direct sum or share a center for the main theorem. Suppose only a sample S subset X is supplied, without labels. For an integer 0<=D<q let M_i=binom(r_i+D,D) be the dimension of the space of polynomials of degree <=D on F_i. Call S complete through degree D on F_i if the evaluation matrix on S intersect F_i has rank M_i in any affine coordinate frame on that flat. This is a finite, exact condition, not an assumption that all its points were observed.

Let I_D be the vector space of polynomials in d variables of degree <=D vanishing on S, computed by linear algebra on the M=binom(d+D,D) monomials. Define the affine tangent candidate at x in S to be

    T_D(x)=x+{v : (dP)_x(v)=0 for all P in I_D},

with formal differentiation over the field.

**Theorem 3 (hyperplane-cover tangent criterion).** Assume completeness through degree D on each F_i. Suppose for each i the other flats can be covered by at most D-1 affine hyperplanes, none of which contains F_i. Then the inclusion-minimal affine spaces among {T_D(x):x in S} are exactly the original flats. This deterministic recovery is polynomial in |S|,M,d,log q. The hyperplane covers and sample labels are not required by the algorithm; they are hypotheses proving its completeness.

Proof. Every P in I_D restricts to the zero formal polynomial on every F_i, by completeness. Thus T_D(x) contains each F_i through x. Fix i and let G be the product of equations of the covering hyperplanes (empty product 1). Its restriction to F_i is a nonzero polynomial: the polynomial ring of the affine flat is an integral domain and no factor vanishes identically there. Since deg G<=D-1, completeness ensures there is x in S intersect F_i with G(x)!=0. This is an anchor outside all other flats; no separate sampling promise on anchors is necessary. For every affine linear form L vanishing on F_i, the polynomial LG belongs to I_D, and its differential at x is G(x)dL. These differentials span the entire conormal space of F_i, so T_D(x)=F_i. Every other tangent contains at least one of the recovered true flats. Since the true flats are incomparable, taking the inclusion-minimal candidates returns exactly them. The evaluation nullspace, derivatives and affine inclusion tests are ordinary finite-field calculations of size polynomial in |S| and M. No enumeration of the full flats or of their points is used. QED.

This uses the classical GPCA derivative principle (Vidal, Ma and Sastry, [Generalized Principal Component Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.4002), IEEE TPAMI 2005). The distinct claims here are the exact finite-field sample condition, the degree guard in terms of hyperplane covers rather than component count, the exact block-support tangent threshold below, and the bounded-redundancy application. We do not claim novelty for learning equations and differentiating them.

### 2.1 Sample complexity, including deterministic unisolvent samples

Completeness may be checked *after* candidate recovery: check that their union contains S, and compute degree-D evaluation rank in the affine coordinates of each returned flat using its incident sample points. Success proves equality of the learned truncated ideal with the formal vanishing space of the proposed union. Without the model promises, it does not prove that some other unsampled component never existed.

A deterministic example of a degree-D complete sample on any r-flat is the affine image of the integer simplex {u_i>=0, sum u_i<=D}. It has exactly binom(r+D,D) points. The polynomials product_i binom(x_i,e_i) for |e|<=D have invertible evaluation matrix on this grid: ordering by total degree gives triangularity and diagonal one, since q>D. Thus samples need not be random thinnings of a complete block ball.

For independent uniform samples on each flat, even with replacement, when the current evaluation rank is deficient choose a nonzero polynomial of degree <=D in its kernel. Schwartz–Zippel bounds the probability that the next point fails to increase rank by D/q. For q>=2D, the successive rank increases, counted as successes also after full rank, stochastically dominate independent Bernoulli trials of probability 1/2 (couple each conditional success probability to a uniform variable). With n_i>=4 M_i draws, the usual binomial lower-tail bound gives failure probability at most exp(-n_i/16). Consequently n_i>=max(4 M_i,16 ln(g/eta)) for each of g flats suffices for simultaneous completeness with probability at least 1-eta. More generally the same binomial tail applies with probability 1-D/q. The algorithm itself is deterministic given S; the probability statement concerns sampling. Duplicate points do not change ranks. This sample bound is linear in the local polynomial dimension, not the number of points of the complete flat.

### 2.2 Sharp degree for unprojected support complexes

Suppose now the formal direct sum is mapped injectively (restrict ambient coordinates to its affine span), and consider any support complex of maximum support size t as in section 1. At a point in a facet F whose block support equals F, define for each j outside F

    delta(F,j)=min{|J| : J subset F, J union {j} not in the support complex}.

The minimum exists by maximality. For the full formal vanishing space of degree <=D, the tangent directions are exactly the sum of V_i for i in F and V_j for outside j satisfying D<1+delta(F,j). In particular the exact degree threshold for recovering this facet by its tangent is max_{j outside F}(1+delta(F,j))<=t+1, when at least one outside block exists. If there are no outside blocks, the facet fills the chosen affine span. Every constant has zero differential, so the degree-zero tangent is already that whole span and the least degree is 0.

Proof. In direct-sum coordinates the ideal of the union of the formal coordinate subspaces consists precisely of linear combinations of monomials whose block support is not a face: restricting to each facet forces every coefficient of a monomial supported in that facet to vanish. (This is the ordinary Stanley–Reisner principle for coordinate subspace arrangements; see Björner, Peeva and Sidman, [Subspace arrangements defined by products of linear forms](https://people.kth.se/~bjorner/files/blockerideals.pdf).) A derivative along outside block j can be nonzero at the full-support point only from a monomial with exactly one factor in block j and all other factors in F. Its support includes a nonface of the form J union {j}, so its degree is at least 1+delta(F,j). Conversely take a minimizing J and linear forms on each block of J that are nonzero at the point. Multiply their product by any linear form on V_j. This polynomial vanishes on every facet and its differential there is a nonzero scalar times the chosen V_j functional. Hence the entire dual of V_j is detected at the threshold. Inside F every derivative vanishes in its directions, which proves the exact formula. Affine coordinate changes preserve degrees. When only finite-field samples are used we apply this with D<q and the completeness condition so that the learned space equals the formal space. QED.

For the usual weight-t ball with at least t+1 blocks, every delta(F,j)=t, so the degree t+1 is exactly necessary at every full-support anchor. For a support complex of pairs (a graph), an outside block is detected at degree two if it forms a forbidden pair with any member of the facet, and otherwise at degree three. This reveals more than the single bound t+1, and is independent of the number of facets. It is not a theorem that this degree is sufficient on every projected complex: linear projections may destroy these coordinate-block polynomials.

### 2.3 Global projection and arbitrary maximal-rank arrangements

A useful instance of Theorem 3 does not assume any direct-sum or flag-lifted geometry. Suppose the affine flats share a center and have linear direction spaces V_i of dimensions 0<r_i<d satisfying

    dim(sum_(i in J) V_i)=min(d,sum_(i in J) r_i)

for every subset J. This is the maximum possible rank profile. Write R=sum_i r_i and rho=max_i r_i. For each i, partition the other groups by a next-fit bin-packing procedure with capacity d-1, using their dimensions as item sizes. Each completed batch except the last has load at least d-rho. Thus the number of nonempty batches is at most ceil((R-r_i)/(d-rho)) (zero if no others). Every batch spans a proper linear space, and the rank profile implies it does not contain V_i. There is a hyperplane containing the batch but not V_i, by linear algebra. Translating it by the common center gives the required covering hyperplane. Therefore Theorem 3 applies with

    D = 1+max_i ceil((R-r_i)/(d-rho))

or any tighter bound from actual batches, provided D<q. For equal r_i=a one may instead use exactly ceil((g-1)/floor((d-1)/a)) batches per facet. For instance if R=O(d) and rho<=c d with fixed c<1, the sufficient degree is bounded by a constant independent of g. This is valid even if R>d and the global direct sum has a large kernel. The algorithm only needs a degree cap, not the hidden packing. The maximal-rank assumption must still be certified separately if needed; section 3 supplies one such certificate, not a general verifier of arbitrary maximal-rank arrangements. This is a concrete recognition theorem for arrangements beyond the previous block-ball or flag model.

## 3. An independent heterogeneous independence certificate via homogeneous CRT

Identify the dual of F_q^d with the vector space H_D of homogeneous binary forms of degree D=d-1, in the monomial basis X^(D-j)Y^j, j=0,...,D. For a nonzero homogeneous form f of degree r<d, define the direction space V(f) by its annihilator

    V(f)^perp = f H_(D-r).

Thus dim V(f)=r. This includes ordinary evaluations at a point on the projective line, repeated-root jet (osculating) spaces, closed points of higher degree, and sums of such local spaces. Characteristic restrictions on ordinary derivative formulas are unnecessary: this definition uses divisibility, including repeated factors, not factorial-normalized ordinary derivatives.

**Theorem 4 (CRT rank profile).** If the nonconstant forms f_i are pairwise coprime, then every subset of their spaces has rank min(d,sum_i deg f_i). Given bases of the spaces in such a coordinate frame, the promise and this full weighted rank profile can be certified in polynomial time in the explicit input and log q, without factoring polynomials or enumerating subsets.

Proof. The annihilator of a sum is the intersection of the annihilators. The intersection consists of the degree-D binary forms divisible by each f_i. Pairwise coprimeness makes this equivalent to divisibility by their product of degree R_J=sum_i deg f_i. If R_J>D the intersection is zero; otherwise it has dimension D-R_J+1=d-R_J. Subtracting from d proves the rank formula. This is the homogeneous version of the Chinese remainder / Hermite interpolation rank argument.

To certify the model, compute the annihilator basis of each supplied V_i of dimension r_i with 0<r_i<d. Take the homogeneous gcd g_i of these basis forms. Dehomogenizing at X=1 gives ordinary univariate gcds over F_q, and the multiplicity of the possible infinity factor X is the minimum of D-degree of the dehomogenized basis forms. Their sum is the degree of the homogeneous gcd. If deg g_i=r_i, then the annihilator is contained in g_i H_(D-r_i) and has the same dimension, hence equals it. Conversely the gcd of all monomials of a fixed homogeneous degree is one, so the criterion is necessary in this model. Check that the homogeneous gcds for each pair are coprime by Euclidean gcd and the possible infinity factor. If any test fails, honestly reject the certificate. The dimension and gcd tests alone establish the conclusion, irrespective of how the coordinate frame was proposed. Ranks zero and d can be treated separately (a full-dimensional group gives rank d in any subset containing it). QED.

This certificate permits heterogeneous dimensions and repeated roots and works in small characteristic, including q=2. It does not test all arrangements, and a failed certificate is not evidence of a rank defect. It is a classical CRT mechanism deployed as an explicit independent check; no priority claim is made for the interpolation identity.

### 3.1 Automatic coordinate-frame recovery from singleton anchors

Suppose the arrangement is an unknown common invertible linear image of the preceding model, and at least d+2 of the supplied groups are singleton directions. Under the coprimeness promise these are distinct projective points on the same disguised rational normal curve of degree d-1. Recover a frame without knowing any root parameters using the elementary formula of PR #30, restated here for arbitrary d.

Use representatives v_1,...,v_(d+2) of the first d+2 singleton groups, let B=[v_1 ... v_d], a=B^-1 v_(d+1), b=B^-1 v_(d+2), and t_i=b_i/a_i. Check that B is invertible, all a_i,b_i are nonzero, and the t_i are distinct. Set P(t)=(1,t,...,t^(d-1)), lambda_i=product_(j!=i)(-t_j)/(t_i-t_j), and

    T=[P(t_i) lambda_i/a_i]_i B^-1.

It maps the first d points to the moment curve and the two extra points projectively to P(0) and the infinity point e_(d-1). To see completeness under the promise, first use a fractional-linear parameter transform sending the two extra original parameters to zero and infinity. In those coordinates, the basis coefficients of those extra points are proportional to the Lagrange weights at zero and the leading Lagrange coefficients, divided by the respective representative scalars. Their ratios are a common nonzero scalar times the first d parameters. A common scaling of the parameter gives exactly the t_i. The transformed curve frame and T agree on the first d projective basis points and the extra projective point with all nonzero coordinates; therefore they agree up to overall scalar as projective linear maps. The fractional-linear change is induced by a change of variables on binary homogeneous forms, which preserves divisibility, degrees and coprimeness (including the infinity factor). It thus carries every V(f_i) to another space of the same homogeneous CRT model, even for repeated or nonrational factors. Apply the independent gcd certificate in these coordinates. There is no need to choose an affine chart avoiding all roots or scan the whole field. No completeness guarantee is asserted when there are fewer than d+2 singleton groups or the hidden model is false; a supplied frame can still be tested. The frame construction is reused from #30, not claimed new.

Combined with section 2.3, this gives two separate stages for a bounded-redundancy hidden CRT arrangement: recover its facets from complete-degree samples with a constant-degree guard, and independently certify every weighted subset rank from enough singleton groups. The sample completeness checks do not assume the CRT certificate; the certificate does not assume the sample reconstruction. Neither step requires enumerating all maximal subsets or every full flat.

## 4. Exact verification and limitations

Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python -B verify.py` (Python 3.10+, NumPy; assertions enabled). The recorded `verification.txt` gives 33 complete facet recoveries and 3,044 consistent point decompositions, including nonpure complexes, unequal dimensions, indistinguishable groups and projected Vandermonde fixtures with a global kernel. For each incident facet the verifier sums the reported layer contributions back to the centered point. A regression rejects zero contributions for a nonzero point. Eight sharp field-size obstruction constructions are checked. The incomplete-sample tests recover 38 facets from 244 distinct simplex-grid sample points, including 13 projected lines in dimension eight, and check 78 exact tangent-threshold comparisons. Over F_5 a full ambient line is already recovered at degree zero. Three coordinate planes give nine additional tangent comparisons. At (1,1,0) the degree-two tangent is the ambient space and the degree-three tangent is the plane z=0. Six samples recover both parallel lines y=1 and y=3. The missing-component control illustrates why a sample from one coordinate line cannot identify a second unobserved component. The additional generic maximal-rank tests check all 317 subset ranks on three independently generated arrangements (not assumed to satisfy a jet or flag model), recover their 20 facets from 574 sample points, including a seeded uniform-sample case. Homogeneous CRT fixtures compare 1,300 exact subset ranks, including mixed dimensions, repeated roots, infinity and F_2; two hidden-frame recoveries and three deliberately invalid certificates are checked. One pair, X and XY, has a shared factor at infinity and combined rank two despite individual ranks one and two. The sample-grid example over F_101 with 94 distinct points represents a union whose sum of facet cardinalities is 105,090,803, without enumerating those flats. That sum is an upper bound on union size, not its exact cardinality. All arithmetic tests use exact integers modulo primes <=101; bounded NumPy intermediate integers fit signed 64-bit storage in these fixtures. The implementation is a small-parameter verifier, not a general large-field or cryptographic algorithm. Seeded fixtures are illustrative, not evidence of asymptotic probability guarantees.

The positive complete-complex result removes symmetry, equal-dimension and complete-ball assumptions; the sample result gives an explicit interpolation/cover criterion for much sparser observations, and exact local degree thresholds in the unprojected case. The generic maximal-rank result allows global dependencies and is independent of a hidden flag model. The CRT check is an additional sufficient and independently verifiable structural certificate, not a classification of maximal-rank configurations. The unproved branch remains automatic discovery and validation of useful low-dimensional structure in arbitrary dictionaries, especially those of unbounded redundancy with no small hyperplane covers or suitable projective frame. The underlying GPCA, Stanley–Reisner and CRT ingredients are classical. No claim of wider literature novelty, independent proof review, general deterministic avoidance, or a new complete ECDLP exponent is made. The main paper is unchanged.
