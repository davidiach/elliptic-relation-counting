# Bit complexity by stage

Let $L=\log_2 b$, $S=\Theta(mb)$, $k=\lceil\log_2(2T)\rceil$, $\beta=4mk$, and $N=N_0+2$. Work on a uniform deterministic multitape machine except for the stated sampling of trial coordinates. All entries below are **logarithms of bit-time bounds**, up to absolute constants and additive polynomial-log terms. Polynomial work on $N$-dimensional data costs $O(\log N+\log b)$ on this scale, even with sequential scans. The common comparison class is a whole prime-order elliptic curve over a general prime field of characteristic above three.

| Stage | Dependence and guarantee |
|---|---|
| Point counting and conversion | Schoof and short Weierstrass conversion cost polynomial bit time. Bounded small inputs use direct enumeration. |
| Deterministic seed | Let $g=|G|$, $n=g-1$, nominal block mean $q=nT/p$. Independent parity-class affine maps give within-block pair retention at most $(T/p)^2$. Conditional expectations require $O(mb)$ closed contractions on $O(mb)$ edges. Cost $O(mb/\sqrt{\log(mb)}+\log b)$. |
| Factor-base density and enumeration | The LZR dense sumset proof uses $q\ge4096m$ and $q^m\ge16g$. The least integral $T$ satisfying these inequalities is $\lceil(p/n)\max(4096m,(16g)^{1/m})\rceil$. Test them by integer powering and binary search. LZR rounds the root up before scaling, with the same asymptotic bound. Increasing $T$ gives no further saving in this estimate. Streaming comparisons and membership give $O(\log(mT)+b/\sqrt L+\log b)$. |
| Universal decomposition and explicit rows | One target costs $O(mb/\sqrt{\log(mb)}+\log b)$ using prefix pinning. Do this for $N$ labeled points; $\log N=O(\log(mT)+1)$. |
| Fixed-shift completeness and separation in LZR | Completeness is algebraic. The filter degree is $8m+2$ and the precision is $O(mk+mb)$. At most $O(Nb)$ violated-row extractions, each with $O(mb)$ prefix calls. Explicit table description and row updates contribute $O(\log N+\log b)$. For prime order a violating row decreases affine dimension by one. |
| Original partial expansion | With $u_j\asymp\lambda/2^j$, depth $D_*=\lceil\log_{32}(4m)\rceil$, $\lambda=\lceil\log S\rceil$, the mass of the depth-$j$ frontier is at most $2^jS$. Summing $2^jS/\sqrt{u_j}$ gives $O(Sm^{3/10}/\sqrt\lambda)$; lifts add $O(m^{6/5}\lambda)$. For $m\asymp L^{1/4}$ the saving is $7/40$. |
| One-step expansion | For $R\asymp m$ groups, $u\asymp\lambda/\log R$, cost $O(S\sqrt{\log R/\lambda}+\lambda)$. Group size must leave $O(\beta/m)$ terminals per child, so take $R\ge4m$ up to fixed factors. Pinned entries cost $O(S/\sqrt\lambda)$. See the proof in PARTIAL_EXPANSION.md. |
| Residual and reconstruction | With $n=O(m)$ residual vertices, the extra log cost for digits is $O(n\lambda)$, the log bit cost for a scalar contraction is $O(\beta/\sqrt{\rho})$ plus entry access, and $\rho=\max(1,\lfloor\log(\beta/\max(\Delta,\lceil\beta^{9/10}\rceil))\rfloor)$. At the balanced arity this is $O(b/\sqrt{\log m})$. Odd denominator inversion, monic reduction and exact guard division cost polynomial in $S$ per term. |
| Gram construction and row information | Fixed explicit rows and the full-rank argument replace candidate certification. Computing $N^2$ entries requires at most $16m^2$ two-pin counts per entry, also covering repetitions. Only single pins enter the right side. No iterative counterexample witness is required. |
| Odd-field evaluation through the dyadic contractor | For address piece width $w_0$, $q=O(\beta/w_0+m+s)$. With digit height $t=\max(1,\lfloor S/(q+1)\rfloor)$, the overhead is $q\log\max(1,\lceil\lceil\log r\rceil/t\rceil)$. Scalar precision is $O(S)$. Choose $w_0=\lceil\sqrt b\rceil$ within the original degree cap. |
| Linear solve and check | Store the dense matrix, eliminate over the known prime field, check singularity and the output by scalar multiplication. Polynomial bit time and space in $N,b$. No randomized sparse solver. |

For growing polylogarithmic $m$, the envelope of the Gram trial is $O(b/m+mb/\sqrt{\log(mb)}+(\sqrt b+m)\log b)$, minimized in order at $m\asymp L^{1/4}$. $O(b)$ expected random bits suffice for all rows together. For the one-step expansion considered in isolation with enumeration, balancing $b/m$ and $mb\sqrt{\log m/L}$ gives $m\asymp(L/\log L)^{1/4}$ and exponent $O(b(\log L/L)^{1/4})$. This comparison uses $m^2=O(\sqrt L)$ for early stops and the associated terminal bound. Restoring the residual term gives the published double-log scale. Across this certified polylogarithmic-arity regime $\log m=O(\log L)$, so those residual estimates cannot certify a positive power of $L$ in the denominator. This is a statement about upper-bound optimization, not a running-time lower bound.

The uniform deterministic algorithm currently guaranteed is LZR (or a bounded Gram trial list followed by LZR), with time $2^{O(b/\sqrt{\log\log b})}$ and space $2^{O(b/L^{1/4})}$. With a good trial specified, the improved time holds; with fresh trials the time is expected. Appending all grid points to a deterministic enumeration changes its cost. No preprocessing is amortized over other targets in the claimed trial bound.

The deterministic-points follow-up tests $C+K(1+(s-1)\binom K2)$ points, with $C=N+1$, $K=C$ and bounded $s$. Its root setup applies multiplicative Stage I and Proposition 2.6 to the $b+O(1)$-bit group-order prime and its predecessor, as in LZR Proposition 3.3. Streaming the evaluations, saving at most $N$ rank-increasing points and checking consistency all cost $2^{O(b/L^{1/4})}$ bit time and at most that space. This upper bound covers the complete test even when its output is a proper kernel. Dense-fiber enumeration is used only by the finite verifier.

[Exact neighboring fibers](NEIGHBORING_FIBERS.md) proves rank on the bounded full-grid branch. A full grid has $r^s=2^{O(b)}$ points and $L^{1/4}\le45$ throughout the explicitly certified $m\le45$ range; this cost cannot be transferred to unbounded arity. In the general regime the all-displacement covariance is evaluable directly in $\operatorname{poly}(N,b)$ time. Extracting a spanning family of displacements uses at most $N_0-m$ rounds of $O(b)$ prefix tests with $\operatorname{poly}(N,b)$ closed counts per test, within the target budget. Fixed-generator right sides remain a separate requirement.
