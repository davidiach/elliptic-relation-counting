# Testing chord equality without the two half lists

**21 November 2026.** On regular sampled grids the two determinant products modulo $p^2$ can be compared exactly from the seeds without storing either original half list. The best exact time bound obtained here still has log time $b/2+O(b/m+m+\log b)$, with $b=\lceil\log_2p\rceil$. The project's conditional general-prime end-to-end bound remains $(24.00245996+o(1))\,b/(\log_2 b)^{1/4}$. [End-to-end assumptions and accounting](../sampled_slots/README.md)

| Question | Result |
|---|---|
| What does avoiding the two lists cost? | With $k$ free slots per side, the exact comparison needs at most $(2w)^{m-k}$ time and $(2w)^k$ space up to polynomial factors. It handles arbitrary row speeds and every hit count on regular grids. Storing a third of the slots per side gives log space $b/3+o(b)$ and log time $2b/3+o(b)$. [Section algorithm](sections.md) |
| Can one evaluate a chord without constructing its addition tree? | In a group of known order prime to $p$, one unary function, evaluable in $O(\log N)$ field operations at a point, gives all the slopes by differences. Its sum over a full kernel coset collapses to one evaluation on the quotient curve. The remaining many-slot quantity is a weighted sum of these values. [Unary function and isogeny trace](algebra.md) |
| Can those cosets cover typical halves? | Call a coset eligible if at least two of its points are half sums and its density of half sums is at least $\eta=2^{-o(b)}$. The union of eligible cosets from all nontrivial rational subgroups covers at most $2^{b/4+o(b)}$ of the $2^{b/2-o(b)}$ distinct sums on a typical successful half. This includes adaptive choices on composite-order groups. [Two-quarter proof](adaptive_cosets.md) |
| Can all the cancellations be taken first? | For a raw abscissa bit at a fixed target $T$, the remaining pole at $x(R)$ is determined by the multiplicity difference $B_{R+T}-B_{R-T}$ in the opposite half, for $2R\ne O$. This gives an exact singleton test with a two-torsion correction. On typical successful seeds, the reduced transform still has $2^{b/2-o(b)}$ distinct poles. [Fixed-target transform](fiber.md) |
| Is an accurate cheap prediction enough? | A bounded-work predictor attains $o((\log b)^{-2})$ error for all binary-speed comparisons together at the project arity under a uniform independent mask of size $\Theta(N)$. On guarded singleton seeds the ordinate-bit comparison alone has error $1/2+o(1)$ under a symmetric mask. The full predicted comparison transcript also has exponentially small witness correlation in the stated fixed-target model. [Predictor and error laws](projection.md) |

The open operation is a joint evaluation from the seeds at one prescribed target that recovers a witness bit on isolated instances. Counting poles limits an algorithm that builds the reduced rational function. Counting covered points limits explicit coset compression. Both leave room for a different arithmetic calculation of a single bit.

The [section comparisons](../../evidence/check_chord_sections.txt) tested 171 literal product comparisons. In three of them all four empty sections had nonzero digits whose sum vanished. The [quarter checks](../../evidence/check_quarter_cosets.txt) tested 91,573 combinations of seed states and subgroups on two complete small curve groups. Further transcripts cover [isogeny traces](../../evidence/check_implicit_chord_algebra.txt), [poles after cancellation](../../evidence/check_implicit_chord_fiber.txt), and [masked versus singleton errors](../../evidence/check_implicit_chord_projection.txt). All checks are in the [reproduction runner](../../reproduce.py).
