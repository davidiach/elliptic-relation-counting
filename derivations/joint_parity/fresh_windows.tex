\paragraph{Result.}
Write $b=\lceil\log_2p\rceil$, $L=\log_2 b$, $g=\log_2 L$, and
$N=\#E(\mathbb F_p)$. All bit costs are classical. The project's
conditional upper bound on $\log_2\E T$ remains
$(26.2933775156+o(1))b/L^{1/4}$. Under its existing outer reduction
it would suffice to save $g$ in the joint relation-evaluation exponent
on \emph{fresh} hashed inputs at one width profile. Two-sided error of
order $1/L^2$ on those fresh inputs is allowed. We give the reduction
and test two proposed evaluators. Neither establishes the required cost.

\section{Recovering an isolated relation from a fresh-input predictor}
Fix $T$ with $2T\ne O$. As in \texttt{../mixed\_coefficient/mixed.tex},
$M=2^b$, each independent slot has hash $h_i(x)=\alpha_ix+\beta_i\bmod M$
with uniform odd multiplier and uniform offset. Its interval is
$[0,\tau_i)$ of dyadic width. Put $\rho_i=\tau_i/M$, $\nu_i=N\rho_i$
and $\lambda=\prod_i\nu_i/N$. Let $H$ be the ordinary ordered count
at $T$ and $X$ its guarded subcount. Set
\[
 A_0=\prod_i(1+2/\nu_i),\quad z=|E(\mathbb F_p)[2]|(2^m-2)/N,\quad
 \gamma=1-3m/N .
\]
We use the finite bounds $\E H\le\lambda$, $\E X\ge\gamma\lambda$,
and $\E[H(H-1)]\le A_0\lambda^2+\lambda z$ proved in that note.
The probability of $H=X=1$ is at least $\gamma\lambda-A_0\lambda^2-\lambda z$.
Balance the integer $\log_2\tau_i$ so that $1/(2L)<\lambda\le1/L$,
by rounding down their total required for $\lambda=1/L$. At the
proposed arity $m\asymp L^{1/4}\sqrt g$ they give $A_0=1+o(1)$,
$Lz=o(1)$, $2\tau_i\le M$, and $Q=\sum_i\log_2\tau_i=b+O(m+\log L)$.

\begin{proposition}
Suppose the same evaluator returns an ordinary parity prediction
at this one width profile. For each prescribed target assume absolute
error probability $O(L^{-2})$ and mean bit cost at most $D_m$, averaged
over independent fresh hashes and its private randomness. All setup
used by the evaluator is charged. Then a complete attempt can return
a verified guarded relation with probability $\Omega(1/L)$, with
worst-case cost at most
\[
 \operatorname{poly}(b,m)(D_m+2^m)\,2^{O(b\log_2 L/L)} .
\]
This proves a distributional sufficient condition for the outer
reduction. The evaluator itself remains a hypothesis.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Cap each call at $O(L^2)D_m$ bit operations, forcing a bit on timeout.
Markov's inequality preserves fresh-input error $\varepsilon=O(L^{-2})$.
For each coordinate of the putative isolated tuple, the ideal search
path is the dyadic path to $u=h_i(x)$ inside its original width $\tau$.
During each query all \emph{other} slots use their original hashes.
Suppose the present interval on this path is $[j,j+s)$ at depth $d$,
where $s=\tau/2^d$ and $s\ge2$. Set $t=s/2$, $q=2^{d+1}-1$ and draw
uniform $\eta\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}$. Replace this one slot by the hash
\[
                  h'(x)=q(h_i(x)-j)+t+\eta\bmod M ,
\]
at the original width $\tau$. The multiplier is odd. At the marked
point $y=u-j\in[0,s)$, and $h'=t+qy+\eta$. Because $(q+1)t=\tau$,
$h'<\tau$ is exactly the predicate $y<t$. The full range is
$[t,2\tau-t)$, with no wraparound.

For the probability argument fix any concrete ordinary tuple of sum $T$
and condition on it being retained in the root. Its $u$ is uniform on
$[0,\tau)$ independently of $\alpha_i$; $u-j$ is uniform on $[0,s)$.
Thus its transformed value is uniform on $[t,2\tau-t)$ independently
of $q\alpha_i$. These variables completely specify the transformed
seed for this fixed mark. Relative to a fresh seed conditional on the
same mark being in $[0,2\tau)$ its density is at most $2$. Root
retention has half the probability of retention in the doubled slot.
The other marked slots retain their original independent laws.
After summing over marks the two factors cancel. This argument conditions
only on retention of the mark, never on isolation or earlier answers.

Write $H_+$ for the count on a fresh transformed seed using doubled
width in this slot. If $H_+=1$, the true ordinary parity at the
queried width gives the correct marked branch, including when the mark
lies in the upper half. The expected number of retained marks for
which an ideal step is wrong is bounded by
\[
 \varepsilon+\E[H_+(H_+-1)]
       \le \varepsilon+4A_0\lambda^2+2\lambda z .
\]
Indeed, when $H_+=1$ the marked contribution is at most the indicator
of an oracle error at the original width; when $H_+\ge2$ the factorial
moment pays for all its marks. This charges wrong answers even if
they depend arbitrarily on the fresh seeds.

Try all subsets of at most $J=\lceil C b/L\rceil$ of the $Q$ decisions,
reversing those answers. Fix the random draws and evaluator tapes by
step before exploring the list and reuse them across its paths.
For any given mark its ideal steps depend only on that mark's true
prefixes. The list entry consisting of the ideal wrong steps reproduces
those prefixes by induction. A recovered abscissa vector has at most
$2^m$ sign choices, all checked against the ordinary equation, original
windows and guards. Return only a verified row or failure.
The complete success probability is at least
\[
 \gamma\lambda-A_0\lambda^2-\lambda z-
 \frac{Q}{J+1}\bigl(\varepsilon+4A_0\lambda^2+2\lambda z\bigr).
\]
This follows by Markov's inequality on the number of ideal errors,
charging only unique guarded roots in the success estimate. A sufficiently
large fixed $C$ gives $\Omega(1/L)$. There are at most
$\sum_{k\le J}\binom Qk=2^{O(b\log_2 L/L)}$ patterns.
Generating and retaining bounded tapes fits the stated time budget.
No improved workspace bound is claimed.
\end{proof}

Use the same randomized row-selection rule in training and online
queries, drawing fresh attempt randomness and verifying candidates.
The project pilot argument has inverse mean success polynomial in $L$.
The adjusted nonzero targets lie in the odd prime-order subgroup on the
supplied coprime-cofactor input class, so $2T\ne O$. With
$B_0=2\sum_i\tau_i$ we have $\log_2 B_0=b/m+O(\log b+\log m)$.
For one target the outer estimate charges, up to polynomial factors,
$B_0 C_m+mB_0^2$, including failed pilots, verification and retries.
If $\log_2 D_m\le(A+o(1))mb/(\sqrt L\,g)$ at
$m\sim L^{1/4}\sqrt{g/A}$, both terms consequently give
\[
 \log_2\E T\le (2\sqrt A+o(1))\,\frac{b}{L^{1/4}\sqrt g}.
\]
Here $A$ is the constant in the hypothetical predictor bound.
The list charge $b\log_2 L/L$ is little-o of this scale.
The current contraction theorem gives an exponent of order
$mb/\sqrt L$; asking for parity alone supplies no extra $g$ in that
theorem. The reduction above need not answer parity on every realized
adaptive window. It finds a usable relation with sufficient probability.

\section{A nonadaptive alternative at smaller density}
There is a second sufficient condition with polynomially many calls
and no search over error patterns. Choose $K=2b$, $D$ the least power
of two at least $K$, and balanced dyadic widths with
$1/(2CmD)<\lambda\le1/(CmD)$ for a sufficiently large fixed $C$.
At the arities in this note $D\tau_i\le M$, $m/\min\nu_i=o(1)$
and $mz=o(1)$. For each slot $i$ let $H_i^+$ count tuples after
enlarging just its width by $D$ with the original hash. Then
\[
 \E[H(H_i^+-1)]\le D\lambda^2 A_0+\lambda z .
\]
For clarity, $H(H_i^+-1)$ counts ordered \emph{distinct} pairs, the
first in the root and the second in the enlarged box. In the changed
slot, for distinct abscissas the joint retention probability is at
most $\rho_i D\rho_i$. Given the first hash value in $[0,\tau_i)$,
their difference is uniform among residues of its dyadic valuation.
If that valuation is at least $\log_2(D\tau_i)$ both cannot be in the
enlargement. Otherwise exactly a fraction $D\rho_i$ hit it. For a
proper set of equal abscissas, the pair count from the original
factorial bound gives the first term above, since every enlarged
$\nu_i$ is at least its root value. With all abscissas equal and
different tuples the same flip-set argument gives $\lambda z$.
Thus the probability that an isolated guarded root tuple is the
only tuple in every one-slot enlargement is at least
$\gamma\lambda-m(D\lambda^2 A_0+\lambda z)=\Omega(\lambda)$.
Indeed the indicator of that event dominates
$X-\sum_i H(H_i^+-1)$ pointwise.

For each odd modulus $3\le q\le K$ and each residue $0\le a<q$,
replace just slot $i$ by $q^{-1}(h_i-a)\bmod M$ at its original width.
The accepted original hash values are exactly $a+qj$ for
$0\le j<\tau_i$, all in $[0,D\tau_i)$. Inside $[0,\tau_i)$ this tests
the residue modulo $q$. On the isolated-enlargement event the bits
identify each marked coordinate uniquely, since the least common
multiple of these odd moduli exceeds $2^b$ eventually. Indeed the
central binomial coefficient $\binom K{\lfloor K/2\rfloor}\ge2^K/(K+1)$
divides $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,K)$: its valuation at each prime
is at most $\lfloor\log_p K\rfloor$ by the factorial valuation formula.
The power of two removed costs a factor at most $K$.
The transform of $(\alpha_i,\beta_i)$ is a bijection on its seed
space. All $O(mK^2)$ calls are predetermined and individually fresh.
A union bound permits per-call absolute error $o(\lambda/(mK^2))$.
Fresh mean costs add by linearity. A polynomial timeout on the whole
attempt can retain $\Omega(\lambda)$ success at uniformly bounded
cost. This version trades smaller tolerated fresh error for a simpler
decoding and cost argument.

\section{Testing joint evaluators}
\paragraph{Parity in randomly merged buckets.}
On the prime-order subclass, take an even balanced arity. Let the odd
supports of the two half-sums be $U,V$, translating and reflecting the
second by $T$ so the target parity is $|U\cap V|\bmod2$. Grant the
two supports for free to this test. The half-population and collision
estimates in \texttt{parity.tex} give $a=|U|$, $c=|V|$ tending to
infinity in probability and $ac=(1+o_{\Prb}(1))N\lambda$ at the
widths above. The sets are disjoint with probability $1-O(\lambda)$
by $\E H\le\lambda$. Hash each distinct group element independently
into one of $k$ bins and take the dot product modulo two of the two
vectors of bin parities.

Conditional on disjoint supports, if exactly $Z$ bins on the left
are odd, the chance of an erroneous odd answer on the right is
$[1-(1-2Z/k)^c]/2$. For $k\ge4$,
\[
 \E Z=\frac{k}{2}\bigl(1-(1-2/k)^a\bigr),\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}Z\le\E Z .
\]
The covariance of two distinct odd-bin indicators equals
$((1-4/k)^a-(1-2/k)^{2a})/4\le0$.
When $k\to\infty$ and $k=o(ac)$, $\E Z\asymp\min(a,k)\to\infty$,
so $Z/\E Z\to1$ in probability. Then $cZ/k\to\infty$ and
$Z/k\le1/2+o_{\Prb}(1)$. The error tends to $1/2$. Even with
$k/(ac)\to d\in(0,\infty)$, $Z/a\to1$ and the limiting error is
$(1-e^{-2/d})/2$. In the high-bucket regime $k\gg N\lambda$, the
error is $(1+o(1))N\lambda/k$. On disjoint supports the $ac$ cross-pair
collision indicators are pairwise independent, including pairs of tests
with one shared endpoint. For their sum $J$,
$1_{\{J\ {\rm odd}\}}\ge J-J(J-1)$ gives conditional error at least
$ac/k-ac(ac-1)/k^2$. Restrict to typical disjoint supports for the
averaged lower bound. For the upper bound on any two supports, only
off-diagonal collisions can change the answer, and
$\E[ac]\le N\lambda$ by independence of the half populations.
Thus a growing bucket universe of order $N/\lambda$ is necessary
and sufficient up to constants for $O(\lambda^2)$ error in this
single iid-hash sketch. This prices its statistical compression.
Sparse storage and injective labels have different costs.

\paragraph{Can nonlinear processing of repeated small sketches help?}
Consider an evaluator whose entire information from these sets consists
of the two bin-parity vectors from each of $R$ fresh independent
random hashes. The decoder may use those vectors jointly in any way.
At $k,R\le 2^{o(b)}$ its fresh-input error is at least $(1-o(1))\lambda$.
In this observation model the decoder receives neither the random
hash descriptions nor auxiliary information about the slot seeds.

Here are the quantitative details. Write $a_*=\prod_{i\in L}\nu_i$
for one balanced half, so $a_*=N^{1/2+o(1)}$. The relative variance
of its population is $O(m/\min\nu_i)$, and its mean is
$(1-1/N)^{m/2}a_*$. The expected relative loss from reducing counts
modulo two is at most
$(a_*/N)A_0+|E[2]|2^{m/2}/N$ by the half-collision bound of
\texttt{parity.tex}. Chebyshev and Markov therefore give
$a,c\ge2N^{1/3}$ except with probability $o(\lambda)$ at the stated
widths and arity. Also $\E|U\cap V|\le\E H\le\lambda$. Thus each
side has at least $N^{1/3}$ distinct unmatched elements except on
an event of probability $o(\lambda)$. The two supports have even
sizes, since each nonempty half contains a symmetric slot on an
odd prime-order curve and each such slot has even size.

For $u$ unmatched elements the vector of their bin parities is the
sum of $u$ independent uniform coordinate vectors in $\mathbb F_2^k$.
Its character at a subset of $j$ bins has value $(1-2j/k)^u$.
The characters for $j=0,k$ are precisely those of the uniform law on
the parity class of $u$. Parseval and Cauchy--Schwarz bound total
variation from that law, for $k\ge2$, by
\[
 \frac12\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\binom kj|1-2j/k|^{2u}\right)^{1/2}
 \le 2^{k/2}(1-2/k)^u .
\]
Given the hash values of the shared elements, the unmatched walks are
independent, and adding the shared vector puts each reference walk in
the even class. The resulting reference consists of two independent
uniform even vectors regardless of the actual supports. For $k=1$
both vectors are zero. On the typical event the error bound for $k\ge2$
is at most $\exp(O(k)-2N^{1/3}/k)$, hence still $o(\lambda)$ after
summing it over $R$ rounds. Coupling with the reference proves that
the sketch transcript and the true bit are within $o(\lambda)$ total
variation of a joint law in which the transcript is independent of
the bit. The factorial bound gives
$\Prb(H\bmod2=1)=(1+o(1))\lambda$. The best independent guess therefore
has error $(1+o(1))\lambda$. This result includes repetition and voting
of the compressed dot product. It gives no lower bound on an evaluator
reading the actual hash seeds or using additional features.

\paragraph{A local determinant construction.}
The factored Boolean compiler in \texttt{../hillclimb\_003/compression.tex}
uses ternary equality to copy bits. We tested replacing local tensors
by signatures of perfect-matching gadgets over a characteristic-two
field after independent invertible basis changes. Any such signature
is supported on one parity of boundary patterns by counting the
remaining vertices. For slices $A_0,A_1$ of a ternary signature
consider the $XY$ coefficient in $\det(XA_0+YA_1)$. Changes on the
two matrix axes scale the binary quadratic by nonzero determinants.
Changing the slice axis substitutes independent linear forms for
$X,Y$; in characteristic two the mixed coefficient scales by their
determinant. Thus its vanishing is invariant. For a single-parity
signature it vanishes since opposite patterns have opposite parities.
For equality the determinant is $XY$. This rules out the proposed
local conversion over any extension of $\mathbb F_2$. It leaves open
constructions operating on blocks of tensors.
