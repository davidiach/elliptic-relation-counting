Use $b=\lceil\log_2p\rceil$, $L=\log_2b$, $g=\log_2L$,
$M=2^b$, and $N=\#E(\mathbb F_p)$. As in \texttt{fresh\_windows.tex},
$p>3$, the adjusted target has $2T\ne O$, and the independently
seeded dyadic widths $w_j$ have $\rho_j=w_j/M$, $\nu_j=N\rho_j$,
$1/(2L)<\lambda=N^{-1}\prod_j\nu_j\le1/L$.
The proposed arity is $m\asymp L^{1/4}\sqrt g$. Let $H$ be the
ordinary ordered target count, $X$ its guarded count, and $V=H\bmod2$.
At these widths $\mu=\E H$ is between $(1-3m/N)\lambda$ and
$\lambda$, $\E(H-X)\le3m\lambda/N$, and
$\E[H(H-1)]=O(\lambda^2)$. These bounds and the two-abscissa hash
law are from \texttt{../mixed\_coefficient/mixed.tex}. Error means
the probability of a wrong bit at the prescribed target.

\section{An error budget that permits rounding}
In each slot round the offset $\beta_j$ down to a multiple of a dyadic
$s_j\mid w_j$, retaining the original odd multiplier. For fixed
abscissa and multiplier, exactly a fraction $\rho_j$ of uniform offsets
accept after rounding: a length-$w_j$ interval contains $w_j/s_j$
grid points. At most $2(s_j-1)$ offsets change the membership of this
abscissa, on the two edges of the interval. Replace slots in sequence.
In every other slot, original or rounded, a specified point has
retention probability $\rho_k$, independently. There are at most
$N^{m-1}$ tuples of sum $T$. Therefore the probability that the two
root tuple sets differ is bounded by $2\lambda\sum_j s_j/w_j$.

Use the \emph{existence of a guarded tuple} on the rounded input as the
parity prediction. Its error is at most
\[
 2\lambda\sum_j s_j/w_j+\tfrac12\E[H(H-1)]+\E(H-X).
\]
Indeed an unchanged tuple set with at most one tuple and $X=H$ gives
the correct answer. Taking $s_j$ the largest power of two at most
$w_j/(mL)$ gives $O(L^{-2})$ error and suppresses
$\sum_j\log_2 s_j=b-m\log_2(mL)+O(m+\log L)=b-o(b)$ low offset bits
at these arities. The cost of deciding existence is still charged. The
available exact guarded count on these legal seeds retains the
$O(mb/\sqrt L)$ log-cost. The circuit already treats the seeds as
public constants. Of its $(36+o(1))mb$ leading Boolean word edges,
$(34+o(1))mb$ are outside the hash component.
Even assigning zero cost to the hash component, while retaining that
construction and its other costs, would only change the optimized
coefficient from $26.29338$ to $26.29338\sqrt{34/36}\simeq25.55257$.
This is a hypothetical accounting comparison, not an achieved bound.

\section{A parity distance for arbitrary shapes in one slot}
Fix slot $i$, $w=w_i$. In inverse-hash coordinates its accepted
residues are $C+a[0,w)$ in $\mathbb Z/M$, with $a$ uniform odd and
$C$ uniform independently. The following bound concerns any shape
given an independent uniform cyclic translation.

\begin{proposition}
Let $D\subset\mathbb Z/M$ have size $d$. Put $D+C$ in slot $i$,
where $C$ is independent of the other original fresh hashes, and let
$H_D$ be the target count. Write $\lambda_D=(d/w)\lambda$,
$A_-=\prod_{j\ne i}(1+2/\nu_j)$ and $\kappa=|E(\mathbb F_p)[2]|$.
Then $\E H_D=(d/w)\mu$ and
\[
 \E[H_D(H_D-1)]\le
 \frac{2M}{N}A_-\lambda_D^2+
 \bigl(8+2\kappa(2^{m-1}-2)\bigr)\frac{d\lambda_D}{N}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first moment follows by translation. For canonical abscissas
separated by $v\bmod M$, set $K(v)=|D\cap(D-v)|/M$.
Joint retention in the special slot is $K(v)$, and $\sum_vK(v)=d^2/M$.
For fixed $P$ there are at most two affine $Q$ per possible abscissa.
Thus summing $K(x(Q)-x(P))$ over $Q$ costs at most $2d^2/M$.

Classify distinct ordered target tuples $P,Q$ by the exact subset $S$
of the other $m-1$ slots with equal abscissas. For $s=|S|\le m-2$,
there are at most $N^{m-1}$ first tuples and $2^s$ choices of signs
in $S$. Sum over $Q_i$ by the preceding bound. One unmatched other
point is forced by the target and at most $N^{m-s-2}$ choices remain.
The other slots give retention at most
$\prod_{j\in S}\rho_j\prod_{j\notin S,\ j\ne i}\rho_j^2$.
Summing the proper subsets gives the first displayed term.

If all other abscissas match, take the indices at which the points
actually differ as the flip set. It cannot be empty for distinct tuples.
For a nonempty proper flip set, fix $P_i,Q_i$. Twice the sum of flipped
points is $Q_i-P_i$, allowing at most $\kappa$ sums. Both blocks of
other points now have prescribed sums, allowing $\kappa N^{m-3}$
first tuples per such pair. Summing $K$ over $P_i,Q_i$ gives at most
$2Nd^2/M$. For the full flip set, $Q_i=2T-P_i$. The function
$P\mapsto x(2T-P)-x(P)$ has two distinct double poles, at $2T$ and
$O$. A fixed field value therefore has at most four preimages.
One residue of the canonical integer difference modulo $M$ gives
at most two possible field values. Thus summing $K$ over these $P_i$
costs at most $8d^2/M$, and the other points have at most $N^{m-2}$
choices. All matched other slots contribute $\prod_{j\ne i}\rho_j$.
These bounds give the stated second term, also for $m=2$.
For the pole of the Weierstrass $x$ coordinate see
\href{https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.783/2019/LectureNotes24.pdf}
{Sutherland, 18.783, lecture 24}.
\end{proof}

For two $w$-point shapes $S,S'$ with the same uniform translation,
$V(S)\mathbin{\rm xor}V(S')=H_{S\triangle S'}\bmod2$ exactly.
The odd probability lies between $\E H_D-\E[H_D(H_D-1)]$ and
$\E H_D$. As $d\le 2w$, Hasse gives $M/N=O(1)$, and $A_-=1+o(1)$,
we obtain uniformly, also for random shapes independent of the center
and the other hashes,
\[
 \Prb(V(S)\ne V(S'))=
 \left(1+O(\lambda+m/N+2^m w/N)\right)
 \lambda\,\frac{\E|S\triangle S'|}{w}.
\]
In particular the permissible mean residue discrepancy for an
$O(\lambda^2)$-error direct replacement is $O(\lambda w)$.

\section{How much multiplier compression survives?}
Let an encoder retain a label $h(a)$ from a dictionary of size $K$
and the full recentered value $C+f(a)$, with any fixed slope-dependent
translation $f$. Give the decoder all other seeds and unlimited
computation. If $2w^2<M$ and $w$ is even, directions $a'\ne\pm a$
give at most $w/2$ common residues, whatever the two centers.
Indeed an intersection point is encoded by a pair $(t,u)\in[0,w)^2$.
Differences satisfy $a\,\Delta t=a'\,\Delta u\bmod M$. The lattice of
differences has determinant $M$. Every two difference vectors form
a determinant of magnitude below $2w^2$, so all points are collinear.
Along that line adjacent pairs differ by at least two in the common
dominant coordinate: a unit step in both coordinates would require
$a'=\pm a$, and a nonzero axis step is impossible. Hence there are
at most $\lfloor(w-1)/2\rfloor+1=w/2$ points.

Draw two independent slopes conditional on the same code and use the
same revealed center and the same other slots. These are conditionally
independent replicas of the unobserved slope given the decoder's data.
Their probability of matching up to sign is at most $4K/M$: in each
code cell of size $s$ it is at most $2/s$, and that cell has mass $2s/M$.
On different directions the shape discrepancy is at least $w$.
For a Bernoulli bit of conditional mean $u$, the optimal error
$\min(u,1-u)$ is at least half the disagreement of two independent
replicas, $u(1-u)$. The preceding parity distance yields
\[
 \Prb(\text{decoding error})\ge
 (1-o(1))\,\frac{\lambda}{2}\max(0,1-4K/M).
\]
Consequently $O(\lambda^2)$ error requires $K\ge(1-O(\lambda))M/4$.
There is an exact $M/4$-code representation: choose the canonical
sign of each odd multiplier and move the center to the other endpoint
when reversing direction. This is a sharp capacity statement for one
specified codec. The inverse center and the original forward offset
are different side information. An arithmetic circuit can read all
the multiplier bits cheaply; the result gives no general time bound.

\section{Direct evaluation along the last forward offset}
Alternatively fix all other hashes and the multiplier of slot $i$,
leaving $\beta_i$ free. Every tuple in the other slots whose required
last point is affine gives an arc of exactly $w$ consecutive offsets
starting at $-\alpha_i x\bmod M$. Counting repetitions, call the number
of starts $B$. Parity is the XOR of these arcs. Thus its exact positive
set is a union of at most $B$ cyclic intervals, with
$\E B=M\mu/w$. The interval endpoints can be generated directly by
enumerating the other-slot tuples.

Suppose a predictor for this offset uses $J$ positive cyclic intervals
whose locations can depend arbitrarily on all the fixed seeds above.
If $8w\le M$ and fresh error is $O(\lambda^2)$, then
\[
                 \E J\ge (1-O(\lambda))\,M\lambda/w .
\]
To see this, mark a start bad if another is within cyclic distance
$3w$, counting distinct occurrences at the same start. In an enlarged
slot of width $8w$, each such ordered pair overlaps for at least $5w$
offsets. The enlarged factorial moment bounds the expected number of
bad starts by $(M/(5w))(64A_0\lambda^2+8\lambda z)$, with $A_0,z$ as
in \texttt{fresh\_windows.tex}. On a good start the true bit is zero
for the preceding $w$ offsets and one for the next $w$. Without an
upward transition inside these $2w$ positions a binary prediction
makes at least $w$ errors there. These segments are disjoint for good
starts. Average this charging argument, allowing a fixed independent
random tape for the predictor, to get the displayed bound.
The optimal expected explicit interval count is thus asymptotic to
$M\lambda/w=2^{(1-1/m)b+O(\log b)}$. A succinct point-query computation
can describe many runs. This bound prices expanding an offset table.

\section{A structured matrix for batches of high seed bits}
For a dyadic $q=2^k$, $D=M/q$ even and $w\le D$, freeze the low words
$\alpha_0,\beta_0$ of a slot. Vary their high words $A,B\bmod q$.
After checking $(\alpha_0x+\beta_0)\bmod D<w$, membership is the
incidence equation $Au+B=v\bmod q$, with $u=x\bmod q$ and
$v=-\lfloor(\alpha_0x+\beta_0)/D\rfloor\bmod q$. The universal
$q^2$-by-$q^2$ binary incidence matrix has rank
\[
 R(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}(q-e_j)_+\sim q^2/\log_2 q,\qquad
 e_j=\sum_{h=1}^j2^{v_2(h)}.
\]
Encode cyclic shifts of $v$ by multiplication by $z=1+t$ in
$\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^q)$. The intercept shifts generate the module
image of the Vandermonde matrix $(z^{uA})_{u,A}$. Over
$\mathbb F_2[[t]]$ change the monomial columns to the monic Newton
polynomials $\prod_{h<j}(Y-z^h)$. Entries above the diagonal vanish.
At row $j$ the valuation is $e_j$, since $v_t(z^s-1)=2^{v_2(s)}$.
At any later row the valuation is at least $e_j$: an interval of $j$
integers contains at least $\lfloor j/2^\ell\rfloor$ multiples of
$2^\ell$ for every $\ell$. Dividing each column by its diagonal
power of $t$ leaves an invertible triangular matrix. Reduction modulo
$t^q$ now gives the rank formula. Finally
$e_j=j+\sum_{\ell\ge1}2^{\ell-1}\lfloor j/2^\ell\rfloor
=(j/2)\log_2 j+O(j)$. The last positive summand has
$j\sim 2q/\log_2q$, and summing the linear leading profile gives the
stated equivalent.

This saves a factor asymptotic to $k$ in the number of independent
coordinates of a full batch. It gives no cheap evaluation rule for a
single fresh joint instance. Reducing the remaining word size from $b$
to $b/g$ in one split makes $k=(1-o(1))b$, where materializing even
one such universal basis has logarithmic size $2k-\log_2k+o(1)$.
