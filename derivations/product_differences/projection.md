# One ordinary field incidence for a joint address-parity condition

For every sufficiently large base prime, a single ordinary elliptic incidence test over $\mathbb F_{p^2}$ can select exactly the tuples of prescribed Walsh parity. The construction keeps the original target and arity. It translates the sampled points and expands just one slot by at most $\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1$. On a regular singleton this answers the corresponding equality between lifted determinant products without constructing either scalar modulo $p^2$. The remaining operation is an exact existence test on the transformed slots.

## The marker point

Write $E:y^2=g(x)=x^3+Ax+B$, $G=E(\mathbb F_p)$, $p>3$, with $m^3=o(p)$. Choose a quadratic nonresidue $d$. The subgroup $G^-$ of $E(\mathbb F_{p^2})$ on which Frobenius acts as negation consists of the point at infinity and the points $(x,y\sqrt d)$ with $x,y\in\mathbb F_p$ and $d y^2=g(x)$. It is the group of rational points of the quadratic twist and has cardinality $p+O(\sqrt p)$. Its intersection with $G$ is the rational two-torsion, of size at most four. [Quadratic twist, Sutherland §7.6](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.783/2023/LectureNotes7.pdf) · [Hasse's bound](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.783/2019/LectureNotes8.pdf)

Sample and check a marker $R\in G^-$ satisfying

$$[j]R\notin G\qquad(1\le j\le 2m+2). \tag{1}$$

For fixed $j$, any bad $R$ maps under $[j]$ to one of at most four points, with at most $j^2$ preimages each. Therefore a uniform twist point fails with probability $O(m^3/p)$. Uniform affine twist points can be sampled by proposing an abscissa and one of two root signs, accepting both signs at a nonzero square and one sign at a zero. Sampling from the affine group changes the bound only by a factor $1+O(1/p)$. These operations use base-field square roots. The explicit test builds successive multiples and can be reused for all challenges and seeds on this curve and arity. Once the check has passed, all assertions below are exact.

## Encoding the weight in the ordinary group sum

The retained $P_i$ stay labelled by their original signed addresses $a_i(P_i)$. At a challenge $r$, let $c_i(P_i)\in\{0,1\}$ be the local dot product $r_i\cdot a_i(P_i)$ modulo two, and set $k=\sum_i c_i(P_i)$ as an integer. Translating each original point to $P_i+[c_i(P_i)]R$ gives an ordinary tuple with sum $T+[j]R$ exactly when its original tuple has sum $T$ and $k=j$, for $0\le j\le m$. Indeed the difference $(j-k)R$ must be in $G$, which by (1) forces $j=k$.

For any allowed set $J\subseteq\{0,\ldots,m\}$ combine these target tests into **one test at $T$**. Use

$$
F'_i=\{P+[c_i(P)]R:P\in F_i\}\quad(i<m),\qquad
F'_m=\{P+[c_m(P)-j]R:P\in F_m,\ j\in J\}.
$$

Every ordinary relation from the transformed slots comes from precisely one original relation with $k\in J$, and every such original relation occurs once. Equality of two constructed points within the last slot forces equal marker coefficients by (1), then equal original points, and finally equal $j$. The sum condition forces $j=k$. Take $J=\{0,2,4,\ldots\}\cap[0,m]$ for the even witness bit, or the odd weights for its complement. On a multiple-witness root the test answers existence in the selected parity class. It does not compute the sum of the signs there.

On an original regular grid, the new grid is regular as well. A translated point at infinity would require its marker coefficient to vanish and its original point to be at infinity. Equality of two cross-slot abscissas implies equality of their points up to sign. Subtracting gives a rational multiple of $R$ with coefficient of magnitude at most $2m+2$. By (1) that coefficient vanishes, forcing an original same-abscissa pair. The same argument with the endpoint and marker coefficient zero rules out a new abscissa at $x(T)$. Thus the usual elliptic evaluation determinant over the quadratic field vanishes precisely at the stated ordinary incidences. Equivalently its field norm to $\mathbb F_p$ vanishes precisely there. [Determinant criterion](../direct_parity/deformation.md)

For a regular singleton, let $V_C$ be the ordinary determinant product in $\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z$ with the slots moved at their local speeds, and let $V_j$ use the same initial lifts with only the evaluated endpoint moved at speed $j$. The [first-order identity](../direct_parity/deformation.md) gives $V_C-V_j=pU(k-j)$, $U\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. The ordinary field test with $J=\{j\}$ hence decides the **exact equality** $V_C=V_j$ on that promise. For an empty base fiber these products are units and that equality is a separate question. For a base fiber with multiple relations all the products vanish in the precision-two ring on a regular grid. The ordinary tagged test retains its own weight-class interpretation in both cases.

There is an optional normalization entirely within the precision-two ring when $p\nmid N=|G|$ and $N$ is known. Choose a smooth lift of the curve and let $K$ be the kernel of reduction on its points. This kernel is the one-dimensional additive tangent space over $\mathbb F_p$ and has exponent $p$. For any lift $\widetilde P$ of $P$ take $u=N^{-1}\bmod p$ and put $P^\circ=\widetilde P-[u][N]\widetilde P$. The term $[N]\widetilde P$ belongs to $K$. Changing the initial lift by an element of $K$ leaves $P^\circ$ unchanged, and applying the formula to sums shows that $P\mapsto P^\circ$ preserves addition. Thus every original relation holds exactly on the normalized lifts. Its determinant also vanishes modulo $p^2$: lift its first $m$ points arbitrarily to the smooth curve over $\mathbb Z_p$ and set the last point to enforce the relation. This has the normalized endpoint modulo $p^2$. The characteristic-zero divisor criterion gives a zero determinant, which remains zero under reduction. On a regular singleton the normalized baseline product is therefore zero, and comparing it to a perturbed product becomes a zero test. Forming this normalization takes $O(\log N)$ group operations per point and still leaves the zero test. The quadratic marker construction works for any $N$.

## Smaller special cases and the cost still charged

An individual address bit already has a simpler test on a singleton. Restrict its original slot to points whose bit is one, then test ordinary incidence over $\mathbb F_p$. Likewise $m$ independent tests of the local challenge bits can recover the full Walsh parity on a singleton. The tagged construction compiles the whole parity condition into one incidence even when several original relations exist.

If the cubic $g$ has a nonrational root $\alpha$, there is an arity- and width-preserving alternative. The nonrational order-two point $Q=(\alpha,0)$ is defined over an extension of degree at most three. The subgroup it generates meets $G$ only at the identity. Translate each point to $P+[c_i(P)]Q$. A test at $T$ now selects the even relations directly and a test at $T+Q$ selects the odd ones. Regularity follows by comparing points up to sign and reducing modulo $\langle Q\rangle$. For $Q=(\alpha,0)$ the point translation uses $x' = \alpha+g'(\alpha)/(x-\alpha)$ and $y'=-g'(\alpha)y/(x-\alpha)^2$. When all two-torsion is rational the quadratic marker construction above applies unchanged.

Building the transformed slots from the public sampled slots needs $O(mw\,\operatorname{poly}(b,m))$ bit operations for $|F_i|\le2w$, as does listing the input slots from their hash seeds by enumerating their $w$ addresses and solving the curve equation. Marker sampling costs expected polynomial time. The strengthened last slot is a union of translates and retains its original labels. The transformed abscissas in general do not form dyadic masks in the quadratic field. A hypothetical cheaper incidence algorithm must accept or exploit this representation.

Building balanced half-sum lists and intersecting them costs at most $O(m(2w)^{\lceil m/2\rceil}\operatorname{poly}(b,m))$, including the enlarged slot. A direct enumeration of the field determinants also remains expensive. Conversely a Boolean evaluator exact on positive and negative transformed grids would already solve the original ordinary existence problem by taking the all-zero challenge, even with the even-weight set $J$ fixed. This observation applies to a full incidence evaluator. The reduction gives no improvement over $b/(\log b)^{1/4}$ in the logarithm of end-to-end time.
