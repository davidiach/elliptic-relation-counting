# Sparse public moduli admit local quotient recovery

For a fixed number of nonzero signed radix limbs in the prime $p$, the arithmetic check can keep just the raw output transform. Quotient digits are recovered inside the existing coefficient checks. Only digits shared between different groups of processors need edges. In the fixed-bit slot construction this reduces the leading Boolean word budget from $30mb$ to $20mb$ under the same contraction assumptions.

## Scope

Let $p$ be a $b$-bit odd prime and use the project's main limb radix $B=2^k$, $k=\Theta((\log b)^2)$. Suppose
$$
p=\sum_{d\in S}p_d B^d,\qquad
0\in S,\quad |p_d|<B,\quad |S|\le s,\quad
\max S\le\lceil b/k\rceil+O(1),
$$
for a bitlength-independent $s$. This includes primes specified by boundedly many signed powers of two when these give a bounded-support limb presentation. The shifts need no particular alignment with processor indices. One may supply the digits or find a minimum-support presentation by scanning the canonical radix digits with a two-state carry. If the ordinary digit is $v$, choose the next carry $c'\in\{0,1\}$ and the signed digit $v+c-Bc'$ of magnitude below $B$. Start and finish with zero carry, allowing one extra top digit, and minimize the number of nonzero digits. This enumerates all allowed representations because the carry out of such a digit can only be zero or one.

The previous coverage, enumeration and guard results still apply. The sparse condition concerns the public modulus. It does not add a probability cost or give a distributional claim about primes. [Fixed-bit construction and starting ledger](../strided_slots/results.md)

## A local, unique certificate

For one of the C38 arithmetic equations let $D$ denote the signed integer obtained by evaluating its canonical coordinate limbs. The inherited bound is $|D|<8p^2$. At the transform middle evaluate the original quadratic and add the public shift $8p^2$, omitting the old quotient term entirely. Let $E_i$ be the integer coefficients of this result. The previous ring width of $2k+O(\log b)$ suffices because the same quadratic coefficient bound applies. Put $E=\sum_i E_i B^i=D+8p^2$. Thus $0<E<16p^2$, and $p\mid E$ exactly when $p\mid D$.

Any valid quotient fits $n_q=\lceil(b+4)/k\rceil$ ordinary radix digits. Force $q_i=0$ for $i<0$ and $i\ge n_q$. Allow at least enough terminal zero positions that both the raw output and $p(B)q(B)$ have degree below the processing length. The original implementation has padding. If needed, append $O(1)$ local zero positions beyond the largest degree, without adding transform words. With $c_0=0$, the equation at position $i$ is
$$
E_i+c_i-\sum_{d\in S}p_d q_{i-d}=B c_{i+1}.
$$
For $0\le i<n_q$, all terms except $p_0q_i$ on the left are known from the current and earlier positions. Oddness of $p_0$ gives exactly one digit $0\le q_i<B$ by reducing modulo $B$. Then $c_{i+1}$ is determined by integer division. At subsequent positions the processor checks divisibility by $B$ with the forced digit zero. Finally it requires terminal carry zero. Multiplying by $B^i$ and summing proves acceptance implies $E=pq$. Conversely the ordinary digits of $E/p$ satisfy the recurrence whenever the integer quotient exists. This also proves unique extension to the auxiliary messages after any consistent pins.

Writing $M_E$ for the public per-coefficient bound, the induction
$$
|c_i|\le 1+\left\lfloor\frac{M_E+(B-1)\sum_d|p_d|}{B-1}\right\rfloor
$$
gives carry widths $O(k+\log b)$. This uses $M_E=\mathrm{poly}(b)B^2$. Canonical signed encodings prevent multiplicity.

## Locality for arbitrary positions of the nonzero limbs

As before, inverse Fourier row $r$ recovers the raw coefficients at positions $i\equiv r\bmod V$, with $V=\Theta(\sqrt b/\log b)$. Merge closure rows using the graph on $\mathbb Z/V\mathbb Z$ generated by $+1$ and by $+d$ for $d\in S\setminus\{0\}$, with reversals. Write $g\le s$ for the number of these generators. We need clusters of polylogarithmic size that cut $o(V)$ directed generator edges in total, counting each positive step separately.

Take $R=\lceil(\log b)^2\rceil$. Inside any induced subgraph a radius-$R$ ball has at most $(2R+1)^g$ vertices, since every path endpoint is a sum of signed generator steps. Starting at any remaining vertex there is a $j<R$ with
$$
\frac{|B_{j+1}|-|B_j|}{|B_j|}
\le (2R+1)^{g/R}-1=O_s(\log R/R).
$$
Otherwise multiplying the successive growth factors contradicts the upper bound. Remove $B_j$ and repeat in the remaining graph. All edges from a removed ball to the unassigned vertices go through its outer boundary, which is incident to at most $2g$ edges per vertex (with multiplicities). Each cut edge is charged at its first endpoint's removal. Consequently the number of cut directed generator edges is $o(V)$. The largest cluster has at most $(2R+1)^g=\mathrm{polylog}(b)$ rows, and finding the partition takes polynomial time. This merging can start from the individual inverse rows. The single affine hash uses its own inherited closure or the same $+1$ boundary rule.

A cluster processes its absolute coefficient indices in increasing order. It reads an incoming digit $q_{i-d}$ or carry $c_i$ from a labeled port only when its owner is outside the cluster. The generating cluster independently recomputes and checks every outgoing value. For fixed boundary messages, inner digits and carries are deterministic. Globally, induction in the absolute index gives exactly the unique sequential trace just proved. Each cut directed edge is used $O(1+L_F/V)$ times per equation. Sending each crossing word through degree-two relays, one per digit or bit, preserves bipartiteness and parallel-multiplicity bounds at cost $O_s(b\log R/R)$ Boolean edges per equation. This remains $o(b)$ even after multiplication by the inherited binary alphabet factor $O(\log\log b)$. Range and nonzero checks on the original sources continue as before. Merging a polylogarithmic number of processors increases their degrees and pair multiplicities by at most that factor. The full degree remains $\sqrt b\,\mathrm{polylog}(b)$.

## Complete coefficient and remaining limitation

In the old fixed-bit ledger, coordinates and slopes, shifted quotient inputs, and arithmetic outputs each contributed $(10+o(1))mb$ Boolean words measured in bits. The new middle and closure omit every quotient input transform. They transmit the same number of raw arithmetic output words, with the same width. The shared quotient digits and new carries contribute $o(mb)$, so the total is $(20+o(1))mb$. Hash and range auxiliaries are lower order as in the earlier construction. The exact verifying equations above are unconditional. Converting the compiled graph into an expected running time uses the supplied finite-packet Assumption A.1 and inherited contraction, witness extraction and training estimates.

Let $K=2\pi\sqrt{14\log_2 90}/(9\ln 2)$. The relation coefficient is $10K$ in $mb/\sqrt{\log_2 b}$. Balancing $b/m+10Kmb/\sqrt{\log_2 b}$ against $2b/m$ gives a time coefficient $2\sqrt{10K}=19.597926494309$ and space coefficient $\sqrt{10K}=9.798963247154$ in $b/(\log_2 b)^{1/4}$. This quantifies the improvement on the stated subclass. The power of the logarithm is unchanged. The proof supplies no bounded-support representation for arbitrary primes.

## Reproduction

Run the following from the project root.

~~~sh
python -B checks/check_sparse.py
~~~

The check compares the distributed closure with ordinary integer divisibility on 1,500 examples over five public primes. The raw coefficient arrays include randomized signed carry redistributions. All 755 divisible examples recovered the exact quotient, 17 changed cross-cluster quotient labels and 41 changed carry labels were rejected, and three different owner graphs met the cluster-size and boundary inequalities. Two additional C38 fixtures run the old quadratic middle with the quotient inputs set to zero, restore its actual raw integer coefficients, and test the distributed closure. Across the original and modified coordinate choices, 37 outputs were divisible and eight were rejected after changing slopes. The original valid choices recovered the exact previous shifted quotients. These are checks of the arithmetic interfaces and their port assignments. [Executable checks](../../checks/check_sparse.py)
