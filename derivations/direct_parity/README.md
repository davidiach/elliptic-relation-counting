# Direct evaluation of the signed zero count

**21 November 2026.** On a regular isolated relation, $M_0(r)-M_{p-1}(r)$ is the sign of the witness address under the binary challenge $r$. We give exact ways to evaluate it without reconstructing a moment recurrence. The most useful reduction makes checking a proposed elliptic section polynomial in the bit length and number of slots, independently of their width. Finding that section from all the seeds at a competitive cost remains open. The project's best conditional log-time bound for general primes in the inherited input class is still $(24.00245996+o(1))b/(\log_2 b)^{1/4}$. [Baseline and outer costs](../sampled_slots/README.md)

## Where the cost went

| Approach | What can be computed | Cost or limitation |
|---|---|---|
| [One coefficient and a section test](coefficient.md) | The signed zero count equals the product of the signed slot sizes minus one coefficient of a product of slot polynomials. Equivalently, on a regular singleton exactly one projective section contributes the witness sign. Given that section, factoring one degree-at-most-$m+1$ polynomial and testing its rational points against the seeds recovers the whole tuple. | When one slot is known only through its incidences with proposed sections, even constant parity correlation on successful roots needs at least $2^{(1-1/m)b-O(m+\log m)}$ queries. A quantitative tradeoff also allows a challenge-independent message computed from every seed. |
| [Exact graded kernel](algebra.md) | A one-dimensional kernel retrieves all address parities together. Its first nonzero degree, omitting slot $j$, is exactly $\sum_{i\ne j}(|F_i|-1)$. | At that degree the quotient already has dimension $\prod_{i\ne j}|F_i|$, typically at least $2^{(1-1/m)b-O(m\log m)}$ on successful roots. The equations themselves have short factorizations. |
| [One scalar equality](scalar_join.md) | One random projection preserves every cross-half equality and every pinned parity with probability $1-O(m^{-2})$ at the thinned widths. A singleton gives a final equality matrix of rank one. | An explicit half-list join takes up to $2^{b/2+O(b/m+m+\log b)}$ bit operations. Forward polynomial powering of the scalar difference has an intermediate of rank at least $2^{b/2-o(b)}$ on typical singleton roots, if intermediate matrices are represented by explicit separated factors. |
| [Fourier sampling](fiber.md) | Group-character orthogonality factors the exact signed count across slots. The independent uniform estimator has one-draw mean squared error $D-1$ on a singleton with $D$ legal tuples. | Even challenge-dependent optimal importance sampling needs $2^{b-O(m\log m)}$ independent draws for constant mean squared error on typical singleton roots. The paper also treats sign prediction from a common ordering or common threshold features. |
| [Sparse Fourier combinations](projection.md) | On a prime-order curve the exact minimum number of complex group-character terms on a realized singleton is the number of distinct represented sums. | Over general prime fields, for each fixed $\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)$, normalized linear combinations of at most $2^{(1/2-\varepsilon)b}$ terms have exponentially small conditional witness correlation in the stated sampler regime. The chosen terms and coefficients can depend on every seed and the target, and must be fixed before the challenge. |

The section test isolates a concrete remaining question. Can one compute the signed contribution of the common section directly from the factored slot equations and the actual hash words? The direct coefficient formula allows such an algorithm to output only a sign. A useful approximate alternative would give a cheaply evaluated bounded score with enough parity correlation for verified recovery under the same seed and target law. Neither the section search bound nor the rank and Fourier bounds cover unrestricted nonlinear seed arithmetic. [Recovery criterion](../sampled_slots/address_parity.md)

## Finite checks

- [Coefficient and section check](../../evidence/check_projective_coefficient.txt). Direct coefficient extraction agrees with determinants on 160 signed grids. On a regular $\mathbb F_{13}$ singleton, factorizations of all 183 sections identify one witness section, and every one of the 64 parity challenges agrees with the coefficient calculation.
- [Graded kernel](../../evidence/check_direct_algebra.txt) and [scalar join](../../evidence/check_scalar_join.txt). The kernel test covers three- and four-slot fixtures, including colliding partial sections and an empty target. On a separate four-slot fixture, projected joins agree with the direct determinant test for all 256 challenges on both an empty target and a singleton.
- [Fourier sampling](../../evidence/check_direct_fiber.txt) and [target-specific projections](../../evidence/check_direct_projection.txt). The importance-sampling comparison on 384 legal tuples gives one-draw mean squared errors of 383 for uniform sampling and 109.559381 with the optimal proposal. The projection check independently compares the Gram identities with explicit address sums.

These small-field experiments check the identities and constructions. The asymptotic cost and correlation bounds use the arguments in the linked notes.
