# Searching whole families of sections

A pencil is a projective line of sections through the prescribed endpoint. Its $p+1$ sections can be tested together using $O(w\,\operatorname{poly}(b,m))$ expected bit operations. A rational curve of parameters or a higher-dimensional flat can also be tested in batches. The resulting saving applies inside a specified family. We bound how often a family can contain the witness, including some families whose coefficients use the final slot seed.

## Batch evaluation on a pencil

Write a member of a pencil as $us_0+vs_1$, with $[u:v]\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p)$ and independent sections $s_0,s_1\in L((m+1)O)$ through $-T$. For a retained point $P$ use its elementary evaluation pair $(s_0(P),s_1(P))$. If both entries vanish then $P$ lies on every member. Otherwise it lies on exactly one, at parameter $[-s_1(P):s_0(P)]$. For slot $i$ sum the Walsh weights of its common points into $B_i$ and those of its other points by parameter into $C_i(z)$. With $S$ the union of parameters appearing in the lists, the signed sum over the entire pencil is exactly

$$
(p+1-|S|)\prod_i B_i+\sum_{z\in S}\prod_i(B_i+C_i(z)).
$$

There are at most $2mw$ entries before aggregation, and sorting by field coordinates suffices. This formula also works with overlaps and on grids with several relations. Taking the field residue counts incidences with this pencil; the unrestricted parity on a regular singleton is recovered if the pencil contains its section. Repeated projective parameters must be handled according to the quantity being summed.

For a regular grid there is another algorithm which expands only **one** slot. Two independent sections cannot share $m$ distinct zeros. Removing those zeros from their divisor of degree $m+1$ would leave two independent sections in degree one on a genus-one curve, contrary to Riemann–Roch. Since $-T$ is already common, at most $m-2$ distinct other points are common. Among any $m-1$ of the pairwise disjoint slots there is one containing no common point. To identify one, factor a single section, check its zeros against the other section, then test the seeds on those points. Expand that slot. Each of its points supplies one parameter; at each candidate parameter factor the section and test the seeds directly. The number of tested sections is at most $2w$. This works with an empty slot as well. [Testing a single section](coefficient.md)

The same procedure applies to a basepoint-free parameterization of a nonconstant curve of sections by homogeneous polynomials of degree $e$ on $\mathbb P^1$. Find common curve points using one section and the coefficient forms of the parameterization. Choose a slot disjoint from them. For each retained point in that slot the equation for its parameter is a nonzero binary form of degree $e$. Finite-field root finding, including the point at infinity, gives at most $2ew$ candidate parameters. Evaluate the remaining slot tests at the distinct resulting sections. For an explicitly supplied map of degree $e$ the cost is $w\,\operatorname{poly}(e,b,m)$, and it covers the parameter values defined over the base field.

For a supplied linear flat of projective dimension $d$, choose a slot with no point common to the whole flat. Intersect the flat with each of that slot's at most $2w$ evaluation hyperplanes, and recurse on the resulting flats of dimension $d-1$. A slot already chosen along a path contains a common point of the child flat, so it will never be chosen again. Linear algebra describes each child. Common points are found by factoring any one section and checking the other basis sections. On regular grids this takes at most $(2w)^d\operatorname{poly}(b,m)$ expected bit operations to find the witness if it is in the flat. At a leaf all slots can be tested from their seeds. For $d=1$ this recovers the pencil algorithm.

## How much can a family cover?

Freeze any $m-1$ visible slots which are mutually regular and avoid the target abscissa. Let $h$ be their largest size, with $h\ge1$. Each transversal of these slots together with $-T$ determines a unique section. Call the resulting set of distinct sections $\mathcal P$. Every member accounts for at most two transversals: the $m$ required distinct zeros leave room for only one extra zero, so at most one visible slot can offer two choices. If a visible slot is empty the candidate set is empty.

**Degree bound.** An irreducible projective family $Y$ of dimension $d$ and degree $\Delta$ over the algebraic closure contains at most $\Delta h^d$ members of $\mathcal P$. For a union of components, sum the bounds for the individual components.

For $d=0$ use the degree bound for points. For $d>0$ take two independent sections in $Y$. The preceding common-zero argument gives a visible slot containing no point common to $Y$. Every eligible section is in one of that slot's at most $h$ hyperplanes. No such hyperplane contains $Y$, so its intersection with $Y$ has dimension $d-1$ and its components have total degree at most $\Delta$. Apply induction to every component and sum. This argument covers singular varieties and counts distinct sections, including ones on multiple components. The use of hyperplane intersection degree is the usual projective Bézout bound. [Milne, *Algebraic Geometry*, chapter on projective varieties](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/AG.pdf)

In particular even a family designed from **all** the seeds that covers an $\eta$ fraction of the visible transversals must have $\Delta h^d\ge(\eta/2)\prod_{\rm visible}|F_i|$. Covering only the successful transversal is a weaker demand.

**Fixed-target success.** Suppose a rule can use those visible seeds and $T$ to choose a family of degree $\Delta$ and dimension $d$, and the last hidden slot retains each prescribed point with marginal probability $\rho=w/2^b$. Given a candidate section, a regular full witness can occur only when it has *exactly one* zero from each visible slot and a further point available for the missing slot. A second visible zero would use up the last place in the divisor and could not belong to the missing slot on a regular root. Thus each candidate forces at most one possible hidden point. For regular guarded singleton probability $q$,

$$
\Pr(\text{witness section belongs to }Y\mid U_{\rm reg})
\le \min(1,\Delta h_{\max}^d\,\rho/q),\qquad h_{\max}=2w .
$$

The bound sums over families. It also applies on the all-empty path of an adaptive search whose only hidden-slot feedback is the list of regular full witnesses inside the family. If the bounded parity score on that path uses only the visible seeds, the absolute conditional Walsh correlation is at most $\sqrt{\rho/q}+2(\rho/q)\sum_j\Delta_j(2w)^{d_j}$. This follows by the empty-transcript Parseval argument for [single sections](coefficient.md). Revealing the whole list of *separate* incidences of the hidden slot with all sections in a family is a stronger oracle.

**Arithmetic access to the last seed.** A family is allowed to change with the last seed if all its possible sections, as that seed varies with the other seeds fixed, lie in a visible-seed-dependent algebraic variety $Y$. Apply the preceding containment bound to $Y$. For example, suppose a proposed section is a rational function of one field word derived from the last seed, with numerator coordinates after a common denominator of degree at most $e$. All its defined values lie on a projective curve of degree at most $e$ (or at a point), by intersecting the image with a generic hyperplane and counting roots in the parameter. That word may itself be an arbitrary function of every seed. For $J$ branches with such curves, success is at most $J\max(1,e)(2w)\rho/q$. The image must include every possible output; exceptional branches or separately supplied values are charged as additional components.

More generally, use $d$ family parameters and $s$ additional field words from joint seed arithmetic, and let $k=d+s$. For a single rational coordinate map of total degree at most $e\ge1$ in those $k$ inputs, the closure of the image has dimension $r\le k$ and degree at most $e^r$. Indeed pull back $r$ generic hyperplanes and restrict to a generic $r$-dimensional linear slice of the parameter space over the algebraic closure. This slice meets the generic fibers in the domain of definition. Each of the image's degree-many generic intersection points contributes at least one isolated preimage on the slice; Bézout gives at most $e^r$ such solutions even with a base locus. Therefore the conditional chance of exhibiting the witness with $J$ such maps is at most $J(2we)^k\rho/q$. At the thinned widths $\log_2 w=b/m+O(1)$ and $\log_2 q^{-1}=O(m+\log m)$. When $k=o(m)$ and $\log_2 J+k\log_2 e=o(b)$ this bound is $2^{-b+o(b)}$. This is a bound on exhibiting a section through the specified maps. Direct sign calculations, maps of high algebraic degree but low evaluation cost, and richer seed-dependent branching remain open.

## Using the actual anchor translation in a parity decision

There is also a direct prediction bound for a different way of using *all* the seeds. Reveal the affine anchor multiplier $\alpha$, all the other slots, the target and the challenge. The remaining raw word is the uniform translation $\beta\in[0,2^b)$. Allow the predictor to use $\beta\bmod p$ through an adaptive tree of polynomial equality tests over $\mathbb F_p$. Coefficients, choice of tests and bounded leaf scores may depend arbitrarily on the revealed data and past answers. They may even depend on the challenge. Suppose the sum of degrees of the nonzero polynomials on the generic path is at most $D_0$ for every such choice. On this path an identically zero polynomial gives the answer true and any other polynomial gives false. Then

$$
\mathbb E_{\sigma\mid U_{\rm reg}}
  \left|\mathbb E_r f_\sigma(r)(-1)^{r\cdot a_*}\right|
\le \sqrt{\rho/q}+\frac{4D_0}{2^b q}.
$$

Indeed the union of the exceptional roots on the path has cardinality at most $D_0$. Each field residue has at most two representatives in the uniform word domain, so a fixed challenge departs from the generic path with probability at most $2D_0/2^b$. Each departure changes a bounded score by at most two. On the generic path the score is independent of $\beta$. Given $\alpha$ and the other masks, a full address fixes all tail points, hence forces one anchor point. Its inclusion probability over $\beta$ is $\rho$. The empty-transcript Parseval argument again gives the first term. For weak correlation $2^{-o(b)}$ one needs $D_0\ge 2^{b-o(b)}$ at the thinned widths. The bound also covers reading out equality to one from a polynomial field expression, by charging the degree of the expression minus one. Modular bit extraction, ordered comparisons and quadratic-character decisions are different readouts. Repeated squaring can attain high degree in few multiplications, so a degree bound alone gives no circuit-size lower bound.

## Finite evidence

The checker compares 11,712 signed pencil sums, including common points, with literal projective sums over $\mathbb F_{13}$. Over $\mathbb F_{101}$ it tests every pencil against an independent list of partial transversals. Four candidate sections suffice for every pencil on the tested masks. Among 8,192 affine anchor seeds with visible slots fixed, 1,196 give a regular guarded singleton. The best fixed pencil covers 442 of those seeds; the counting bound is 512. The section chosen on a smooth conic using both affine seed words is also tested against its full set of possible sections. These checks concern the particular finite fixtures.
