\paragraph{Scope and outcome.}
The complete binary-packet polynomial space is full in the certified
cases $L=2,4,8$. More significantly, at $L=8$ it is already full for a
fixed, explicitly pinned C38 source predicate with arbitrary source
assignments. Thus a universal smaller polynomial space cannot be
inferred just from the fact that the tensors arise from that predicate.
We also solve the distinct problem of recovering only parity on the
integer-generated family. At $L=8$ its optimal geometric precision
scale is $102$, the same as the full-space exact readout from checkpoint
008. The lower certificate applies to the specified observation model,
not to algorithms that inspect or exploit the input tensors directly.
No complete C38 graph contraction or ECDLP exponent improvement follows.

\section{Literal polynomial families}
For $L=2^a$, write
\[
 C(T)=\prod_{t=-L}^{L-1}(T-t),\qquad c_0=C'(0),\qquad
 W_\delta(T)=\frac{C(T)}{c_0(T-\delta)},\quad -L<\delta<L.
\]
For a source assignment $X\in\{0,1\}^L$ put
$s_x(T)=\sum_y W_{y-x}(T)X_y$. A target indicator assignment $Y$
produces precisely
\begin{equation}
 F_{X,Y}(T)=\prod_{x=0}^{L-1}s_x(T)^{Y_x}
                                      (1-s_x(T))^{1-Y_x}.
 \label{eq:literal}
\end{equation}
This is the literal binary specialization of the simplex selectors in
the native contractor, with arbitrary source and target indicator
tensors. It has degree at most $D=L(2L-1)$ and
$F_{X,Y}(0)=\mathbf1_{X=Y}$.
Its numerator $G_{X,Y}=c_0^L F_{X,Y}$ is an integer polynomial.
The sum over all $Y$ is one before clearing the denominator.
In particular, $X=Y=0$ gives the constant one: there is no universal
factor $T+L$ for the complete deformation, even though each $W$ has it.

For the C38 family, the inherited construction pins the actual first
source predicate at public parameters $p=13$, arithmetic length $8$,
and four-bit limbs, leaving independent lower quotient bits. It gives
the verified equality predicate
\[
 P(Y)=\prod_{(x,z)\in\mathcal P}\mathbf1_{Y_x=Y_z}.
\]
The source assignment $X$ then gives
\begin{equation}
 F_X(T)=\prod_{(x,z)\in\mathcal P}
        (1-s_x(T)-s_z(T)+2s_x(T)s_z(T)).
 \label{eq:copy}
\end{equation}
The factor two is retained. This is derived by summing the actual
accepted target assignments in \eqref{eq:literal}, not by postulating a
new predicate. The audit regenerates the pins, evaluates every Boolean
assignment of the surviving ports, and compares the resulting jets
against the native selector implementation.

All vertices receive equal label words for this local test. The native
packetizer makes one complete packet; its deterministic order gives
\[
 \begin{array}{c|l}
 L&\mathcal P\\\hline
 2&(0,1)\\
 4&(0,2),(3,1)\\
 8&(0,2),(7,6),(4,1),(5,3).
 \end{array}
\]
This is a legal complete local packing, not the asymptotic balanced
packing schedule. Arbitrary weights on the source assignments are
realized by a joint source tensor adjacent to this fixed predicate.
They are not asserted to be the source tensors of an unrestricted
full C38 relation instance.

\section{Exact rank certificates}
The rational span can be certified using integer numerators.
The saved labels specify a square coefficient minor whose determinant
is nonzero modulo $1000000007$. The audit verifies primality by trial
division, rebuilds the polynomials, and computes the determinant with
an independent elimination routine. A nonzero modular determinant is
an exact rational-rank lower bound. The number of generators or the
ambient polynomial degree gives the matching upper bound.
\[
 \begin{array}{c|r|r|r}
 L&D+1&\operatorname{rank}\{F_{X,Y}\}&
                    \operatorname{rank}\{F_X\}\\\hline
 2&7&7&4\\
 4&29&29&16\\
 8&121&121&121.
 \end{array}
\]
For $L=2,4$ the generic family is also checked exhaustively by exact
integer elimination. For $L=8$, the selected $121$ legal generators
suffice to prove the full rank; exhaustive generation is unnecessary
for verification. The C38 source family has $2^L$ generators, all of
which are generated and checked in these cases.

\begin{proposition}
For every positive integer $k$, products of $k$ independent complete
size-eight packets of the certified C38 predicate family span all
rational polynomials of degree at most $120k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
One packet spans every monomial $T^j$ for $0\le j\le120$ by the full
rank certificate. For $0\le d\le120k$, write $d=\sum_{i=1}^k d_i$
with $0\le d_i\le120$. Expand each $T^{d_i}$ in its packet family and
multiply. Products of the underlying legal source assignments occur
among the independent packet inputs. Conversely all such products
have degree at most $120k$.
\end{proof}
This is a fixed-packet-width extension, not a full-rank theorem for all
growing $L$. It concerns disjoint products of predicate restrictions,
not a newly solved full C38 network. A rational-span certificate also
does not imply that every polynomial has an integral combination of
the generators; those denominators matter for modular-only recovery.

\section{The parity problem on an integer-generated family}
Let $F_1,\ldots,F_N$ be the fixed rational generator polynomials, each
with integral values at zero and integral dyadic jets at the chosen
centers and local scales. Inputs are
\[
 F=\sum_{k=1}^N z_k F_k,\qquad z\in\mathbb Z^N.
\]
The goal is $F(0)\bmod2$, from jet row $r=(\tau,j)$ known modulo
$2^{p_r}$, where $p_r=1+b_\tau$.
The geometric profile is $b_\tau=\lfloor\lambda/2^{v_2(\tau)}\rfloor$.

The implementation clears the common dyadic part of $c_0^L$ while
retaining its odd part $o$. The integer jet matrix $E$ then equals
$o$ times the actual integral jet matrix. Multiplication by $o$ is
invertible at every precision, so this changes neither feasibility nor
the available information. Write $c_k=F_k(0)\bmod2$.

Set $B=\max b_\tau$, $K=B+1$, and
\[
 S_r=2^{B-b_\tau}E_r\pmod {2^K}.
\]
The desired certificate is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_r \lambda_r S_r\equiv2^B c\pmod {2^{B+1}}.
 \label{eq:parity}
\end{equation}
It yields weights $\lambda_r/2^{b_\tau}$ on the odd-normalized jets,
or $o\lambda_r/2^{b_\tau}$ on the original jets. Ambiguity in each
observed jet changes the answer by an even integer. On a legal input,
the combined numerator is divisible by $2^B$ and its quotient gives
the desired parity. Individual weighted terms need not be integral.

Capped Smith elimination over $\mathbb Z/2^K$ gives
$USV=\operatorname{diag}(2^{\nu_1},\ldots,2^{\nu_r},0,\ldots)$,
with $\nu_i<K$; rectangular and rank-deficient matrices are permitted.
The transformed target $2^B cV$ has entries either zero or $2^B$.
Every nonzero diagonal coordinate can therefore be solved. Feasibility
is equivalent to $cV$ being even in each zero-diagonal column.

If infeasible, choose such a column $z$ of an integer lift of $V$.
It satisfies
\begin{equation}
 Ez\equiv0\pmod {2^{1+b_\tau}}\text{ on row }(\tau,j),
 \qquad cz\equiv1\pmod2.
 \label{eq:collision}
\end{equation}
Thus $z$ and the zero input have identical prescribed jet observations
but different target parities. This excludes any readout, including a
nonlinear one, \emph{from those observations on the integer-generated
family}. It does not exclude using additional information about the
input weights or restricting them to a smaller promised set.
Upper certificates and collisions are checked directly without
repeating the elimination. Binary search is valid because increasing
$\lambda$ only supplies more bits.

\section{Certified finite results}
Use degree $L(2L-1)$ and local scale $2^{a+1}$. For $L=4,8$, the
standard triangular profile is
$q_\tau=\lceil L/(a-1)\rceil(a-v_2(\tau))$.
For $L=2$, use four jets at each of $-1,1$.
The following entries are denominator-loss/profile scales; actual jet
precisions are $1+\lfloor\lambda/2^{v_2(\tau)}\rfloor$.
\[
 \begin{array}{c|l|r|r|r}
 L&\text{family}&\text{full Hermite}&
       \text{optimal exact full space}&\text{optimal family parity}\\\hline
 2&\text{generic}&10&9&9\\
 2&\text{C38 copy}&10&9&4\\
 4&\text{generic}&45&27&27\\
 4&\text{C38 copy}&45&27&8\\
 8&\text{C38 copy}&128&102&102.
 \end{array}
\]
The smaller copy cases have proper rational spans, so their savings
cannot be attributed to coefficient-lattice information alone. The
size-eight case has both full span and a parity collision at the next
smaller precision profile. It closes both finite loopholes there.
For example, at scale $101$ the size-eight lower certificate has zero
observations modulo $2^{102}$ at odd centers, $2^{51}$ at valuation-one
centers, and $2^{26}$ at valuation-two centers, but an odd value at zero.
Scale $102$ is feasible and certified.

A separate positive control distinguishes the two mathematical models.
For generators $1,2T$, the rational span is all linear polynomials, but
one bit at $T=1$ recovers the constant modulo two. Replacing $2T$ by
$T$, and supplying the first scaled derivative with scale $2$, requires
two bits. The audit certifies both outcomes. It also tests random
integer combinations with independent changes of jet representatives
by multiples of their observation moduli.

\paragraph{Costs and limits.}
Generating the generic family costs $4^L$ polynomial entries; the copy
family uses $2^L$. Explicit matrix construction, capped elimination,
polynomial coefficient heights, odd-unit normalization, and storage are
all real costs. This experiment has not provided a uniform efficient
construction at the packet sizes needed for the ECDLP recurrence.
The saved rank and parity certificates are finite, reproducible facts,
not evidence of an asymptotic speedup. Other predicate structure,
non-additive input promises, different observation sets and profiles,
and representations avoiding the mode catalogue remain open.
The square-root-in-$b$ general-prime-field ECDLP target remains unmet.
