# Can nonlinear signed moments find the correct tuple?

**20 November 2026.** A combination of the first two signed moments distinguishes the correct tuple on a small exact population. We found no nonlinear readout with a proved advantage at growing arity large enough to improve the general-prime log-time bound $(24.00245996+o(1))b/(\log_2 b)^{1/4}$. That bound uses the previously derived mixed sampler, finite-packet Assumption A.1 and the inherited contraction and outer-reduction estimates. [Baseline and cost assumptions](../sampled_slots/README.md)

## What the finite gain amounts to

On a root with one guarded relation, let $v_*$ be its binary address. Under a random challenge $r$, the desired sign is $(-1)^{r\cdot v_*}$. For each bounded score we measure its absolute Walsh coefficient at $v_*$ and at every other legal address on the same root. The wrong-address average gives each root equal weight under the specified conditional law. The paired rank is the probability that the correct coefficient magnitude exceeds that of a random wrong legal tuple, counting a tie as one half.

* **Exact optimization.** On $\mathbb F_{13}$ at target $2G$, the optimal lookup on the nine joint quadratic-character values of $(M_1,M_2)$ has mean absolute witness coefficient **0.144509**, compared with **0.119782** for the best lookup using either character alone. The same joint lookup gives **0.103856** on wrong legal tuples. At target $G$ the optimal joint values are **0.102992** at the witness and **0.103175** at wrong addresses. Both statements exhaust the 8,192 weighted roots at that target, then condition on unique guarded hits. [Exact optimization and controls](collisions.md)
* **Larger held-out populations.** Over $\mathbb F_{65521}$ at six slots, the fixed score $\chi(M_2+M_1^2)$ gave paired ranks **0.5003** at $G$ and **0.4963** at $2G$, each with standard error **0.0041**, in independent samples of 5,000 guarded singletons per target. A separately fitted joint score gave ranks 0.4949 and 0.4933 on four- and five-slot trials over $\mathbb F_{1031}$. These finite measurements cannot resolve very small asymptotic advantages. [Score construction, sampling and results](probe.md)
* **Using the raw field residues.** A field-residue table can assign scores to dense regions of the two-dimensional output space. Isolating the part of a fitted table that depends on both coordinates gave ranks **0.5055, 0.4975, 0.4947 and 0.4985** across two targets at each of four and six slots. Each evaluation used 1,600 fresh guarded singletons. The corresponding paired coefficient-magnitude gaps were all at most 0.00010 in absolute value. The algebraic bounds below leave such dense deterministic readouts open. [Residue rules, training and controls](dense_residues.md)
* **Removing ordinate-sign cancellation.** A uniform sign guess matches on a fraction $2^{-m}$ of isolated roots, an affordable factor at the proposed arities. Evaluating moments directly on the matched sign grid also failed to give a reproducible distinction. In six-slot width-four trials, the median restricted grid contained 144 legal tuples. We tested translation of the address origin to remove a symmetry that can force every legal coefficient to vanish. [Pinned signs and recentering](pinned.md)

## What we can prove

| Question | Result |
|---|---|
| Can a weak nonlinear signal be measured without finding a fresh witness? | Two challenges on one planted mark measure a signed collision energy. If that energy has a uniform lower bound $\gamma$, a random binary encoding of the response followed by verified Walsh recovery succeeds on fresh roots with probability $\Omega(\lambda\gamma^2)$ under the thinned sampler. The estimator and reduction account for roots with multiple tuples. [Criterion and transfer proof](collisions.md) |
| How much can hashing or a sparse lookup on full field values reveal? | On $n$ uniform eligible targets, a vector of all raw moments through order $K$ has mean singleton-weighted collision energy at most $(1+(m+1)K)/n$. Even tables tailored to the seed, target and witness cannot attain the desired advantage if only $2^{o(b)}$ raw signatures differ from a constant default and $(m+1)K=2^{o(b)}$. [Collision bound](collisions.md) |
| Does trying many random nonlinear coefficients help? | A random shift followed by a quadratic character obeys the same squared bound for a field expression of bounded target pole degree. Varying the relative coefficient of $M_1^2$ in $M_2+\alpha M_1^2$ leaves an uncontrolled term on $M_1=0$. The added signal on $M_1\ne0$ stays exponentially small even after choosing the best of $2^{o(b)}$ independently sampled coefficients. A fixed exceptional coefficient is still possible. [Character-mixture proof](character_mixtures.md) |
| Can a nonlinear decoder recover the exact address parity? | On regular singletons it equals $M_0-M_{p-1}$ in the field. A scalar recurrence gives an exact alternative. On almost all singleton roots at the thinned widths, its **mean degree over the challenge** is at least $2^{b-O(m\log m)}$. The lower bound prices reconstructing that recurrence. [Exact decoder and actual-curve support proof](signed_spectrum.md) |
| How could one test a fixed, dense field character analytically? | A fourth-moment identity reduces an upper bound to the frequency of challenge quadruples whose product of target functions has no odd-order zero or pole. On one selected four-slot root all 196,096 such quadruples among 16,777,216 came from repeated pairs. The frequency on growing, freshly sampled roots remains unknown. [Character-sum argument and exact fixture](fourth_moment.md) |

Additional derivations cover adaptive tests of nonlinear polynomial equalities, the information lost by giving each point a fresh hidden random coefficient, and a control in which the residual values are randomly permuted. The last is a comparison model with a different law from an elliptic curve. ## End-to-end accounting

Let $w=2^{b/m+O(1)}$ be the dyadic width. Computing the signed first and second moments for one challenge costs $(w+2^m)\operatorname{poly}(b,m)$ expected bit operations by determinant multilinearity and the mixed Gram determinant formula. A uniformly computable score with absolute correlation at least $2^{-O(b/m)}$ on fresh guarded singletons, and with setup and query cost at most $2^{O(b/m)}$, would give complete log cost $O(b/m)$ for $m=O(\sqrt b)$ in the isolating regime. This includes list decoding, independent verification, seed retries, the factor table and the sparse solve. Taking $m\asymp(\log_2 b)^{1/4}\sqrt{\log_2\log_2 b}$ would already beat the existing scale. At $m\asymp\sqrt b$ it would meet the square-root-in-bitlength goal. [Verified extraction](../sampled_slots/address_parity.md) · [Outer reduction](../sampled_slots/README.md)

The per-query cost of the two moments meets that benchmark. The missing result is a lower bound on the useful signal of a specific, affordable dense readout as the field and arity grow, or a way to compute the signed zero multiplicity directly without reconstructing the spectrum. A near-chance ranking on a finite fixture gives no impossibility theorem for those approaches.
