# Primitive shared-translation relation polynomials, collective resultants, and exact inverse planting

Research checkpoint, 24 November 2026. Continues `shared_translation_note.md` (especially its degree identity D1, one-translation average coverage, and inverse query). This note proves an exact, exceptional-factor-free bivariate relation equation, an exact collective resultant representation, a cheap inverse planting law, and precise limitations of full-spectrum reconstruction and random-target inverse-only relation collection. It does **not** establish a faster forward evaluator or beat the general-prime log-time scale `b/(log b)^(1/4)`. The older coefficient 11.31486814 remains an unrevalidated conditional candidate. No novelty or peer-review claim is made.

## 1. Setting

Let `E/F_p: y²=f(x)=x³+ax+c` be nonsingular, p>3, N=#E(F_p)=hr, r odd prime, gcd(h,r)=1, h=poly(b), b=ceil(log2 p). Write `π=[h]`, whose rational image is the subgroup G of order r. Use one common translation A uniformly in F_p and public pairwise disjoint slots S_i. The cubics `f(A+s)`, for distinct offsets s in their union S, are pairwise coprime over the algebraic closure. The preceding checkpoint supplies a public slope search to ensure this, at cost `2^{O(b/m)}` for the balanced-width sampler. Exclude zero ordinates in the actual rational slots. Write `W=∏w_i`, w_i=|S_i|, and `λ=W/r`. For asymptotic statements take m→∞, m≤sqrt(b), log2 w_i=(1+o(1))b/m, and poly(b)^(-1)≤λ≤O(1).

For a tuple s=(s_1,...,s_m) in the Cartesian product, C_s is the smooth complete curve with function field

    F_p(A)(y_1,...,y_m),  y_i²=f(A+s_i).

Over the algebraic closure, disjoint simple branch loci imply independence of the square classes, giving a geometrically integral Galois cover of P¹_A with group (Z/2)^m. Define morphisms ρ_i:C_s→E and Φ_s=[h]∑ρ_i. From the degree identity D1 of the preceding checkpoint, for any nonzero integer coefficient vector c,

    deg([h]∑[c_i]ρ_i) = h² 2^(m-1) ∑c_i².       (1)

For completeness, the theorem of the square gives `deg(U+V)+deg(U-V)=2deg(U)+2deg(V)` using the symmetric divisor (O). A sign involution of C_s exchanges the two terms on the left for V=[c_i]ρ_i if U uses the other coordinates. Induction, with degρ_i=2^(m-1) and deg[n]=n², proves (1). No ordinary-curve or prime-order-full-group assumption is required. The proof is over the algebraic closure, and counts degrees with multiplicity.

## 2. Exact primitive bivariate relation polynomial

**Theorem 1.** For each address tuple s there is an absolutely irreducible polynomial F_s(A,X), unique up to a nonzero constant, defining the affine image of `(A, x(Φ_s))`. Its bidegrees are exactly

    deg_A F_s = h² m 2^(m-1),
    deg_X F_s = 2^(m-1).                           (2)

For any finite A_0 and X_0 in the algebraic closure, F_s(A_0,X_0)=0 iff there is a choice of the m algebraic ordinates at A_0+s_i whose projected sum is finite and has abscissa X_0. This includes legitimate additions through intermediate identities and doubling. It does not require those ordinates to be rational over F_p.

Proof. The stabilizer in the Galois group of x(Φ_s) is exactly the two-element group generated by simultaneous negation of all ordinates. Indeed, equality of x-coordinates of two curve-valued morphisms implies that they agree or are negatives as morphisms (over the generic point, the two y-coordinates differ by a sign). For a sign vector not all plus or all minus, either equality would make a nonzero signed subset sum, after multiplication by h, constant O. This contradicts (1). Thus the function field generated by A and x(Φ_s) is exactly the fixed field of simultaneous negation, of degree 2^(m-1) over the A-line. This proves the X degree and geometric irreducibility. The morphism x∘Φ_s on C_s has degree 2h²m2^(m-1). Factoring it through the degree-two quotient gives degree h²m2^(m-1) on the image's normalization. This proves the A degree. The image in P¹×P¹ is closed since C_s is complete; its finite chart is precisely the zero set of the primitive image equation. A finite A ensures all leaf abscissas are finite. At a finite branch point there is still a lift to the normalization: the disjoint simple branch loci give smooth affine fibers in the total curve. This proves the exact image criterion, including exceptional additions. For m=1,h=1 the equation is simply X-A-s_1. ∎

**Exact construction.** Use the mixed Jacobian addition formulas in `shared_translation_note.md` to construct (U,V,Z) for Φ_s, adding scalar multiplication by h using projective formulas. Compute

    Norm over flips of y_2,...,y_m of (Z² X - U).

The coefficients Z² and U are invariant under global negation: for the mixed addition formulas U,Z are unchanged and V changes sign; Jacobian doubling or addition preserves the property up to a sign on Z, so Z² and U are invariant. Alternatively, take the full norm, remove its A-content, and take the squarefree part in X over F_p(A) (p odd). Generically the conjugate abscissas are distinct by the stabilizer proof. The half norm therefore has degree 2^(m-1) in X. Its primitive part after removing the gcd of its A-coefficients is exactly F_s, up to a nonzero scalar. This removes *all* exceptional-addition factors, not just known denominator factors. Weighted-degree estimates from the previous checkpoint, followed by O(log h) projective additions, bound the univariate coefficient degrees and the algebra sizes by `2^{O(m)}poly(h)`. Exact polynomial arithmetic, gcd and finite-field factorization therefore take `2^{O(m)}poly(b,h)` expected bit time. Specializing X to any specified finite x(T) leaves a nonzero polynomial (otherwise the irreducible F_s would contain the horizontal line X=x(T)). Factor and verify all signed rational lifts at its roots to obtain all inverse solutions for projected T. The fixed-target signed lift count is at most D=h²m2^(m-1) by (1), even including multiplicities as an upper bound. The construction covers the earlier inverse query without extraneous exceptional roots; algebraic/twist roots still require rational verification.

## 3. Distinct factors and an exact collective resultant circuit

**Theorem 2 (branch-locus fingerprint).** If m≥2, the F_s for different address tuples are pairwise nonassociate over the algebraic closure. At each root of f(A+s_i), inertia in the multiquadratic extension is the sign flip of coordinate i: all other radicands are local units and their square roots are unramified. This flip survives in the quotient by simultaneous negation (m≥2). No other finite point ramifies. Thus the finite branch locus of the normalized image's projection to A consists exactly of the 3m shifted roots. The public branch-avoidance assumption makes each such root identify its unique offset in the full pool S. Equality of image curves implies equality of their branch loci and hence their offset sets. Since slots are disjoint, the address tuples are equal. ∎

It follows that the collective polynomial

    G(A,X)=∏_{s∈∏S_i}F_s(A,X)

is squarefree over the algebraic closure and has bidegrees `(W h² m 2^(m-1), W 2^(m-1))`. Every polynomial vanishing on the whole algebraic union must contain this product as a factor. A dense representation of either variable's degree needs at least `2^{b-O(log b)}` positions. This is not a lower bound on the number of nonzero coefficients, on arithmetic circuit size, or on evaluation at one finite-field point.

**Theorem 3 (resultant recurrence).** First take h=1. Define

    S3(Y,V,X)=(Y-V)²X²-2((Y+V)(YV+a)+2c)X+(YV-a)²-4c(Y+V).

This is the classical symmetric third summation polynomial (Semaev, 2004). It describes the abscissa of a signed sum over the algebraic closure. If I,J are disjoint nonempty blocks of a fixed address tuple, then

    F_{I∪J}(A,X) ∝ Res_Y(F_I(A,Y), Res_Z(F_J(A,Z), S3(Y,Z,X))).   (3)

The symbol ∝ denotes equality up to a nonzero constant in F_p, independent of A,X. All resultants are homogeneous resultants at their declared degrees, or equivalently symbolic Sylvester determinants before specializing A,X; retaining the declared degrees after specialization is important for sums at infinity.

Proof. Write d_I=2^(|I|-1), a_I=|I|d_I, and likewise for J. The double resultant is nonzero: over the generic A, its factors evaluate S3 on algebraic signed partial sums, which are generically finite and have distinct abscissas across I,J by (1). Each factor is a nonzero polynomial in X. It vanishes on the full relation image, since every sum is the sum of partial sums. Its degree in X is at most `2d_I d_J=2^(|I|+|J|-1)`. Its A degree is at most `2a_I d_J+2d_I a_J=(|I|+|J|)2^(|I|+|J|-1)`, by homogeneity of each resultant in the coefficient vectors of its arguments. Both bounds exactly match (2). The irreducible full image equation divides the resultant, and equality of the bidegrees forces the quotient to be a constant. This also proves the sequential version adjoining one leaf. ∎

By multiplicativity of resultants, define unprojected block aggregates `G_I(A,X)=∏_{s_I} F_{s_I}(A,X)`. For each slot the aggregate is `∏_{s∈S_i}(X-A-s)`, of degree w_i in X. Then the same identity (3) holds with G in place of F, up to a constant. In particular for a leaf j, one may equivalently take

    G_{I+j} ∝ Res_Y(G_I(A,Y), ∏_{s∈S_j} S3(Y,A+s,X)).

This is an exact compact nested resultant description of the collective norm, with only O(m) join nodes and leaf polynomials of total size O(∑w_i). For projection, let `x([h]P)=U_h(x(P))/V_h(x(P))`, the classical rational x-coordinate multiplication map of degree h², with coprime numerator and denominator. Then apply one further resultant of the unprojected aggregate with U_h(Y)-X V_h(Y), at degree h² in Y. For each tuple the same bidegree argument gives the projected F_s: it vanishes on the projected image, is nonzero generically, and degrees are at most h²m2^(m-1) in A and 2^(m-1) in X. Multiplicativity then gives the projected aggregate. The degree-h² rational map can be computed by ordinary elliptic multiplication and polynomial simplification in poly(h,b) bit operations. The compact nested description does **not** imply a fast evaluation of its resultants. In particular explicit intermediate block polynomials have degree `2^(|I|-1)∏_{i∈I}w_i`, exponential in |I|b/m. Balanced list-based evaluation of the final relation test remains at the usual half-list scale; no improved cost is established by (3).

## 4. An exact algebraic-to-rational error bound and search transfer

We now use G as an algebraic existence predicate at a finite A and projected target T∈G\{O}. Every genuine relation makes it zero. False positives arise from algebraic nonrational ordinates or from the excluded zero ordinates, not from spurious chord denominators.

For a fixed tuple s, all its algebraic leaves at A∈F_p lie in F_{p²}. If the nonrational index set I is nonempty and Φ_s=T is rational, Frobenius gives

    [2h] ∑_{i∈I}ρ_i = O.

If Φ_s=-T, simultaneous negation gives a solution at T. For each fixed I≠∅, (1) bounds the number of geometric points satisfying this equation by `4h²2^(m-1)|I|`. Summing over I gives `h²m4^m`. The exceptional branch translations are at most 3m, with at most 2^m choices of lifts, adding at most `3m2^m` pairs. Each signed lift determines at most one nonzero projected target. Union over all W tuples yields

    Pr_{A,T}(some bad algebraic signed relation)
        ≤ W (h²m4^m + 3m2^m) / (p(r-1))
        = 2^(-b+O(m+log b)).                         (4)

This bound is average over uniform A and nonidentity subgroup target T, not a uniform targetwise bound. It bounds the event of *any* bad relation, including when a good one also exists. On its complement every subgrid of the full grid has correct algebraic existence answers. Thus a fast evaluator that tests G(A,x(T))=0 for arbitrary subsets of the public slots (interval subgrids suffice for interval slots) would recover a witness by repeatedly bisecting slots, in O(∑log w_i)=O(b) oracle calls and polynomial additional work. Enumerate signs of the resulting tuple and verify the projected sum. On bad roots reject failed verifications. The preceding one-shift theorem gives average success at least `2^(-O(m))/poly(b,λ^-1)`, much larger than (4); hence this self-reduction preserves that average-success guarantee up to 1-o(1).

A uniform evaluator including setup with log-cost O(b/m), plus the existing outer reduction, would give log expected time O(b/m+m+log b), attaining O(sqrt b) at m≈sqrt b. More modestly, an evaluator of log-cost O(b/m+m²) would give O(b^(2/3)) by choosing m≈b^(1/3). These are **sufficient targets**, not obtained costs. The one-pilot, fixed-column and identical-selection-law assumptions must still hold for the actual verified finder; the error bound alone does not waive them.

## 5. A Kronecker operator with an exponentially large spectrum

There is an equivalent collective norm formulation. Let R_{m,h}(z_1,...,z_m,X) be the universal summation polynomial built by (3) on individual variables z_i, and then the degree-h² projection resultant. The same degree bounds show degree at most h²2^(m-1) in each z_i, and at most 2^(m-1) in X. Its specialization z_i=A+s_i is a nonzero constant multiple of F_s, by (2). Thus all these specialized polynomials are distinct, even allowing their respective scalar factors. For each slot set Q_i(U)=∏_{s∈S_i}(U-s) and form the product algebra

    B=⊗_i F_p[U_i]/(Q_i(U_i)).

Multiplication by `R_{m,h}(A+U_1,...,A+U_m,x(T))` is a W×W operator L. Chinese remainder decomposition on the distinct roots of each Q_i diagonalizes it; the eigenvalues are exactly the specialized relation polynomial values at all addresses. Its determinant is proportional to G(A,x(T)), with nonzero proportionality constant. Expanding R into monomials expresses L as a sum of at most `(h²2^(m-1)+1)^m` Kronecker products of powers of the individual companion matrices. The explicit coefficient polynomial has `2^{O(m²+m log h)}` possible monomials and can be constructed by ordinary polynomial resultant algorithms within a polynomial in that size and degree. For polylogarithmic m, this is small relative to the target exponent. For m≈sqrt b it need not be affordable; the compact resultant description itself is shorter. The structure suggests a determinant-specific algorithm but no such algorithm with the needed cost is proved.

**Theorem 4 (spectral energy).** The multiplication operator has at least `2^{b-O(m+log b)}` distinct eigenvalues with probability 1-O(1/b), even conditional on a successful rational relation root in the balanced regime.

Proof. For two different tuples s,t their universal specialized bivariate polynomials differ, since their primitive irreducible factors differ by Theorem 2. Their difference is a nonzero polynomial of degrees at most D_A=h²m2^(m-1) in A and D_X=2^(m-1) in X. A nonzero coefficient in X can vanish for at most D_A values of A; at every other A the polynomial has at most D_X roots in X. Since a fixed abscissa belongs to at most two nonidentity subgroup points, the probability of equal eigenvalues over uniform A,T is at most

    ε = D_A/p + 2D_X/(r-1).

For multiplicities n_v the energy E=∑ n_v² counts ordered collisions. Hence E[E]≤W+W(W-1)ε≤W 2^{O(m)}poly(b). Conditional expectation on any event of probability κ≥2^(-O(m))/poly(b) is at most the latter bound divided by κ. The one-shift successful-root event has such probability by the preceding theorem. Markov at a factor b and Cauchy–Schwarz `W²≤(# distinct eigenvalues) E` prove the assertion. The same proof holds for the individually normalized F_s. ∎

Consequences: L is diagonalizable, so its minimal polynomial is the product over distinct eigenvalues. Explicitly reconstructing this polynomial has exponential degree. The scalar sequence tr(L^j), j≥0, is ∑_v n_v v^j over F_p (take 0^0=1). At most floor(W/p) different values have multiplicity divisible by p. Every linear recurrence valid from j=0 must have a characteristic polynomial vanishing at all other values: substitute the trace formula and use the Vandermonde matrix for the distinct roots. Hence explicit reconstruction of the full trace recurrence also requires exponentially many coefficients. This is a limitation of those reconstruction methods, not a lower bound on computing det(L), testing whether zero is an eigenvalue, or nonlinear decoding from short traces. The small Kronecker description and the large spectrum coexist.

## 6. Exact diagonal-orbit batching for interval slots

If S_i=t(c_i+[0,w-1]) with a common nonzero slope t and pairwise disjoint intervals in the offset labels, use the common-shift symmetry. Represent each address as a digit vector d∈[0,w-1]^m. Write j=min_i d_i, e_i=d_i-j. Then e has min zero and j ranges from 0 through w-1-max e_i. For fixed e the actual offsets of d are the offsets of e plus tj. Consequently

    F_d(A,X) ∝ F_e(A+tj,X).

One inverse polynomial root factorization for e and a fixed T answers *all* j in its orbit at a prescribed A: for each inverse root A', test whether the residue (A'-A)/t is one of those j, and verify the signed tuple. Cost `2^{O(m)}poly(b,h)` per orbit, independent of w. The number of orbits is exactly `w^m-(w-1)^m`, since exactly those digit vectors with min zero are representatives. With w≫m it is (1+o(1))m w^(m-1). Thus the full orbit algorithm has log-cost at most `(m-1)log2 w+O(m+log b)`, saving one slot-width factor relative to enumerating all tuples, but remaining worse than the balanced half-list algorithm at growing m. This is an exact batch mechanism, not an exponent improvement. More generally, a d-dimensional algebraic family of degree Δ covers at most Δ w_max^d addresses of a Cartesian product grid (induction by slicing a varying coordinate with the w_max coordinate hyperplanes). On a uniform independent target T and fixed A, each address accounts for at most 2^m projected target values. Thus selecting a subexponential number of low-dimensional, low-degree address families without using T, or on an all-miss transcript that does not otherwise depend on T, has negligible target coverage if d=o(m) and Δ=2^{o(b)}. This last observation is only for that restricted family-selection model.

## 7. Exact inverse planting and why random-target reuse fails

**Theorem 5 (fast exact planting).** We can sample exactly the distribution of a uniformly random successful signed address/translation/nonidentity-target quadruple in expected `2^{O(m)}poly(b,h)` time, without a forward evaluator or enumerating the full factor bases.

Choose a uniform address s and independent uniform nonidentity subgroup target T. For example choose random known masked coefficients u,v, with T=[h]([u]G+[v]Q) and reject T=O, as in the inherited reduction. Use the inverse algorithm to enumerate the list L(s,T) of *all* distinct successful pairs (A, signed tuple). There are at most D=h²m2^(m-1) by (1). Choose a uniform integer J∈{1,...,D}; accept and return the Jth record if J≤|L|, otherwise restart. Every genuine quadruple has exactly one ticket and the same per-trial mass `1/(W(r-1)D)`; the procedure is exact. For a fixed s the number of rational points on C_s with finite A and nonzero ordinates is `p+O(m2^m sqrt(p))` by Hasse–Weil and the genus/branch calculation of the previous checkpoint. Excluding Φ_s=O costs at most D points. Thus the expected list length over random (s,T) is `(p+O(m2^m sqrt(p)+D))/(r-1)`, asymptotic to p/(r-1) at m≤sqrt b and h poly(b). The acceptance probability is this quantity divided by D and is at least 2^(-O(m))/poly(b). The inverse list can be generated in expected `2^{O(m)}poly(b,h)` time by Theorem 1; repeated independent trials preserve the stated scale. The marginal law of (A,T) is exactly proportional to H_T(A) under the fresh law, rather than simply proportional to the existence indicator. A genuine planted example and a known masked equation can be produced this way; this does not give multiple equations on the same reusable factor base or automatically replace the one-pilot selection law.

**Theorem 6 (reuse collision barrier in a restricted inverse-only model).** Fix the public supports and consider a sequence of J inverse calls. The next address may be selected using the complete past, but must be fixed before drawing that call's fresh uniform nonidentity target. The algorithm may retain every genuine inverse solution (at most D translations per call). Define the projected factor base at A to contain every nonidentity projected point from the public slots, with sign classes identified. For h=1 let B=|S-S|. For general h, take B=2h|S|². Then

    Pr(any two calls yield overlapping projected factor bases)
       ≤ min(1, binom(J,2) D B 2^m/(r-1)).             (5)

Proof. For a previous translation A0, in the prime-order case any overlap requires A-A0∈S-S. In the cofactor case, for each of at most |S| projected sign classes of the previous base, there are at most 2h rational preimages of that class. Matching any of their abscissas to any offset of S gives at most 2h|S|² possible A. After j-1 calls, there are at most (j-1)D previously retained translations, hence at most (j-1)DB translations that overlap a previous base. The next chosen address at any one fixed A produces at most 2^m possible projected target values. Since its target is still uniform conditional on the past and address, the conditional chance of an overlapping genuine inverse solution is at most `(j-1)DB2^m/(r-1)`. Union over the calls proves (5). It holds even when all valid inverse solutions rather than one sampled solution are retained. Selecting addresses after seeing the fresh target is outside this theorem. A strategy with independent fresh proposals and a rejection step must count unsuccessful proposals as calls.

For a common interval pool of size mw, B≤2mw-1 in the prime-order case. The bound implies that constant probability of any such overlap requires log2 J≥`b/2 - b/(2m) - O(m+log b)` at balanced widths. For arbitrary pool shape or cofactors B≤2h(mw)², giving log2 J≥`b/2-b/m-O(m+log b)`. Both are exponential in b at growing m. The theorem concerns overlaps of projected factor bases of raw inverse-query proposals; it is not a lower bound on every possible use of inverse polynomials.

This also has a linear-algebra interpretation. Suppose all rows from each call refer to that call's single nonzero masked target and its inverse bases, no prior known factor-base logs are supplied, and the projected log-variable sets of different calls are disjoint. A linear combination of rows eliminating every factor-base log must eliminate the variables of each call separately. Summing their actual group equalities gives `(sum of that call's row coefficients) T_j=O`. Since T_j has prime order r and is nonzero, that sum is zero modulo r. The same masked coefficients u_j,v_j occur on every row in that call, so the combined right side is identically zero. Thus ordinary linear elimination of these disjoint-base equations cannot yield a nonzero coefficient of the unknown log. This argument does not cover algorithms exploiting additional arithmetic relations among variables, preknown logs, or target-dependent address construction. It explains why the exact fast inverse plant is not already a faster discrete-log algorithm.

## 8. Reproduction and evidence

The package includes the prior checkpoint's `verify.py` and `inverse_translation_verify.py` as unchanged helpers, and three new scripts. Run from the package directory, with Python and NumPy:

    OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python -B verify_norm_aggregate.py
    OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python -B verify_inverse_law.py
    OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python -B verify_collective_resultant.py

Recorded transcripts are `verification.txt`, `inverse_law_verification.txt`, and `collective_verification.txt`. Tests check primitive image degrees, every finite A and target abscissa against direct group addition over F_{p²}, projection by h=1,2,3,4,8 (some arbitrary multiplication factors, and actual cofactor 4 and 8 examples), branch and identity paths, diagonal orbit partitions, exact inverse-law tickets and projected-base overlap bounds, homogeneous Sylvester resultants (including degree drops), and the actual spectrum of a toy prime-order curve. The two squarefree sign-conjugate norm computations and direct group additions are independent arithmetic checks. Tiny-field inverse roots are checked by exhaustive field evaluation, not by a benchmark of the asymptotic factorization algorithm. The spectrum experiment uses fixed pseudorandom uniform A,T samples; all its field arithmetic and energy counts are exact.

Recorded results: 7,449 exact algebraic incidence comparisons, 550 algebraic signed finite paths, 332 rational nonbranch signed finite paths, 83 additional algebraic-coordinate hits, 18 branch-path hits, 34 infinity paths; 28 interval orbit partitions covering 34,608 tuples, 2,888 shifted incidence comparisons and 361 complete batched queries; 4,880 inverse target/translation checks, 464 successful tickets, 58 algebraic extra candidate incidences, 476 reuse-bound checks; 3,676 sequential and 3,676 balanced resultant comparisons, 361 collective-resultant comparisons. All recorded assertions passed. No actual large-field forward relation algorithm was run. The proofs give asymptotic estimates independently of these toy regressions.

## 9. Frontier

The sharpened problem is a determinant of a structured multiplication operator (or an equivalent nested resultant circuit) at a single prescribed A,T. We no longer need to manipulate generic Jacobian denominators or exclude mixed twists through additional expensive constraints on typical roots. Exact inverse planting and diagonal orbit batching work. The simple explicit product, spectrum/trace recurrence reconstruction, and fresh-target inverse-only reuse strategies do not beat the desired scale. An implicit zero/determinant evaluator for the compact collective resultant that does not expand its high-degree intermediate polynomials remains missing. Neither the general-prime exponent nor the previous numerical coefficient is improved by this checkpoint.
