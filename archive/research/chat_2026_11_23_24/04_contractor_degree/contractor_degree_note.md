# A degree-sensitive tensor contractor and two barriers to its ECDLP transfer

24 November 2026 — research checkpoint. This follows the uploaded v3 paper and `affine_digit_sampler_note.md`. The positive theorem is an extension of the published quantitative Boolean contractor, conditional only on the correctness of its stated proof. It achieves a stronger asymptotic bound on a specified subclass of networks. The transfer to the elliptic relation compiler is **not** established; the rank obstruction below explains why the straightforward transfer fails. No improved general-prime ECDLP exponent is claimed. Novelty relative to all unpublished work has not been established.

## 1. Degree-sensitive first-level contraction

Let M be the number of Boolean edges of a bipartite multigraph, Lg=log_2 M, and let its maximum degree be at most M^(4/5). Assume polynomial-size descriptions and polynomially many vertices. Assume entries in Z/2^P0 Z, 1<=P0=O(M), with entry time poly(M) exp(O(M^(4/5))) and polynomial space. Isolated vertices are handled separately. The same proof works over an unramified coefficient extension of degree O(log M). For each row-side vertex v, let d_v be the degree of its unique multilinear polynomial over the input ring on its incident Boolean bits. Let D=sum_v d_v. The product of row-side tensors has a multilinear extension of degree at most D since row-side port sets are disjoint.

**Theorem.** Fix beta>0. If

    D <= M / ((log_2 M)^beta log_2 log_2 M),

then the public quantitative contractor admits a degree-sensitive modification running in

    poly(M) exp(O(M / (log_2 M)^((1+beta)/2)))

bit time and polynomial space. In particular beta=1/3 gives log time O(M/(log M)^(2/3)). Beta=1 gives log time O(M/log M). Constants may depend on beta and the entry-access and precision bounds. This is a promise on one side of the *input* network, not on every recursive descendant. One can choose either bipartition side if it meets the promise.

### 1.1 Low-degree lifts, rather than canonical truth-table lifts

A low degree modulo the output precision does not imply that the canonical integer truth table has low rational degree. First compute the unique multilinear coefficients of each row tensor by Boolean Mobius inversion at precision P0. Choose integral representatives of those coefficients, with zero lifted to zero. This defines an exact low-degree polynomial lift P_v whose values reduce to the original tensor modulo 2^P0. An evaluation of the lift at a Boolean assignment, at any polynomially bounded higher precision, can be implemented by streaming subsets and their Mobius sums. If v has d incident ports, this costs at most 3^d times the original entry cost and polynomial space. This is exp(O(M^(4/5))), which is lower order in the target exponent for every fixed beta. The columns can use their original canonical lifts. This change is essential: do not silently use low-degree properties of a reduced polynomial with unrelated canonical truth-table lifts.

### 1.2 Growing group count and packet packing

Set r=max(64,ceil(Lg^(beta/2))) (a constant lower cutoff is harmless), a=floor(Lg/(40 log_2 r)), u=floor(a/2), and packet length Lp=2^a. Then a,u=Theta(log M/log log M), Lp=M^o(1), and D_arr=r^(2a)<=M^(1/20). Label row and column vertices by independent uniform vectors in [r]^a. The collision-packing variance estimate from the public proof is E sum_gamma (X_gamma-M/D_arr)^2 <=2M Delta, where Delta is the maximum degree. The conditional-expectation construction tests polynomially many labels and evaluates these second moments in polynomial time. Padding each ordered pair-label class to the common maximum rounded to a multiple of Lp yields at most 3M/Lp packets: the required conditions D_arr^2 Delta=o(M), Lp D_arr=o(M), and Lp=o(Delta_bound) follow from the choices above. Each packet fixes an unordered label pair per coordinate and its slot bits encode orientations, just as in the public proof. For any coordinate and symbol, at most 3M/r row ports and 3M/r column ports are in that group. The child for a group thus has at most 6M/r edges. Variable r does not require a new packing theorem; all arrays of labels and conditional-expectation work remain polynomial.

### 1.3 First-level deformation and readout

Use the published Cauchy kernel

    C(T)=prod_{t=-Lp}^{Lp-1}(T-t),
    W_xy(T)=C(T)/(C'(0)(T-(y-x))).

It is block diagonal by packet. Set alpha=2 Lp. The local integrality proof at T=t+alpha z is unchanged. If A(Y) is the product of the chosen row lifts and B(X) the product of the lifted columns, the deformed contraction is

    F(T)=sum_X B(X) P_A(W(T) X),

where P_A is the exact multilinear polynomial of the row-side product. Equivalently expand P_A using its Boolean cardinal selectors l_0(z)=1-z, l_1(z)=z; this gives the original deformation with the chosen lifts. Hence deg F <= D(2Lp-1), F(0) is the intended contraction modulo 2^P0, and all F(t+alpha z) are locally integral. This is the entire source of the improvement.

To handle D very small, use D*=max(D,ceil(M/Lg^(beta+2))) as a paid upper bound. Set h=ceil(3D*/u). For active t of valuation i=v2(t)<u, use Hermite multiplicity h(u-i) and write D_t=Lp/2^i. Repeat the published tapered Hermite readout with D* in place of M in its *degree* parameter. The same identity and valuation counts give sufficient precision

    p_t=P0+8 h D_t+8 D*(a+1)=O(D* D_t/u).

Indeed D_t>=2^(a-u) is larger than any fixed power of log M. Thus the initial precision and the local Taylor substitution loss are absorbed by D* D_t/u, even with the floor on D*. The total degree and number of interpolation points are polynomial in M, and the unramified coefficient extension can have O(log M) degree.

### 1.4 First-level crossing modes and charged children

At node t, group by coordinate i. Same-group terms remain local. For crossing terms partition columns into tiles modulo 2^(i+1), and use the public geometric expansion. Each tile has a linear mode of weight j+1 for every j>=0; its coefficient is divisible by 2^(j+1). There are at most 6M/D_t modes of any one weight. Consequently the number of exponent tuples of total weight below p_t is

    exp(O(sqrt(M p_t / D_t)))=exp(O(sqrt(M D*/u))).

This uses the generating product bound sum_{j>=1} -ln(1-e^(-theta j)) <= pi^2/(6 theta). Mode factorization and the child identity are identical to the published proof. There is no assumption that low-degree cancellations occur before grouping, nor any need to pass the low-degree promise to children: the ordinary literal selector expansion, with the reduced precision cutoff, suffices. Child tensors have at most 2Lp ports and polynomial-size descriptions; entries are computed by expanding at most Lp factors with polynomially many terms each, costing exp(O(Lp log M)) time and polynomial space. Retained root row entries use the special lift procedure above, reduced to the child's creation precision.

Each child has at most s=ceil(6M/r) edges, maximum degree at most max(M^(4/5),2Lp)<=s^(9/10) eventually, and entry time poly(M) exp(O(M^(4/5)+Lp log M))=exp(O(s^(9/10))) times polynomial. Apply the **ordinary** public recursive contractor to each child. Two small interface extensions must be made explicitly:

* The coefficient extension chosen at the root can be reused; its residue field can be chosen larger than M^4 and thus larger than every child's needed interpolation set. Its O(log M) dimension and polynomial-bit ring arithmetic preserve the proof.
* Input precision for a child is p_t<=M^(1+o(1))=s^(1+o(1)), rather than O(s). In the ordinary proof, the root active call has a_0=2u_0 with u_0=Theta(log s) (a small fixed positive multiple). Its retained D_t is at least 2^u_0=s^epsilon for a fixed epsilon>0 (up to rounding). Since an active call has M'>s/sqrt(log s), p_t=o(M' D_t/u_0). Thus the root precision estimate still holds. At every later depth the extra initial precision is dominated by the inherited polynomial-times-2^(a_{d-1}) precision bound. All other packing, valuation, degree and entry estimates in the ordinary proof are unchanged. Small leaves are evaluated directly. This verifies the use of s^(1+o(1)) precision; it is not an uncharged invocation of a theorem with an incompatible hypothesis.

The ordinary child contraction therefore has log cost O(s/sqrt(log s)). The parent loops over polynomially many nodes, interpolation values and groups and the above exponent tuples. All work is charged, including entry lifting. Depth-first computation uses polynomial space. The resulting first-level total log time is

    O( sqrt(M D* log r / log M) + M/(r sqrt(log M)) + M^(4/5) ).

Plug in r=ceil(Lg^(beta/2)) and the degree promise. Both first and second terms are O(M/Lg^((1+beta)/2)). This proves the theorem, subject to the public contractor proof itself. It also identifies a concrete degree threshold, rather than assuming a new fast elliptic evaluator. If the promise were established on the relation networks with M=O(mb), beta=1/3 would balance m b/(log b)^(2/3) against b/m at m~(log b)^(1/3), giving the hoped-for ECDLP exponent O(b/(log b)^(1/3)). That implication is conditional on a compiler meeting the promise and the inherited outer reduction.

## 2. Why the plain Fourier relation compiler fails the degree promise

A nonzero multilinear Boolean polynomial on N bits over any field, of degree d, is nonzero at at least 2^(N-d) Boolean inputs. Proof by induction: if f_1-f_0 is nonzero, its degree is at most d-1 and each nonzero value gives a nonzero value of f at one of the two corresponding inputs. If the difference vanishes, f is independent of that bit. Thus d>=N-log_2(support size). Reduction modulo two suffices for indicator tensors with odd accepted entries; a lift to higher dyadic precision cannot have smaller degree.

In the abscissa-only compiler, a short internal-coordinate row of l coefficients of k+O(log b) bits is transmitted in l Fourier base words of W=(4+o(1))k bits, even after conjugate compression. Its row predicate accepts at most 2^((1+o(1))lk) encoded values. It follows that its Boolean degree is at least (3-o(1))lk. A quotient row has coefficient width at most (3+o(1))k, also giving a linear-degree lower bound if all quotient digits are independently checked. Summing the coordinate contribution alone over the source owners and m-2 internal coordinates gives Omega(mb), whereas the new degree promise needs o(mb). Thin leaves do not remove the internal-coordinate arrays. This is only a lower bound for the existing predicate representation; it does not preclude a different compiler.

## 3. Stronger Fourier-side rank obstruction, invariant under local gauges

Support size is not invariant under local changes of basis. A flattening rank is. Here is a stronger obstruction for the prime-length row construction in the previous checkpoints.

**Lemma (half-frequency injectivity).** Let l be an odd prime tending to infinity, lambda in {1,2}, q an odd prime with q=-1 mod l, Q=q^h. Work in the unramified quadratic extension of Z/Q Z, with a primitive l-th root xi, a real unit theta satisfying theta^l=lambda, and conjugation xi -> xi^-1. The l-point row transform of coefficients c_j in a common integer interval of size B is evaluation of f(X)=sum_{j<l} c_j X^j at theta xi^s, s=0,...,l-1. Common public unit multipliers on a row do not change the claim. Assume log l=o(log B) and log Q >= (4-o(1)) log B. Partition the conjugate frequency pairs nearly equally between A and C, putting the zero-frequency root in A. Then projection on A is injective. Projection on C is injective if lambda=2, and has fibres of size at most B if lambda=1.

Proof: for two coefficient tuples take their difference polynomial g with |g_j|<B. On every complex root of X^l-lambda, |g(z)| <=2lB. If lambda=2, X^l-2 is irreducible by Eisenstein. A nonzero g of degree less than l has nonzero integer resultant R=Res(X^l-2,g), with |R|<=(2lB)^l. Vanishing modulo Q at s roots forces Q^s to divide R: compute the product over all roots in the unramified q-adic extension where all roots exist (lift theta and xi by Hensel), and all other factors are integral. Each half contains (1/2-o(1))l roots, so Q^s>(2lB)^l eventually. This contradiction proves injectivity. For lambda=1 use the irreducible cyclotomic polynomial Phi_l and its nonzero-frequency roots, with norm bound (lB)^(l-1). A collision implies g=t Phi_l, hence a common integer shift of all digits. The zero-frequency value in A forces lt=0 mod Q, and |lt|<lB<Q forces t=0. On C there are at most B allowed common shifts. This proves the lemma. For finite instances the sufficient inequalities Q^s>(2lB)^l and Q>lB can be checked directly.

**Corollary (flattening rank).** Form the 0/1 tensor of all legal transformed rows. Flatten with A on one side and C on the other, including canonical binary encodings and any unused illegal alphabet values (which contribute zero). Each occupied A-row has exactly one entry 1. Distinct nonempty columns have disjoint supports, hence are linearly independent over *every* field. The rank is at least B^(l-1), and equals B^l for lambda=2. In fact for lambda=1, when each digit runs over the entire same consecutive interval, C identifies exactly the common-shift classes and its rank equals B^l-(B-1)^l.

Any invertible local change of basis applied separately on each word or bit preserves the flattening rank, since the induced left and right transformations are invertible. The flattening rank of any Boolean polynomial of degree d on N_A+N_C bits is at most sum_{j=0}^d binom(N_A,j), by collecting monomials in A. For these rows N_A=(1/2+o(1))l log_2 Q=(2+o(1))l log_2 B and log_2 rank>=(1-o(1))l log_2 B. Therefore d>=Omega(l log B) after any such invertible local gauges (using the binary entropy upper bound on the partial binomial sum). This applies over F_2 and hence to reductions of invertible dyadic gauges. It is not necessary that transformed tensor entries remain 0/1.

For the actual multi-array source owner, pin other variables to valid values and consider the independent range predicates of its internal-coordinate arrays. Their flattening is a Kronecker product across the arrays, and the full tensor has at least that rank; adding illegal states just pads with zeros. Choose the split per array, then keep the split fixed under the gauge. The root compiler's range predicates for the internal arrays are independent of its leaf membership restrictions. Thus the log-rank lower bound sums across these arrays, and summing the resulting degree lower bounds over owners still gives Omega(mb). This rules out an entire class of local basis changes as a way of reaching the degree promise. It does not rule out transformations that mix distinct words across this cut, alter the graph, or compute an alternative function. The proof is tailored to the balanced odd-prime-length Fourier construction, not a general theorem about arbitrary FFT designs.

## 4. The arithmetic side has a comparable obstruction

One might swap sides and apply the degree-sensitive theorem to the arithmetic middle tensors instead. The following argument covers the natural compiler in which the auxiliary modulus prime q is chosen to avoid the rational norms of M and the nonzero primitive component of the short projector E. For the binomial case X^n-2 is irreducible and E has nonzero norm; for the cyclotomic case E(gamma)!=0 implies Phi_n does not divide E, and primitive frequencies account for (1-o(1)) of all frequencies. The norm of M is nonzero by diagonal dominance. These norms have polynomial bit lengths in the representation construction. Avoiding their polynomially many prime divisors is compatible with the inherited effective least-prime-in-progression construction: take an extra coprime polynomial-size prime modulus with more nonzero residues than excluded primes, and apply the least-prime bound to the distinct compatible classes. This produces polynomial-size candidate primes, one of which avoids the excluded set. One can search and check in polynomial time. This is an allowed choice of auxiliary modulus, not a claim about every possible q.

At each middle tensor, after the invertible second transform, all variable coordinates can be treated as free spectral values over the appropriate base or quadratic unramified ring. The equations EF=MQ determine every quotient spectral value uniquely because M is a unit at every frequency. Therefore in the flattening with all non-quotient input words on the row side and all quotient words on the column side, each occupied row has exactly one entry. Its rank is exactly the number of distinct possible quotient assignments.

For each primitive frequency, fix leaf spectra arbitrarily (choose zero for the thin variable polynomial, leaving its public offset). In Omega(m) mutually disjoint internal-coordinate pairs choose one internal state W so that W-V=1, with V the leaf spectrum. Vary the other state U over the local ring. The chord S_3(U,V,W) is quadratic in U with leading coefficient (V-W)^2=1. The projector/multiplier ratio E/M is a unit on primitive frequencies. A quadratic with unit leading coefficient over an unramified ring of odd residue characteristic, residue field size q^f and total size Q^f, has at least (1-1/q^f)Q^f/2 distinct values: all but one residue class have unit derivative, and each output has at most two such simple preimages by Hensel uniqueness. Thus for each disjoint pair the quotient projection has at least a constant times the entire local-ring cardinality possible outputs. These choices can be made independently across disjoint pairs and conjugate frequency classes. Unselected equations merely determine their other quotients; they impose no restriction on the free values in this middle tensor. The full transforms between the incoming canonical row words and these spectral values are bijective, including the independent conjugate representatives.

The middle flattening consequently has log-rank Omega(m times the number of primitive base frequency components owned by it times log Q). Its input-side bit count is O(m times the number of all base frequency components times log Q). Summing over middle owners gives Omega(mb) log-rank and O(mb) total port count. Since each individual log-rank is at most its port count, a constant fraction of the total port mass has log-rank a constant fraction of its size (discarding low-ratio owners loses at most that ratio times total mass). The preceding monomial/entropy bound then gives Omega(mb) total degree, also invariant under separate invertible word/bit gauges. The result covers the specified arithmetic layout and auxiliary-prime choice. Different layouts, nonunit-modulus variants, or cross-word transformations are outside the claim. In particular, it is not a complexity lower bound for ECDLP.

## 5. Joint precision/jet-order obstruction for the consecutive-grid readout

Previous project checkpoints prove a geometric precision lower bound for fixed rational linear readouts. The following witness strengthens it by allowing arbitrary node omission, individual coefficient precisions, and nonlinear readout on the locally integral polynomial class used in the public contractor. It also closes the simple proposal to avoid expensive enumeration by requesting very short Taylor jets.

Let Lp=2^a, 1<=u<=a, M>=8u, grid S={-Lp,...,Lp-1}, alpha=2Lp. Consider polynomials of degree <=M(2Lp-1) that are locally integral at every T=t+alpha z, t in S, over the 2-local rationals. Suppose the readout only observes coefficients at nodes t!=0 with i=v2(t)<u. Individual coefficients can have arbitrary, nonuniform precision and the observations need not be prefixes. Write D_t=Lp/2^i, A=floor(M/4), h=floor(M/(4u)). Define

    a_t=A+h(u-v2(t))   (active nodes),
    f(T)=prod_{t active}(1-T/t)^(a_t).

Its degree is <=2Lp A+2Lp h u<=M Lp<=M(2Lp-1), and f(0)=1. At an inactive center including 0, each factor has integral local coefficients and unit constant because the center's valuation exceeds that of every root (or center=0). At an active center t, the local polynomial starts at order a_t>=A. Its first nonzero coefficient has valuation

    V_t=(a+1-i)a_t + sum_{s!=t} a_s (v2(t-s)-v2(s)),

where a_s=0 at inactive nodes. Each remaining coefficient has at least the same valuation: after factoring the known 2-power from each linear factor, its scaled z coefficient is integral, its constant is a unit except at s=t, and the first nonzero coefficient of the product is a product of units. Counting consecutive-grid differences gives

    V_t=(a+1-i)a_t + D_t sum_{j=1}^{a-i} (j/2^j)(a_i-a_{i+j}),

with a_j here denoting the root order at valuation j (zero when j>=u). Lower valuation levels cancel; at each higher level the two distance classes have D_t/2^j points. Since a_i-a_{i+1}>=h and all differences are nonnegative, V_t>=h D_t/2. This also proves local integrality at every active node, not just a rational-polynomial identity. The polynomial is a legal member of the generic locally integral class.

**Corollary.** Any readout that always recovers f(0) modulo two on this class must, somewhere, observe a coefficient of order at least A and precision strictly greater than h D_t/2. Otherwise f and the zero polynomial have identical observed residues (even for adaptive coefficient requests, along the common all-zero observation path), but opposite output parity. This holds for every choice of subsets of nodes and coefficient precisions; it does not rely on a linear readout. It does *not* prove a collision in the family generated by actual tensor networks with fixed predicates: the latter is a narrower, still open realization question.

The consequence for raw weighted-mode enumeration is quantitative. Take a fixture of M/Lp complete crossing packets at the required node, with all 2^(i+1) tiles available per packet. For each tile choose the first k=floor(D_t/(32 sqrt(u))) modes, of weights 1,...,k. In the asymptotic schedule D_t/sqrt(u)->infinity. The total number of chosen modes is K=(2M/D_t)k=Theta(M/sqrt(u)), their total weight is (M/D_t) k(k+1)<=h D_t/2 for all sufficiently large parameters, and K<=A. Every binary subset lies inside both the degree cap for the expensive coefficient and its precision budget. Thus a procedure that enumerates the whole weight/degree-feasible universe at such a node still has 2^Omega(M/sqrt(u)) candidates, even if it computes only the needed jets. Normalized weights bounded by O(a-i+j) give the same conclusion after a fixed smaller k when D_t is superpolylogarithmic. This is a candidate-universe obstruction for full crossing fixtures, not a lower bound that all candidates contribute to a particular contraction. Sparsity-aware cancellation, alternative deformations, and more powerful coefficient pairing remain outside it.

## 6. XOR packet geometry diagnostic

A natural alternative uses W_xy(T)=w_(x XOR y)(T), where w_d are the cardinal polynomials on 0,...,Lp-1. At an integer node t this flips every set bit of t, so multiple label coordinates can be grouped at once. However its crossing blocks are XOR-circulant rather than Cauchy. At t=Lp+1, the even-difference block has kernel w_d(t)=(-1)^(Lp-1-d) binom(t,d) binom(t-d-1,Lp-1-d) for even d. The exact Walsh eigenvalues were computed. At widths 16,64,256,1024,4096, the median valuations are 9,15,21,29,37 (maximum 15,63,255,1023,4095). Most eigenvalues in these finite tests do not exhibit the desired linearly growing weight spectrum. These eigenvalues are not Smith invariants (Walsh normalization is not unimodular over the 2-adics), and the tests do not establish an asymptotic lower bound on XOR deformations. No improved algorithm is inferred from them.

## 7. Reproducibility and status

Run `python contractor_degree_audit.py` (standard library only). The exact tests check the joint obstruction on 9,228 centers, including 68 explicitly expanded local polynomials and 4,932 coefficients; 628 exact degree/weight inequalities for the candidate universe; 24 exact low-degree rational deformations and parity comparisons; all 65,535 nonzero four-bit Boolean truth tables for the support-degree inequality; 10,518 legal row assignments over six prime-length, binomial/cyclotomic Fourier fixtures; 36 local middle-quadratic image fixtures with 16,116 assignments; and the five XOR spectra. The finite tests check the algebraic components, not an end-to-end faster contractor or ECDLP solver.
