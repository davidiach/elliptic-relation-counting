## Independent checks of the LZR chain

* **Sections 2–3.** Canonical modular products use an exact product and quotient; canonical slope and zero-test branches fix all internal witnesses even for inverse points. The power-of-two transform length divides $2J$ and paired terms differing by half a period cancel since two is a unit modulo $2^J+1$. The radix convolution bound avoids wrap. Subdivision and equality trees preserve multiplicity and give $O(mb)$ edges with degree $O(b^{3/4})$. A generic-group lower bound in the introduction has no force as a lower bound for all coordinate algorithms; it is unused.
* For the Stage I potential let $g=|G|$, $q=nT/p$, $\mu=q^m$. Distinct same-parity abscissas land at two distinct uniform field values. Opposite classes use independent maps. Thus $\mathrm{Var}(W)/\mu^2\le(1+1/q)^m-1\le1/2048$. For an exact coincidence set of size $j<m$, choose all tuple entries except the forced last unmatched one; the energy bound is $\mathbb E C\le\mu+\mu^2(1+1/q)^m/n$. With $\mu\ge16g$ this yields $373/336<64/57$. Conditional averages are ratios of integers with a common rational $\mu$; zero-completion prefixes are discarded. Minimizing $x^2/\delta+64(x-1)^2$ yields $64/(1+64\delta)$, hence $\delta>7/8$. Doubling the support covers the group. This proves seed selection, enumeration and per-target decomposition in the stated bit bound. The multiplicative primitive-root construction scans the union for each prime-power component after deterministic factoring.
* **Proposition 4.1.** Common successors give fiberwise label equality. The generator shift and its normalized representative force the correct homomorphism, including $O$ and duplicated columns. This works also for cyclic composite order. See the proof in MAIN_RESULT.md.
* **Lemma 4.2.** The signed integer difference is in $[-w(r-1),w(r-1)]$. The roots $1+jr$, $|j|\le w$, catch exactly the legal residues there. Finite differences give the integer Newton coefficients for all integer arguments, including negative exponents via the truncated inverse of $1+z$. The paper's precision exceeds the logarithm of the number of pairs plus the sum of the logarithms of the absolute linear-factor bounds. Hence each prefix total lifts canonically and nonnegativity lets one extract a witness. This does not require each coefficient of the intermediate series to be nonnegative before reduction.
* **Lemma 4.4.** The image of the new row on the solution lattice has cardinality $M/\gcd(M,a^tB_1,\ldots,a^tB_N)$. An inconsistent current representative forces a proper cut. Products with a unimodular transformation need polynomial **bit lengths** for polynomial-time HNF. If a reduction routine returns larger intermediate representatives than the claimed magnitude bound, reduce generator coordinates modulo $M$ and append $MI$ before HNF. This preserves the lattice. General moduli need up to $O(N\log M)$ cuts; prime order needs at most $N$.
* **Lemma 5.1.** At fixed open bits the source deforms exactly the closed network with those entries pinned. The edge-class variance is at most $2M\Delta$; each terminal coordinate has total variance at most $\beta_c\tau$. The chosen potential gives simultaneous bounds. The packet orientation is constant within an unordered symbol pair. A crossing tile has a unit after dividing its known dyadic denominator, and the $j$th geometric coefficient gains at least $j+1$ bits. There are at most $6M/D_t$ modes of each weight. In the Hermite formula the loss in the node prefactor is bounded by $hD_t\sum_{j\ge1}j^2/2^j=6hD_t$. Evaluation of a truncated jet loses at most $4M(a+1)$ further bits. Unit differences in interpolation introduce no extra loss. Root coefficients are boundary independent. Each real port appears in precisely one child, giving total internal mass $2M$ and preserving each open host.
* **Lemma 5.2.** For bounded $\rho$, exhaustive summation suffices. Otherwise $H\asymp\beta/\sqrt\rho$, $u_d\asymp\rho/2^d$, and active depths have $8^d=O(\sqrt\rho)$. The factor $2^{u_d}$ absorbs the previous precision times all polynomial-in-$\rho$ factors. The per-path mode exponents sum as $(\beta/\sqrt\rho)\sum_d(\sqrt2/8)^d$. Sequential sibling multiplication justifies charging a path here. Distinguish an input precision bound involving $a_{d-1}$ from the new increment involving $a_d$; the proof on pp. 21–22 gives the indexing needed for both schedules.
* **Lemma 5.3.** This time the product of child sums is distributed. The sum of their internal masses at depth $d$ is at most $2^dS$. The resulting power $m^{3/10}$ and the overhead for nodes and lifts are derived in COMPLEXITY_AUDIT.md. Early stops satisfy $\beta_c\le2M$ because the boundary host retains an internal port. Entry evaluation continues on absolute depth and keeps creation precision. Treating it as a fresh low-precision network would lose information.
* **Theorem 4.3.** With digit height $\lfloor\beta/(n+1)\rfloor$, the product degree in $U$ is below $K_0=n(\kappa-1)+1$. Thus ordinary integer digits can be decoded after substituting $W^{i+K_0j}$. The coefficient bound follows from the product of local nonnegative $\ell_1$ norms and $2^\beta$ assignments. Reduce modulo $f$ only after decoding. The full guarded sum is integral because each completed parent congruence is integral at its own precision. Shifting by $2^{Q_{\rm guard}}$ before accumulation and dividing its canonical residue exactly prevents loss from the individual rational weights.

## Independent checks of the v4 chain

The genus-one divisor criterion gives the regular section test, including the sign at $-T$. Distinct abscissas imply simple norm roots, which exclude a common zero of the two coefficient polynomials. Normalizing the unique highest pole coefficient fixes the field section. Different private polynomial-ring lifts can still encode the same section: existence and literal unique assignment counting are different guarantees. The raw disjointness test must precede rational-point filtering. The interval in a field-affine mask has pair inclusion $w(w-1)/(p(p-1))$; targetwise second moments support the pilot reduction but do not imply one fixed mask works at every target.

For the balanced auxiliary ring, two same-type primes exist among three. The small coefficient resultant gives injectivity of the thin box. The public projector and strict diagonal dominance bound every proposed quotient by $J=\lfloor H/g\rfloor$, forcing a congruence modulo $q^e>2(H+\|A_M\|_\infty J)$ to be an integer identity. The $m$ section arrays, $m+O(1)$ quotient arrays and fraction $2/3+o(1)$ thin rows at word width $(5/2+o(1))k$ yield $(20/3+o(1))mb$ bits; central-binomial packing gives $tS=(10/3+o(1))mb$. Root degrees and multiplicities are respectively $\sqrt S\,\mathrm{polylog}(S)$ and $\mathrm{polylog}(S)$. The short-vector setup costs $2^{O((b/L^2)\log b)}$, below the reported scale. The one-pilot proof conditions on the size-biased seed law and needs fresh successful-row laws under adaptive histories. Balancing $b/m+(10K/3)mb/\sqrt L$ with $2b/m$ gives $2\sqrt{10K/3}=11.3148681370\ldots$, with $K=2\pi\sqrt{14\log_2 90}/(9\ln2)$. This reconstructs the conditional coefficient; the premise supplying the recursive uniform estimates was not independently proved here.

The v4 marginal planting bound, target derivative tests, two-orbit moments and mixed-characteristic traces concern narrower prediction or decoding models. They supply neither arbitrary signed label weights nor the universal worst-case row rank. In particular the twelfth moment over $\mathbb F_{13}$ uses the exponent $p-1$ and a count below $p$; that finite decoder does not have bounded degree at growing primes.

## Formal evidence

The public top-level statement quantifies a primitive machine before curve inputs, encodes a unique scalar answer and assumes an explicit proposition. That proposition has two fields. Its closed part takes a schedule generator and a charged entry evaluator; its one-step part quantifies the step before boundary assignments, specifies child lifts, guarded recovery, and access to individual branch records. This is a more detailed formal premise than citing only the upstream SAT theorem. The public OpenAI scope note describes a SAT theorem with a $(\log\log n)^2$ factor. It does not certify every quantitative resource bound used here.
