# One expansion with a growing number of vertex groups

## Theorem

Under the explicit Cauchy–Hermite construction in OpenAI Appendix D, take the arithmetic core of LZR Theorem 4.3 with $m=\Theta((\log b)^{1/4})$, $S=\Theta(mb)$, $\lambda=\lceil\log S\rceil$, $\beta=\Theta(b)$ and one terminal per boundary host. A pointwise expansion of the form (5.3) exists with

$$
 \log_2|\mathcal I|=O\left(S\sqrt{\frac{\log(2m)}{\lambda}}+\log S\right).
$$

A term has $O(m)$ stopped tensors, each with $O(\beta/m)$ terminal bits. Its coefficients have polynomial guard precision, its tensors are canonical polynomial representatives at creation precision, and a pinned entry costs $(S+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}2^{O(S/\sqrt\lambda)}$ time and polynomial space in $S+\mathcal L$. Here $\mathcal L$ is description length. The new expansion at the original arity costs $2^{O(b\sqrt{\log\log b}/(\log b)^{1/4})}$. The residual contraction still costs $2^{O(b/\sqrt{\log\log b})}$.

## Derivation

Substitute $R=2^{\lceil\log\max(64,4m)\rceil}$ groups for 64 **at the first step only**. Write $u=\lfloor\epsilon\lambda/\log R\rfloor$, $a=2u$, $W=2^a$, $D=R^{2a}$, with $\epsilon=2^{-17}$. For large $b$, $u\ge1$, $D^2\le S^{8\epsilon}$, $WD\le S^{6\epsilon}$, and all arrays and labels can be enumerated in polynomial time. The extension degree is $4\lambda$. The scalar continuation uses 64 groups. The constant-size initial range is handled directly.

Let $M$ be the root's internal edge count. If $M\le H=\lceil S/\lfloor\sqrt\lambda\rfloor\rceil$, keep a single tensor. Each terminal host retains its distinct internal port, so $\beta\le2M=O(\beta/m)$ in this branch at the stated tuning; asymptotically the branch cannot occur for the full specified boundary. Its evaluation costs $2^H$ times a polynomial. Otherwise $M>H$ and $(a+2)D^2\lceil S^{9/10}\rceil=o(H)$. The packing guards $M\ge WD$, $2W\le\lceil S^{9/10}\rceil$, and $M(2W-1)+1\le 2^{4\lambda}$ also hold.

Choose labels in $[R]^a$ by conditional expectation. For oriented edge-class loads $X_\gamma$ and terminal-symbol loads $Y_{ig}$, use the squared-deviation potential of Lemma 5.1 with centers $M/D$ and $\beta/R$ and terminal multiplier $M\Delta/\beta$ where $\Delta=\lceil S^{9/10}\rceil$. Disjoint edges contribute zero covariance. The total edge variance is at most $2M\Delta$. Since the terminal hosts are distinct, the summed terminal variance over the $R$ bins is at most $\beta$ per coordinate. The potential is at most $(a+2)M\Delta$. Try each of the $R^a$ candidate labels per vertex, enumerating pairs of edges or hosts to compute exact conditional expectations. Thus $X_\gamma\le M/D+\sqrt{(a+2)M\Delta}$ and $Y_{ig}\le\beta/R+\sqrt{(a+2)\beta}=O(\beta/m)$. All polynomial exponents here are absolute.

Round the maximum load up to a multiple of $W$. Each class now has at most $3M/D$ slots. As in the original packing proof, for an unordered pair word with $j$ unequal coordinates, a packet allocates $2^{a-j}$ slots to each of the $2^j$ orientations. There are at most $3M/W$ packets in total. A given symbol has at most $3M/R$ real ports on either side at each coordinate. A child has at most $6M/R\le M/8$ internal edges. The total is $2M$. A packet still involves **at most two groups at any one coordinate**, irrespective of $R$. This is why the $6M/D_t$ bound on modes of each weight has no factor $R$. Here $D_t=W/2^{v_2(t)}$.

For a fixed boundary assignment pin the hosts. This leaves the same packet allocation at every assignment. The deformation polynomial has degree at most $M(2W-1)$ and its translated samples are integral as in the original consecutive-grid calculation. Apply the same multiplicities $\lceil3M/u\rceil(u-v_2(t))$, lift set and precision $p_t=P_0+8\lceil3M/u\rceil D_t+8M(a+1)$. Since $D_t\ge2^u$, $P_0\le S=O(M\sqrt\lambda)$, and $2^u$ dominates every fixed power of $\lambda$, $p_t=O(M D_t/u)$. The weighted partition bound is $\exp(O(\sqrt{M p_t/D_t}))=\exp(O(M/\sqrt u))$. There are polynomially many nodes and lifts. One root choice gives a product of $R$ child contractions; **stop them all here**. Enumeration log cost is $O(S/\sqrt u+\lambda)$.

Each child contributes the polynomial of canonical integer coordinates in $[0,2^{p_t})$, with $U$-degree below $4\lambda$ and $z$-degree below $\ell$. Hermite's coefficient valuation is at least $-6\lceil3M/u\rceil D_t-4M(a+1)$; the negative bound and $p_t$ are $S^{O(1)}$. Changing a whole child sum by a multiple of $2^{p_t}$ survives multiplication by integral siblings and the loss on its own parent coefficient. Individual distributed terms need not be integral before recombination.

To query one stopped entry, pin its terminals and continue at absolute depth $j=1$ with $u_j=\lfloor\epsilon\lambda/2^j\rfloor$ and $a_j=a+2\sum_{i=1}^j u_i$. Keep the threshold $H$. Active descendants have $H<M_j\le S8^{-j}$, $j\le(\log\lambda)/6+O(1)$, $u_j=\Omega(\lambda^{5/6})$, $a_j\le4\epsilon\lambda$ and polynomial precisions. Inductively the input precision at depth $j$ is at most $S\lambda^{O(1)}2^{a_{j-1}}$, starting with the actual child creation precision. The next $D_t\ge 2^{a_{j-1}+u_j}$ absorbs this inherited term in $O(M_j D_t/u_j)$. Thus the cumulative log branching on a scalar path is $O(\sum_{j\ge1} S8^{-j}/\sqrt{\lambda/2^j})=O(S/\sqrt\lambda)$. Stopped leaves cost $2^H$ and the largest packet entry costs $\exp(O(2^{4\epsilon\lambda}\log S))=2^{o(H)}$. Depth-first scalar traversal uses polynomial space. This argument never restarts the recursion at a smaller root precision.

Reattach the $4m$ lookups. There are $n=O(m)$ residual vertices on $\beta$ binary edges and degree $O(\beta/m)$. In LZR's digit reconstruction take $h=\lfloor\beta/(n+1)\rfloor$, $K=n(4\lambda-1)+1$ and truncation $K\ell=O(m^2\lambda)=o(\beta)$. The digit-tuple overhead is $2^{O(m\lambda)}$; the exact integer coefficient cap is $\beta+nh+n\lceil\log(4\lambda\ell)\rceil+2=O(\beta)$. Decode before reducing modulo the monic extension polynomial; accumulate with the guard, then divide exactly. This completes the local improvement and explains its limit for deterministic end-to-end time.
